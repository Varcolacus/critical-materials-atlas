Research Library · Zinc (the host)

Zinc chain — the invisible host

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

A zinc smelter's everyday job is galvanizing steel. But gallium, germanium, indium and cadmium are all recovered from zinc ores and the residues of zinc smelting — so refining capacity and whether a smelter installs the recovery circuits, not any gallium "mine", gate that whole by-product cluster. The mine is the least concentrated stage; the host is the leverage.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Zinc ore (diversified mine) → ② smelting to refined zinc (China ~half) — where gallium, germanium, indium and cadmium are recovered — → galvanizing / die-casting / brass, plus the trace-metal stream — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Zinc — the abundant, widely-mined host 4

The everyday base metal: mined across China, Peru, Australia and Mexico, mostly galvanizing steel — which is why zinc rarely makes a critical-materials list.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Zinc · USGS (2026) Annual world/US zinc mine and refined production, reserves, trade and uses. Abstract. USGS annual commodity summary for zinc — world and US mine and refined production, reserves, net import reliance and end uses, with galvanizing the largest. The 2026 MCS PDF returned HTTP 403 on two passes and could not be retrieved; scope stated from the USGS series format, with the use split corroborated by the USGS Zinc Statistics page below (about three-fourths of zinc consumed as metal, led by galvanized coatings). Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade and price data; sphalerite (zinc sulphide) the principal ore.
  • USGS — Zinc Statistics and Information · USGS USGS hub for monthly, annual and historical zinc production, trade and use data. Summary. USGS National Minerals Information Center hub for zinc: monthly industry surveys, annual Mineral Commodity Summaries (from 1996) and Minerals Yearbooks. Zinc is the fourth-most-produced metal; roughly three-fourths is consumed as metal (galvanized coatings, brass/bronze, die-casting), one-fourth as compounds. Principal ore mineral: sphalerite. Data. USGS surveys and historical statistical series (production, trade, recycling), 1994–present.
  • International Lead and Zinc Study Group (ILZSG) · ILZSG (2025) Intergovernmental supply, demand and stock statistics and forecasts for zinc. Summary. The International Lead and Zinc Study Group — 25+ member governments — publishes a Monthly Statistical Bulletin covering zinc mine and metal production, usage, concentrate and refined-metal trade, secondary recovery, prices and stocks, alongside a World Zinc Factbook and regular supply-demand forecasts. Data. Member-government returns compiled into monthly and annual zinc supply-demand balances and stock data.
  • International Zinc Association (IZA) · IZA Global zinc industry body: galvanizing, uses, market data and sustainability. Summary. The International Zinc Association, the global zinc industry body (offices in the US, Belgium, India and China), documents zinc's uses — galvanizing and corrosion protection foremost, plus die-casting, brass, batteries and chemicals — a market it puts near $40 billion a year, with recycling and sustainability programmes. Data. Industry-body reporting; production/market figures (e.g. ~13.5 Mt high-grade zinc in 2019 plus ~6 Mt recycled) — no primary dataset.

② Smelting & by-product recovery — where the cluster is gated 5

Gallium, germanium, indium and cadmium have no mines of their own — they are pulled from the residues of zinc smelting, if the recovery circuits are installed.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Gallium · USGS (2026) Gallium recovery, world production, uses and import reliance — the host-coupling source. Abstract. USGS annual commodity summary for gallium — a by-product metal (recovered in bauxite/alumina refining and zinc processing, per the recovery literature below), with China dominating primary output and 2023 export controls in force. The 2026 MCS PDF returned HTTP 403 on two passes; the USGS Gallium Statistics page (retrieved) confirms no US primary production, imports from Canada/China/Germany/Japan, and ~79% of use as GaAs/GaN/GaP wafers. Data. USGS annual gallium canvass and trade data; the zinc/bauxite by-product coupling is corroborated by Reuter, Whitworth and Moss/JRC below.
  • USGS — Gallium Statistics and Information · USGS USGS hub for gallium supply, use and material-flow data. Summary. USGS gallium hub: no low-purity gallium is produced in the United States; imports come from Canada, China, Germany and Japan. About 79% of gallium is consumed as GaAs, GaN and GaP wafers — 74% in integrated circuits, the remainder in optoelectronics. Publication archive spans 1996–2026. Data. USGS gallium surveys and material-flow statistics; import-source and end-use breakdowns.
  • Lead, zinc and their minor elements: enablers of a circular economy · Reuter, Matusewicz & van Schaik — ERZMETALL (2015) How zinc/lead smelting acts as the carrier that releases minor by-product metals. Abstract. Reuter, Matusewicz & van Schaik (World of Metallurgy — ERZMETALL 68(3), 2015, pp. 132–146) argue lead and zinc are the "carrier metals" of a circular economy: their smelting circuits carry and release vital minor elements and — with copper, nickel and cobalt — form the metallurgical infrastructure through which by-product metals are recovered, or lost. Data. Metallurgical process/flowsheet analysis of carrier-metal systems — synthesis, no single primary dataset.
  • Review on advances in mineral processing technologies for critical metal recovery from mining and processing wastes · Whitworth et al., Cleaner Engineering and Technology (2022) Recovery routes for critical by-product metals from mining/processing wastes. Abstract. Whitworth, Forbes, Verster, Jokovic, Awatey & Parbhakar-Fox (Cleaner Engineering and Technology, 2022) review mineral-processing routes to recover eleven critical metals — including gallium, germanium and indium — from mining and processing wastes, arguing re-processing of tailings and residues can help meet demand for metals both important and vulnerable to supply disruption. Data. Literature review of mineral-processing/recovery technologies for eleven critical metals (Co, Ga, Ge, In, Li, Ni, Re, Te, Sn, W, V).
  • Modeling indium extraction, supply, price, use and recycling 1930–2200 using the WORLD7 model · Sverdrup, van Allen & Haraldsson, Natural Resources Research (2024) Long-horizon dynamic model of indium supply as a zinc by-product. Abstract. Sverdrup, van Allen & Haraldsson (Natural Resources Research 33(2), 2024, pp. 539–570) model indium extraction, supply, price, use and recycling 1930–2200 with the WORLD7 model. Because indium is a zinc by-product its supply is inelastic; they estimate about 312,000 t ultimately extractable and warn photovoltaic demand could outrun it. Data. WORLD7 system-dynamics integrated-assessment model (mass balance plus supply-demand price dynamics); open access (Creative Commons).

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 3

List methodologies and assessments that classify the zinc-hosted by-products — and the policy backdrop to the export controls.
  • European Commission — Critical Raw Materials · European Commission (2023) The EU's CRM criticality assessment and the 2024 Critical Raw Materials Act. Summary. European Commission Critical Raw Materials assessment: the 2023 exercise screened 70 candidates down to 34 CRMs on Economic Importance × Supply Risk, evaluating the highest-risk "bottleneck" supply stage. Gallium and germanium — zinc-hosted by-products — are listed CRMs; the 2024 CRM Act frames EU supply security. Data. EU criticality methodology (Economic Importance × Supply Risk) applied over 70 candidate raw materials.
  • Critical metals in strategic energy technologies (supply-chain bottlenecks) · Moss, Tzimas, Kara et al., JRC (2011) Early EU assessment classing indium/gallium as by-product supply-chain bottlenecks. Abstract. Moss, Tzimas, Kara, Willis & Kooroshy (JRC, 2011) assess metal-supply bottlenecks for six low-carbon energy technologies and flag 14 metals; indium, tellurium and gallium rank highest and are identified as "by-products from the processing of other metals" — their supply bounded by the host metal, not by their own demand. Data. Stress-ratio method: projected EU 2020–2030 demand divided by 2010 world production, per metal.
  • CSIS — Critical Minerals Security Program · CSIS Think-tank hub on minerals-supply security and export-control competition. Summary. CSIS Critical Minerals Security Program: analysis and commentary on building resilient US mineral supply chains through market-led solutions and allied partnerships, countering concentration (notably China) across mineral production and processing — the policy backdrop to the zinc-hosted by-product export controls. Data. Programme analysis, reports, commentary and events — no primary dataset.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 3

The datasets the chain's own figures are built from — and the reason the by-products stay invisible in trade.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral HS6 trade (zinc ore 260800, unwrought zinc 790111) used for the chain's trade panel. Summary. CEPII BACI V202601 (updated 30 Jan 2026) reconstructs bilateral trade for ~200 countries and ~5,000 HS6 products (HS92 back to 1995, through 2024) from UN Comtrade, converting CIF imports to FOB and reliability-weighting reporters to reconcile mirror discrepancies. Data. UN Comtrade declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (Gaulier & Zignago, CEPII).
  • UN Comtrade · United Nations The UN's official bilateral merchandise-trade database, by country, commodity (HS) and year. Summary. UN Comtrade is the United Nations' repository of official bilateral merchandise-trade statistics by reporter, partner, commodity (HS) and year — the reported import/export declarations that BACI reconciles into the atlas's zinc-trade figures. The live portal is a client-side app shell (renders "Loading…" to a fetch) — scope stated from the database's documented role. Data. National customs declarations submitted to the UN Statistics Division; HS bilateral by country and year.
  • BGS World Mineral Statistics · British Geological Survey Long-run world zinc mine-production series, by country. Summary. The British Geological Survey's World Mineral Statistics — compiled by BGS and predecessor bodies since 1913 — carries production and trade for 70+ commodities by country, with an extractable archive spanning 1970–2021 (latest edition 2020–2024); an independent long-run cross-check on zinc mine output. Data. BGS compilation of national production/trade returns; downloadable series plus an OGC API.

Gaps & good next searches

  • No clean HS6 code isolates the by-products. BACI carries only zinc ore (260800) and unwrought zinc (790111); the gallium, germanium, indium and cadmium recovered alongside hide in shared minor-metal baskets. The chain's whole point — by-product recovery — is invisible in trade data and can only be read through production and the recovery literature.
  • By-product recovery economics is under-covered. A dedicated source on why Western zinc smelters stopped recovering gallium and indium (recovery-circuit capex vs the Chinese price) would strengthen the "recovery circuits, not new mines" argument. Next: "companion metal by-product supply Resources Policy Nassar", "gallium germanium indium recovery zinc smelter economics".
  • The ~half-China refining figure rests on aggregates. ILZSG/USGS give the national refined-zinc share, but a smelter-level capacity map — which smelters actually run recovery circuits — is the missing dataset that would pin down how much by-product capacity exists outside China.
  • A hand-listed SSRN working paper on indium-as-zinc-by-product could not be verified as a live, real record on this pass and was dropped rather than cite an unconfirmed link; the Sverdrup WORLD7 indium model above covers the same ground with a citable, open-access record.
