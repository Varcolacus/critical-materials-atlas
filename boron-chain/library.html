Research Library · Boron

Boron / borate chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

The world's boron sits in the ground in just two places — Turkey, which holds the world's largest borate reserves, and one large mine in California. Between them they supply most of the glass, fertilizer, borosilicate and NdFeB magnets that boron enables. Here the chokepoint is the deposit itself, not a plant that capital can rebuild.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Borate reserves (Turkey holds the largest + one California mine) → refined borax / boric acid / boron oxides → ② end uses: glass & fibreglass, fertilizer, borosilicate, NdFeB magnets, neutron capture — then the cross-cutting production and trade series the shares are built from.

① Borate reserves — the geological chokepoint 5

Refining — borax, boric acid & boron oxides 0

The first-refined forms — and, as in most atlas chains, the thinnest evidence base of the stages.
No dedicated public source maps the refining step on its own: borate ore is processed to borax, boric acid and boron oxides largely by the same two producers (Eti Maden and Rio Tinto), so the stage is not separately concentrated beyond the reserves that feed it. The Turkish-processing detail sits inside the paywalled 1992 mining-and-processing paper above; see Gaps & good next searches for the Rio Tinto Boron, California lead worth chasing.

② End uses — glass, fertilizer, magnets, nuclear 2

Where the borates go: borosilicate and glass fibre (the largest use), the fertilizer micronutrient, NdFeB magnets and neutron capture.
  • Fibers, 5. Synthetic Inorganic · Ullmann's Encyclopedia of Industrial Chemistry (2000) Reference chapter covering borosilicate / glass-fibre chemistry — boron's largest end use. Scope. Ullmann's Encyclopedia of Industrial Chemistry chapter (E. Fitzer, 2000) on synthetic inorganic fibres, including the borosilicate glass and glass-fibre chemistry that is boron's single largest use. Chapter paywalled (Wiley, HTTP 403) — full abstract not retrievable; scope taken from the citation. Data. Encyclopaedic reference chapter — no primary dataset.
  • The Role of Chemistry in the Development of Boron Neutron Capture Therapy of Cancer · Angewandte Chemie Int. Ed. (1993) Peer-reviewed review of boron neutron capture therapy — a real, niche boron end use. Abstract. Review (M. F. Hawthorne, 1993) of the chemistry behind boron neutron capture therapy: boron-10 captures a thermal neutron and emits energetic helium and lithium ions that travel only about one cell diameter in tissue, destroying a targeted cell. Surveys boron-delivery vehicles and their synthesis across chemistry, biology, nuclear physics and medicine, with representative tumour-localisation data. Data. Interdisciplinary literature review with representative tumour-localisation data — no new primary dataset.

Across the chain — production & trade data 4

The production and trade series the chain's shares and its duopoly reading are built from.
  • Boron Statistics and Information · USGS National Minerals Information Center USGS boron data portal: US (California) and world supply, demand and pricing, linked to the full MCS and Minerals Yearbook archives. Summary. USGS National Minerals Information Center portal for boron: worldwide supply, demand and material-flow statistics. States that US boron is produced only in California and that the US and Turkey lead world production; links the Mineral Commodity Summaries (1996–2026), the Minerals Yearbook and the historical-statistics series. Data. USGS voluntary producer / consumer surveys, aggregated into the MCS and Minerals Yearbook series.
  • Historical Statistics for Mineral and Material Commodities · USGS (Data Series 140) USGS long-run US/world production, trade and price history (Data Series 140), covering boron. Summary. USGS Data Series 140: long-run US statistics on about 90 nonfuel mineral commodities, many back to 1900, tracking production, imports, exports, stocks, consumption and unit prices (nominal and inflation-adjusted). Boron is included; figures are standardised to metric tons and dollars per metric ton for consistency. Data. USGS compilation from 18,000+ producer / consumer establishments via surveys, site visits and agency / trade-association coordination.
  • BGS World Mineral Statistics — borates · British Geological Survey (2024) Long-run global mine-production series by country, including borates. Summary. BGS World Mineral Statistics: annual global production data for 70+ mineral commodities by country, the current volume covering 2020–2024, with the dataset extending back to 1913 (online from 1970). Borates are among the commodities covered, giving an independent long-run read on the Turkey / US supply split. Data. BGS compilation from government departments, national statistical offices, commodity authorities and company reports; PDF, Excel and JSON API.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6) used for the chain's borate trade panel. Summary. CEPII BACI: reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF freight costs to FOB-equivalent values and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Used here for HS 252800 (natural borates) and 281000 (boron oxides / boric acid). Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
