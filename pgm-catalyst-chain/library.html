Platinum-group-metal / catalyst chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Platinum, palladium and rhodium clean car exhaust and coat fuel cells — and their mine supply comes from essentially two places, South Africa's Bushveld Complex and Russia. But this chain breaks the usual pattern twice: its own scrap relieves the supply, and the EV transition — not a supply cut — is the structural threat. The chokepoint here is geological; the risk sits on the demand side.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Mine (South Africa's Bushveld + Russia) → ② refining & the market balance → ③ use (autocatalysts, fuel cells & the demand question) → ④ the recycling loop — then the cross-cutting criticality frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Mine — South Africa's Bushveld + Russia 7

The geological base: about 78% of world PGM reserves in one South African province, with Russia the only other large primary source.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Platinum-Group Metals · USGS (2025) Annual reserves, mine production and US import-source data for the six PGMs. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2024 world mine production ~190,000 kg palladium and 170,000 kg platinum, led by South Africa (the leading producer of mined PGMs) and Russia (leading palladium). South Africa holds ~63 of >81 million kg of world reserves (~78%); the Bushveld Complex hosts the largest reserves and resources. About 120,000 kg of platinum-plus-palladium was recovered from scrap in 2024. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade, price and recycling data.
  • Global platinum group element resources, reserves and mining — A critical assessment · Science of the Total Environment Critical assessment of the geological base of PGE resources, reserves and mining. Summary. Peer-reviewed critical assessment of global platinum-group-element resources, reserves and mining constraints, centred on South Africa's Bushveld Complex — the dominant host of world PGE reserves. (ScienceDirect served only a JavaScript shell on this pass; scope taken from the paper's title and journal record.) Data. Assessment/review of published reserve, resource and mine-production data — no new experimental dataset.
  • The Platinum Group Element Deposits of the Bushveld Complex in South Africa · Johnson Matthey Technology Review Geological review of the Bushveld Complex's PGE deposits — the source of the bulk of world reserves. Abstract. The Bushveld Complex holds enough platinum-group elements to supply world demand for many decades or a century using current mining techniques. Reserves and resources reported by miners extend only ~20 years, but geological evidence continues far deeper — of order 350 million oz of platinum per 1 km of depth, against ~5 million oz mined annually. Explains reserves vs resources vs deposits. Data. Geological review of Bushveld reef stratigraphy and company-reported reserves/resources.
  • Mapping global platinum supply chain and assessing potential supply risks · Frontiers in Energy Research (2023) Network mapping and supply-risk assessment for platinum specifically. Abstract. Maps the global platinum upstream supply chain — 28 mines, 15 companies, 11 countries — and its trade network, scoring disruption risk across concentration, governance and environmental dimensions. Finds both the supply and trade networks sparse and poorly connected, so the overall network has weak resistance to disruption. Data. Network analysis on UN Comtrade trade, HHI concentration, World Governance and Environmental Performance indices, and company ESG scores.
  • South Africa's Blackouts Threaten Platinum Mine Supplies · Bloomberg (2023) Reporting on Eskom load-shedding's direct hit to Bushveld mine output. Summary. News report on how South African electricity load-shedding (Eskom) directly threatens Bushveld platinum-mine output. (Bloomberg article behind a paywall — not retrievable; noted from the headline only.) Data. Trade-press reporting — no primary dataset.
  • Load-shedding threatens global platinum supply · Daily Investor South African power-crisis impact on platinum production, with production-decline estimates. Summary. Reports that South African load-shedding threatens global platinum supply, citing production-decline estimates of roughly 12–20% for South Africa and about 9% globally over 2023–2027. (Page behind a Cloudflare/JS challenge — figures noted from the source's own trailer; full text not retrievable.) Data. Trade-press reporting citing analyst production-decline estimates.
  • South African PGM Supply Risk: The Geological Reality in 2026 · Discovery Alert (2026) 2026 market analysis on the Bushveld's structural supply risk amid new demand. Summary. Trade-press analysis arguing South African PGM supply risk is a geological reality that policy, capital and diversification cannot quickly resolve, while new demand emerges from hydrogen electrolysers, data-centre servers and electronics connectors. Data. Market-analysis commentary; no primary dataset.

② Refining & the market balance 9

Six co-occurring metals separated in a handful of complex refineries — and the market data that tracks each metal's supply/demand balance.
  • USGS World Minerals Outlook — palladium & platinum through 2029 (SIR 2025-5021) · USGS (2025) Forward supply/demand outlook, including EV-driven demand risk for autocatalyst metals. Abstract. USGS Scientific Investigations Report estimating mine-production capacity through 2029 for eight commodities including palladium and platinum. Finds platinum capacity growing only stably-to-moderately above current output, while much palladium/platinum supply comes from countries with concentrated, at-risk production — informing supply adequacy as demand shifts. Data. USGS capacity-utilisation modelling of reported mine production to 2029, with a companion data release.
  • Supply and demand of platinum group metals and strategies for sustainable management · Renewable & Sustainable Energy Reviews (2024) Review of PGM supply/demand balance and sustainable-management strategies. Summary. Review of the platinum-group-metal supply–demand balance and strategies for sustainable management — recycling, substitution and efficiency — across the autocatalyst-dominated demand base. (ScienceDirect served only a JavaScript shell; scope taken from the paper's title and journal record.) Data. Literature review / synthesis — no primary dataset.
  • World Platinum Investment Council — Platinum Quarterly · WPIC / Metals Focus Quarterly platinum supply/demand balance — the standard market dataset. Summary. WPIC's quarterly platinum supply-and-demand balance, researched independently by Metals Focus — the standard market dataset. Current headline: a fourth consecutive market deficit forecast for 2026 (~297 koz), with above-ground stocks nearing three months of cover. Data. Metals Focus supply/demand accounting (mine, recycling, demand by segment), quarterly.
  • WPIC Platinum Quarterly Q4 2025 (PDF) · World Platinum Investment Council (2026) Full-quarter data release with the multi-year deficit forecast. Summary. Platinum recorded a third consecutive deficit in 2025 of 1,082 koz — the largest in the WPIC series since 2013 — as total supply fell 1% (mine −4%, recycling +10%) and demand rose 1%. The 2026 forecast was revised from balance to a 240 koz deficit. Data. Metals Focus quarterly supply/demand estimates for platinum, 2013–2026f.
  • Johnson Matthey PGM Market Report, May 2026 (PDF) · Johnson Matthey (2026) Twice-yearly PGM market report — per-metal supply/demand and mine/recycling splits. Summary. All five PGMs were in deficit in 2025 as primary supply from South Africa and North America contracted through rationalisation and mine closures, and autocatalyst recycling stayed weak, while resilient internal-combustion-vehicle output and firm industrial demand (petrochemicals, electronics) held demand up. Platinum, rhodium and ruthenium prices more than doubled. Data. Johnson Matthey's proprietary per-metal supply/demand accounting (platinum, palladium, rhodium, ruthenium, iridium).
  • Johnson Matthey publishes 2026 PGM Market Report · Johnson Matthey (2026) Summary release of the May 2026 PGM report. Summary. Press release for the 2026 PGM Market Report: platinum, ruthenium and iridium in deficit; palladium and rhodium closer to small surpluses; with growth demand from data-centre expansion and green-hydrogen (iridium) electrolysis. Data. Press release summarising the JM report — no primary dataset.
  • Johnson Matthey 2026 PGM Report: Platinum Deficit and Palladium Surplus · Discovery Alert (2026) Digest of the May 2026 JM report's per-metal balances. Summary. Trade-press digest of Johnson Matthey's 2026 PGM report, explaining platinum's deficit and why palladium and rhodium are shifting toward surplus. Data. Secondary digest of the JM report; no primary dataset.
  • World Platinum Investment Council — 2 to 5 Year View · WPIC Medium-term platinum supply/demand scenario analysis. Summary. WPIC medium-term outlook: platinum expected to stay in deficit, averaging ~331 koz per year from 2026f–2030f, with demand spanning automotive, industrial, jewellery, investment, hydrogen and fuel-cell uses. Data. WPIC / Metals Focus scenario projections to 2029–2030.
  • Fourth consecutive platinum market deficit forecast for 2026 · PR Newswire / WPIC Press-release detail on the ~297 koz platinum deficit forecast for 2026. Summary. WPIC press release: a fourth consecutive platinum market deficit is forecast for 2026, with supply falling ~297 koz short of demand and above-ground stocks depleting to about three months of cover. Data. WPIC / Metals Focus Q1 2026 estimates — press release.

③ Use — autocatalysts, fuel cells & the demand question 4

Autocatalysts set most PGM demand — which is exactly why the demand side, not the mine, is the risk.
  • Platinum Group Metal Catalysts Supply Chain Deep Dive Assessment · U.S. Department of Energy (2022) US government deep-dive on PGM catalyst supply-chain vulnerability, incl. fuel-cell demand. Abstract. US Department of Energy deep-dive (Executive Order 14017) on the platinum-group-metal catalyst supply chain from extraction to recycling. Flags reliance on scarce, by-product PGMs from concentrated foreign mines; a potential decline in autocatalyst scrap as combustion vehicles retire; and immature recovery technology for PEM electrolyser and fuel-cell PGMs. Data. DOE industrial-base assessment synthesising production, trade, price-volatility and recycling data.
  • Will there be enough platinum for a large deployment of fuel cell electric vehicles? · International Journal of Hydrogen Energy Models platinum-supply adequacy under large-scale FCEV rollout. Summary. Models whether platinum supply is adequate for large-scale deployment of fuel-cell electric vehicles, weighing catalyst loadings, recycling and mine supply against rollout scenarios. (ScienceDirect served only a JavaScript shell; scope taken from the paper's title and journal record.) Data. Scenario / material-flow modelling of platinum supply vs FCEV demand.
  • Critical Iridium Demands arising from future Expansion of Proton Exchange Membrane Electrolysis · arXiv (2025) Quantifies iridium demand growth from PEM electrolyser scale-up. Abstract. Models iridium demand for green-hydrogen PEM electrolysis under business-as-usual and IEA net-zero pathways to 2050, including end-of-life replacement and recycling. Finds shortages could emerge as early as 2030; meeting net-zero targets needs major catalyst-efficiency gains and access to roughly 30% of annual global iridium output. Data. Recursive model on IEA capacity targets, iridium loadings, electrolyser lifetimes, 70–97% recycling and a ~7.5 t/yr supply estimate.
  • Platinum Demand Scenarios Show Hydrogen's Fatal Constraint · CleanTechnica (2025) Commentary connecting hydrogen-economy platinum demand scenarios to the supply base. Summary. Opinion piece arguing platinum scarcity is a binding constraint on mass hydrogen fuel-cell vehicle adoption: even modest penetration would absorb most global platinum supply and lift prices, favouring batteries over fuel cells. Data. Commentary / scenario reasoning — no primary dataset.

④ The recycling loop — PGMs come back 4

The relief valve most critical materials lack: spent catalytic converters and fuel cells are economically worth recovering.
  • A new approach to recover platinum-group metals from spent catalytic converters via iron matte · Resources, Conservation and Recycling Pyrometallurgical route recovering PGMs from spent autocatalysts via iron matte. Summary. Presents a pyrometallurgical iron-matte process to collect platinum-group metals from spent catalytic converters at high recovery, an alternative to conventional smelting-only routes. (ScienceDirect served only a JavaScript shell; scope taken from the paper's title and journal record.) Data. Laboratory experimental study of an iron-matte PGM-collection process.
  • Extraction of platinum group metals from catalytic converters · Heliyon Recent survey of extraction techniques for PGMs from end-of-life catalytic converters. Abstract. Reviews extraction of platinum-group metals from spent catalytic converters. PGMs' chemical stability at high temperature makes them widely demanded across industry, but gradual reserve depletion makes recycling of PGM-bearing waste essential; recovering spent automotive catalysts is more cost-effective and environmentally sustainable than mining from natural ore. Data. Literature review of pyro-/hydrometallurgical PGM recovery routes from spent autocatalysts.
  • A Critical Review of the Hydrometallurgy and Pyrometallurgical Recovery Processes of PGMs from End-of-Life Fuel Cells · PMC / NCBI Review of PGM recovery routes from spent PEM fuel cells. Abstract. Critical review of pyrometallurgical and hydrometallurgical routes to recover platinum-group metals from end-of-life PEM fuel-cell components. Favours hydrometallurgy over pyrometallurgy on environmental grounds — avoiding harmful high-temperature emissions — weighing health, technical, economic and future-opportunity aspects of sustainable PGM recycling. Data. Critical literature review of PGM recovery from spent fuel cells.
  • Studies on the Formation of Catalytically Active PGM Nanoparticles for the Recycling of Spent Catalysts · PMC / NCBI Basic-science study underpinning catalyst-recycling process design. Abstract. Laboratory study of how platinum, palladium, rhodium and ruthenium nanoparticles form when precipitated from model acidic solutions, testing reducing agents, stabilising polymers and pH for their effect on particle size, shape and catalytic activity — groundwork for recovering PGMs from spent automotive catalysts. Data. Experimental precipitation study on model PGM solutions.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 6

EU/US assessments and industry frameworks that classify the whole chain's inputs.
  • DG ENTR — Report on Critical Raw Materials for the EU · European Commission JRC/RMIS (2014) Foundational EU criticality methodology and material list, including PGMs. Abstract. The EU ad-hoc working group's Report on Critical Raw Materials (May 2014): sets out the EU criticality methodology — economic importance against supply risk — and the resulting critical-raw-materials list, including the platinum-group metals. It is the foundation of later EU CRM assessments. Data. EU criticality methodology applied to candidate raw materials (economic importance, supply risk).
  • Raw Materials & the war in Ukraine — Platinum: impact assessment for supply security (JRC133245) · European Commission JRC (2023) EU platinum supply-risk briefing; South Africa supplies most EU platinum. Abstract. JRC/RMIS briefing on platinum supply security after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. South Africa mined nearly three-quarters of world platinum in 2021; Russia was second at ~10–13%. The EU imported about half its platinum supply; secondary (recycled) supply moderates risk, but availability from spent autocatalysts is set to decline. Data. JRC analysis of production, EU import shares and trade flows within the RMIS methodology.
  • Special Report 04/2026 — Critical raw materials for the energy transition · European Court of Auditors (2026) EU audit of critical-raw-materials policy delivery, covering the energy-transition metals including PGMs. Abstract. European Court of Auditors performance audit of whether EU actions secure critical raw materials. Finds the Critical Raw Materials Act sets a strategic course but rests on incomplete foundations — targets lacking justification, blind spots in trade data, and import-diversification, domestic-production and recycling efforts that have yet to deliver by 2030. Data. Performance audit of EU CRM policy, funding and strategic projects.
  • Critical Raw Materials Act — EU policy page · European Commission (ongoing) Official CRMA policy hub; PGMs among the EU strategic raw materials. Summary. European Commission policy hub for the Critical Raw Materials Act — the EU response to supply-disruption risk, setting the critical and strategic raw-materials lists (PGMs included) and benchmarks for domestic extraction, processing, recycling and diversified sourcing. Data. Policy page — no primary dataset.
  • IPA — International Platinum Group Metals Association: Sustainability Principles · IPA Industry sustainability framework for PGM miners and fabricators. Summary. International Platinum Group Metals Association page setting out the industry's sustainability principles — a decade-old framework in which PGM miners and fabricators commit to understanding life-cycle impacts and collaborating across the value chain. Data. Industry-association framework — no primary dataset.
  • IPA — Fact sheets · IPA Reference fact sheets on each PGM's properties, production and uses. Summary. International Platinum Group Metals Association reference fact sheets covering each platinum-group metal's properties, production and end uses — background material rather than a dataset. Data. Industry-association reference sheets.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 1

The dataset the chain's trade layer is built from — read exporter shares as trading/refining hubs, not the mine base.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's trade layer. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Supplies the chain's unwrought and semi-manufactured PGM trade layer. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).

Gaps & good next searches

  • No CSIS or RMI-branded think-tank piece surfaced on PGMs this session — worth a targeted pass on csis.org and rmi.org for critical-minerals framing of platinum/palladium.
  • Benchmark Mineral Intelligence PGM coverage is subscription-gated and did not surface directly; a targeted search or direct site check would add a market-intelligence voice.
  • The South African DMRE Critical Minerals Strategy is referenced only secondhand (via Lexology / SFA Oxford summaries) — pull the DMRE strategy PDF itself.
  • Russian / Norilsk Nickel primary reporting is thin in open English sources given sanctions-era opacity; Nornickel's own investor disclosures (nornickel.com) are the next stop for palladium mine-side detail.
  • The EV-driven autocatalyst demand cliff for palladium/rhodium is asserted in JM/WPIC market reports but no dedicated academic or IEA study modelling the autocatalyst demand curve to 2035–2040 was retrieved — try "IEA autocatalyst platinum group metal demand electric vehicle 2035".
  • Refined vs recycled flows are invisible in trade data: BACI's HS 7110 codes mix unwrought/semi-manufactured metal and cannot separate the secondary (scrap-derived) supply that this chain turns on — the recycling loop needs its own dataset (WPIC/JM balances or national scrap statistics).
