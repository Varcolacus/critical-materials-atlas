Molybdenum — alloy metal & refining-catalyst chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Molybdenum turns ordinary steel into stainless, tool and superalloy steel and makes the catalysts that strip sulfur out of diesel and jet fuel — yet about half of it falls out of copper mines as a by-product, and the rest from primary mines spread across Chile, China, the United States and Peru. This is the atlas's low-drama case: essential to industry, but genuinely well-supplied — there is no single chokepoint to find.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① by-product & primary mines (raw source) → ② roasting to oxide & ferro-molybdenum (the midstream) → ③ superalloys, steel & desulfurisation catalysts (end use) — then the cross-cutting price / market frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

② Roasting to oxide & ferro-molybdenum — the midstream 0

Molybdenite is roasted to technical oxide, then to ferro-molybdenum or chemicals — and this is the thinnest evidence base in the chain.
No public, country-level source maps the roasting-to-oxide and ferro-molybdenum conversion stage on its own: a targeted search returned no dedicated supply-chain study and no production series for the midstream, only the industry life-cycle inventory (see #10) that documents the oxide and ferro-molybdenum products. Customs picks the stage up as roasted molybdenum ore (HS 261310) and ferro-molybdenum (HS 720270) — see the trade section. That thin midstream evidence is itself the finding — see Gaps & good next searches for the leads worth chasing (roaster/converter capacity by country; China Molybdenum Co. disclosures).

③ Superalloys, steel & clean-fuel catalysts — the uses 4

Where the metal earns its keep: over 80% of demand is alloying — stainless, tool and superalloy steel — plus the catalysts that clean fuels.
  • International Molybdenum Association — uses and supply · IMOA (2024) Trade-association overview of molybdenum's uses and supply. Summary. IMOA — the non-profit representing most of the world molybdenum industry — describes how small additions raise the strength, corrosion resistance and fire performance of carbon and stainless steels and nickel alloys, across construction, vehicles and oil-and-gas; molybdenum is also an essential trace nutrient. Data. Trade-association overview; no primary dataset on the landing page (market figures sit on the linked market page).
  • IMOA — Molybdenum uses in stainless and alloy steels · IMOA Technical detail on molybdenum in stainless, superalloy and tool steels, and the alloying share of demand. Summary. IMOA technical page: over 80% of molybdenum consumption goes to metal alloying. About 2% Mo in Type 316 stainless sharply improves pitting and crevice-corrosion resistance; in nickel superalloys it resists high-temperature creep; in carbon and tool steels it raises hardenability and wear resistance. Data. Trade-association technical summary citing the >80% alloying demand share; no primary dataset.
  • Molybdenum: A Key Component of Metal Alloys (Fact Sheet 2009-3106) · USGS (2009) Plain-language explainer on molybdenum's role in steel and superalloys. Summary. USGS fact sheet explaining molybdenum's role as an alloying element that strengthens and heat-proofs steels and superalloys, and its by-product and primary sources. (Full text not retrievable this pass — the DOI resolves to pubs.usgs.gov, which returned HTTP 403; scope taken from the fact sheet's title.) Data. USGS public explainer / fact sheet — descriptive, no primary dataset.
  • Flow Studies for Recycling Metal Commodities in the United States (Circular 1196) · USGS Material-flow / recycling studies for US metal commodities, including molybdenum recycling flows. Abstract. USGS circular of material-flow and recycling studies for US metal commodities; its molybdenum chapter is the basis for the chain's "recycling from steel scrap is significant" point. (Not retrievable this pass — pubs.usgs.gov returned HTTP 403; scope taken from the circular's title.) Data. USGS material-flow accounting of production, use, scrap and recycling by commodity.

Across the chain — price, market & sustainability 3

Why molybdenum is low-drama: the analysis and market data behind "price, not availability, is the concern".
  • Chromium, Copper, Manganese, Molybdenum, Nickel, and Steel Commodity Price Influences (OFR 2007-1257) · USGS (2007) Analysis of the drivers of prices across molybdenum and related alloy/steel metals. Abstract. USGS open-file report analysing the influences on prices of chromium, copper, manganese, molybdenum, nickel and steel — support for the chain's point that for molybdenum, price rather than availability is the usual concern. (Not retrievable this pass — pubs.usgs.gov returned HTTP 403; scope taken from the report's title.) Data. USGS price-driver analysis across chromium, copper, manganese, molybdenum, nickel and steel.
  • IMOA — Molybdenum Market Information · IMOA IMOA's market-data hub — first-use statistics, the price series, and the MolyReview / Annual Review. Summary. IMOA's market hub reports 2025 first-use of molybdenum at 671.8 million pounds and tracks roasted-molybdenite prices as quarterly averages from Argus Media; it carries production and consumption by region, MolyReview and the Annual Review, with detailed data member-only. Data. IMOA market surveys (first-use, regional production/consumption) plus an Argus Media price series; detailed data member-restricted.
  • IMOA — Life Cycle Assessment Program · IMOA (2024) Industry LCA/LCI data on molybdenum products — the one public source touching the oxide / ferro-molybdenum midstream. Summary. IMOA's LCA program (running since the 1990s) publishes life-cycle-inventory data for metallurgical molybdenum products — technical-grade oxide and ferro-molybdenum — and eight molybdenum chemicals, with a 2024 carbon-footprint update, following ISO 14040/14044. Data. IMOA LCI studies on oxide, ferro-molybdenum and chemical products; ISO 14040/14044 methodology.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 2

The datasets the chain's own figures are built from.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6) used for the chain's molybdenum-ore + ferro-molybdenum trade table. Summary. CEPII BACI reconciles UN Comtrade into bilateral flows for ~200 countries and ~5,000 HS6 products (HS92 1995–2024; version 202601, Jan 2026), removing CIF freight costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies into one figure per flow — the source for the chain's molybdenum-ore (261310) and ferro-molybdenum (720270) trade. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting), 1995–2024.
  • USGS — Molybdenum Statistics and Information · USGS National Minerals Information Center The USGS data portal indexing every annual and monthly molybdenum data release. Summary. The USGS National Minerals Information Center's molybdenum portal indexes every data release — Mineral Commodity Summaries (1996–2026), Minerals Yearbook (1994–2019) and monthly Mineral Industry Surveys — now migrating to the ScienceBase platform. It is the entry point to the production data behind the chain. Data. USGS NMIC annual and monthly molybdenum statistics series (production, imports, reserves, use).

Gaps & good next searches

  • No dedicated public source maps the midstream conversion (stage ②) — roasting to technical oxide, then ferro-molybdenum — on its own; only the industry LCA touches those products. Next: "roaster / ferro-molybdenum converter capacity by country", or China Molybdenum Co. (CMOC) disclosures to split China's primary-mine output from its by-product.
  • Molybdenum is the atlas's low-drama contrast case, so current price / press commentary is thin (it is not a crisis commodity). Search Fastmarkets / Reuters / MINING.com directly for the latest molybdenum price cycle.
  • No peer-reviewed criticality-index paper scoring molybdenum specifically (vs copper, vs the alloy-steel basket) surfaced; worth "molybdenum by-product copper supply elasticity criticality Resources Policy" given the "essential but not concentrated" framing.
  • The desulfurisation-catalyst link to the sulfur chain would be strengthened by a dedicated refinery-catalyst-market source (e.g. S&P Global / IHS Markit hydro-desulfurisation catalyst reports).
  • China's own primary-mine data (vs by-product) is folded into the USGS/BGS aggregate; a China-specific industry source would separate the two and test the diversification claim from the largest producer's side.
