# Molybdenum chain — research library

Molybdenum hardens and heat-proofs steel and enables refinery desulfurisation catalysts, but about half of it comes as a by-product of copper mining, spread across Chile, China, the US and Peru — a genuinely diversified, low-drama supply chain. A curated reference library for deeper study — public sources, gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source.

## Core references (already cited in the chain)
- [USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Molybdenum](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-molybdenum.pdf) — USGS (2026). Mine production, reserves, world output by country.
- [International Molybdenum Association — uses and supply](https://www.imoa.info/) — IMOA (2024). Trade-association overview of molybdenum uses and supply.
- [BGS, World Mineral Statistics — molybdenum](https://www2.bgs.ac.uk/mineralsuk/statistics/worldStatistics.html) — BGS (2024). Long-run mine-production series by country.
- [CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade](https://www.cepii.fr/CEPII/en/bdd_modele/bdd_modele_item.asp?id=37) — CEPII. Harmonised bilateral trade database used for the atlas's own flows.

## Government & agency reports
- [Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 (main volume)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/mcs2026) — USGS (2026). Cross-commodity reserves/production reference.
- [USGS — "Molybdenum: A Key Component of Metal Alloys" (Fact Sheet 2009-3106)](https://doi.org/10.3133/fs20093106) — USGS (2009). Plain-language explainer on molybdenum's role in steel and superalloys.
- [USGS — "Cr, Cu, Mn, Mo, Ni, and Steel Commodity Price Influences" (Open-File Report 2007-1257)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2007/1257/) — USGS (2007). Price-driver analysis across molybdenum and related alloy metals.
- [USGS — "Flow Studies for Recycling Metal Commodities in the United States" (Circular 1196)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/circ1196/) — USGS. Includes molybdenum recycling flow data referenced in the chain's "recycling from steel scrap" point.
- [USGS — "U.S. Mineral Dependence — Statistical Compilation, 1990-2010" (Open-File Report 2013-1184)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2013/1184/) — USGS (2013). Long-run US import-reliance statistics covering molybdenum.

## Gaps & good next searches
- Molybdenum is explicitly the atlas's "low-drama" contrast case — the flip side is that market/press coverage of it is thin (it isn't a crisis commodity), so Fastmarkets/Reuters-style current pricing commentary wasn't captured this pass; search directly for the latest price cycle.
- No peer-reviewed criticality-index paper scoring molybdenum specifically (vs. copper, vs. the alloy-steel basket) was found — worth a dedicated search given the "essential but not concentrated" framing.
- The desulfurisation-catalyst link to the sulfur/sulfuric-acid chain (cross-referenced in the record) would benefit from a dedicated refinery-catalyst-market source (e.g. IHS Markit / S&P Global catalyst market reports).
- China's own primary molybdenum mine data (vs. by-product) is available in USGS/BGS aggregate figures but a China-specific industry source (e.g. China Molybdenum Co. disclosures) was not pulled this pass.
