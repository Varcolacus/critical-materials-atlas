Research Library · Sulfur

Sulfur / sulfuric-acid chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Sulfuric acid is the most-produced industrial chemical on Earth, yet almost no one mines the sulfur it is made from — it falls out as a by-product of cleaning sulfur from oil, gas and metal ores, then dissolves phosphate rock into fertilizer and leaches the metals out of battery ores. The chokepoint runs backwards: as the world burns less fossil fuel, the by-product supply that feeds fertilizer and battery metals shrinks just as demand climbs.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① By-product recovery (oil/gas desulfurization + smelter SO₂) → ② what the acid feeds — fertilizer, food and battery-metal leaching → ③ the supply outlook, the backwards chokepoint — then the industry frameworks, criticality context and trade data that sit across every stage.

① By-product recovery — no one mines sulfur any more 2

Sulfur is produced because we clean fossil fuels and ores — not because anyone wants sulfur.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Sulfur · USGS (2026) Annual recovered-sulfur production, the by-product share, world output and end uses. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2025 US elemental-sulfur output ~7.6 Mt, recovered at petroleum refineries, gas-processing and coking plants; byproduct sulfuric acid came from five nonferrous-metal smelters. About 90% of sulfur consumed becomes sulfuric acid. World production ~84 Mt (China 19, US 8.1, Russia 7.5, Saudi Arabia 7.2). Data. USGS annual producer canvass (86 operations across 26 states) plus reported import, export, price and apparent-consumption data.
  • Materials Flow of Sulfur (Open-File Report 02-298) · USGS (2002) A full mine-to-use material-flow study of the US sulfur economy. Abstract. USGS material-flow study (Joyce A. Ober) tracing US sulfur from source to use. Argues that, unlike most minerals, essentially all North-American sulfur is an involuntary product of environmental measures — desulfurizing petroleum and natural gas and capturing smelter SO₂ — so the industry's problem is disposing of a growing excess supply, not sustaining production. Data. Material-flow accounting of the US sulfur economy (sources, forms, end uses) drawing on USGS production and consumption series.

② What the acid feeds — fertilizer, food & battery metals 1

Sulfuric acid's single largest use is dissolving phosphate rock into fertilizer — a food-security issue hiding inside a bulk chemical.
  • Fertilizers — Sustaining Global Food Supplies (Fact Sheet FS-155-99) · USGS (1999) USGS statement of the nutrient basis of fertilizers and the food-supply link. Summary. USGS fact sheet on soil fertility: nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium are the three primary crop nutrients, with some plant scientists counting sulfur a fourth for its benefit to plant health. Frames mineral fertilizers as underpinning global food supply — the food-security link the chain rests on. Data. Descriptive USGS fact sheet — nutrient overview and mineral-fertilizer background, no primary dataset.

③ The supply outlook — the backwards chokepoint 1

The load-bearing academic source for the "decarbonization drains sulfur" thesis.
  • Sulfur: A potential resource crisis that could stifle green technology and threaten food security as the world decarbonises · The Geographical Journal (2022) The peer-reviewed source for the sulfur supply-shortfall projection. Abstract. Maslin, Van Heerde & Day: over 80% of sulfur is recovered from desulfurizing fossil fuels, so decarbonization erodes supply while sulfuric-acid demand — phosphate fertilizer and battery-metal leaching — rises from ~246 Mt toward 400+ Mt, opening a projected shortfall of 100–320 Mt (40–130% of current output) by 2040. Data. Scenario model on a USGS 2021 sulfur baseline (~81 Mt ≈ 246 Mt sulfuric acid), with supply and demand projected to 2040 under decarbonization pathways.

Across the chain — industry, criticality & policy 3

The industry bodies behind the sulphur/sulphuric-acid trade and the criticality frame that leaves sulfur untracked.
  • The Sulphur Institute (TSI) · TSI The global association for sulphur and sulphuric-acid professionals. Summary. The Sulphur Institute (Washington DC): the global association for sulphur and sulphuric-acid professionals, with 30+ energy, chemical and fertilizer members. Runs programs in sulphur-in-agriculture, sulphuric acid, EHS, transport and logistics, and the biennial Sulphur World Symposium. Data. Trade-association landing page — organizational description and membership, no statistics.
  • International Fertilizer Association (IFA) · IFA The global fertilizer-industry association covering the phosphate chain the acid feeds. Summary. IFA describes itself as the only global fertilizer association — about 500 members across ~80 countries spanning all actors in the fertilizer value chain that sulfuric acid feeds. Cites ~$250 bn annual fertilizer trade; focus on food security, sustainability and climate. Data. Association landing page — membership and industry-scale figures, no primary dataset.
  • U.S. Geological Survey Releases 2022 List of Critical Minerals · USGS (2022) The US criticality list — notable here for what it omits. Summary. USGS news release: the 2022 List of Critical Minerals holds 50 commodities (up 15 from 2018) under the Energy Act of 2020; nickel and zinc were added, helium, potash, rhenium and strontium removed. Sulfur is absent from the list entirely — despite being the highest-volume industrial chemical. Data. USGS criticality evaluation (supply-risk / trade-disruption methodology) mandated by the Energy Act of 2020.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 5

The datasets the chain's own figures are built from — and the provenance indexes behind them.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's trade layer. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Read here on sulfur (250300) and sulfuric acid (280700). Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • Historical Statistics for Mineral and Material Commodities · USGS (2024) Long-run US production/consumption series, including sulfur back through the Frasch era. Summary. USGS compilation of long-run US statistics for ~90 nonfuel mineral commodities back to 1900 — production, trade, stocks, apparent consumption and unit value, plus end-use tables — including the sulfur series that documents the Frasch-mining decline into recovered by-product supply. Data. USGS Historical Statistics series (production, imports, exports, stocks, apparent consumption, unit value), 1900–present.
  • USGS National Minerals Information Center — Sulfur Statistics and Information · USGS The hub indexing all sulfur Mineral Commodity Summaries, Minerals Yearbook chapters and special reports. Summary. USGS hub for "statistics and information on the worldwide supply of, demand for, and flow of" sulfur: indexes Mineral Commodity Summaries (1996–2026), Minerals Yearbook chapters (back to 1932), the Materials Flow report and fertilizer fact sheets — the provenance behind the chain's sulfur figures. Data. Index page; the underlying USGS production, consumption and trade series it links.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries archive · USGS The annual by-commodity data series, sulfur 1996–2026. Summary. USGS periodicals index for the annual Mineral Commodity Summaries: downloadable per-commodity chapters (including sulfur) covering domestic and world production, consumption, trade and reserves — the annual data engine behind the recovery figures used across years. Data. USGS annual Mineral Commodity Summaries series (production, imports, reserves, use).

US Census Bureau Current Industrial Reports — Fertilizer Materials (MQ325B): a US production series for fertilizer materials that consume sulfuric acid, cited from the USGS sulfur hub. Gaps & good next searches

  • Only one peer-reviewed paper (Maslin et al. 2022) anchors the decarbonization-squeeze thesis. A second independent study would strengthen it — try Resources Policy / Journal of Cleaner Production for "sulfur deficit forecast circular economy" or "sulfuric acid demand battery-metals leaching life cycle".
  • No market-intelligence source (CRU, Argus, Fastmarkets, Mining.com, Reuters) was retrieved — these usually carry the sharpest numbers on the "sulfur shortfall by mid-century" claim and should be chased first.
  • No EU JRC / Critical Raw Materials treatment of sulfur or sulfuric acid was located — worth confirming whether the EU tracks it as a strategic bulk chemical rather than a critical raw material.
  • Battery-metal leaching (stage 3b) has no dedicated country-level source; the acid demand is inferred from HPAL-nickel and copper heap-leach mentions. A hydrometallurgy sulfuric-acid-demand dataset (nickel/cobalt/lithium/copper) would firm it up.
  • The US Census MQ325B fertilizer-materials series page did not resolve directly — verify the current Census/USGS link, then reproduce the US acid-into-fertilizer series.
