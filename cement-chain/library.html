Cement chain — the diffuse counter-example

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Limestone is one of Earth's most abundant rocks, a cement kiln is mature technology built wherever there is construction, and cement is too cheap and heavy to ship far — so it is made locally almost everywhere. This is the atlas's counter-example: no stage concentrates into a chokepoint, and the only binding constraint is carbon — cement alone is about 8% of global CO₂.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Limestone (quarried everywhere) → kiln → clinker (~1450 °C) → cement → concrete (poured locally) → ② the carbon constraint (~8% of CO₂, most of it process chemistry) — then the trade data that sits across every stage.

① Production & supply — the diffuse base 4

Limestone → kiln → cement: the numbers that show supply is abundant, stable and made locally almost everywhere.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Cement · USGS (2026) Annual US/world cement and clinker production, trade and reserves data by country. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2025 US portland/blended cement output about 82 Mt (clinker 69 Mt) from 97 plants in 34 states; shipments ~100 Mt worth $17 billion. World cement production ~3.8 billion t, of which China ~1.7 billion t (≈45%). US net import reliance 21%; imports Turkey 32%, Canada 20%, Vietnam 13%. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported import/export trade and price data.
  • Cement Statistics and Information · USGS National Minerals Information Center The primary US cement data portal, linking every annual summary and yearbook chapter. Summary. USGS National Minerals Information Center hub for cement: monthly Mineral Industry Surveys (1965–2026), annual Mineral Commodity Summaries (1996–2026) and Minerals Yearbook (1994–2023), plus links to methodological open-file reports on US and world cement supply and demand. Data. USGS monthly and annual mineral-statistics series (production, shipments, imports, stocks, prices).
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2022 — Cement · USGS (2022) A prior-year annual summary, for cross-year comparison of a stable, local industry. Abstract. USGS commodity summary for 2021: US portland cement output ~90 Mt (clinker 79 Mt) from 96 plants in 34 states; shipments valued $13.4 billion. Net import reliance 18%; imports Canada 32%, Turkey 20%, Greece 13%, China 8%. A benchmark showing supply that shifts little year to year. Data. USGS annual producer surveys plus reported import/export trade data.
  • Background Facts and Issues Concerning Cement and Cement Data (OF-2005-1152) · USGS (2005) Methodological background on how USGS compiles its cement statistics. Abstract. USGS Open-File Report documenting how the cement data series is built. Explains that portland, blended and masonry cement are the binder in concrete and most mortars, and sets out the definitions, data sources and survey methods behind USGS cement statistics — the provenance of the production numbers cited across the chain. Data. USGS methodological documentation of its cement data-collection and estimation procedures — no new dataset.

② The carbon constraint 5

The real bottleneck is not geography but chemistry: heating limestone releases CO₂ that no fuel switch can remove.
  • IEA — Cement: tracking & net-zero roadmap · IEA (2023) IEA sector page tracking cement's energy use, production and emissions against a net-zero pathway. Summary. IEA cement tracking hub: charts of energy demand, cement production and CO₂ emissions 2015–2024 (from World Energy Outlook 2025), linked to the IEA cement net-zero guide and transition-measures reports. Headline CO₂-share figures are held in the embedded charts, not in page text. Data. IEA World Energy Outlook 2025 modelling of cement energy use, output and CO₂.
  • Global Cement and Concrete Association — Concrete Future roadmap · GCCA (2024) The cement/concrete industry's own 2050 net-zero roadmap and decarbonization levers. Summary. Industry roadmap: GCCA members commit to carbon-neutral concrete by 2050, promising a further ~20% CO₂ cut this decade (matching 30 years' progress) via clinker efficiency, supplementary cementitious materials and carbon capture. GCCA represents ~80% of the concrete industry outside China. Data. GCCA member commitments and sector emissions accounting; roadmap synthesis, no primary dataset on the page.
  • Trends in global CO₂ emissions: 2014 Report · PBL / EC-JRC (2014) Government/EU-agency emissions accounting that sizes cement's independent CO₂ share. Abstract. PBL/EC-JRC annual accounting from the EDGAR database: cement clinker production is the largest source of non-combustion CO₂, contributing about 4.8% of the global total (about 10% including the fuel burned to heat kilns); China accounted for more than half of world cement output in 2013. Data. EDGAR 4.2 emissions database — IPCC-2006 Tier-1 process-emission factors per tonne of cement, adjusted for the falling clinker share.
  • The sponge effect and carbon emission mitigation potentials of the global cement cycle · Cao et al., Nature Communications (2020) Peer-reviewed global material-flow and carbon-cycle analysis of cement. Abstract. Cement acts like a sponge in the carbon cycle: production emits heavily, yet hydrated products slowly reabsorb atmospheric CO₂ (carbonation). Modelling the full cycle, the authors find future reabsorption is significant (~30% of cumulative 2015–2100 emissions), but net reduction needs radical technology (carbon capture) plus material efficiency beyond current roadmaps. Data. Dynamic global cement-cycle model (production, use, demolition) quantifying material flows, energy, emissions and carbonation uptake to 2100.
  • Making Concrete Change: Innovation in Low-carbon Cement and Concrete · Chatham House (2018) Widely-cited think-tank report on cement decarbonization pathways. Summary. Chatham House report: deep, Paris-aligned cuts to cement emissions urgently require significant changes in how cement and concrete are produced and used. Page body served as a JS shell — summarised from the report's own published description. Data. Policy analysis of low-carbon cement/concrete innovation and its barriers; no primary dataset.

Across the chain — trade data 1

The dataset behind the chain's trade panel — a thin traded margin over an overwhelmingly local industry.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 2002–2024) used for the chain's trade table (HS 252310 clinker, 252329 cement). Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Here it carries HS 252310 clinker and 252329 portland cement. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).

Gaps & good next searches

  • This chain's search pass was cut short by a session-wide search-budget limit, so market and trade-press coverage (Reuters, Fastmarkets) and CSIS/RMI-style think-tank analysis were not confirmed live — worth a fresh pass with budget restored.
  • The ~8% of global CO₂ and ~60% process-chemistry figures rest mostly on IEA/GCCA framing; a dedicated newer read (Global Carbon Project, or an IEA CO₂-from-cement update) would firm up the split and reconcile it with the PBL/JRC ~4.8% non-combustion figure — search "IEA cement CO₂ emissions 2024 update."
  • Carbon capture on kilns (CCUS) and clinker-substitution SCMs (fly ash, slag, calcined clay / LC3) have no dedicated technical source here yet — search "cement carbon capture CCUS pilot" and "supplementary cementitious materials calcined clay LC3."
  • No non-USGS source independently quantifying China's ~50% world-production share — the USGS 2026 world table gives ≈45%, but an academic or IEA cross-check would strengthen it — search "China cement production share world 2025."
  • No concrete-stage-specific supply source exists (there is nothing to concentrate); the Cao et al. carbonation model is the closest, and is itself a planned enrichment.
