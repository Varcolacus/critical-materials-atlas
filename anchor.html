Method · the reconciliation, improved

The production-constrained anchor

A trade reconciliation should respect an attribution bound: a country can’t be credited as the mine origin of more than it produces minus what it absorbs at home. Feed it a consumption estimate built without trade, and that becomes a testable constraint — where it binds — that repairs the origin gap, where customs credit the refiner and re-exporter, not the mine. Honest caveat up front: the consumption term does real work in only about half the corrected rows — each row below shows exactly how far it moves the answer.

Research / method layer. This demonstrates a technique on top of the atlas’s reconciled trade. The consumption input is an estimate (activity × calibrated intensity), and the blend weight is a documented default (w = 0.5). The direction and magnitude of the corrections are robust; treat the exact corrected percentages as indicative.
How it works. Three independent inputs: production (World Mining Data, per country) · consumption (trade-independent bottom-up = national activity × a calibrated material intensity) · observed exports (the atlas’s reconciled mirror trade). For each producer, exportable supply = max(0, production − (1−R)·consumption), where R is the recycled share. Two levers: a hard guardrail (no origin can export more than it can supply) and a soft prior (pull a deleted origin back toward what production says it must be). We assert only the safe direction — a mine leader credited far less than it can supply (classic refiner-fronting) — and flag the rest. Reproducible: build_anchor.py → anchor.json.

Why not imports + recycling? The full material balance is exports = production + imports + recycling − consumption − losses. But the anchor answers a narrower question — whose mine did this come from? — not “how much can this country ship?” Imports are deliberately excluded: if China refines imported Congolese cobalt, that cobalt is Congo’s origin, not China’s — adding imports would re-create the very origin gap we correct. Re-exported imports still show up, as the review flag (a country exporting more than it makes minus uses). Recycling IS included (the R term): a country’s own recycling meets part of its use, so only (1−R) of consumption draws on primary supply — R is an approximate world recycled share (USGS/EU CRM), 0 where negligible. The honest remaining gaps are losses and stock changes, which are poorly measured everywhere.

Why this is the moat

Mirror-trade reconciliation (matching what exporters and importers each report) is well-trodden. Constraining it with physical production and an independent consumption estimate is not — and it is exactly what fixes the atlas’s hardest problem: the origin gap, where a material’s true source disappears from trade because it is refined and re-exported by someone else (DR Congo’s cobalt fronted by Chinese refiners; Indonesia’s nickel; South Africa’s platinum). The anchor puts the origin back, not by assertion but by a bound the data has to respect. Two honest boundaries: (1) stage matching — the constraint holds only where the mined form is the traded form (lithium spodumene→carbonate, graphite flake→processed need the two-stage version, the next build); and (2) the consumption term only bites where a country both mines and consumes at scale — in critical materials that means China (fluorspar, tungsten, phosphate). Everywhere else the mine leader barely consumes what it digs, so the repair rests on production alone, a mine-share prior. The consumption input is an order-of-magnitude estimate with partial end-use capture. w=0.5 is a documented knob, not a result, so each corrected share is the midpoint of [observed trade, exportable] — read the direction and the Bind Δ, not the exact number. Only under-attribution (a producer credited less than its exportable supply) is corrected; over-attribution is flagged, not adjusted, because domestic refining may not be captured. Materials whose consumption split is paired to a wrong-industry driver (gallium/germanium/beryllium on silicon-fab spend) are held, not corrected.
