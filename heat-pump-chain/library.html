Research Library · Heat pumps

Heat-pump chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

A heat pump wraps copper, aluminium and steel around a compressor and a refrigerant to make a box that heats a house — and no step is gated by a scarce mineral. The one input under pressure, the refrigerant, is being reshaped by regulation, not shortage; the one concentrated component is the compressor; and the binding limit on deployment sits downstream of all of it. The chokepoint is not a mine — it is a labour market.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Refrigerant (the regulated input, F-gases) → ② copper, magnets & the compressor (the components) → ③ the heat-pump unit (technology, market & distributed manufacturing) → ④ installation (the workforce bottleneck) — then the trade data that sits across every stage.

① Refrigerant — the regulated input 5

The one input under real pressure — and the pressure is a phase-down law, not a shortage.
  • The path to Kigali — HFC phase-down timeline · UNEP OzonAction (2026) The official Kigali Amendment schedule for phasing down hydrofluorocarbon refrigerants. Scope. UNEP OzonAction reference for the Kigali Amendment HFC phase-down: the stepwise cut in production and consumption of high-warming refrigerants, on separate timelines for developed and Article-5 developing countries, that is reshaping heat-pump and air-conditioning chemistry. (Page returned HTTP 403 on this pass — scope taken from the source title and the corroborating EPA / Harvard entries.) Data. Montreal Protocol / Kigali Amendment treaty schedule — a regulatory timeline, not a dataset.
  • Frequent questions on the phase-down of hydrofluorocarbons · US EPA Official AIM Act guidance on the US HFC phase-down for refrigeration and heat pumps. Summary. EPA FAQ on the American Innovation and Manufacturing (AIM) Act: an 85% phase-down of HFCs from baseline by 2036, via stepwise cuts (10% in 2022, 60% of baseline by 2024), an allowance-allocation program and sector technology-transition restrictions effective January 2025. Data. EPA regulatory guidance — allowance schedules, GWP-weighted baselines and compliance rules; no primary dataset.
  • Hydrofluorocarbons and the Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol · Harvard EELP Legal/regulatory tracker for HFC rules, the AIM Act and Kigali ratification. Summary. Harvard Environmental & Energy Law Program tracker of HFC regulation: the 2020 AIM Act's 85%-by-2036 phase-down and the 2022 US Senate ratification of the Kigali Amendment, recording EPA rule changes, compliance deadlines, court challenges and policy reversals across administrations. Data. Legal/regulatory chronology — statutes, rules and litigation; no primary dataset.
  • Everything you need to know about the AIM Act and HFC phase-down · CoolSys Industry explainer on the US refrigerant phase-down for HVAC/refrigeration operators. Summary. Industry explainer on the AIM Act's HFC phase-down (10% cut by 2024 rising to 85% by 2036), stressing the very high global-warming potential of legacy refrigerants (e.g. R-404A ≈ 3,920× CO₂) and the transition to CO₂ and other low-GWP alternatives for compliance. Data. Vendor commentary citing the AIM Act schedule and refrigerant GWP values; no primary dataset.
  • HFC quota management & the Kigali Amendment: refrigerant transition guide · CKY HVAC Engineering Industry guide to the refrigerant transition and HFC import-quota mechanics. Summary. Industry guide showing how Taiwan's July 2025 HFC quota caps refrigerant imports by CO₂-equivalent, making high-GWP fluids (R-410A, R-404A) scarce and costly; it maps a short-to-long transition — leak reduction, then R-32, then natural refrigerants. Data. Trade guidance on quota mechanics and refrigerant GWP tiers; no primary dataset.

② Copper, magnets & the compressor — the components 1

The metals are commodities with no gate; the one concentrated component is the compressor.
Copper, aluminium and steel are ordinary commodity inputs with no supply chokepoint, and the permanent magnets in some compressor motors are only a minor link (see the rare-earth magnet chain). The single component that is concentrated is the compressor — reported at ~95% Chinese unit production below — but no public, country-level compressor-production series surfaced to size it independently; that absence is itself a finding (see Gaps & good next searches).
  • Producing heat pumps: why it's cheaper in China than in the US · Rinnovabili Cost-comparison analysis tracing China's heat-pump price edge to component (compressor) manufacturing. Summary. Analysis finding heat-pump production ~50% cheaper in China than the US (40–60% cheaper than the EU), driven by components — 60–80% of unit cost — with China holding ~95% of global compressor production; assembly itself is simple, so the edge is in the integrated component supply chain. Data. Trade-press analysis citing IEA cost-structure figures and compressor-concentration estimates; no primary dataset.

③ The heat-pump unit — technology, market & manufacturing 13

Where the box is made: mature technology, unusually distributed factory capacity, demand that swings with prices and subsidies.
  • Heat Pump Monitor 2026 — Key findings · IEA (2026) The IEA's latest global/regional heat-pump market and manufacturing assessment. Summary. IEA Monitor: global sales reached ~108 GW in 2025, avoiding ~50–53 bcm of natural gas for heating in 2024–25. Manufacturing capacity is spread — China ~35%, the US ~25%, the EU ~20% — with heat pumps meeting ~5% of global heating and set to more than double by 2035. Data. IEA analysis of sales, stock and manufacturing-capacity data across major markets; stated-policies projection.
  • Heat Pump Monitor 2026 — full report & dashboards · IEA (2026) The full Monitor report plus interactive country-level sales/stock dashboards. Summary. The complete IEA Heat Pump Monitor 2026, with interactive dashboards giving country-level sales and stock series behind the key findings — the granular evidence for reading equipment trade against actual deployment. Data. IEA country-level heat-pump sales and stock series (interactive dashboards).
  • Heat Pump Monitor 2026 — Trends and market opportunities (PDF) · IEA (2026) The full-text Monitor report, including gas-use-avoidance figures. Summary. Full-text IEA Monitor documenting heat pumps' avoidance of ~50 bcm of gas use for heating in 2024, rising to ~53 bcm in 2025, alongside sales, manufacturing and deployment-barrier analysis. (PDF served as binary on this pass — figures cross-read from the report's HTML key findings.) Data. IEA analytical report — sales, stock, gas-avoidance and capacity estimates.
  • The Future of Heat Pumps — Executive summary · IEA (2022) Foundational IEA report on heat-pump deployment barriers and manufacturing. Summary. IEA's foundational assessment: heat pumps run three-to-five times more efficient than gas boilers but face higher upfront cost (air-to-water 2–4× a boiler) and installer shortages — with installer demand set to quadruple by 2030 as global capacity rises from ~1,000 GW (2021) toward ~2,600 GW (2030). Data. IEA scenario modelling of capacity, cost, gas savings, investment and installer-workforce needs.
  • Heat Pump Taxonomy · IEA A technology-classification reference for heat-pump types. Summary. IEA reference framework classifying heat pumps by source, sink and product characteristics, to standardise monitoring and data collection across regions and help policymakers compare deployment and infrastructure needs. Data. Classification framework — a taxonomy, not a dataset.
  • Technology: Heat pumps — Global Energy Review 2026 · IEA (2026) Annual energy-review chapter tracking 2025 heat-pump demand. Summary. IEA review: global heat-pump sales fell ~2% in 2025, with an 11% EU recovery (Germany outselling gas boilers for the first time), stable China, and a ~13% US drop attributed partly to refrigerant shortages and slower housing — heat pumps still beating gas boilers there for a fourth year. Data. IEA annual sales/demand tracking across major markets.
  • The Future of Heat Pumps in China — Executive summary · IEA China-specific heat-pump manufacturing and market assessment. Summary. IEA finds China the world's largest heat-pump maker and market — producing ~35% of units sold worldwide and over a quarter of global sales — employing more than 300,000 people in the sector, with employment projected to double by 2050 under the Announced Pledges Scenario. Data. IEA analysis of Chinese production, sales and sector-employment data with scenario projections.
  • EHPA Market Report 2025 — Executive summary · European Heat Pump Association (2025) European heat-pump sales, stock and workforce statistics. Summary. EHPA reports ~2.31 million heat pumps sold across 19 European countries in 2024, down 22% (Germany −48%, Czechia −64%, UK +56%). Installed stock reached ~25.5 million (~12% of households), avoiding ~21 bcm of gas imports, with the sector employing ~433,000 people. Data. EHPA survey of national heat-pump associations — country-level 2024 sales, stock, per-household and employment figures.
  • Market and industry trends — Heat pumps (GSR 2024) · REN21 Cross-country heat-pump market-and-industry trend module. Summary. REN21's Global Status Report module on the world heat-pump sector: 2023 sales trends by market (China +12%, Germany +60%, declines in Canada and several European countries), efficiency (3–4 units of heat per unit of electricity), plus workforce and energy-pricing challenges. Data. REN21 synthesis of 2023 market data from IEA, EHPA and national sources.
  • Heat Pumps Step Up: what the IEA's Heat Pump Monitor 2026 means · HPT TCP (IEA) Industry-facing analysis of the 2026 Monitor for heating, industry and energy security. Summary. Heat Pumping Technologies TCP reads the 2026 Monitor: heat pumps meet under 15% of global heating but are set to more than double by 2035; success hinges on aligning "price signal, workforce and will to build," while managing China's compressor dominance and the low-GWP refrigerant transition. Data. Commentary on IEA Monitor findings; no primary dataset.
  • IEA ETP 2026 highlights heat pumping technologies · HPT TCP (IEA) Summary of Energy Technology Perspectives 2026 on heat pumps' role. Summary. HPT TCP on IEA's Energy Technology Perspectives 2026: heat pumps and air conditioning framed as "core technologies of the Age of Electricity," a market of ~USD 50 billion in 2024 that has doubled over the decade, held back by upfront cost, compressor-concentration supply risk and short-term policy. Data. Commentary citing IEA ETP 2026 market figures; no primary dataset.
  • Is a turnaround in sight for heat pump markets? · IEA commentary Commentary on recovery signals after the 2024 heat-pump slump. Summary. IEA commentary on an uneven recovery from the 2024 declines: US sales up almost 15% year-on-year by November and China holding double-digit growth, while the EU and Japan still struggled — Germany expecting a 30%+ rebound in 2025 as subsidies took effect. Data. IEA market-tracking commentary — partial-year sales growth by region.
  • Global heat pump sales continue double-digit growth · IEA commentary Commentary on the 2022 double-digit sales-growth year. Summary. IEA commentary: global heat-pump sales grew 11% in 2022, a second straight double-digit year, led by a record 40% rise in Europe, with US heat-pump sales overtaking gas furnaces for the first time and the Chinese market broadly stable. Data. IEA market-tracking commentary — 2022 sales-growth figures by region.

④ Installation — the workforce bottleneck 7

The binding constraint — a skilled trade in short supply, invisible to every trade statistic.
  • Workforce planning for clean heat — where will the heat pump workforce come from? · Aldersgate Group (2025) UK report quantifying the heat-pump installer workforce gap to 2035. Summary. Aldersgate Group finds UK heat-pump installer demand must rise from ~4,500 full-time equivalents in 2023 to 41,000 by 2028 and 122,000 by 2035; it recommends expanded training and apprenticeships, better installer pay and conditions, and coordinated government–industry–education action. Data. Workforce modelling of installer FTEs against UK deployment targets.
  • Written evidence on heat-pump skills · UK Parliament Committee written evidence on the heat-pump skills and training pipeline. Scope. Written evidence submitted to a UK Parliament committee on the heat-pump skills gap and installer-training pipeline. (Page returned HTTP 403 on this pass — not retrievable; noted from the record title only.) Data. Submitted committee testimony — qualitative evidence, no primary dataset.
  • Heat pumps need people: closing the skills gap · European Heat Pump Association EHPA-led project (SKILLS4HP) on the European installer skills gap. Summary. EHPA's SKILLS4HP cluster of five EU-funded projects addresses heat-pump workforce shortages, calling a shortage of skilled installers, planners and technicians "one of the main bottlenecks to scaling deployment," against large potential (e.g. ~2 million heat-pump-ready homes in the Netherlands). Data. Project description citing EU deployment-potential figures; no primary dataset.
  • UK HVAC & heat pump skills shortage — where do we go from here? · Modern Building Services Trade reporting on the UK HVAC/heat-pump engineer shortage. Summary. Trade report: UK heat-pump-qualified engineers rose from ~3,000 (2022) to ~14,800 by mid-2024 but remain far short of the ~33,700 needed for 2028 net-zero targets, with ~39% of trainees never going on to install — a demand and support problem, not just training capacity. Data. Trade-press reporting citing UK engineer-count and training figures.
  • Heat pump skills competition puts UK warm-homes ambition at risk · MCS Foundation Report on competition for skilled workers threatening UK heat-pump targets. Summary. MCS Foundation warns competition for skilled labour risks the Warm Homes Plan: ~76,000 workers in England hold relevant skills but many are in high-demand trades, small firms or nearing retirement, while the heat-pump workforce must reach 41,000 by 2028 and 122,000 by 2035. Data. Report analysis of England's skilled-worker pool against workforce targets.
  • Heat pump engineers held back on installations by staff shortages · Nesta Survey of UK installers on the barriers to scaling installations. Summary. A Nesta survey of 345 UK installers finds staffing the second-ranked barrier to growth (30% overall, 41% among firms of 6+ employees) after weak demand (41%); 81% of larger firms planned to hire, but smaller ones doubted apprenticeship quality on practical skills. Data. Nesta survey of 345 UK heat-pump installers.
  • Heat pumps and the shortage of skilled workers · European Commission BUILD UP EU portal article on the installer shortage across the bloc. Summary. European Commission BUILD UP notes that while the EU aims to install 10 million heat pumps over five years to cut Russian-gas dependence, "there is a general lack of skilled workers" able to install them — though it gives no single quantified EU-wide gap figure. Data. EU portal commentary; no primary dataset.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 1

The dataset the chain's equipment-trade figures are built from — and the caveat that trade is not deployment.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 2002–2024); heat pumps sit in the HS 8415 / 8418 headings. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Heat-pump and air-conditioning equipment falls in the HS 8415 / 8418 headings. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
