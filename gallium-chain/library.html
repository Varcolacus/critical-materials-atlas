Gallium value chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

No one runs a gallium mine. Gallium is a trace by-product of refining aluminium and zinc, and China makes about 98% of the world's primary supply — then, in July 2023, put it under export licensing. The chokepoint is the recovery circuit, not the ore.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① host ore (bauxite / zinc) → by-product gallium (~98% China) → ② high-purity metal, GaN / GaAs compounds & devices → ③ the July-2023 export-control lever → ④ the recover-and-recycle response — then the cross-cutting criticality frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Host ore → by-product gallium 4

The raw input, and a metal no one mines for: recovered at parts-per-million from bauxite (and some zinc), ~98% China.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Gallium · USGS (2026) Annual U.S./world gallium production, use, price and trade data; the primary-share and by-product read. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: no U.S. primary (low-purity) gallium has been recovered since 1987; globally it is recovered predominantly as a by-product of bauxite (and some zinc) processing. China accounted for 99% of world primary low-purity gallium, with Japan and Russia the rest. Integrated circuits took 73% of U.S. consumption; net import reliance 100%. DOE and a Defense Production Act award now fund domestic recovery. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported import/export and price data; world low-purity production and capacity table.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Gallium · USGS (2025) Prior-year baseline: gallium production, uses, import reliance and price data by country. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: no U.S. primary gallium since 1987; recovered globally as a by-product of bauxite and zinc processing. In 2024 gallium-metal imports were ~$4 million and GaAs-wafer imports ~$140 million. About 83% of U.S. consumption was in GaAs, GaN and GaP wafers (ICs 79%); net import reliance 100%. One New York plant refined high-purity gallium from imported metal and new scrap. Data. USGS voluntary producer surveys plus reported import/export trade and price data.
  • Tracking the global anthropogenic gallium cycle during 2000–2020: a trade-linked multiregional material flow analysis · Global Environmental Change (2024) Material-flow analysis of world gallium stocks and flows, and the recycling headroom in the by-product stream. Abstract. Maps the global and regional gallium cycles for 2000–2020 using a trade-linked multiregional material-flow analysis. Finds 79% of gallium co-mined from bauxite ended up in red mud or entered the aluminium cycle as an impurity — a large untapped recycling potential — with regions playing different but complementary roles in the global cycle. Data. Trade-linked multiregional material-flow analysis on production, trade and use data, 2000–2020.
  • Gallium faces growing pressure as aluminium production shifts to recycling · AlCircle Aluminium-industry trade press on the by-product coupling risk. Summary. Gallium occurs at only 20–80 ppm in bauxite and under 1 ppm in secondary aluminium, so as aluminium output shifts toward recycling the primary Bayer-process stream that yields roughly 90% of gallium shrinks — tightening supply despite rising prices, with China controlling ~98%. Data. Trade-press commentary citing concentration and production-share figures — no primary dataset.

② High-purity, compounds & devices — GaN / GaAs 2

Where the metal becomes 6N–7N wafers and then the chips behind radar, 5G and power electronics.
  • SCRREEN — Gallium CRM factsheet · EU SCRREEN (2020) Value-chain diagram, purity grades and criticality factsheet for gallium. Abstract. SCRREEN factsheet: gallium (upper-crust abundance 17.5 ppm) never occurs natively. "Primary/crude" gallium is 3N–4N metal from first processing; "refined" is 6N–7N. As a by-product of aluminium and zinc, its supply risk tracks recovery capacity. It is assessed under trade code CN 81129289, is not exchange-traded, and prices are negotiated bilaterally. Data. EU SCRREEN/CRM criticality assessment; CN 81129289 trade code, USGS/BGR price series 2012–2016.
  • Risk Evaluation and Early Warning Study on Supply of Critical Minerals for China's Chip Industry · Computational Economics (2025) Dynamic supply-risk / early-warning model for chip-critical minerals including gallium. Abstract. Examines global developments and conducts a supply-risk and early-warning analysis of critical minerals for China's chip industry. A stochastic multi-criteria acceptability analysis (SMAA) dynamically assesses supply risk for four minerals — gallium, germanium, arsenic and silicon — over 2010–2021, then explores the availability risk of each resource. Data. Stochastic multi-criteria acceptability analysis over gallium/germanium/arsenic/silicon indicators, 2010–2021.

③ The lever — July-2023 export licensing 11

The archetype of the export-control playbook: a defence-critical chip metal placed under licence, and what followed.
  • Understanding China's Gallium Sanctions · CSIS (2023) Explainer on the July 2023 gallium/germanium export licensing and the defence stakes. Summary. In July 2023 China restricted gallium and germanium exports on national-security grounds, targeting U.S. defence supply chains — radar, high-frequency radio, satellite communications. CSIS argues China controls ~29% of gallium exports (versus ~98% of primary production), so alternative capacity in Japan, Germany and Australia limits the sanctions' bite. Data. CSIS analysis of government announcements, industry and supply-chain data (published July 2023).
  • China Imposes New Export Controls on Two Minerals Critical to the Manufacture of Semiconductors · Mayer Brown (2023) Legal / trade-compliance analysis of the 2023 measures. Summary. On 3 July 2023 China announced export-licensing requirements for gallium and germanium (effective 1 August 2023), extending to derivative products such as gallium nitride and zone-refined germanium ingots; exporters must obtain provincial Ministry of Commerce approval. Data. Legal analysis of China's Export Control Law, Foreign Trade Law and Customs Law; Joint Announcement No. 23 (2023).
  • China releases latest export control measures on gallium and germanium · Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer (2023) Law-firm regulatory summary of the July/August 2023 measures. Summary. Law-firm regulatory note on China's July 2023 gallium and germanium export-licensing measures and their compliance implications for exporters. (Page not retrievable on this pass — bot-blocked 403; noted from the article title.) Data. Law-firm regulatory commentary — no primary dataset.
  • Germanium and Gallium: U.S. Trade and Chinese Export Restrictions · U.S. International Trade Commission (2024) Trade-flow and price-impact briefing on the 2023 controls. Summary. USITC executive briefing (March 2024): U.S. 2022 net import reliance was 100% for gallium; the general customs value of U.S. gallium imports rose ~997% from 2021 to 2022; and global gallium prices rose ~68% after the July 2023 licensing regime took effect. Data. USITC analysis of official U.S. trade statistics and reported price series.
  • SEMI Europe Comments on the Export Controls on Gallium and Germanium · SEMI Europe (2024) Semiconductor-industry association response to the licensing regime. Summary. SEMI Europe position paper to the European Commission, Parliament and Council setting out the semiconductor industry's priorities on the gallium and germanium export controls and their impact on supply — the downstream industry's own reading of the lever. Data. Industry-association policy submission — no primary dataset.
  • China's Germanium and Gallium Export Restrictions: Consequences for the United States · Stimson Center (2025) Downstream impact assessment of the restrictions on U.S. supply. Summary. Stimson analysis finding that despite China's export restrictions, germanium and gallium still reach the United States through indirect trade routes — a caveat on how binding the licensing regime is in practice. Data. Stimson trade-flow analysis of indirect routing — no primary dataset shown.
  • De-risking Gallium Supply Chains: The National Security Case for Eroding China's Critical Mineral Dominance · CSIS Policy case for diversification away from Chinese gallium. Summary. Argues China's ~98% control of raw gallium — essential to semiconductors for military radar and defence — was exposed by the 2023 restrictions, and makes the national-security case for domestic investment and diversified sourcing to cut dependence. Data. USGS assessments, DOE reports, DOD contracts, trade data and Chinese policy documents.
  • Beyond Rare Earths: China's Growing Threat to Gallium Supply Chains · CSIS Broader framing of gallium within China's mineral leverage. Summary. Documents China's ~98% control of global gallium and progressively tightening export restrictions since 2023, creating shortages that threaten U.S. and allied defence systems reliant on gallium semiconductors; alternative production is emerging but needs sustained government investment. Data. Chinese customs data, USGS reports, Rotterdam market pricing and industry interviews.
  • China's Critical Mineral Export Controls: Background & Chokepoints · ORF America Cross-mineral chokepoint framing that includes gallium. Summary. Background paper: China controls ~60–90% of several critical minerals and has imposed export controls on germanium, gallium, graphite and antimony since 2023, signalling economic-coercion capability in retaliation for U.S. semiconductor limits and threatening allied supply chains. Data. USGS projections, Chinese customs data and production statistics; historical coercion precedents.
  • Critical Minerals: Global Gallium Supply & Demand · Geopolitical Monitor Market and geopolitical overview of gallium. Summary. Overview of gallium as a semiconductor metal with China producing ~99% of world supply, examining supply-and-demand dynamics and recent progress toward self-sufficiency in Western supply chains. Data. Trade-press market overview — no primary dataset.
  • Assessment of critical minerals supply chain for the United States in perspective of trade restriction by foreign countries · Resources Policy Supply-chain risk modelling under Chinese export restrictions, gallium among the cases. Summary. Models U.S. critical-minerals supply-chain risk under trade restrictions imposed by foreign countries — gallium among the assessed minerals. (Publisher/preprint shell — full abstract not retrievable; summary from the paper's title and venue.) Data. Supply-chain risk assessment under trade-restriction scenarios.

Gaps & good next searches

  • Gallium has no clean HS6 trade code — unwrought gallium sits in the shared HS 811292 basket with indium and germanium, so BACI cannot isolate it (the same limit the atlas flags for indium). Customs (CN 81129289 / HS 811292) will not verify the USGS production read.
  • No dedicated gallium industry association surfaced — the closest are downstream semiconductor bodies (SEMI) and aluminium trade press. Next: check the Minor Metals Trade Association (MMTA) directly for a gallium page.
  • Quantified Western recovery-restart capacity is still thin. USGS MCS 2026 now records a DOE ~$6M R&D initiative and a $29.9M Defense Production Act (Title III) award for a Louisiana gallium/scandium recovery demonstration — a fuller capacity series would let the "rebuildable slowly" claim be sized.
  • Price-series data (Fastmarkets/Argus/USGS historical tables) was not directly retrieved; the USITC "+68% since July 2023" figure and the USGS unit-value series are only partial proxies. Next: "gallium price history post-2023 export controls."
  • Several academic abstracts sit behind Elsevier/SSRN application shells; their DOIs are listed above (e.g. 10.1016/j.hydromet.2021.105722, 10.1016/j.psep.2024.03.039) for retrieval through an institutional library.
