Displays / indium chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Every LCD and touchscreen carries a transparent conductor — indium tin oxide. Indium is a trace zinc by-product no mine targets, its refining is ~70% China, and China placed it under export control in February 2025. The mine is nowhere; the chokepoint is the refinery.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Zinc by-product indium (refined ~70% China) → ② ITO sputtering targets → ③ flat-panel displays & touchscreens — with an ITO-scrap recycling loop in Japan and Korea — then the cross-cutting criticality frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Indium — a zinc by-product, refined ~70% in China 6

The raw input, and a by-product no one mines for: recovered from zinc-ore residues, then refined mostly in China.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Indium · USGS (2026) Latest annual indium production, consumption, price and China-share figures. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: the US recovered no indium from ores in 2025, and ITO for flat-panel displays remained most of global consumption. China leads refined-indium output at 70% of the world total; after China's February-2025 export restrictions, Asian Metal reported unwrought-indium exports fell ~72% year-on-year. US net import reliance 100%; refined price ~$350,000/t. Data. USGS annual producer canvass, reported trade and Argus price data; world refinery-production figures estimated from trade, capacity and zinc/lead-smelter proxies.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Indium · USGS (2025) Prior-year comparison; source for the ITO / Japan–Korea recycling line. Abstract. Prior-year USGS summary: US indium consumption ~250 t, entirely imported (net import reliance 100%). China leads global production at 70% and is the leading exporter — 347 t in Jan–Sep 2024, ~74% to the Republic of Korea. Indium is most commonly recovered from ITO scrap in Japan and Korea; 5G and data-centre demand raise indium-phosphide use. Data. USGS import/consumption estimates, Argus prices, reported Chinese export statistics, and country-level refinery-production estimates.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2024 — Indium · USGS (2024) Earlier-year baseline for the production and price series. Abstract. Earlier USGS baseline: US indium consumption ~300 t, all imported. China the leading producer and exporter (381 t in Jan–Aug 2023). Illustrates the zinc coupling — an indium-producing zinc smelter at Auby, France was idled then reopened — and a Utah zinc-copper-silver-indium project with a historical ~1,530 t of contained indium. Data. USGS consumption estimates, Argus prices, reported world refinery production by country, and company project disclosures.
  • Mineral Commodity Profile: Indium (Open-File Report 2004-1300) · USGS, Jorgenson & George Long-form profile of indium geology, production, uses and history. Abstract. Long-form USGS profile: indium is a volatile chalcophile by-product of the electrolytic refining of zinc, found in ores of zinc, copper and tin. About 65% of consumption is ITO thin films for LCDs; it is also used as indium phosphide in semiconductors. Covers geochemistry, production (e.g. Kidd Creek), scrap recovery, uses, substitutes and market history to 2000. Data. USGS historical production, import/consumption and price series (1967–2000) with process and resource description.
  • The world's by-product and critical metal resources part III: A global assessment of indium · Ore Geology Reviews (2017) Global inventory of indium in mineral deposits. Abstract. Deposit-by-deposit global assessment: at least 356 kt of indium sit in 1,512 known mineral deposits (VMS, skarn, epithermal, sediment-hosted Pb–Zn), plus a minimum ~24 kt in mine wastes — figures the authors call a floor. That is enough to meet this century's demand, yet supply remains a concern given indium's by-product status and spatial concentration. Data. New critical-metals resource-assessment methodology applied to 1,512 deposits (101 with reported indium contents; Werner, Mudd & Jowitt).
  • How Indium's Price is Tied to Zinc Mining Production · Quest Metals Plain-language explainer of the by-product coupling to zinc. Summary. Explainer: indium has no primary mines and occurs only in trace amounts in zinc ore, so roughly 95% of refined indium is produced during zinc processing — tying its supply and price to zinc-industry economics rather than an independent indium market. Data. Plain-language commentary; no primary dataset.

③ The relief valve — ITO-scrap recycling 3

Sputtering ITO onto glass is inefficient, so much of the metal is recovered — chiefly in Japan and Korea, where the display industry sits.
  • ITO (Indium-Tin Oxide) Sputtering Target Reclaim/Recycling · Indium Corporation Industry explainer on ITO target reclaim — the mechanism behind Japan/Korea scrap recycling. Summary. Vendor explainer: sputtering uses ITO very inefficiently, leaving "race-track"-eroded spent targets and unusable chunks. Users break up the remaining material and send it to recyclers, where it is converted back to indium metal to restart the cycle — the mechanism behind the Japan/Korea scrap loop. Data. Technical explainer; no quantitative dataset.
  • Looking Down Under for a Circular Economy of Indium · Environmental Science & Technology (2018) Circular-economy / recycling-potential study of indium flows. Abstract. Dynamic material-flow analysis of Australia's indium finds ~46.2 kt in mineral resources and 14.7 kt in mine wastes — decades of global demand (~0.8 kt/yr) — versus a negligible 43 t in discarded products. Resilience is best gained through mining efficiency (e.g. domestic refining) rather than end-of-life recycling; the same logic applies to gallium and germanium. Data. Dynamic MFA on Australian Pb–Zn/Cu deposit resources, mining activity 1844–2013 and indium-product trade 1988–2015 (Werner, Ciacci, Mudd, Reck, Northey).
  • Modeling Indium Extraction, Supply, Price, Use and Recycling 1930–2200 Using the WORLD7 Model · Natural Resources Research (2023) Long-run system-dynamics model of indium supply and price. Abstract. WORLD7 system-dynamics model of indium 1930–2200 estimates ~312 kt extractable, with production peaking 2025–2030. Only ~25% of solar-cell indium demand can be met, and insufficient recycling is a key constraint; the study frames indium supply as a limit on the energy transition and Europe's 2050 sustainability goals. Data. WORLD7 system-dynamics model coupling mass balance with supply–demand price dynamics.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 6

List methodologies and EU/US assessments that classify indium among the by-product critical metals.
  • SCRREEN — Indium CRM 2020 Factsheet · EU SCRREEN / JRC (2020) EU criticality factsheet: supply chain, substitution, recycling. Abstract. EU SCRREEN/JRC factsheet: ~95% of primary indium comes from zinc-ore processing; world production 2013–2016 averaged 827 t, China 48% and South Korea 21%. Indium's major EU use is ITO in flat-panel displays; substitutable in some transparent conductive oxides but not in CIGS/CIS semiconductors, and secondary output is chiefly new (manufacturing) scrap, not end-of-life. Data. JRC/SCRREEN value-chain analysis on World Mining Data, Eurostat-Comext trade and USGS figures (EU averages 2012–2016).
  • Study on the EU's list of Critical Raw Materials (2020) — Final Report · European Commission / JRC (2020) EU-wide criticality methodology and indium supply-risk scoring. Summary. The EU's fourth technical criticality assessment (2020, 83 materials via Economic Importance × Supply Risk): indium is critical, assessed at the processing stage, with Economic Importance 3.3 and Supply Risk 1.8; China supplies 48% of global indium and France 28% of EU sourcing. Data. EI × Supply-Risk methodology over candidate materials using production, trade, substitution and recycling data.
  • RMIS — More on Critical Raw Materials · EU JRC Raw Materials Information System Portal for EU CRM criticality factsheets, including indium. Summary. EU JRC Raw Materials Information System — a dashboard and factsheet portal tracking criticality-assessment results for candidate raw materials across 2011–2023, including indium (identified as critical in the 2011, 2014 and 2017 assessments and again in 2023). Data. Portal aggregating EU criticality assessments and supply-chain factsheets.
  • Critical Mineral Resources: National Policy and Critical Minerals List (R47982) · Congressional Research Service (2024) Legislative / policy background on the US critical minerals list process. Summary. CRS report: reviews US minerals policy, the Energy Act of 2020 definition of a critical mineral, and USGS list development (2022: 50 minerals; 2025: 60) — the statutory frame around indium and other listed inputs. Data. Secondary synthesis of USGS lists/methodology, statute and production/import-reliance data; no new primary dataset.
  • Emergency Access to Strategic and Critical Materials: The National Defense Stockpile (R47833) · Congressional Research Service Background on the US stockpile mechanism referenced by indium-stockpiling proposals. Scope. CRS report on the National Defense Stockpile — the statutory mechanism (managed by the Defense Logistics Agency) for acquiring and holding strategic and critical materials for defence emergencies, the vehicle behind proposed indium stockpiling. (Report page returned 403 — not retrievable; scope taken from the report title.) Data. CRS policy synthesis — page not retrieved.
  • Selecting and prioritizing material resources by criticality assessments · One Earth (2021) Criticality-methodology primer that situates indium among by-product critical metals. Abstract. Primer on criticality assessments — how countries and companies select and prioritise materials by supply risk, vulnerability and environmental/social impacts. Illustrated with copper and indium, it lays out four steps: define goal and scope, select and evaluate indicators, choose an aggregation approach, and interpret and communicate the results. Data. Methodological review/primer (Helbig, Schrijvers, Hool); no primary dataset.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 2

The datasets the chain's own figures are built from — with indium's trade-code limit flagged.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 2002–2024) used for the chain's flagged basket context. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Unwrought indium sits in the shared HS 811292 basket with gallium and germanium, so it cannot be isolated. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • USGS — Indium statistics and information · USGS National Minerals Information Center Landing page for the historical indium production and price data series. Summary. USGS landing page for indium — the production data engine behind the chain — offering Mineral Commodity Summaries (1996–2026), Minerals Yearbook data (1994–2024) and Historical Statistics for the commodity, plus materials-flow and critical-minerals research. Data. USGS annual mineral-commodity statistics series (production, imports, use, price).

Gaps & good next searches

  • No clean, isolated indium trade series exists — BACI/Comtrade lump unwrought indium with gallium and germanium in HS 811292 (confirmed by the chain's own trade note), so the customs table is context, not an indium series. Next: national customs data (China, Japan, Korea) for finer indium-specific codes.
  • Indium phosphide (InP) is a distinct, fast-moving 2026 story — AI optical interconnects — adjacent to but not identical to the ITO/display chain. Worth a dedicated pass on optical-transceiver supply commentary and TrendForce/SemiAnalysis coverage if the chain is extended toward compute.
  • End-of-life recycling is near-zero: the secondary loop is overwhelmingly new (manufacturing) scrap. A quantified secondary-recovery series (tonnes recovered from ITO scrap by country) is missing — USGS and SCRREEN both note the data gap.
  • Good next searches: "DERA Germany indium critical raw material study", "Fraunhofer ISI indium raw material list", "China indium export volume statistics customs", "SCRREEN2 indium factsheet 2023 update", "Indium Corporation gallium refining North America" (for the by-product-metals cluster).
