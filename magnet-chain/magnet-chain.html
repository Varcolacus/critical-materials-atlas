The mine is only the first bottleneck

Rare-earth output expanded more than two hundred-fold over the last century. But the strategic magnet chain tightened after the ore left the ground: separating individual elements, making metals and alloys, and manufacturing high-performance magnets became more concentrated than mining itself.

Mine ≠ separation ≠ magnet factory ≠ customs origin · each measure and time period stays separate

The correction. “Rare earths” are not one homogeneous input, and trade in magnets is not the same as magnet production. Nd, Pr, Dy and Tb matter for high-performance magnets; their processing chain is the analytical unit.

The chain in one glance

A century of scale

The USGS world series begins in 1900, long before today’s magnet market. It measures all rare earths in oxide equivalent. It establishes scale—not which country or technology caused each change—and ends in 2020.

The mining geography moved—and then loosened

BGS’s country panel begins in 1992. China’s share rose above nine-tenths around 2000–2010, then fell as production expanded in the United States, Australia, Myanmar and elsewhere. This panel covers all rare earths, not only magnet elements.

The tighter bottleneck is downstream

IEA’s magnet-specific 2024 estimates follow Nd, Pr, Dy and Tb through physical production. They show why a mining-only story understates dependency.

Manufacturing concentration deepened

China’s share of sintered permanent-magnet production rose from about 50% in 2005 to 94% in 2024. These are two IEA anchors, not an invented annual series between them.

What the magnet contains

Neodymium and praseodymium provide the core magnetic performance. Dysprosium and terbium can improve high-temperature coercivity. The precise recipe varies, so total rare-earth mine output is only a rough upstream denominator.

Global customs trade: useful, but broad

BACI supplies a consistent 2002–2024 ledger. HS 850511 includes every metallic permanent magnet—not just NdFeB or even rare-earth magnets. The two upstream codes also include non-magnet uses and elements.

A cleaner product code, in a smaller universe

EU CN 85051110 identifies rare-earth permanent magnets specifically. For extra-EU imports, partner is the origin country. The 2024 complete-year result is therefore cleaner than global HS6—but it describes only the EU import market.
