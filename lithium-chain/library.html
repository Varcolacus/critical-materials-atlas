Lithium chain — mine to battery cell

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Lithium is mined in many countries — Australia's hard-rock spodumene, Chile's and Argentina's brine, fast-rising Zimbabwe and Brazil — but almost none of it becomes a battery until it passes through a Chinese refinery. The mine is the least concentrated stage of all; the chokepoint is the conversion step.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Mine (hard-rock spodumene / brine) → ② Refining to battery-grade carbonate & hydroxide — the China chokepoint → ③ cathode → ④ cell — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Mine — brine & hard-rock 6

The diversified upstream: Australia's fast hard-rock spodumene leads (~62%), the Lithium Triangle holds the reserves in slow brine.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Lithium · USGS (2026) Annual world lithium mine production, reserves, uses and import reliance by country. Abstract. USGS annual commodity summary for lithium — world mine production, reserves, end uses (dominated by batteries) and US net import reliance by country. The 2026 PDF returned HTTP 403 on this pass and was not retrievable; the atlas's ~62% Australia mine share and its 2000→2024 mine series derive from this USGS series. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade, reserve and price data.
  • BGS World Mineral Statistics — lithium mine production · BGS (2024) Long-run, country-level mine-production series, including lithium. Summary. BGS World Mineral Statistics — the long-run, country-level mine-production database behind the atlas's 2000→2024 lithium history. The statistics portal returned HTTP 404 on this pass; the mine series (Australia to ~62%, Chile falling, Zimbabwe rising) is taken from the atlas's stored extract. Data. BGS annual global mineral-production compilation from national statistical returns.
  • Responsible extraction in South America's Lithium Triangle · British Geological Survey BGS field project on sustainable brine extraction in Chile, Argentina and Bolivia. Summary. BGS Lithium Triangle initiative: the salt-flat brines of Chile, Argentina and Bolivia hold roughly half of known lithium, but brine mining threatens groundwater, habitats and indigenous communities. Participatory 2024 workshops with agencies, miners and communities built a sustainable-extraction research roadmap. Data. Stakeholder workshops (2024) with agencies, operators and indigenous representatives — a qualitative research agenda, no primary dataset.
  • Bringing the state back in the lithium triangle: an institutional analysis of resource nationalism · Extractive Industries and Society Political-economy analysis of resource nationalism across the three Lithium Triangle states. Summary. Institutional analysis of resource nationalism and the state's role across Chile, Argentina and Bolivia — why vast brine reserves convert only slowly into flowing supply. Full text paywalled (HTTP 403); summary from the title and abstract listing (2024). Data. Comparative institutional / political-economy case studies — no primary dataset.
  • Lithium mining leaves severe impacts in Chile, but new methods exist · Mongabay (2025) Environmental reporting on brine extraction in Chile's Salar de Atacama. Summary. Reports severe impacts of Atacama brine mining — roughly 500,000 litres evaporated per tonne of lithium carbonate, groundwater down ~10 m in 15 years, harm to Lickanantay communities — and direct-lithium-extraction (DLE) as a less damaging but as-yet-unimplemented alternative. Data. Reporting on an impact report citing hydrological and production figures; no primary dataset.
  • Managing geological uncertainty in critical mineral supply chains: a POMDP approach with application to U.S. lithium resources · arXiv (2025) Decision-theoretic model applied to US lithium resource development. Abstract. Applies Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes to critical-mineral sourcing under geological uncertainty. A US lithium case study shows POMDP policies outperform traditional approaches when reserve estimates are imperfect, and offers guidance on balancing domestic resource development against international supply diversification. Data. Decision-theoretic POMDP model on a US lithium resource case; simulated policies, no new field data.

② Refining — the China chokepoint 3

Neither spodumene nor raw brine is usable in a cell; converting either to battery-grade carbonate or hydroxide is concentrated in China.
  • IEA — Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 (executive summary) · IEA (2025) Refining-concentration analysis across critical minerals, including lithium conversion. Abstract. IEA outlook on critical-mineral supply security. Refining concentration rose across nearly all minerals: the average market share of the top three refining nations of key energy minerals climbed from ~82% (2020) to ~86% (2024), and is projected to ease only marginally to ~82% by 2035. Data. IEA supply-demand modelling on production, refining and trade data across critical minerals.
  • IEA — Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 (executive summary) · IEA (2026) 2026 update on refining records, lithium prices and the projected deficit. Abstract. IEA's 2026 update: the average share of the top refining country rose to 72% in 2025 (from 70% in 2023). Lithium prices more than doubled between January 2025 and April 2026, yet producers cut investment ~40%; the projected 2035 lithium deficit narrowed from ~30% to ~25%. Data. IEA supply-demand and price modelling; refining-concentration and investment series.
  • IEA — Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 (outlook) · IEA (2026) Demand-growth and processing-capacity scenarios to 2040. Abstract. IEA scenarios: critical-mineral demand nearly doubles by 2040, with lithium rising over threefold — the steepest of any mineral. Mining capacity expands globally while refining and downstream manufacturing lag, keeping the midstream the binding constraint across lithium, graphite, nickel and cobalt. Data. IEA Stated Policies / scenario projections of demand, supply and processing capacity to 2040.

Market & price — the lithium whipsaw 3

Not scarce, just slow to build: a boom to 2022, then a crash as supply caught up — the price signal that runs across the chain.
  • OEMs and battery makers on alert as lower lithium prices push into 2024 · Benchmark Mineral Intelligence (2024) Market commentary on the lithium price collapse and downstream response. Summary. Battery-grade lithium carbonate fell 71.8% from $81,375/t (Dec 2022) to $22,950/t (Oct 2023) as supply outpaced Chinese demand and cell makers ran down inventories; Benchmark saw a small 2024 surplus but a return to deficit from 2028. Data. Benchmark Mineral Intelligence price assessments and market forecasts — no primary dataset.
  • Benchmark launches industry-first Lithium Forward Curves · Benchmark Mineral Intelligence New forward-price infrastructure for battery-grade lithium and spodumene. Summary. Benchmark launched year-ahead forward price curves for battery-grade lithium carbonate, hydroxide and spodumene, aggregating producer, trader and OEM expectations — infrastructure aimed at a metal it calls uniquely volatile, and at the China-domestic-versus-seaborne price gap. Data. Aggregated forward-price submissions across the lithium supply chain (Benchmark index).
  • Lithium prices crash below $10K, hitting a 4-year low · Carbon Credits (2025) Reporting on the price collapse, oversupply and underwater producers. Summary. Lithium carbonate fell below $10,000/t in February 2025 to $9,550/t, a four-year low, on oversupply (Chile, Mali, Argentina) and LFP-driven demand softening; Albemarle and SQM cut output and several hydroxide-refinery expansions were suspended. Data. Market reporting citing price, export and project-capacity figures; no primary dataset.

③ ④ Cathode & cell 0

Downstream of the refinery — where the lithium chemical becomes cathode powder and then the cell — the country-level trail runs cold for lithium alone.
No lithium-specific, country-level public source maps the cathode (③) and cell (④) stages: past the battery-grade chemical, the chain merges into the broader battery build-out, and the lithium share is no longer separable in trade data. That downstream is carried by the battery chain instead — see Gaps & good next searches for the cathode-precursor and LFP-versus-NMC leads worth chasing.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 8

List methodologies, EU/US assessments and network analyses that classify the whole chain — and, repeatedly, land on the refining step.
  • Critical Raw Materials Act · European Commission The EU regulation setting 2030 domestic-capacity benchmarks; lithium listed as critical and strategic. Summary. The EU Critical Raw Materials Act sets 2030 targets — 10% extraction, 40% processing, 25% recycling of annual consumption, and no more than 65% from any single third country. Lithium is listed as both critical and strategic, with EU demand seen rising twelvefold by 2030. Data. EU legislative benchmarks and Commission demand projections.
  • Geography of control: a deep dive assessment on criticality and the lithium supply chain · Mineral Economics (2023) Criticality assessment mapping the control points along the lithium supply chain. Abstract. Assesses criticality across the lithium supply chain, mapping where control concentrates from deposit to battery. Full text behind a Springer login (303 redirect); summary from the title and journal listing (Mineral Economics, 2023). Data. Supply-chain criticality analysis (Mineral Economics) — full text not retrievable this pass.
  • Critical risks in an industry chain-based global lithium supply network: static structure and dynamic propagation · Process Safety and Environmental Protection (2025) Network model of static structure and dynamic risk propagation in global lithium supply. Summary. Models the global lithium supply network's static structure and the dynamic propagation of risk along the industry chain. Full text paywalled (HTTP 403); summary from the title and abstract listing (2025). Data. Industry-chain network model with risk-propagation simulation — full text not retrievable this pass.
  • Technological risks disrupting trade stability in the global lithium supply chain network · PMC (2025) Multi-layer patent-and-trade network analysis of upstream-to-battery lithium flows. Abstract. A three-layer patent-and-trade network of the global lithium chain. The US holds 56–79% of core technologies across stages versus China's 6–25%; full technological disruption could cost ~$90 billion. Risk spreads mainly horizontally within layers, through a few core-nation nodes. Data. Patent analysis, Granger-causality tests and an SI epidemic model with Monte-Carlo simulation.
  • Revising the 'Economic importance' dimension: the European framework for critical raw materials, illustrated using lithium · Resources Policy (2024) Methodology paper revising the EU 'Economic Importance' criticality dimension, worked through lithium. Abstract. Revises the 'Economic Importance' dimension of the EU criticality framework, completing and illustrating the method with lithium as the worked example. Full text paywalled (HTTP 403); summary from the title and journal listing (Resources Policy, 2024). Data.   • Securing European lithium: assessing future demand, primary supply, trade and the role of recycling · npj Materials Sustainability (2026) EU lithium demand, primary supply, trade and recycling balance to future horizons. Abstract. Assesses future EU lithium demand against primary supply and trade, and the role recycling can play in closing the gap. Full text behind a Nature login (303 redirect); summary from the title and journal listing (npj Materials Sustainability, 2026). Data.   • Self-sufficiency of the European Union in critical raw materials for e-mobility · Sustainable Production and Consumption (2024) Quantifies EU import dependence on lithium and other e-mobility raw materials. Summary. Quantifies the EU's self-sufficiency in critical raw materials for e-mobility, including lithium import dependence. Argues the EU cannot secure critical materials domestically: exposure is market concentration, not cost. Analysis of EU import-concentration data and CRM-Act benchmarks; no primary dataset.

Gaps & good next searches

  • No dedicated conversion / refining trade association exists (ILiA covers the whole chain), and the chokepoint stage itself is thinly documented at company level — next: "China lithium carbonate hydroxide conversion capacity" for refiner-level detail.
  • Direct-lithium-extraction (DLE) technology and cost data is thin here; the IDTechEx "Direct Lithium Extraction 2025–2035" report surfaced only in search snippets with no stable public URL — try IDTechEx or S&P Global Commodity Insights directly.
  • No peer-reviewed life-cycle-assessment comparing spodumene-versus-brine carbon intensity was captured — worth a dedicated search ("lithium LCA spodumene brine comparison").
  • Bolivia's YLB / Uyuni brine project appears only in passing — search "Bolivia lithium YLB Uyuni 2026" for a current-state check on the reserve-rich, output-poor corner of the Triangle.
  • The cathode (③) and cell (④) stages have no lithium-specific public source here; the downstream is carried by the battery chain. A cathode-precursor capacity source (LFP vs NMC) would close the trail past the refinery.
