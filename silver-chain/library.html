Research Library · Silver

Silver — a by-product wearing a precious-metal coat

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Most silver is never mined for itself: it comes up as a by-product of lead, zinc, copper and gold, is refined, then flows into solar-cell paste, electrical contacts, electronics and jewellery. The mine cannot answer silver's own price — and solar is eating the slack.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Host mine (Pb / Zn / Cu / Au — silver rides along) → ② recover & refine, and the market balance → ③ solar-cell paste, the swing demand → ④ industrial, EV & jewellery demand — then the cross-cutting criticality / scarcity frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Host mine — silver rides on base metals 2

The raw input, and a by-product no one mines for: ~70% recovered from lead-zinc, copper and gold ores.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Silver · USGS (2026) Annual world production, reserves and by-product supply data for silver. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2025 US mine output ~1,100 t, produced at 4 silver mines and as a by-product or co-product at 31 base- and precious-metal operations; world mine production ~26,000 t, led by Mexico, China and Peru. Silver was primarily obtained as a by-product of lead-zinc, copper and gold mines; net US import reliance 77%. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade and price data; world figures drawn from Metals Focus / the World Silver Survey.
  • Methodology and Technical Input for the 2021 Review and Revision of the U.S. Critical Minerals List (OFR 2021-1045) · Nassar & Fortier, USGS (2021) Explains how USGS screens metals — including by-product ones — for criticality. Abstract. USGS Open-File Report documenting the methodology behind the 2021 draft critical-minerals list: a quantitative supply-risk score (disruption potential × economic vulnerability × trade exposure) across dozens of commodities, plus a by-product / host-commodity classification and a single-point-of-failure screen. Silver is among the commodities assessed. Data. USGS supply-risk indicators, 2007–2018, with by-product-status and single-point-of-failure analysis over production, trade and NAICS end-use data.

② Recover, refine & the market balance 3

Silver is separated during base-metal refining; the benchmark surveys set the supply/demand balance and the deficit.
  • World Silver Survey · The Silver Institute (2025) The industry's benchmark annual supply / demand / fabrication report. Abstract. The industry benchmark: an annual report on the global silver market, produced for The Silver Institute by Metals Focus since 1990. It covers mine and scrap supply; industrial, jewellery, silverware and investment demand; fabrication, prices and the market balance — the source of the chain's structural-deficit and by-product-share figures. Data. Metals Focus proprietary supply / demand fieldwork and modelling; the USGS silver summary cites it directly.
  • World Silver Survey — series access page · The Silver Institute Access point for the full annual survey series and free prior editions. Summary. Access route to the full World Silver Survey series: the latest edition is purchasable while prior editions and summary press releases are free — the provenance path to the benchmark balance data the chain rests on. Data. No dataset — a publications access / provenance page.
  • Interim Silver Market Review · Metals Focus for The Silver Institute (2025) Mid-year update to the annual survey (Newman & Tomlinson, Metals Focus). Summary. Metals Focus mid-year update (Nov 2025): calls 2025 one of the most dramatic years for silver in living memory — a record price, a London liquidity squeeze, and resilient physical demand, all on a continued structural deficit. Data. Metals Focus market analysis using Bloomberg, LBMA and CME / COMEX warehouse-stock data.

③ Solar — the swing demand 4

Silver paste carries current off every cell; PV is the fastest-growing use, pulling on a supply that cannot easily grow.
  • Solar PV Global Supply Chains · IEA (2022) Maps the PV manufacturing chain, including silver paste use in cells. Abstract. IEA report mapping the five stages of PV manufacturing — polysilicon, ingots, wafers, cells and modules — with their supply, demand, cost, trade and geographic concentration. Silver enters at cell metallization, the conductive paste that collects current; the report frames PV supply-chain risk and opportunities for diversification. Data. IEA compilation of manufacturing-capacity, cost, trade, emissions and employment data across the PV value chain.
  • Silver's Important Role In Solar Power · CRU for The Silver Institute (2020) Market-trend report specifically on silver demand from PV. Summary. CRU report for The Silver Institute: ~100 Moz of silver (~11% of fabrication demand) went into PV cells in 2019. Thrifting cuts silver per cell, but rising installations sustain roughly 70–80 Moz/yr of PV silver demand out to 2030. Data. CRU electricity-generation and PV-capacity forecasts with per-cell silver-loading estimates.
  • Evaluation of long-term silver supply shortage for c-Si PV under different technological scenarios · Li & Adachi, Natural Resource Modeling (2019) Models whether by-product silver supply can keep pace with PV cell demand. Abstract. Models silver supply versus c-Si PV demand to 2050 using Hubbert theory (mine supply), a Weibull distribution (recycling) and demand intensity. It finds a silver shortage for PV manufacturing beginning around 2030; technology improvement delays but cannot prevent it, so lower silver loadings and recycling of retired panels are recommended. Data. Scenario model of mine, recycling and manufacturing-demand series, 2015–2050.
  • A critical review of leaching pathways for silver recovery from EoL photovoltaic modules and prospects for flotation · Saffarian, Galvin & Firouzi, Mineral Processing and Extractive Metallurgy (2026) Recycling routes for silver from retired solar panels. Abstract. Critical review of silver recovery from end-of-life PV modules, which carry 300–500 ppm silver — comparable to high-grade ore — with more than 60 Mt of panels expected by 2050. It surveys acid-leaching parameters and, for the first time, proposes froth flotation as a pre-concentration step and greener deep-eutectic-solvent lixiviants. Data. Systematic literature review of leaching studies; no new experimental dataset.

④ Industrial, EV & jewellery demand 2

Beyond solar, silver is the best conductor there is — contacts, electronics, EVs, brazing — with industrial use now over half of demand.
  • Fabrication Demand Drivers for Silver in the Industrial, Jewelry and Silverware Sectors Through 2033 · Oxford Economics for The Silver Institute (2023) Long-run fabrication-demand forecast by end-user industry and country. Abstract. Oxford Economics forecast for The Silver Institute: output of the industries that consume silver rises 46% in real terms over 2023–2033, led by electrical and electronics (+55%); jewellery +34% and silverware +30%. Combined fabrication output grows ~42% — roughly double the prior decade — concentrated in Asia, especially China. Data. Oxford Economics industry-output modelling across silver end-user sectors and countries.
  • Silver, The Next Generation Metal · Oxford Economics / The Silver Institute (2025) Industrial-demand-focused positioning report. Summary. Oxford Economics / Silver Institute report on industrial demand: installed PV capacity grew tenfold in a decade (China ~51% of it) and PV silver use tripled despite thrifting; EVs, electronics and data centres are framed as the next legs of demand. Data. Oxford Economics demand modelling by application (PV, automotive / EV, data centres).

Across the chain — criticality, scarcity & outlook 4

List methodologies, EU/US assessments and material-scarcity studies that classify the whole chain's inputs.
  • Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 · IEA (2026) Annual assessment of critical-mineral supply/demand — context for by-product metals. Summary. IEA annual assessment of critical-mineral supply, demand, prices and investment across ~37 minerals, flagging supply concentration, export restrictions and under-investment — useful context for by-product metals whose output is set by their host commodities. Data. IEA supply / demand and investment tracking with scenario-based demand projections.
  • Supply chain analysis and material demand forecast in strategic technologies and sectors in the EU — a foresight study · Carrara, Bobba, Blagoeva et al., JRC (2023) Widely-cited EU foresight study on material demand across clean-tech chains, silver included. Abstract. JRC foresight study forecasting EU and global materials demand to 2050 across the full value chains of 15 key technologies in five strategic sectors — renewables, e-mobility, industry, digital and aerospace/defence. It underpins the Critical Raw Materials Act and maps the supply dependencies and bottlenecks of the materials each clean-tech chain draws on. Data. Bottom-up technology-deployment scenarios linked to material-intensity data across 15 technologies.
  • Resource scarcity challenges of photovoltaics in Europe 2050 · Kanz, Wulf & Zapp, Sustainable Energy & Fuels (2026) Long-run material-scarcity modelling for PV, including silver. Abstract. Assesses resource-scarcity risk for EU photovoltaic deployment using life-cycle assessment with the abiotic-depletion impact category. It finds that tellurium, gold and silver dominate the depletion impacts of PV — placing silver among the scarcest inputs constraining Europe's solar build-out to 2050. Data. Life-cycle assessment (abiotic depletion) over PV material inventories and EU deployment scenarios.
  • Critical Minerals and the Global Supply Chain · Chepeliev, Bekkers, Iyer, Qiu et al., Annual Review of Resource Economics (2026) Economics-literature survey of critical-mineral supply chains. Abstract. Annual Review survey of critical-mineral supply chains under rising clean-energy, electrification and computing demand. It examines upstream extraction, refining bottlenecks and downstream manufacturing, and the forces shaping them — long lead times, co-production constraints, trade policy and environmental limits — then outlines modelling frameworks for resilience. Data. Literature synthesis; no original dataset — a multi-mineral review of supply-chain structure and policy.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 4

The datasets the chain's own figures are built from — traded metal that mixes consumption with bullion and investment flows.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII (2026) Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's trade table. Summary. CEPII's reconciled bilateral trade database: ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. It powers the chain's silver-ore (261610) and unwrought-silver (710691) trade shares. Data. UN Comtrade import / export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • UN Comtrade Plus · United Nations Official international merchandise-trade statistics platform underlying BACI. Summary. The UN's official international merchandise-trade database — reporter-declared imports and exports by HS product and partner, and the primary source BACI is built from. The live portal is a JavaScript application that did not render to a static fetch this pass; described from provenance. Data. UN Statistics Division reporter-declared merchandise trade, by HS classification.
  • Commodity Markets Outlook, April 2026 · World Bank (2026) Cross-commodity price and supply outlook covering precious and industrial metals. Summary. World Bank semiannual flagship on commodity markets — price forecasts across energy, metals and minerals, precious metals and agriculture (April 2026 edition; next update October 2026). The report PDF sits behind the Open Knowledge repository's JavaScript viewer; described from the World Bank commodities research page — the file itself was not retrievable this pass. Data. World Bank monthly and annual commodity price series and forecasts.
  • Commodity Markets Outlook — data files · World Bank (2026) Underlying price/production data behind the Outlook report. Summary. The price and forecast data workbook behind the Outlook — monthly and annual commodity price series, precious and industrial metals included. Served through the Open Knowledge repository's JavaScript download shell; the data file was not directly retrievable this pass. Data. World Bank monthly / annual nominal and real commodity price series.

Gaps & good next searches

  • Silver is not on the EU's Critical Raw Materials list — it fails the "economic importance" screen despite by-product concentration risk. Worth a direct search for academic critiques of that omission.
  • A dedicated material-flow-analysis quantifying the true by-product vs primary-silver-mine share would harden the core ~70% claim — search Resources, Conservation and Recycling or the Journal of Industrial Ecology.
  • Independent market-intelligence coverage (Metals Focus / CRU standalone, Fastmarkets, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence) was not reachable this session — search these directly for 2025/2026 price and by-product-supply commentary.
  • The USGS National Minerals Information Center silver page carries a historical time series, not just the annual MCS PDF — worth fetching directly for the long production and price history.
  • No clean single HS6 code separates refined-silver consumption from bullion / investment trading, so the trade layer mixes metal and money; a dedicated study on silver's monetary-vs-industrial flows would sharpen it.
