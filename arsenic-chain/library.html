Research Library · Arsenic

Arsenic — the poison that is also a semiconductor

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Arsenic is a toxic by-product of copper and gold smelting that producers would rather not make at all — yet the small high-purity fraction is half of gallium arsenide, the compound semiconductor behind radar, RF and satellite chips. The chokepoint is the reluctant recovery step, not a mine.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① By-product recovery (copper/gold smelter flue dust; China → Peru) → ② trioxide, materials flow & toxicology → ③ high-purity arsenic → gallium-arsenide chips → ④ legacy wood preservative (declining) — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① By-product recovery — the toxic Cu/Au smelting by-product 6

Arsenic is not mined for itself: it is captured as arsenic trioxide from copper and gold smelter flue dust. China ~40%, Peru rose to the lead.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Arsenic · USGS (2026) Annual US/world arsenic production, trade, uses and import-source data. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: no arsenic metal or trioxide has been produced in the US since 1985, so net import reliance is 100%. Over 80% of domestic use is herbicides, insecticides and CCA wood preservatives; high-purity (99.9999%) arsenic makes GaAs and InGaAs semiconductors. 2025 imports ~$8.4M; trioxide from China 61%, Morocco 27%. Data. USGS producer/import canvass — US salient statistics, import sources 2021–24, tariff and warehouse-price data.
  • BGS, World Mineral Statistics — arsenic · British Geological Survey (2024) National production and trade statistics portal covering arsenic among ~80 commodities. Summary. BGS World Mineral Statistics: production, imports and exports by country for about 80 commodities including arsenic; online series 1970–2021 (trade to 2002, selected Europe to 2018), earlier years in the archive, downloadable as CSV. The source of the chain's China→Peru production share series. Data. BGS annual compilation of national mineral production and trade returns; the atlas's history chart is ground-truthed against mined_years.json.
  • A review of arsenic reaction behavior in copper smelting and its disposal techniques · J. Central South University (2023) Technical review of how arsenic partitions in copper smelting and how it is disposed of. Summary. Review of how arsenic behaves in the copper-smelting process — partitioning between slag, matte and flue dust — and of the techniques used to fix and dispose of it. Full text paywalled (Springer 303 auth redirect); summarised from title and journal. Data. Literature review (Journal of Central South University, 2023) — no primary dataset retrievable this pass.
  • The Role of Arsenic in the Mining Industry · SME Technical briefing on arsenic's occurrence and handling in mining/smelting. Summary. SME briefing: arsenic — the 20th most abundant element — occurs as an arsenopyrite/pyrite impurity in copper, gold and lead ores; smelter scrubbers and precipitators capture over 99% of emissions. Notes 100% US import reliance and gallium arsenide as a growing, more strategic use. Data. SME technical briefing — descriptive; cites global production figures and control practices.
  • Mining: Production and Control of Arsenic · SME Companion briefing on arsenic production and environmental controls at mine/smelter sites. Summary. SME briefing: scrubbers, electrostatic precipitators and baghouses remove up to 99.7% of dust and fumes at smelters; about 25% of atmospheric arsenic is natural. World production was ~59,000 t in 2021, all US supply imported (China, Morocco), under RCRA/EPA control. Data. SME technical briefing — control-technology description within the RCRA/EPA regulatory frame.
  • China mulls lower impurity thresholds on copper concentrate imports · SMM (Shanghai Metals Market) Trade-policy reporting on China's arsenic limits for imported copper concentrate. Summary. Trade report (Aug 2019): China's customs weighed cutting the arsenic limit on imported copper concentrate from 0.5% to 0.4%. High-arsenic concentrate is ~8% of global supply and rising (Chile, Peru), so tighter limits reshape where arsenic-bearing ore can be smelted. Data. Trade-press reporting on a proposed Chinese customs standard — no primary dataset.

② Trioxide, materials flow & toxicology 3

From flue dust to refined trioxide — and the toxicology that governs how much anyone wants to make.
  • Arsenic (Information Circular 9382) — The Materials Flow of Arsenic in the United States · U.S. Bureau of Mines / USGS (1991) Classic material-flow study tracing arsenic from smelter by-product to disposal. Summary. USBM Information Circular 9382 (1991): a materials-flow study tracing arsenic from smelter by-product through manufacture, dissipative use and disposal in the United States. The PDF is a scanned image with no machine-extractable text; described from the circular's title and known scope. Data. US Bureau of Mines material-flow accounting — historical; text not extractable this pass.
  • USGS Materials Flow · USGS National Minerals Information Center Index of USGS material-flow / supply-chain studies, including the arsenic circular. Summary. USGS index of material-flow and supply-chain studies across 40+ commodities, tracking extraction → processing → product plus recycling rates, stocks-in-use, import reliance and by-product recovery. The gateway to the arsenic circular and to USGS foreign-reliance work. Data. USGS program index page — links to the underlying studies, not a dataset itself.
  • Toxicological Profile for Arsenic — Production, Import/Export, Use, and Disposal · ATSDR / NCBI Bookshelf Federal toxicology profile chapter on arsenic production, trade and disposal. Abstract. ATSDR profile: arsenic is a by-product of copper, lead, cobalt and gold smelting, accumulating in flue dust up to ~30% arsenic trioxide before refining. US production ended in 1985 (ASARCO Tacoma); over 90% of pre-2004 trioxide use was CCA wood preservative; wastes are landfilled under hazardous-material rules. Data. ATSDR/NCBI federal synthesis of production, trade and disposal literature — no new dataset.

③ The semiconductor half — gallium-arsenide chips 6

Where the small high-purity fraction goes: half of gallium arsenide — radar, RF, satellite front-ends, LEDs. Pairs with the gallium chain.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Gallium · USGS (2026) Gallium / gallium-arsenide semiconductor context — the paired chain arsenic feeds. Abstract. USGS gallium summary: no primary US gallium since 1987; imported GaAs-wafer value ~$120M in 2025. Gallium arsenide makes compound-semiconductor wafers for integrated circuits and optoelectronics — laser diodes, LEDs, photodetectors, solar cells — used in defence, telecom and aerospace. GaAs is where high-purity arsenic goes. Data. USGS gallium canvass — imports, reported consumption, GaAs-wafer values and end uses.
  • Accurate Electronic, Transport, and Bulk Properties of Gallium Arsenide (GaAs) · arXiv (2016) Materials-physics reference for the GaAs properties underlying its device uses. Summary. Ab-initio (LDA, LCAO/BZW-EF) computation of GaAs's electronic, transport and bulk properties: direct band gap 1.429 eV (1.520 eV low-temperature), lattice constant 5.632 Å, bulk modulus 75.5 GPa — all matching experiment. The physics behind GaAs's high-frequency device use. Data. Density-functional calculation (Diakite et al., 2016) — theoretical, no experimental dataset.
  • GaAs Substrates for AI: Manufacturing, Market Growth & Gallium Supply Chain Risks · Exponential Industry (2026) Market analysis of GaAs substrate demand and upstream gallium/arsenic risk. Summary. Market analysis (Jul 2026): the GaAs-wafer market is ~$1.4B in 2026, 10–13% CAGR, driven by AI-datacentre VCSEL optical interconnects. Upstream risk is acute — China ~99% of primary gallium; three firms (Sumitomo, Freiberger, AXT) dominate substrates; end-of-life recycling below 1%. Data. Trade analysis citing market-size, producer-share and recycling estimates — no primary dataset.
  • Are We Running Out of Gallium? The Future of GaAs Wafers and Challenges to Come · WaferWorld Industry commentary on gallium/GaAs wafer supply constraints. Summary. Industry commentary: GaAs supply is economically, not physically, scarce — 80–90% of gallium is produced in China, none refined in the US since 1987, and China's mid-2023 export curbs strained chains. Indium phosphide and recycled wafers are partial substitutes. Data. Trade commentary — qualitative, cites production-share figures.
  • Mineral Demands for Resilient Semiconductor Supply Chains · CSIS (2024) Analysis of the mineral inputs (including arsenic-linked GaAs) behind semiconductor resilience. Summary. CSIS analysis (May 2024, Berg et al.): ranks five semiconductor minerals by disruption risk — gallium highest, then germanium, fluorine and arsenic (moderate, paired with gallium for GaAs wafers). Argues China's processing dominance, not extraction, is the vulnerability; build Western-Hemisphere processing. Data. Policy analysis citing gallium-share (98%/86%) and semiconductor-market figures.
  • From Mine to Microchip · CSIS (2024) Broader supply-chain analysis tracing critical minerals into chip manufacturing. Summary. CSIS brief (Oct 2024, Baskaran & Schwartz): traces gallium, germanium, palladium and silicon into chipmaking; a 30% gallium disruption is modelled at a $602B US GDP loss. Context for arsenic's GaAs partner, gallium, rather than arsenic itself. Data. Policy brief citing production-share and economic-impact estimates — no primary dataset.

④ The uses it is losing — legacy wood preservative 1

The bulk use is going away: chromated-copper-arsenate wood preservative, phased out for homes on health grounds.
  • Chromated Arsenicals (CCA) · US EPA Official page on the CCA wood-preservative residential phase-out. Summary. EPA page on CCA (chromium/copper/arsenic) wood preservative: manufacturers voluntarily ended residential CCA-treated wood in December 2003; commercial uses (poles, pilings, foundation beams) continue under 15-year registration review. Documents the decline of arsenic's former bulk use. Data. EPA regulatory / registration-review information — no production dataset.
