Ammonia / nitrogen-fertilizer chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Synthetic nitrogen fertilizer feeds roughly half the world — and its raw material, the nitrogen in the air, is free. What is not free is the natural gas that Haber-Bosch uses as both the hydrogen feedstock and the energy to run. The chokepoint here is a fuel price, not a mine.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Natural gas (the feedstock and the energy) → ② Haber-Bosch (air + gas → ammonia) → ③ the green / blue decarbonisation branch → ④ urea, nitrogen fertilizer and the harvest → ⑤ the 2022 European gas-price shutdowns — then the cross-cutting geopolitics, policy and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Natural gas — the feedstock and the energy 2

There is no ore: the nitrogen is the air. Gas is both the hydrogen source and the process heat — ~70–90% of the cost of making ammonia.
  • Unraveling the effect of variable natural gas feedstock on an industrial ammonia process · Computers & Chemical Engineering (2022) How natural-gas composition variability affects an industrial ammonia synthesis process. Abstract. Process-systems study of how variation in natural-gas feedstock composition propagates through an industrial ammonia plant's reforming and synthesis loop, affecting conversion, energy use and operability. (Full text not retrievable on this pass — publisher 403; scope taken from the title and venue, Computers & Chemical Engineering, 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.compchemeng.2022.107951.) Data. Process simulation / systems analysis of an industrial ammonia process — no public full text retrieved.
  • Life cycle assessment of ammonia synthesis in China · Int. J. Life Cycle Assessment (2021) Cradle-to-gate LCA comparing coal-, coke-oven-gas- and natural-gas-based ammonia routes. Abstract. Cradle-to-gate LCA (ISO 14040, CML 2001) of Chinese ammonia by raw-material route, on industrial data. Coal- and coke-oven-gas routes carry 1.43× and 1.7× the environmental burden of the natural-gas route, chiefly in greenhouse effect, acidification and fossil-energy depletion; advanced coal-water-slurry gasification cuts GHG from 3.88 to 2.18 kg CO2 per kg ammonia. Data. Actual industrial process data for coal-, coke-oven-gas- and gas-based routes; LCA via CML 2001 with sensitivity analysis.

② Haber-Bosch — fixing nitrogen from the air into ammonia 2

Where inert N₂ and gas-derived hydrogen become ammonia — at high temperature and pressure, sited wherever gas is cheap.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Nitrogen (Fixed)–Ammonia · USGS (2026) Annual world ammonia production/trade data; ties US capacity to natural-gas reserves. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: in 2025, 18 US companies made ammonia at 38 plants, ~57% of capacity in Louisiana, Oklahoma and Texas for their large natural-gas reserves — the dominant feedstock. About 88% of output went to fertilizer (urea, ammonium nitrate and others). China (~49,000) and India (~15,000 thousand t N) lead world output; Henry Hub gas averaged ~$3.44/MMBtu. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade, capacity and Henry Hub natural-gas price data.
  • IEA, Ammonia Technology Roadmap · IEA (2021) The reference roadmap for decarbonising ammonia (Haber-Bosch) production. Summary. IEA roadmap on making ammonia sustainable: ~70% of ammonia goes to fertilizers, and its production is ~1.3% of energy-system CO2 emissions. It uses three scenarios (Stated Policies, Sustainable Development, Net Zero by 2050) to chart technology pathways and set out roles for governments, producers and financiers. Data. IEA scenario modelling (Stated Policies / Sustainable Development / Net Zero) of ammonia production, energy use and emissions.

③ The decarbonisation branch — green & blue ammonia 4

Two futures for a gas-bound chain: electrolytic hydrogen (green) or gas-plus-carbon-capture (blue) — plus new demand as ammonia becomes a fuel.
  • Ammonia as an energy carrier: global supply-chain cost and greenhouse-gas emissions · Energy & Environmental Science (2025) Integrated techno-economic and GHG assessment of global ammonia supply chains across routes. Abstract. Builds a techno-economic and environmental database for global ammonia supply chains across 63 countries, comparing production technologies (grey, blue, yellow, pink, green) with downstream logistics on cost and greenhouse-gas emissions, to map where and how low-carbon ammonia can become competitive as an energy carrier. Data. Techno-economic + life-cycle emissions database spanning 63 countries and five production routes plus shipping logistics.
  • Green ammonia supply chain and associated market structure: an analysis based on transaction cost economics · arXiv (2023) Economic-structure analysis of the emerging green-ammonia supply chain. Abstract. Analyses green-ammonia supply chains from an industry-structure perspective using transaction-cost economics and lessons from oil and gas. Proposes three market-structure prototypes across the industry's phases — vertically integrated in infancy, disintegrating for efficiency, then competitive — and tests them with a regression model on a gas-industry case. Data. Transaction-cost-economics framework plus a multi-linear regression on a gas-industry case; conceptual — no ammonia production dataset.
  • Blue Ammonia and the Supply Chain: Pioneering Sustainability Assessment for a Greener Future · Energies (2025) Life-cycle sustainability assessment of blue ammonia (natural gas + carbon capture). Abstract. Life-cycle sustainability assessment (environmental, economic, social) of blue ammonia — natural gas with carbon capture — at 1.2 Mt/yr from feedstock to maritime transport, via Aspen HYSYS simulation. The synthesis converter dominates emissions, energy and cost; capture removes 6.5 Mt CO2/yr; transport distance and LNG fuel price are the leading sensitivities. Data. Aspen HYSYS V12 process simulation plus a maritime-transport sustainability model for a 1.2 MMTPA blue-ammonia chain.
  • Ammonia Production from Clean Hydrogen and the Implications for Global Natural Gas Demand · Sustainability (2023) Models how electrolytic-hydrogen ammonia could displace gas-based Haber-Bosch demand. Abstract. Ammonia's ~183 Mt/yr production consumes ~4% of global natural-gas supply; 1.5°C pathways see demand rising 3–4× by 2050 as shipping, power and hydrogen-transport markets emerge. Assesses the technical and economic viability of green (electrolytic) and blue (gas + CO2 storage) routes at a systems level, stressing certification, standards and finance. Data. Systems-level techno-economic assessment of green vs blue ammonia routes with process-integration modelling.

④ Urea, nitrogen fertilizer & the harvest 2

Ammonia becomes urea, ammonium nitrate and other nitrogen fertilizers — the yield that feeds roughly half the planet.
  • FAO, World fertilizer trends and outlook · FAO (2022) Global fertilizer supply, demand and trade outlook by nutrient, including nitrogen. Summary. FAO outlook projecting world and regional fertilizer nutrient supply, demand and balances for nitrogen, phosphate and potash over the medium term, drawing on IFA and FAO statistics to flag prospective surpluses or deficits by nutrient. Data. FAO/IFA fertilizer supply, demand, trade and capacity statistics by nutrient.
  • FAO Food Outlook — Fertilizer market update, June 2025 · FAO (2025) FAO's latest fertilizer market feature, post-2022-shock recovery data. Summary. FAO fertilizer feature: 2024 output rose (nitrogen/urea ~+3%, led by India, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE) and stresses that nitrogen fertilizer supply is inextricably linked to natural-gas prices — 2024's calmer European TTF gas (avg ~€35/MWh, −15%) steadied production. Data. FAO Food Outlook drawing on IFA production statistics and the Dutch TTF natural-gas price index.

⑤ The 2022 gas-price shutdowns 6

The chain's clearest stress test: when European gas spiked, ammonia plants closed — an energy crisis transmitted straight into food supply.
  • Understanding the resilience of fertiliser markets to shocks · OECD (2024) How fertilizer markets absorbed the 2022 gas-price and Russia/Belarus export shocks. Abstract. OECD paper taking stock of fertilizer policies after the 2021–22 price surges and building a framework to classify them, in a highly concentrated, import-dependent market prone to disruption. Finds low or zero import tariffs, non-transparent and shifting export restrictions by major exporters, and a policy shift from supporting mineral fertilizer to curbing its use or backing organic alternatives. Data. OECD inventory of member/partner-country fertilizer policies plus farmer-support estimates and trade data across N, P and K.
  • Ensuring availability and affordability of fertilisers — COM(2022) 590 · European Commission (2022) EU policy response to the 2022 gas-driven ammonia plant curtailments. Abstract. Commission communication on ensuring fertiliser availability and affordability after high gas prices and Russia's invasion shut a large share of EU ammonia capacity. Proposes prioritising gas for producers during rationing, state-aid and agricultural-reserve support, suspending ammonia/urea tariffs, a market observatory, import diversification (Egypt, Oman, Qatar) and conversion to renewable-hydrogen ammonia. Data. EU policy document — no primary dataset; cites prevailing gas-price and ammonia-capacity conditions.
  • Global Fertilizer Market Challenged by Russia's Invasion of Ukraine · USDA Economic Research Service (2023) US government analysis of the war's effect on global fertilizer prices and trade. Summary. USDA ERS analysis: Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine drove fertilizer prices up over 50% (Feb–Apr 2022); Russia and Belarus together supplied ~40% of global potash exports and Russia alone ~16% of urea. Later, lower European gas prices let production recover. Data. USDA ERS using 2020 trade data (latest available), World Bank prices and FAO statistics through March 2023.
  • EU industry under threat of shutting down — Act now · Fertilizers Europe European producers' association campaign on the 2022 gas-price crisis and closures. Summary. Fertilizers Europe campaign: with 2022 gas prices up over 1000% year-on-year and gas up to 90% of variable cost, ~70% of European ammonia capacity was curtailed. It urges EU action on gas pricing and reduced import reliance to protect food security. Data. Industry-association advocacy citing member production-capacity curtailment and natural-gas cost shares — no primary dataset.
  • Yara curtails production due to increased natural gas prices · Yara International Producer press release on 2022 curtailments at its Ferrara (Italy) and Le Havre (France) plants. Summary. Yara press release (March 2022): record European gas prices forced curtailment of ammonia and urea at its Ferrara (Italy) and Le Havre (France) plants — together ~1.0 Mt ammonia and ~0.9 Mt urea a year — cutting European ammonia/urea output to ~45% of capacity. Data. Company press release — self-reported plant capacities and utilisation.
  • INSIGHT: Crashing gas spurs fertilizer ramp up, petchems still shuttered · ICIS (2022) Trade-press reporting on European ammonia plant restarts as gas prices eased. Summary. Trade-press report that easing European natural-gas prices in late 2022 prompted fertilizer producers to restart ammonia capacity even as petrochemical plants stayed shut. (Article not retrievable on this pass — site access blocked; noted from the headline and standfirst only.) Data. Trade-press market reporting — no primary dataset; full text not retrieved.

CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • USGS — Nitrogen Statistics and Information · USGS, National Minerals Information Center Ongoing nitrogen/ammonia production and trade statistics hub. Summary. USGS National Minerals Information Center hub for nitrogen: annual Mineral Commodity Summaries and Minerals Yearbook chapters plus historical statistics on ammonia and nitrogen-compound production, trade and use. Data. USGS annual nitrogen/ammonia production, trade and use series.
  • USGS — Mineral Commodity Summaries · USGS Annual by-commodity production/reserves/trade data, including nitrogen. Summary. Landing page for the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries — the earliest US government publication estimating nonfuel-mineral industry data, with five-year statistics, tariffs and policy notes for 90-plus minerals including nitrogen. Data. USGS annual mineral-commodity statistics series (production, imports, reserves, use).
  • IFASTAT — Global Fertilizer Statistics & Market Data · International Fertilizer Association Production, trade and consumption data by nutrient (N/P/K). Summary. IFASTAT, the International Fertilizer Association's statistics service: production, trade and consumption data by nutrient (N, P, K), with consumption series back to 1961 and supply figures over recent decades. Data. IFA member and national statistics on fertilizer production, trade and consumption by nutrient.
  • Global Fertilizer database — IFASTAT plant-nutrition query · IFASTAT Queryable plant-nutrient database underlying IFA's published statistics. Summary. IFASTAT's queryable plant-nutrition database: supply, demand and trade indicators for fertilizers and raw materials by nutrient and geography, with consumption data from 1961 to present. Data. IFA plant-nutrient database (production/trade/consumption), user-queryable.
  • International Fertilizer Association (IFA) · IFA Global fertilizer industry association; publisher of IFASTAT and the Ammonia Technology Roadmap summary. Summary. The International Fertilizer Association — the only global fertilizer association, ~500 members across some 80 countries spanning the whole value chain — publishes IFASTAT and, with the IEA, the Ammonia Technology Roadmap summary. Data. No primary data — association profile and membership.
  • Mid-Year 2025 Fertilizer Market Update · The Fertilizer Institute (2025) US industry association's market snapshot of nitrogen supply/demand and pricing. Summary. The Fertilizer Institute's mid-year 2025 update (Veronica Nigh): a snapshot of global and US fertilizer supply and demand, 2025 geopolitical challenges, US import sources and strong domestic demand heading into the fall application season. Gaps & good next searches

  • No BGS (British Geological Survey) equivalent exists for nitrogen — nitrogen has no ore, so BGS's mineral-statistics remit largely does not cover it. Next: check BGS's occasional gas/energy-criticality notes instead.
  • No EU JRC Critical Raw Materials entry — nitrogen/ammonia is not on the EU CRM list (an energy commodity, not a mineral), so JRC coverage would come via its gas/energy-security work rather than the CRM Foundation series.
  • Coal-based ammonia in China — the main non-gas route — is thin here beyond one LCA paper. Next: "China coal-to-ammonia capacity" and NDRC/CNCIA sources for the non-gas half of world production.
  • Ammonia as shipping fuel and hydrogen carrier is only lightly covered. Next: "IMO ammonia bunker fuel" and "ammonia hydrogen carrier shipping" for the demand-side branch flagged in the chain's cards.
  • Paid market-pricing services (Benchmark Mineral Intelligence / Fastmarkets style) were not surfaced by open search; their public commentary, if any, would need a targeted site search.
  • Five entries could not be fully retrieved on this pass (two ScienceDirect papers, two IFPRI blogs, one ICIS report) — flagged in italics above; a return pass with library access or an alternate mirror would close them.
