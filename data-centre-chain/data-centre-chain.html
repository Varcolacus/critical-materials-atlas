A GPU is not a connected data centre

Accelerators matter, but usable compute also requires memory, networking, servers, racks, UPS systems, cooling, transformers, fibre, a permitted site and a grid able to supply concentrated load. The fastest-moving component sits inside a slower physical system.

chip shipments ≠ IT MW ≠ facility MW ≠ annual TWh ≠ digital service

The correction. Data-centre deployment can be constrained before chips arrive—or after. A facility may be built in two to three years, while cables and large transformers can take as long or longer to procure, before permitting and construction are counted.

The global number hides geographic concentration

Data-centre electricity by region, 2024

Rounded IEA shares of electricity consumption—not compute capacity or investment.

Local impact is the issue. Data centres remain a small global share, but capacity clusters in a handful of markets and grid nodes. The United States and China together account for nearly 80% of projected global consumption growth to 2030.

Inside the facility, there is no universal energy split

Cooling is not a fixed multiplier. Its share ranges from about 7% in efficient hyperscale facilities to more than 30% in less-efficient enterprise sites. Climate, density, air versus liquid systems and operating conditions matter.

Customs data cannot isolate AI infrastructure

BACI gives 2002–2024 context for four mixed-use baskets. Processing units are not GPU shipments, converters include far more than UPS systems, and facility construction and digital services do not cross customs as equipment.
