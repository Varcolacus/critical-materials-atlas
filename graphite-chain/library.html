Research Library · Graphite

Graphite / battery-anode chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Every lithium-ion cell needs a graphite anode. China mines about three-quarters of natural flake — but it turns nearly all of that, plus the synthetic graphite, into the spherical, purified, coated anode powder cells actually use. The chokepoint is the processing, not the flake in the ground.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Natural flake & synthetic graphite (the raw feedstock) → ② spheronising, purifying & coating into anode-grade graphite (the ~100% China chokepoint) → ③ the anode, the cell & the response — then the December-2023 export-control lever, the cross-cutting frameworks and the trade data that sit across every stage.

① Natural flake & synthetic graphite — the raw feedstock 6

Where the chain starts: flake mined ~74% in China, plus synthetic graphite baked from petroleum coke — and this is the least concentrated stage.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Graphite (Natural) · USGS (2026) Annual natural-graphite production, trade and tariff data; US consumption, imports and uses. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: the US mined no natural graphite in 2024; about 100 firms consumed 52,000 t ($115M). Imports ~60,000 t (87.7% flake/high-purity). Major uses are batteries, refractories and steelmaking. Apparent consumption fell ~21% on weaker battery demand and cheaper Chinese synthetic anode material; a 25% tariff on Chinese natural graphite begins 2026. (Direct PDF returned 403 on this pass; figures from the USGS MCS 2026 graphite text.) Data. USGS annual producer/importer canvass plus reported trade and price data.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Graphite (Natural) · USGS (2025) Prior-year graphite summary; flags the 2026 US tariff increase and US mining projects. Abstract. USGS commodity summary (2025): the US produced no natural graphite, and imports plus battery demand dominate use. Notes the announced increase of the tariff on Chinese natural graphite to 25% from 2026, and several US natural-graphite mining projects under exploration as potential domestic supply. (Direct PDF returned 403 on this pass; figures from the USGS MCS 2025 graphite text.) Data. USGS annual canvass and reported import/export trade data.
  • Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Graphite (Natural) data release · USGS Science Data Catalog (2025) Machine-readable data release behind the MCS 2025 graphite chapter. Summary. The structured data release accompanying the 2025 Mineral Commodity Summaries graphite chapter — the production, import-reliance and world-statistics tables in reusable form, the source data behind the printed summary. (Catalog page did not load on this pass; described from the catalog listing.) Data. USGS MCS 2025 underlying data tables (production, imports, world statistics).
  • Graphite Statistics and Information · USGS National Minerals Information Center Portal to the graphite MCS, Minerals Yearbook and historical statistics. Summary. USGS National Minerals Information Center portal for graphite: annual Mineral Commodity Summaries (1996–2026), Minerals Yearbook volumes (1994–2022) and Data Series 140 historical statistics on US and world supply, demand and material flow — the reference behind the mine-production figures, now migrating to ScienceBase. Data. USGS annual mineral statistics (production, consumption, trade).
  • BGS World Mineral Statistics — graphite mine production · British Geological Survey Historical mine-production series by commodity and country (China / Madagascar / Brazil). Summary. British Geological Survey database of world mineral production by commodity and country, spanning several decades — the source of the natural-graphite mine-production time series (China, Madagascar, Brazil) used for the chain's history chart. (Page did not load on this pass; described from the known BGS World Mineral Statistics database.) Data. National-statistics returns compiled annually by BGS.
  • Graphite: the new critical mineral · Nature Reviews Materials (2025) High-level review of graphite's rise as a critical mineral for batteries. Abstract. Review: graphite is the backbone of the lithium-ion industry as the primary anode material, yet natural supply is geographically concentrated with sluggish mining scalability. China controls ~90% of anode production and ~98% of graphitisation; purification, spheronisation and coating happen almost entirely there. Argues green synthesis and spent-anode recycling are the way out. Data. Literature review / perspective (Rice University authors) — no primary dataset.

② Spheronising, purifying & coating — the ~100% China chokepoint 8

Turning flake or synthetic graphite into rounded, >99.95%-carbon, carbon-coated anode powder — the wasteful, know-how-heavy step that is almost entirely in one country.
  • IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 — Executive summary · IEA (2025) Graphite demand and processing-concentration analysis. Summary. IEA outlook: China supplied ~90% of refined-graphite growth in 2020–2024; the top-three refiners hold ~82%, and outside the leader only about half of remaining 2035 demand is met. Energy uses drove 85% of battery-mineral demand growth; graphite prices fell 10–20% in 2024. Data. IEA supply/demand modelling on production, trade and project-pipeline data.
  • IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 — Outlook · IEA (2026) Updated outlook; refining concentration and export-restriction context. Summary. IEA 2026 outlook: for graphite (with gallium, manganese and rare earths) the top refiner, China, exceeds 90% of global supply; demand grows 50–90% to 2040; midstream/downstream build-out lags upstream mining, and many such materials already face export restrictions. Data. IEA scenario modelling (Stated Policies and announced pledges).
  • Benchmark Mineral Intelligence — Natural Graphite & Anode · Benchmark (also Natural Graphite) Dedicated graphite/anode price, capacity and forecast service. Summary. Benchmark's price, capacity and forecast service for flake, spherical and synthetic graphite and anode active material: facility-level capacity to 2035, supply/demand to 2050, graphitisation tolling fees and satellite-verified plant assessments — the market-intelligence base for anode-capacity questions. Data. Proprietary price assessments, facility surveys and satellite verification — subscription service, no open dataset.
  • Infographic: China controls three-quarters of the graphite anode supply chain · Benchmark Mineral Intelligence Visual summary of processing concentration across the chain. Summary. Benchmark infographic: China holds ~74% of the whole graphite-anode supply chain, with under 1% of uncoated spherical-graphite output forecast outside China, and 69% of the low-sulphur petroleum coke that feeds synthetic anode (Japan 11%, US 10%). Data. Benchmark supply-chain modelling and forecasts.
  • Global Value Chains: Graphite in Lithium-ion Batteries for Electric Vehicles · USITC working paper Global-value-chain analysis of the graphite-to-anode chain. Abstract. USITC Office of Industries working paper mapping the graphite global value chain for EV batteries. Finds the unrefined (mining) stage geographically diverse, but the refining stage before cell manufacture clustered in Asia, especially China; about 65% of anode active material was natural graphite, ~30% artificial (2016 base). Data. USITC industry analysis of production and trade data across the four key battery materials.
  • Overview of graphite supply chain and its challenges · Geosciences Journal (Springer, 2025) Supply-chain-wide review from mine to anode. Abstract. Peer-reviewed review of the graphite supply chain from mine to battery anode and its supply-security challenges. Full text is behind a publisher paywall — not retrievable on this pass (a research-sharing mirror also blocked access); the mine-to-anode scope is taken from the article's title and listing. Data. Literature review (Geosciences Journal, 2025) — no primary dataset.
  • Why does China make 97% of the world's battery anodes? · Decoding the Dragon Explainer on the processing chokepoint and why it stays concentrated. Summary. Explainer on the anode chokepoint: flake is spheronised then purified to 99.95% carbon with hydrofluoric acid (hard to permit in the West); synthetic graphite is graphitised near 3,000°C, where electricity is 25–50% of cost. Three Chinese firms dominate; China made ~97% of anode material. Data. Trade-press analysis citing producer capacities and cost structure.
  • Toward Sustainable Anode Materials: LCA of Natural Graphite Processing in Québec · Batteries / MDPI Life-cycle assessment of an alternative (non-China) anode-processing route. Abstract. Life-cycle assessment of a Québec graphite mine and processing plant making anode active material (AAM). Producing one tonne of AAM in Québec emits ~1.44 t CO₂-equivalent, far below the ~9.6 t per tonne reported for Chinese production — quantifying the emissions case for a North American route. Data. LCA on industrial primary data from a Québec operation.

③ The anode, the cell & the response 3

The graphite is cast onto copper foil as the anode; the response — recycling and Western reshoring — runs on a battery-qualification timescale, not a policy one.
  • Recycling strategies for renewable graphite and other carbon nanomaterials from used batteries: A review · Journal of Cleaner Production Anode-recycling review — relevant to the "response" stage. Abstract. Review of strategies to recycle graphite and carbon nanomaterials from spent batteries: physical routes (flotation, roasting, microwave), chemical routes (alkali etching, acid leaching), electrochemical recovery and functionalisation, plus synthesis of carbon nanomaterials and the environmental impacts of battery-waste recycling — a secondary-supply path for anode graphite. Data. Literature review (Journal of Cleaner Production, 2025) — no primary dataset.
  • The U.S. is building a homegrown synthetic graphite supply chain for EV batteries · Pamir Consulting US reshoring analysis of synthetic-graphite capacity. Summary. Analysis of US synthetic-graphite reshoring: Anovion (DOE-backed, ~$800M, 40,000 t/yr Georgia plant), Novonix, Graphex and Epsilon among projects, aided by IRA incentives. Synthetic could be ~two-thirds of the anode market, but the transition takes years and natural-graphite sourcing stays a risk. Data. Industry analysis citing company announcements and funding figures.
  • Mapping resilient supply solutions for graphite · Rice University News (2025) Research-communication piece on diversification options (companion to the Nature Reviews review). Summary. Rice University summary of its Nature Reviews Materials review: graphite demand is outpacing lithium, supply is concentrated and carbon-intensive, and the resilient paths are synthetic graphite from biomass or captured CO₂, spent-anode recycling and greener graphitisation. Data. Research communication accompanying the peer-reviewed review.

The lever — China's graphite export controls 9

Because the anode step is ~100% China, the December-2023 licensing was a lever on the whole battery industry — extended to anode materials in October 2025, then suspended.
  • China's New Graphite Restrictions · CSIS Policy analysis of the December 2023 export licensing. Summary. CSIS analysis of China's 1 December 2023 graphite licensing (high-purity synthetic and natural flake graphite and their products). China refines over 90% of graphite; the measure hits allies hardest — South Korea sources 93% and Japan 90% of anode material from China. Data. Policy analysis citing trade and import-dependency figures.
  • China's Export Controls on Critical Minerals · FTI Consulting Cross-mineral analysis (gallium, germanium, graphite). Summary. FTI Consulting places the December 2023 graphite licensing in the same playbook as the August 2023 gallium and germanium controls: MOFCOM approval required to export, initial shipments halted, and a "significant risk" to EV battery supply chains outside China. Data. Consulting analysis of the control measures and their trade impact.
  • China restricts exports of graphite used to make battery anodes · C&EN (ACS) Trade-press coverage of the December 2023 controls. Summary. Chemical & Engineering News: from December 2023 Chinese firms need permits to export several natural and synthetic graphite forms used for anodes. Chinese producers make 99% of spherical graphite and 93% of all graphite anode material — corroborating the near-total processing grip. Data. Trade-press reporting citing production-share figures.
  • China's Jan-Feb graphite exports plunge amid export controls · Fastmarkets Market reporting on the export-volume impact of the controls. Summary. Fastmarkets: in Jan–Feb 2024, after the December controls, Chinese flake-graphite exports fell 78% year-on-year (to 3,236 t) and spherical-graphite exports 65% (to 2,567 t); spherical went mostly to Japan (61%), the US (29%) and South Korea (9%). Data. Chinese customs export-volume statistics.
  • Trade Compliance Flash: Graphite Export Controls · Miller & Chevalier Legal / trade-compliance analysis of the controls. Summary. Law-firm trade-compliance note on China's graphite export-licensing countermeasures affecting electric-vehicle supply chains and the compliance obligations they create. (Page not retrievable on this pass — 403; scope taken from the publication title.) Data. Legal analysis of the export-control regime — no primary dataset.
  • China imposes export controls on lithium-batteries and artificial graphite anode materials · Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer (2025) The October 2025 extension of controls to anode materials — and its immediate suspension. Summary. Law-firm update: on 9 Oct 2025 China (MOFCOM Decision 58) put lithium batteries, cathode and artificial-graphite anode materials and related equipment on the dual-use export-control list — then on 7 Nov 2025 (Decision 70) suspended it until 10 Nov 2026, so those exports currently remain unrestricted. Data. Chinese MOFCOM/Customs decisions (official policy record).
  • Impact of graphite export restrictions in China · Minor Metals Trade Association Trade-association market commentary. Summary. MMTA commentary: China holds over 60% of flake and ~80% of synthetic graphite, and 99% of spherical / 93% of anode material; the December 2023 permits raised concern over spherical-graphite availability and gave short-term price support amid pre-deadline stocking. Data. Trade-association market commentary — no primary dataset.
  • China's Temporary Easing of Graphite Export Controls · Crux Investor Market-outlook piece on the suspension and non-China supply. Summary. Outlook on China's November 2025 suspension of enhanced graphite controls to the US (to Nov 2026): tactical relief, not a structural shift. China holds ~75% of natural graphite at ~$257/t; non-China projects (e.g. Malawi's Kasiya, ~$241/t) gain optionality under IRA content rules. Data. Investor analysis citing production-cost and project figures.
  • Decoding China's Graphite Export Controls: A Global Battery Supply Chain Crisis? · Shanghai Metal Market Market reporting on the scope and impact of the controls. Summary. Shanghai Metal Market: China's permits on high-purity, high-strength graphite (effective December 2023) target EV, aerospace and semiconductor uses; the controls shrink supply and raise costs, prompting pre-deadline stockpiling and a push toward synthetic graphite and recycling. Data. Market reporting — qualitative, no primary dataset.

Gaps & good next searches

  • Quantitative Western spheronising / synthetic-anode capacity build-out (US IRA-backed plants, EU projects) is present here only qualitatively, not as hard tonnage. Next: "spherical graphite plant capacity United States Europe 2025 2026 announced" — or Benchmark facility data and company filings.
  • No HS6 customs line isolates spherical/coated anode graphite from ordinary flake or refractory graphite (or from artificial graphite HS380110), so BACI reads the raw forms only. Worth checking national customs schedules directly, including China's own export-control HS lines.
  • Academic material-flow analysis specific to anode-grade graphite (parallel to the gallium/indium MFA work) was not retrieved. Next: "material flow analysis battery anode graphite China".
  • The binding step — the spherical-anode stage itself — has no public country-level production series; it hides inside broad customs baskets and market-intelligence subscriptions. That invisibility is itself the finding, and mirrors the single-crystal-blade gap in the aerospace chain.
