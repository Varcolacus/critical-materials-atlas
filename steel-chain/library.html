Primary / green-steel chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Steel turns an abundant ore into the metal civilisation is built from — yet the supply squeezes are not at the mine. The bottlenecks are a fuel and a transition: metallurgical coking coal to reduce the ore, and, for low-carbon "green steel", clean hydrogen plus a scarce grade of ore. The iron ore is the least concentrated stage of all — the concentration is downstream, in who smelts it (China ~52%) and in the coal that reduces it.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Iron ore (abundant, Australia & Brazil) → ② the reduce stage — metallurgical coking coal, and the DRI / hydrogen-DRI route → ③ crude steel (China ~52%) → ④ the green-steel transition (DRI-grade ore + clean hydrogen) — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Iron ore — abundant and diversified 1

The raw input, and the least concentrated stage in the chain: Australia and Brazil dominate, but no single-country geological chokepoint at the mine.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Iron Ore · USGS (2026) Annual world iron-ore production/reserves/trade data by country. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: eight US open-pit mines shipped 98% of domestic usable iron ore in 2025, ~1.4% of world output. World mine production is led by Australia (~980 Mt) and Brazil (~420 Mt); US import sources are Brazil 58%, Canada 21%, Sweden 10%. Global 62%-Fe fines averaged ~$99/t (Tianjin). Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported world production, reserves, trade and price data.

② The reduce stage — coking coal, DRI & hydrogen-DRI 3

Where the ore becomes iron: coke-fired blast furnaces (~70% of steel) burn metallurgical coking coal — the real carbon chokepoint — while direct reduction with gas or hydrogen is the low-carbon alternative.
  • Iron and Steel Technology Roadmap · IEA (2020) Reference roadmap for steel-sector decarbonisation pathways, including hydrogen-DRI. Abstract. IEA roadmap: steel is ~7% of energy-sector CO2. It maps decarbonisation to 2050 — material efficiency cuts demand about a fifth, hydrogen-DRI reaches just under 15% of primary steel where renewable power is cheap ($20–30/MWh), and CCUS-equipped blast furnaces and smelting reduction supply roughly another quarter of cumulative abatement. Data. IEA scenario modelling (Stated Policies vs Sustainable Development to 2050) on plant-level production data and technology-readiness (TRL) assessments.
  • A Review of the Thermodynamic, Kinetic, and Economic Aspects of H2-based Direct Reduced Iron (DRI) Process · Journal of Sustainable Metallurgy (2026) Technical review of hydrogen-DRI, the core green-steel process route. Abstract. Evaluates hydrogen-DRI's thermodynamic feasibility up to 1600 K and its kinetics, with a techno-economic comparison of BF, COREX, MIDREX, HYL III, H2-DRI and EAF in the Indian context. Finds EAF the most economically favourable near-term route, natural-gas DRI the practical interim, and H2-DRI the highest decarbonisation potential despite high CAPEX and operating costs. Data. Thermodynamic/kinetic modelling plus techno-economic (CAPEX/OPEX, raw-material, environmental) assessment normalised to July-2024 Indian market conditions.
  • The case for green hydrogen steel in China: A strategic analysis · International Journal of Hydrogen Energy (2026) Strategic analysis of hydrogen-steel adoption in the world's largest steel producer. Summary. Strategic analysis of adopting hydrogen-based steelmaking in China, which makes over half the world's steel, weighing the case for shifting from coke-fired blast furnaces to H2 direct reduction. (Full text paywalled — server returned 403; the abstract could not be retrieved, so this is summarised from the title and journal listing only.) Data. Strategic / techno-economic analysis — full text not retrieved this pass.

③ Crude steel — the concentration is industrial 2

Not a mined-resource chokepoint but a smelting one: China makes roughly 52% of the world's crude steel in the largest blast-furnace fleet on Earth.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Iron and Steel · USGS (2026) Annual finished-steel production, route mix and trade data. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: the US produced 82 Mt of raw steel in 2025 (~$149 bn). Two companies ran integrated mills; 47 companies ran 102 electric-arc minimills, ~105 Mt/yr capacity. Construction took 31% of shipments, automotive 15%. Net import reliance was 13%; imports from Canada 23%, Mexico 15%, Brazil 13%. Data. USGS voluntary producer surveys plus reported steel-mill-product import/export trade data.
  • World Steel in Figures 2025 · worldsteel (2025) The industry's standard annual statistical reference for crude-steel production by country. Summary. worldsteel's standard annual statistical digest — crude-steel production by country and region, apparent steel use, trade and per-capita figures — and the reference behind the chain's ~52%-China crude-steel share. (The live page served only a title / JS app shell this pass, so the figures were not re-read from it here.) Data. worldsteel member-association production returns and national statistics; annual compilation.

④ Green steel — the transition moves the chokepoint again 5

Decarbonising steel does not remove the bottleneck — it relocates it to clean hydrogen, clean power, scrap and a scarce high-grade "DRI-grade" ore.
  • Green Iron Opportunities in Australia · OECD (2025) Assesses Australia's potential to export green iron rather than raw ore, addressing the high-grade-ore chokepoint directly. Abstract. OECD: Australia can add value to its iron ore by shifting from a "dig-and-ship" model to direct-reduction green iron, aligned with the steel transition. Its green-iron sector stays nascent — limited investment, infrastructure gaps, policy fragmentation — as competitors advance; success needs scaled infrastructure, policy coordination and international industrial alliances. Data. OECD policy analysis of Australia's iron-ore reserves, renewable-energy potential, project pipeline and evolving trade policy.
  • Australia's path to green iron · IEEFA (2025) Analyses the shift from raw-ore exports to green-iron / DRI production. Abstract. IEEFA briefing note (Basirat, Sep 2025): Australia risks missing the first wave of low-emissions iron — already supplied by Vale, Rio Tinto's IOC (Canada) and LKAB (Sweden). It must pursue two paths in parallel: leverage its magnetite deposits to join higher-grade-ore suppliers, and develop technology to upgrade low-grade Pilbara ore for hydrogen-DRI. Data. IEEFA analysis plus its Australian Green Iron Tracker of magnetite-mine and green-iron projects (latest public data).
  • Resource and energy conservation in co-production of steel and chemicals · Energy Conversion and Management (2026) Resource-efficiency angle on integrated steel production. Summary. Analyses resource- and energy-efficiency gains from integrated systems that co-produce steel and chemicals, an efficiency route rather than a full process switch. (Full text paywalled — server returned 403; the abstract could not be retrieved, so this is summarised from the title and journal listing only.) Data. Process/systems modelling of integrated steel–chemicals co-production — full text not retrieved this pass.
  • Decarbonizing the steel industry: a case study in Brazil utilizing nuclear-renewable hybrid energy systems · Clean Technologies and Environmental Policy (2025) Energy-supply case study for low-carbon steelmaking. Abstract. Compares four routes for a 2 Mt/yr Brazilian plant: coal BF-BOF, grid-electrified BF-BOF, hydrogen-DRI on a nuclear-renewable hybrid, and H-DRI on grid power. H-DRI cuts emissions sharply — lowest with the hybrid; carbon taxes above $15/t CO2, high coal prices and SMR/electrolyser improvements raise its competitiveness. Low-carbon energy, not the ore, is the pivot. Data. Scenario case study (GHG / CO2e accounting) with sensitivity analysis on coal price, carbon price, SMR and electrolyser costs.
  • ASEAN-5 Steel Sector Decarbonization and Green Demand · RMI (2026) Regional analysis of steel decarbonisation and green-steel demand creation. Summary. RMI: Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Thailand and Vietnam can meet fast-rising steel demand without carbon lock-in by favouring electric-arc-furnace-compatible capacity, stronger domestic scrap collection and clean metallic inputs, plus market standards that reward low-emission steel. Data. Regional analysis of ASEAN-5 steel capacity, blast-furnace relining cycles and scrap availability — no primary dataset on the page.

Across the chain — frameworks & policy 2

The criticality and price-outlook lenses that frame where steel's real constraints sit.
  • Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 · IEA (2026) Context on supply concentration for energy-transition minerals; confirms iron ore/coking coal sit outside the critical-minerals frame. Summary. IEA (Jul 2026): supply/demand projections for 37 critical minerals, warning that concentration, export restrictions and falling investment threaten mineral security. Iron ore and metallurgical coal sit outside its scope — so steel is context here, not a listed criticality case. Data. IEA Critical Minerals Data Explorer — demand projections across three scenarios and eleven technology-specific cases.
  • Commodity Markets Outlook, April 2026 · World Bank (2026) Cross-commodity price outlook including iron ore and coal. Summary. The World Bank's semi-annual flagship price outlook, covering energy, metals and agricultural commodities — including iron ore and coal — with near-term forecasts. (The April-2026 bitstream returned 403 / a damaged file this pass, so specific figures were not read; described from the report series.) Data. World Bank price forecasts and historical commodity price series — file not retrieved this pass.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 3

The datasets the chain's own trade figures are built from — with the caveat that customs codes do not separate coking from thermal coal, nor DRI-grade from ordinary ore.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6) used for the chain's trade table. Summary. CEPII database: BACI reconciles bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products (HS92 back to 1995, latest to 2024), derived from UN Comtrade by converting CIF imports to FOB and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB adjustment, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • UN Comtrade Plus · United Nations The official international merchandise-trade statistics platform underlying BACI. Summary. The UN Statistics Division's official international merchandise-trade database — annual and monthly flows by HS commodity and partner country — the primary customs layer that BACI reconciles. (The page served only a "Loading" app shell this pass; described from the platform's established role.) Data. National customs declarations compiled by UNSD — the raw reporter data beneath BACI.
  • Commodity Markets Outlook data files · World Bank (2026) Underlying price/production data behind the Outlook, including iron-ore and coal series. Summary. The downloadable monthly and annual price and index series behind the Commodity Markets Outlook — including iron-ore and coal series — the data layer for any price overlay on the chain's trade figures. (Download not retrieved this pass; described from the series.) Data. World Bank monthly/annual commodity price and index series (the "Pink Sheet" family).
