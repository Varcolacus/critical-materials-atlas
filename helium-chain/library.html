Research Library · Helium

Helium chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Helium cools the superconducting magnets in MRI scanners and carries semiconductor and fibre-optic processes — yet it cannot be manufactured, only captured as a by-product of a few helium-rich natural-gas fields, and once released it escapes to space. Supply is welded to gas fields, not to demand.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① helium-rich gas field (US / Qatar / Russia) → ② cryogenic separation & the Federal Helium Reserve → ③ end uses — MRI, chips & rockets → ④ non-renewable: recovery & conservation — then the cross-cutting shortage/geopolitics analysis and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Helium as a by-product — gas fields, US / Qatar / Russia 5

The raw input, and a gas no one drills for on its own: only economic where natural gas already carries it in unusual concentration.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Helium and Rare Gases · USGS (2026) Annual world helium production/reserves/trade data plus the US end-use breakdown. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2025 US Grade-A and gaseous helium sales an estimated 81 million m³ ($970 million). World production ~190 million m³ — US 381, Qatar 63, Russia 18, Algeria 11. US uses span analytical/specialty gases (22%), controlled atmospheres/fibre/semiconductors (17%), lifting (17%), MRI (15%). Nothing substitutes for helium below −429 °F. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade and price data; rare-gas figures from Intelligas Consulting and TECHCET.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Helium · USGS (2025) Prior-year baseline; documents the January 2024 sale of the Federal Helium System. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2024 helium sales ~81 million m³. Records that the Helium Stewardship Act of 2013 mandated privatisation; management passed from BLM to GSA in December 2022, and on 25 January 2024 the Federal Helium System assets were sold in two lots (~28 and ~22 million m³ of crude helium), transferred to one company on 27 June 2024. Data. USGS producer surveys plus Census Bureau import/export data; reserves revised on Government reports.
  • USGS Minerals Yearbook 2023 — Helium (advance release) · USGS (2026) Annual detail chapter with the full Helium Stewardship Act phase-out chronology. Abstract. USGS annual chapter: 2023 US helium consumption 56.0 million m³, sales 81.2 million m³ (up 5%). Details the Helium Stewardship Act of 2013 four-phase privatisation timeline — continuing the 1996 Helium Privatization Act's mandated sale of the Cliffside Field reserve, structured to mitigate shortages while stimulating private helium sources through market-driven auctions. Data. USGS domestic survey data and tables; Census Bureau import/export figures; statutory record of the HSA.
  • World minerals outlook — cobalt, gallium, helium … through 2029 · USGS SIR 2025-5021 Forward supply-capacity outlook for eight commodities, including helium. Abstract. USGS Scientific Investigations Report analysing production-capacity outlooks for 2025–2029. Cobalt and lithium are expected to see large capacity growth; for gallium, helium, palladium and platinum, capacity should stay stable or grow moderately, still above current production, leaving room for future output. Magnesium and titanium outlooks are opaque owing to nonmarket-economy production. Data. USGS analysis of 2018–2024 production and production-capacity data by commodity and country.
  • A review of helium resources and development · Natural Gas Industry B (2025) Comprehensive review of helium sources, enrichment and supply sustainability. Abstract. Review (Hu et al., June 2025) of the value, sources, production methods, supply dynamics and sustainability of helium, whose scarcity demands efficient use. It analyses helium enrichment processes and exploration methods and recommends developing preservation, recovery and extraction technologies — especially for helium-poor fields — concluding no imminent global helium-supply crisis is expected before 2060. Data. Literature and industry review of helium geology, enrichment and market outlook — synthesis, no primary dataset.

② The Federal Helium Reserve — the buffer that was removed 8

For decades the US reserve absorbed shocks; its 2013 mandated wind-down and 2024 sale removed that cushion just as demand grew.
  • US BLM / GAO — Federal Helium Reserve disposal · GAO (2024) Listed in the chain record as the reserve-disposal oversight source. Abstract. Flagged: the cited GAO product number (GAO-24-106390) resolves to an unrelated report — "Veterans Community Care Program: VA Needs to Strengthen Contract Oversight" — not a Federal Helium Reserve document. The reserve-sale facts the chain relies on are instead carried by USGS MCS 2025 (the 25 Jan 2024 two-lot sale) and the GAO/GSA/BLM items below. Data. Not applicable — the linked report's subject does not match the cited topic; see USGS MCS 2025 and items 9–13 for the reserve record.
  • Economics, Helium, and the U.S. Federal Helium Reserve: Summary and Outlook · Natural Resources Research (2018) Economic review of helium markets and the drawdown of the Federal Helium Reserve. Abstract. Reviews why helium markets lack flexibility, so drawing on the Federal Helium Reserve cannot easily offset the loss of a major producer. Names the sector's structural issues: inelastic demand, availability only as a by-product, just 4–5 major producers, helium's escape from the crust, no FHR-scale storage elsewhere, and no penalty for venting. Notes a lack of empirical helium-market models. Data. Economic synthesis of helium supply/demand and FHR drawdown literature; no new empirical estimation.
  • The Impact of Selling the Federal Helium Reserve · National Research Council (2000) Congressionally mandated study of the 1996 Helium Privatization Act. Abstract. National Research Council committee assessing whether the Helium Privatization Act's mandated liquidation of the Federal Helium Reserve would substantially harm US scientific, technical, biomedical and national-security users. It maps helium applications and market dynamics and builds disposal scenarios to test consequences for helium-dependent research and industry. Data. Committee evidence: site visits (Amarillo reserve, ExxonMobil), supplier/user workshops, and analysis of helium demand and global resources.
  • BLM: More Information Needed to Implement the Helium Stewardship Act of 2013 (GAO-15-394) · GAO (2015) Oversight review of BLM's early implementation of the 2013 Act. Summary. GAO found BLM lacked information to verify refiners' compliance with the Act's "tolling" provision (excess refining capacity offered to competitors), because reporting was voluntary and incomplete, and had not assessed whether its auction method maximised federal revenue as the law required. Data. Review of BLM's first (2014) helium auction results, the 2013 statute, and interviews with BLM and registered refiners/non-refiners.
  • Helium Stewardship Act of 2013: BLM Needs More Information (GAO-15-734T) · GAO (2015) Congressional testimony restating the April 2015 findings. Summary. July 2015 testimony reprising GAO-15-394: BLM lacked full assurance that refiners satisfied the tolling provision and had chosen a live-auction format without confirming it maximised revenue, after two refiners bought all 93 million cubic feet of the 2014 auction above competing bids. Data. Drawn from the April 2015 report — 2014 auction analysis and interviews with 12 of 13 registered refiners/non-refiners.
  • Helium Program: Urgent Issues Facing BLM's Storage and Sale of Helium Reserves (GAO-13-351T) · GAO (2013) Testimony on the reserve-debt and storage backdrop before the 2013 Act. Summary. February 2013 testimony before the House Natural Resources Committee: the federal government has produced, stored and used helium since the early 20th century. Interior bought ~34 billion cubic feet of crude helium in the 1960s–70s for conservation and federal needs; the purchase debt is repaid through crude-helium sales into the revolving Helium Fund. Data. GAO review of BLM's helium program operations, storage, and the Helium Fund accounts.
  • BLM announces disposal process for Federal Helium System · US Bureau of Land Management (2020) Press release on BLM's exit and the handover to GSA. Summary. BLM announced it would cease managing the Federal Helium System, with remaining assets transferring to GSA for disposal per the Helium Stewardship Act. The Amarillo, Texas reserve was then the world's only helium storage facility; since FY2005 the self-funded program returned nearly $2 billion to the Treasury. Data. Agency announcement — no dataset.
  • GSA Announces Sale of Federal Helium System Assets · US General Services Administration (2023) Press release opening the sale of the reserve's physical assets. Summary. GSA announced the sale of the Federal Helium System (June 2023), covering the Cliffside Gas Plant near Amarillo, ~38,000 acres of mineral rights, the Bush Dome storage reservoir (~4+ billion cubic feet of federal helium), 23 gas wells and a 423-mile helium pipeline delivering to refineries in Texas, Oklahoma and Kansas. Data. Agency announcement listing the asset inventory — no dataset.

③ End uses & the semiconductor stakes — MRI, chips, rockets 2

Why nothing else will do: the 4 K coolant for MRI magnets and the inert atmosphere for chips and fibre, with no substitute for the coldest jobs.
  • Helium, Semiconductors, and the Strategic Risk of Hormuz · Global Policy Journal (2026) Argues the Strait of Hormuz is a critical-material, not only an oil-and-gas, chokepoint. Summary. Zamani and Farzanegan (May 2026) argue the Strait of Hormuz should be treated as a helium chokepoint too: helium is essential to semiconductors, medical imaging and cryogenics and depends on Gulf gas infrastructure and specialised maritime transport. They propose mapping exposure, building strategic reserves and improving helium-market transparency. Data. Policy analysis of helium supply-chain geography; no primary dataset.
  • Helium crisis puts US in control of semiconductor supply chain · The Oregon Group (2026) Analysis of how a Qatar disruption concentrates helium leverage in the US. Summary. Argues that with Qatar's ~one-third of world helium (63 million m³ in 2025) removed by a March 2026 disruption, the US — already the largest producer (81 million m³) — becomes the dominant supplier of an irreplaceable semiconductor, cryogenic and imaging input, with spot prices up ~50% and demand rising toward 2030. Data. Market commentary citing USGS production figures and spot-price reporting.

④ Non-renewable — recovery, conservation & the economics of a finite gas 3

Since helium cannot be remade and escapes the planet once vented, capturing more streams and recovering boil-off on-site is the real supply strategy.
  • A Review of the Sustainability of Helium: An Assessment of Its Past, Present and a Zero-Carbon Future · RSEE / MDPI (2024) Sustainability-framed review linking helium supply to fossil-fuel decline. Abstract. Wilkinson and Gerth (2024) review the economics of a non-renewable gas recovered mainly as a by-product of natural gas. They examine scarcity, price volatility and supply disruption, and warn that the transition to net-zero — by cutting natural-gas production — directly threatens future helium availability, making recovery and recycling central. Data. Economic and literature review of helium supply, price and sustainability — synthesis, no primary dataset.
  • Policy report on helium proposes long-term solutions · MRS Bulletin (2016) Coverage of the MRS/APS/ACS policy report on preserving research access to helium. Summary. Reports the MRS/APS/ACS policy response to liquid-helium cost and supply risk from the Federal Helium Reserve's closure. Long-term fixes: cut usage with advanced closed-cycle systems and recycle helium by capturing and reliquefying it; near-term, a DLA brokerage program bought liquid helium for federally funded researchers at ~15% average savings. Data. Journalistic coverage of a policy report; program savings figures cited from the DLA/APS/ACS program.
  • Joint Natural Resources and Government Policy: Helium and Natural Gas · Eastern Economic Journal (1991) Historical economics paper on jointly extracted helium and natural gas. Abstract. Hughey (1991) builds an economic model of jointly extracted resources to study taxation policy. Under competitive owners with perfect foresight, he characterises intertemporal equilibrium and how subsidies shift prices and extraction. Applied to helium and natural gas, the jointness of the resources yields results different from a single-resource model. Data. Theoretical resource-economics model — no empirical dataset.

Across the chain — shortages, geopolitics & disruption 9

The recurring cycle of shortages that follows from the coupling — supply-risk indices, conflict impacts and contemporary reporting.
  • Helium resource global supply and demand: Geopolitical supply risk analysis · Resources, Conservation & Recycling (2023) Applies the GeoPolRisk method to helium imports for ten economies. Abstract. First study to map global helium flows and score import supply risk with the GeoPolRisk method for ten economies over 2015–2019. Helium imports carry a relatively high GeoPolRisk index (0.15–0.30), consistent with a "critical" classification. 2019 world output was ~27,000 t (160 million m³), mostly from the US, Qatar and Algeria, moved as time-constrained cryogenic liquid. Data. Bilateral helium trade flows plus governance/concentration indicators feeding the GeoPolRisk index, 2015–2019.
  • The Impact of Conflict on the Global Helium Shortage · USITC (2022) Executive briefing tying the 2022 shortage to conflict and plant outages. Summary. USITC briefing (May 2022): the 2022 shortage combined pre-existing tightness with war stress. Unexpected shutdowns at BLM's Crude Helium Enrichment plant (summer 2021, January 2022) coincided with fires at Russian gas plants (October 2021, January 2022) halting Amur output and scheduled Qatar maintenance (February–March 2022), forcing suppliers into allocation. Data. USITC trade-analysis briefing citing production-outage timelines and helium market reporting.
  • Helium Supply and the Strait of Hormuz Crisis · C4MR (2026) Scenario analysis of a Hormuz blockade's effect on helium. Summary. C4MR (March 2026): a Strait of Hormuz blockade disrupting Qatar removed roughly one-third of global helium supply in a single event, with no spare capacity elsewhere. It warns the risk is not just price but unavailability for MRI, semiconductors and research, urging strategic reserves, recycling and helium-efficient technologies. Data. Scenario analysis of market conditions and supply-chain vulnerability — no primary dataset in the summary.
  • Russia Introduces Helium Export Controls Amid Global Shortage · The Moscow Times (2026) News report on Russia restricting helium exports during the 2026 shortage. Summary. April 2026: Russia — the third-largest producer — introduced helium export controls through 2027, requiring government approval for exports outside the Eurasian Economic Union, to prioritise domestic supply. Its main facility is Gazprom's Amur plant near the Chinese border, with China the primary buyer of Russian helium. Data. News reporting citing Russian government statements — no dataset.
  • The Real Helium Crisis Begins After the Ceasefire · Santiago & Company (2026) Argues the crisis outlasts any ceasefire because of physical and governance factors. Summary. April 2026: after the March 2026 Ras Laffan attack cut ~one-third of world helium and QatarEnergy declared force majeure, the crisis persists beyond any ceasefire — liquid helium boils off in 35–45 days, and two nations produce 76% of supply. It frames helium as a strategic utility, not a commodity. Data. Analyst commentary citing USGS production shares and industry sourcing/inventory figures.
  • Helium Shortage 4.0 & the alternatives for your GC · Peak Scientific Industry account of the 2022 shortage and gas-chromatography alternatives. Summary. Industry account of "Helium Shortage 4.0": an explosion and fire at Russia's Amur plant, a four-month BLM reserve maintenance shutdown (from July 2021, ~40% of US supply), Ukraine-conflict export barriers and Qatar/Algeria maintenance drove prices up ~20%. Frames hydrogen and nitrogen carrier gases and generators as GC alternatives. Data. Trade commentary — no primary dataset.
  • Helium is in short supply again · C&EN / ACS (2022) Chemistry-trade reporting on the causes of the 2022 shortage. Summary. C&EN (2022): the shortage was driven by a fire at Gazprom's new Amur plant — which had started up in September 2021 with capacity heading toward 60 million m³/yr — plus a second January fire, and prolonged technical problems at the US Federal Helium Reserve whose July 2021 maintenance ran months long. Data. Trade-press reporting citing producer and BLM outage accounts — no dataset.
  • Sanctions, Plant Maintenance, and How We Reached Helium Shortage 4.0 · Rocky Mountain Air Distributor account of the overlapping causes of Shortage 4.0. Summary. Distributor account: Gazprom's Amur facility (designed to supply ~30% of world helium) suffered fires and a minor explosion amid sanctions, BLM's Amarillo reserve went offline for four months from July 2021, and Qatar and Algeria took maintenance outages in spring 2022 — three of the world's largest facilities down within months, lifting prices ~20%. Data. Trade commentary — no primary dataset.
  • Helium: The World's Most Predictable Supply Crisis · Real Price of Risk (2026) Argues the shortage cycle is a recurring, plannable risk, not a surprise. Summary. April 2026: helium crises recur roughly every four years yet are treated as surprises. It tracks the pattern — 2006–07, 2011–13, 2018–20, 2021–23 (Russian plant explosions, US equipment failure, Qatar maintenance) and the 2026 Ras Laffan attack — and argues helium risk belongs in financial planning and contracts. Data. Historical narrative of shortage episodes with price commentary — no primary dataset.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 2

The datasets the chain's own figures are built from — and the customs line that hides helium.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database; helium sits in HS 280429 with neon/krypton/xenon. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Helium has no clean HS6 code — it shares 280429 "rare gases" with neon, krypton and xenon, so customs cannot isolate it. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Appendixes · USGS (2026) Definitions and data-source notes underlying the USGS commodity chapters. Summary. USGS reference appendixes to the 2026 Mineral Commodity Summaries: resource and reserve definitions and classification (Appendix C), source and methodology notes, and the country coverage behind each commodity chapter — the provenance for the helium production, reserve and resource figures. Data. USGS methodology and definitions document — reference, not a dataset.
