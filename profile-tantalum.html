The chain — from the ground to the buyer

A critical material passes through distinct stages, and the country that leads each stage is often different — that gap is what this atlas exists to show. Each layer comes from a different public source with its own vintage, labelled below; shares are % of the world total.

● Reserves — where it could come from

Reserves are the deposits known to exist and economically worth mining — the long-run ceiling on supply, distinct from what is actually produced today. USGS does not publish country-level reserves for tantalum — typically because it is a by-product of another ore or is drawn from an effectively unlimited source (e.g. quartz, seawater), so a national reserve map does not apply. ● Mined — where it is dug up today

Mining is where ore leaves the ground — the stage most people equate with “the source”, though it is rarely where the supply risk actually concentrates. Output is moderately concentrated: 🇨🇩 Congo [DRC] supplies 41%, with 🇷🇼 Rwanda (22%) next. Note: much of Rwanda’s reported mine output is widely assessed (UN Group of Experts and others) as Congolese coltan re-exported across the border, so DRC’s true share is higher and part of Rwanda’s “mine production” is an origin artefact rather than a measured mine. ● Refined / processed — where it becomes usable metal

Refining / processing converts ore into the metal or compound buyers actually purchase; it concentrates in fewer hands than mining and sits at the buyer's doorstep, which is why it is usually the true chokepoint. 🇨🇳 China processes 40% — broadly diversified. So although 🇨🇩 Congo [DRC] mines the most, China controls processing: the dependency the mine map hides, and the reason import-origin statistics misread the real source. On the buffers: 1% of supply is recovered from end-of-life recycling and substitutes are scarce, while the US imports 100% of what it consumes.
