Aluminium value chain — bauxite to metal

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Aluminium is congealed electricity. Bauxite is mined in three places at once — Australia, Guinea and China, roughly a quarter each — but smelting is one of the most electricity-hungry industrial processes there is, so it concentrated where power was cheapest. China's share jumps from ~21% at the mine to ~59% at the smelter — the chokepoint is the powerline, not the pit.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Bauxite (the ore, mined AU / GN / CN) → ② Alumina refining (the Bayer step, and the Indonesia–Guinea ore shift) → ③ Smelting (Hall–Héroult, ~40% electricity by cost — China ~59%, energy-sited: the chokepoint) → ④ the metal (carbon footprint, recycling, end use) — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

② Alumina refining & the Indonesia–Guinea shift 4

The Bayer step, and the ore's own re-sorting: Indonesia banned raw exports to force domestic refining; Guinea fills the gap into China.
  • Diversifying Investment in Indonesia's Mining Sector · CSIS Analysis of Indonesia's raw-bauxite export ban and downstream refining push. Summary. CSIS commentary on Indonesian resource nationalism: the raw-bauxite export ban collapsed output ~95% (55.7 Mt in 2013 to 2.6 Mt in 2014) and export revenue from $1.3 bn to $46 m, while Chinese investors shifted to Guinea — permanently diminishing Indonesia's market position. Data. Historical Indonesian production and export-revenue figures around the 2014 export ban.
  • Scramble for bauxite: beware Chinese promising gifts · Peterson Institute (PIIE) Analysis of Chinese-financed bauxite investment in Guinea after Indonesia's ban. Summary. PIIE blog (Marcus Noland) arguing Chinese pledges to build alumina refining in Guinea (and Jamaica) lack credibility: high energy costs and hydrocarbon dependence make refining/smelting there uneconomic without subsidy — reinforcing that cheap power, not ore, sites the industry. Data. Commentary citing Chinese investment announcements and host-country energy-cost conditions — no primary dataset.
  • Guinea bauxite can make up for Indonesia ban, China's Antaike says · Reuters, via Investing.com Market reporting on Guinea substituting for Indonesian bauxite supply. Summary. Reuters report: China's Antaike says Guinea can fully offset any Indonesian export restriction. Chinese-invested Guinean projects exceed 100 Mt/yr capacity, nearly all shipped to China; Guinea imports were expected to reach ~58 Mt/yr with room to expand. Data. Market reporting citing Antaike (China) capacity and trade-flow estimates.
  • Indonesia's bauxite export ban to have limited impact on China supply · S&P Global Commodity Insights Market analysis of the 2023 Indonesian export ban's effect on Chinese alumina supply. Scope. Market analysis arguing Indonesia's 2023 raw-bauxite export ban would have limited impact on China's alumina supply, given Guinea and Australian ore. Page returned HTTP 403 — full text not retrievable; scope from the headline. Data. Market analysis of bauxite trade flows and Chinese alumina supply — no primary dataset.

④ The metal — carbon, recycling & end use 8

Same metal, very different footprint: the smelter's power source sets the carbon, and recycled metal needs about 5% of the energy.

IAI 2024 dataset of cradle-to-gate GHG emissions intensity for primary aluminium (mining through casting) across global facilities. Methodology and sourcing are described on the page; specific CO₂e-per-tonne values by region sit in the underlying series, not printed inline. Data. Industry life-cycle inventories (published 2005/2010/2015/2019) with annual energy and PFC updates; pre-2019 not comparable to later years.
