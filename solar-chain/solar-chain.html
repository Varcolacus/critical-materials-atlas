Solar manufacturing grew by moving—and by using less silicon

In the 1990s, module manufacturing was divided among North America, Europe and Asia. By the 2020s, Asia held more than nine-tenths of output, while China controlled the tightest upstream steps. At the same time, silicon use per watt fell by almost 88%.

The centre moved upstream

The cleanest comparison uses two IEA capacity charts with identical definitions. In 2010, China already led wafers and cells but polysilicon capacity remained distributed. By 2021, China’s share had risen at every manufacturing step—especially wafers—even though it represented only 36% of global demand.

What factories actually produced in 2023

Production concentration is highest before the cell becomes a module. Module assembly is easier to distribute geographically than polysilicon purification or wafer production.

Scale was not the only change

Silicon intensity fell from 16 to 2 grams per watt between 2004 and 2024. Higher efficiency, thinner wafers, diamond-wire sawing and larger ingots reduced material demand per unit of output by 87.5%.

The current capacity picture

China held about 85% of solar supply-chain manufacturing capacity in 2024. The wafer step was tighter still, at roughly 95%. These are capacity measures, not 2024 production shares.

The customs ledger is broader than the factory

BACI provides a long and useful record of what crosses borders. It does not provide a clean solar manufacturing series: HS 280461 mixes solar and electronic polysilicon, while pre-HS2022 code 854140 mixes PV cells, assembled modules and panels, LEDs, and other photosensitive semiconductor devices.
