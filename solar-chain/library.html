Solar-PV chain — polysilicon to module

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

A solar module is silicon refined into polysilicon, grown into wafers, cut into cells and laminated into panels — and China makes roughly 85–98% of every one of those stages. The chokepoint is not a scarce element: silicon is the second-most-abundant in the crust, and a panel uses ever less of it. The chokepoint is the factory.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Polysilicon (silicon purified to PV-grade) → ② wafer · cell · module (manufacturing concentration, China ~85–98%) → the dematerialisation story (silicon per watt, 16 → 2 g/W) → then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Polysilicon — silicon purified to PV-grade 1

The first hard step: metallurgical silicon refined to PV-grade polysilicon — ~92% China.
  • Solar-Grade Silicon in the Energy Transition: A Strategic Commodity for the Global Photovoltaic Market · Ramírez-Márquez, Commodities (2025) Reframes polysilicon from a technical material to a strategic, concentrated commodity. Abstract. Short communication reframing solar-grade silicon — which enables over 90% of PV technologies — as a strategic commodity of geopolitical and economic weight rather than a mere technical material. Analyses price trends, regional production asymmetries and trade dependencies, identifies supply-chain vulnerabilities, and argues perovskite/organic alternatives remain commercially immature. Data. Techno-economic review integrating price, production-asymmetry and trade-dependency data with policy and investment analysis — a short communication, no new primary dataset.

② Wafer · cell · module — manufacturing concentration 4

China ~85–98% at every stage; the wafer (~98%) is the stage nobody has replicated at scale.
  • Solar PV Global Supply Chains · IEA (2022) The reference IEA study on manufacturing concentration across the PV supply chain. Abstract. The reference IEA study mapping solar-PV supply chains from raw materials to finished product across the five manufacturing segments — polysilicon, ingots, wafers, cells and modules. Documents extreme geographic concentration in China and covers supply, demand, production, energy use, emissions, employment, costs, investment and trade. Data. IEA analysis of PV manufacturing capacity, production, cost, trade and investment across the five supply-chain segments.
  • Trends in Photovoltaic Applications 2024 · IEA-PVPS (2024) Annual global PV deployment and manufacturing-trend report; source for the stage-share bars. Abstract. IEA-PVPS Task 1 annual report on global PV deployment and manufacturing. Records record installations against a China-led manufacturing build-out whose overcapacity drove module prices to levels threatening the industry's viability, and details installed capacity, industrial/manufacturing policy, module prices and production over 2013–2023. Data. IEA-PVPS national-survey network: country-level installed capacity, module production and capacity, and price data.
  • Diversifying the solar photovoltaic supply chain to secure Europe's energy and climate roadmap and sovereignty · Evans, Grand & Dupuis, iScience (2025) Analyses options for reducing China-concentration risk in EU PV supply. Abstract. Examines Europe's exposure to China's overwhelming dominance of PV modules and upstream components, offshored and upscaled over fifteen years into low-cost gigafactories. Warns the concentration risks disruption — critical for repowering and once thermal plants retire — and assesses sovereignty options under the EU's Industrial Green Deal. Data. Supply-chain and scenario analysis of European PV manufacturing capacity, module/component trade positions and diversification options.
  • A UK foresight study of materials in decarbonisation technologies: the case of photovoltaic cells · Petavratzi, Josso, Shaw, Horn & Singh (2024) UK government-affiliated foresight study on PV-cell material demand. Summary. British Geological Survey foresight study on materials in decarbonisation technologies, taking PV cells as its case: solar as a near-infinite renewable source and the materials underpinning domestic- and industrial-scale panels. A 45-page BGS report, internally reviewed but not externally peer-reviewed. Data. British Geological Survey foresight assessment (45 pp) of PV-cell material demand; funded by BGS and the Department for Business & Trade.

The panel dematerialised — intensity, cost & circularity 3

Silicon per watt fell about eight-fold in twenty years — the chokepoint hardened anyway.
  • Photovoltaics Report · Fraunhofer ISE (2026) Technical reference on cell/module technology, efficiency and material-intensity trends. Abstract. Fraunhofer ISE's regularly updated technical reference on PV markets, cell/module efficiency, material intensity, life-cycle assessment and prices. Documents silicon-per-watt falling from about 16 g/Wp (2004) to about 2.0 g/Wp (2024) via thinner wafers and higher efficiency, and wafer-based crystalline silicon at ~98% of 2025 production. Data. Fraunhofer ISE / PSE compilation of PV market, efficiency, wafer-thickness, silicon-usage and price data from public industry and research sources.
  • Evaluating the causes of cost reduction in photovoltaic modules · Kavlak, McNerney & Trancik, MIT (2018) Decomposes the historical fall in PV cost and material intensity. Abstract. Advances a framework quantifying the causes of PV-module cost decline, separating low-level mechanisms (efficiency, materials) from high-level ones (R&D, learning-by-doing, scale). Finds increased module efficiency the leading low-level cause over 1980–2012 (~25% of the decline) and R&D the dominant high-level driver, with scale economies rising after 2001. Data. Cost-model decomposition of PV-module cost changes 1980–2012 using historical cost, efficiency and material-input data.
  • Role of anthropogenic mineral circularity in addressing dual challenges of resource supply and waste management in global photovoltaic development · Yuan, Song, Liu, Huang & Wang, Nature Communications (2025) Global PV material-circularity / recycling modelling. Abstract. Models secondary-material supply potential across five PV technologies under different energy scenarios. Projects cumulative material demand of 705–1879 Mt and waste of 238–529 Mt by 2050; a circularity strategy could raise the annual secondary-supply ratio from 3.3% (2020) to 43–102% by 2050, with silver and tellurium potentially in surplus. Data. Tailored global material-flow / circularity model of five PV technologies across energy-transition scenarios to 2050.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 6

List methodologies, EU/US assessments and industry outlooks that classify or size the whole chain.
  • Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 · IEA (2026) Annual outlook covering the metals (silicon, silver, copper) that feed PV manufacturing. Summary. The IEA's 2026 outlook on 37 critical minerals for energy transitions — including silicon, silver and copper that feed PV — with supply–demand projections across three scenarios and analysis of concentration, export restrictions and underinvestment as security risks. Data. IEA market assessments and medium/long-term projections across three scenarios; published 16 July 2026.
  • Energy Technology Perspectives 2026 — supply-chain risks and industrial competitiveness · IEA (2026) Cross-technology assessment of supply-chain and industrial-competitiveness risk. Abstract. Cross-technology assessment of clean-energy supply-chain risk and industrial competitiveness. Finds manufacturing for solar PV, batteries, wind and heat pumps heavily concentrated in China (~85% of solar capacity), with non-Chinese production unable to meet demand and geographic diversification unlikely before 2030 on current commitments. Data. IEA analysis (ETP 2026) drawing on BNEF, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and S&P Global manufacturing-capacity data.
  • Study on the Critical Raw Materials for the EU 2023 — Final Report · European Commission (2023) The EU's fifth CRM assessment; covers silicon metal, relevant to polysilicon supply. Abstract. The EU's fifth technical criticality assessment (2023), feeding the Critical Raw Materials Act. Screens candidate materials against Economic Importance and Supply Risk thresholds — governance-weighted concentration adjusted for recycling and substitution — identifying 34 critical raw materials, among them silicon metal, the feedstock for polysilicon. Data. EU criticality methodology (Economic Importance × Supply Risk) over the candidate-material pool, using production, trade, value-added, substitution and recycling data.
  • Critical Raw Materials Act (Regulation 2024/1252) · European Union, Official Journal (2024) Legal text of the EU's policy response to raw-material supply concentration. Summary. EU Regulation 2024/1252 establishing a framework to secure critical-raw-material supply. Sets 2030 benchmarks — 10% domestic extraction, 40% processing and 25% recycling of EU consumption — and caps any single third country at 65% of a strategic raw material's supply. Data. EU legal instrument — Official Journal L 2024/1252; a regulation, not a dataset.
  • Global Solar Market Outlook 2026-2030 · SolarPower Europe (2026) Flagship annual global demand/supply outlook for the solar industry. Summary. SolarPower Europe's flagship annual outlook: a record 664 GW of new solar installed in 2025 lifting global capacity to 3 TW, with regional and country-level forecasts to 2030 and analysis of policy, grid and manufacturing constraints on continued growth. Data. SolarPower Europe industry data and demand/supply modelling; published 22 June 2026.
  • Solar & Storage for EU Energy Security · SolarPower Europe (2026) Thematic report linking PV/storage deployment to import-dependence reduction. Summary. Thematic briefing linking solar-plus-storage deployment to reduced EU energy vulnerability — cutting fossil-fuel imports and price exposure, with EU solar generation expected to avoid tens of billions of euros in gas imports in 2026 alone. Data. SolarPower Europe modelling of solar's contribution to EU energy security (April 2026).

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 2

The datasets the chain's own trade figures are built from.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII (2026) Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's trade layer. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by converting CIF-valued imports to FOB and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Version 202601 released 30 January 2026. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB conversion, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • UN Comtrade Plus · United Nations Official international merchandise-trade-statistics platform underlying BACI. Summary. The United Nations' official international merchandise-trade-statistics platform — the reported import/export declarations that CEPII reconciles into BACI. The live portal is a JavaScript application shell and returned no document text on this pass; described from its known role. Data. UN Statistics Division merchandise-trade declarations — the provenance layer beneath BACI.

Gaps & good next searches

  • The granular, stage-by-stage China market-share numbers the chain relies on come partly from independent market-intelligence firms (BloombergNEF, Wood Mackenzie, Bernreuter Research on polysilicon) that were not reachable this session — a direct pass on those would sharpen the poly/wafer/cell/module split.
  • The wafer stage (~98% China) is the true lock, yet no clean public country-level wafer-output series isolates it from cells and modules; ITRPV (International Technology Roadmap for Photovoltaic) reports are the best next source for wafer-thickness and capacity detail.
  • SEIA and CSIS's Critical Minerals Security Program pages returned access errors this session — worth a follow-up for US-specific PV supply-chain policy analysis.
  • The EU Net-Zero Industry Act analysis of European PV manufacturing capacity — the counter-move to China concentration — is a natural next search alongside the CRM Act benchmarks already listed here.
  • A dedicated series behind the "eight-fold" silicon-per-watt decline (g/W over time, not just the 2004 and 2024 anchors) would firm up the dematerialisation claim — Fraunhofer ISE and ITRPV are the places to look.
