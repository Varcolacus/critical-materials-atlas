Research Library · Fibre-optics

Fibre-optics / telecom chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Almost all intercontinental data crosses the ocean on optical fibre — ultra-pure silica doped with a trace of germanium, drawn from giant preforms by a handful of firms, then laid as submarine cable by just four companies. There are chokepoints at both ends — the germanium dopant upstream, the cable-makers downstream — and the preform capability sits in between.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Germanium (the dopant, a trace element) → ② the preform & drawn-fibre stage (the capability chokepoint, US / Japan) → ③ submarine-cable systems (a four-firm oligopoly) — then the cross-cutting export-control frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Germanium — the dopant chokepoint 3

A trace element that carries the light: fibre optics is one of germanium's largest single uses — and germanium is China-dominated and export-controlled.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Germanium · USGS (2025) Annual world production/reserves/uses data for germanium; China's refining lead and germanium's use as the fibre dopant. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: fibre optics is germanium's largest US end use — germanium dioxide and tetrachloride are consumed in fibre-optic glass. US net import reliance exceeds 50%; China leads world refinery production, imposed export licensing in August 2023, and in December 2024 banned all germanium exports to the United States. Prices roughly doubled in 2024. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported import/export trade and Argus Media price data.
  • Quantifying potential effects of China's gallium and germanium export restrictions on the U.S. economy · USGS Open-File Report 2024-1057 Economic modelling of the 2023 germanium/gallium export-licensing shock on US GDP. Abstract. USGS Open-File Report presenting a new model of mineral supply-disruption effects on US GDP. Using post-disruption equilibrium prices, a nonlinear-optimization routine and economic input–output tables, it estimates that a complete Chinese cutoff of gallium and germanium net exports could cut US GDP by $3.1 billion and $0.4 billion separately, or $3.4 billion simultaneously. Data. Nonlinear-optimization model over economic input–output tables; USGS production and trade data on gallium and germanium.
  • Germanium and Gallium: U.S. Trade and Chinese Export Controls · U.S. International Trade Commission Executive briefing on US trade exposure to China's germanium/gallium export controls. Summary. US ITC executive briefing on US trade exposure to China's germanium and gallium export controls and the affected end uses, including fibre optics. (PDF blocked this pass — the server returned a WAF/403 shell rather than the document; noted from the briefing's title and series.) Data. US ITC trade-data briefing built on official germanium/gallium import statistics — no primary dataset beyond reported trade.

② Preform & drawn fibre — the capability chokepoint 6

The chain's hard capability: metre-scale germanium-doped silica blanks drawn to hair-thin fibre — a process held by a handful of firms in the US and Japan.
  • Fiber Preforms — fabrication, MCVD/OVD/VAD methods · RP Photonics Encyclopedia Technical reference (not peer-reviewed) on the preform-fabrication methods that define this chain's capability chokepoint. Summary. RP Photonics Encyclopedia technical reference: silica fibre preforms are metre-scale germanium-doped glass rods made by vapour-deposition methods (MCVD, OVD, VAD), then drawn in a tower into hair-thin fibre that preserves the preform's refractive-index profile — the germanium doping raising the core's index. Data. Technical encyclopedia reference describing preform-fabrication methods — no dataset.
  • IEEE Milestone: Vapor-phase Axial Deposition Method for Mass Production of High-quality Optical Fiber, 1977–1983 · Engineering & Technology History Wiki / IEEE History of the VAD process (NTT + Sumitomo / Furukawa / Fujikura) now used for much telecom fibre. Summary. IEEE Milestone (dedicated 2015): in 1977 NTT's Dr. Tatsuo Izawa invented the vapor-phase axial deposition (VAD) method; NTT, Furukawa Electric, Sumitomo Electric and Fujikura developed it from 1975–1983 into a mass-production process that accelerated optical-fibre network construction worldwide. Data. IEEE Milestone citation / historical record — no dataset.
  • The VAD Method is Recognized as an IEEE Milestone · Sumitomo Electric (2015) Manufacturer's account of the VAD fibre-fabrication method it helped develop. Summary. Sumitomo Electric press release (June 2015): NTT, Furukawa, Sumitomo and Fujikura received an IEEE Milestone for the VAD optical-fibre mass-production method, which their 1975 joint R&D team developed and which accelerated global construction of optical-communication networks. Data. Manufacturer press release — no dataset.
  • Optical Fiber Manufacturing Excellence — Outside Vapor Deposition (OVD) · Corning Manufacturer's own account of the OVD preform process. Summary. Corning's own account of its Outside Vapor Deposition (OVD) process for making optical-fibre preforms — context for how few firms hold the low-loss preform stage. (Live page blocked — Corning served a WAF shell rather than the content; described from the page's stated subject.) Data. Manufacturer process description — no dataset.
  • Vapor Deposition Process — Science of Glass · Corning Explainer on preform "laydown" and consolidation before drawing. Summary. Corning explainer on the vapor-deposition preform process — soot "laydown" and consolidation of a germanium-doped silica blank before it is drawn into fibre. (Live page blocked — Corning served a WAF shell; described from the page's stated subject.) Data. Manufacturer explainer — no dataset.
  • Corning, Hemlock (Michigan) preform-capacity expansion announcement · Corning (2024) Corning's own preform-capacity investment as evidence of stage concentration. Summary. Corning news announcing preform-capacity investment (Hemlock, Michigan) — evidence of how few firms hold the preform stage, the basis for the chain's "capability, not tonnage" finding. (News-index landing not directly retrievable — served a WAF shell; noted from the announcement's subject.) Data. Company announcement — no dataset.

③ Submarine-cable systems — the four-firm oligopoly 6

The physical internet: cable and repeaters laid by only four firms across the US, France, Japan and China — the most geopolitically contested stage.
  • Safeguarding Subsea Cables: Protecting Cyber Infrastructure amid Great Power Competition · CSIS (2024) Security analysis of submarine-cable vulnerability and policy response. Abstract. CSIS report (Runde, Murphy & Bryja, Aug 2024): subsea fibre-optic cables carry over 95% of international data, yet ~98% are built and installed by four firms — SubCom, ASN and NEC held an 87% share in 2021, with China's HMN Tech at 11% — making the roughly 600-cable network a theatre of great-power competition. Data. Policy analysis citing FCC, TeleGeography and industry figures on cable counts, length and supplier market shares.
  • Securing Europe's Subsea Data Cables · Carnegie Endowment (2024) Europe-focused subsea-cable security and resilience analysis. Abstract. Carnegie Endowment analysis (Dec 2024) of European subsea-cable security. From 2020–2024 France's ASN led supply at ~34%, SubCom 19% and China's HMN 10% (falling to 4% of planned systems); only five firms operate seven-plus cable ships. It warns Europe's repair capacity is limited and recommends naval patrols and supply-chain security. Data. Policy analysis citing supplier market-share and cable-ship-fleet data and recent cable-cutting incidents.
  • Submarine Cabling Systems Market · Mordor Intelligence (2024) Market-share data for the wet-plant / cable-laying industry. Summary. Mordor market report: the submarine-cable-systems market (~$14.9bn in 2025, ~10% CAGR) has wet-plant system integration concentrated among four integrators — SubCom, ASN, HMN Technologies and NEC — the basis for the chain's four-firm oligopoly. Data. Proprietary Mordor Intelligence market modelling — supplier shares approximate; no public primary dataset.
  • Submarine Cable Systems Market Report 2025–2030 · MarketsandMarkets Market-research sizing/forecast for the subsea cable industry. Summary. MarketsandMarkets sizing: the submarine-cable-systems market grows from $19.95bn (2025) to $33.75bn (2030) at 11.1% CAGR, driven by offshore wind and data-centre demand; names Prysmian, Nexans, Sumitomo, ASN and SubCom among suppliers. Data. Proprietary market-research modelling — no public primary dataset.
  • Submarine Cable System Market · Fortune Business Insights Market sizing with named-supplier competitive landscape (ASN, SubCom, NEC, Prysmian, Nexans). Summary. Fortune Business Insights sizing: the submarine-cable market grows from $24.5bn (2025) to $55.3bn (2034) at 9.84% CAGR; it names five dominant suppliers — SubCom, ASN, NEC, Prysmian and Nexans — a named competitive landscape. Data. Proprietary market-research modelling with a named-supplier landscape — no public primary dataset.
  • Submarine Cables Market · Grand View Research Market-share analysis of the submarine-cable supplier landscape. Summary. Grand View Research market-share analysis of the submarine-cable supplier landscape. Across the chain — export-control & minerals frameworks 6

The analysis and regulatory tracking that frame germanium as a China-controlled chokepoint — provenance and status for the upstream node.
  • Germanium Chokepoint: China's Grip on AI Fiber · Introl (2026) Recent framing of germanium as a fibre / AI-infrastructure chokepoint. Summary. Introl analysis framing germanium as an AI-infrastructure chokepoint: China controls ~60% of production and licenses exports; prices tripled to ~$8,600/kg by 2026 while a single GPU rack consumes ~36× the fibre-optic cable of a traditional CPU rack. Data. Vendor commentary citing USGS shares, the Chinese export-control timeline and a germanium price series.
  • China's Export Controls on Critical Minerals · FTI Consulting Overview of the gallium / germanium / graphite export-control regime. Summary. FTI Consulting overview of China's critical-mineral export controls: MOFCOM licensing on gallium and germanium (1 Aug 2023) and graphite (1 Dec 2023); exports briefly halted in August before resuming for licensed producers, with graphite judged the larger near-term risk. Data. Advisory commentary on the export-control regime — no primary dataset.
  • 5 Critical Minerals Vulnerable Due to China's Production Control · Z2Data Supply-chain-risk vendor's take on China-controlled minerals including germanium. Summary. Z2Data supply-risk brief on five China-controlled minerals (gallium, germanium, graphite, antimony, rare earths); on germanium it cites ~60% of world production and 100% US import reliance, with USGS warning that controls could disrupt advanced-optics and telecom infrastructure. Data. Supply-chain-risk vendor synthesis citing USGS production and import-reliance figures.
  • China Eases U.S. Export Controls on Gallium, Germanium, Ultra-Hard Materials, Graphite · CIRS Group Regulatory-tracking update on the germanium licensing regime's evolution. Summary. CIRS regulatory update: on 9 Nov 2025 MOFCOM suspended, through 27 Nov 2026, the export bans imposed on 3 Dec 2024 on gallium, germanium, antimony, graphite and ultra-hard materials to the US — but the prohibition on exports for US military end-uses stays in full effect. Data. Regulatory-tracking summary of MOFCOM notices — no dataset.
  • China Germanium Export Controls Explained: Licensing Rules and Affected Products · Invest In Germanium Detail on which germanium products / end uses (incl. fibre) are licensed. Summary. Explainer detailing which germanium products China licenses: germanium dioxide and tetrachloride — the fibre-optic preform precursors — plus metal and epitaxial substrates. MOFCOM review runs ~45 working days, stretching supply lead times from 4–6 to 9–10 weeks. Data. Regulatory explainer of the germanium licensing regime by product / end use — no dataset.
  • Tiny Metals, Massive Leverage: How China's Grip on Gallium and Germanium Is Quietly Rewiring Global Power · Rare Earth Exchanges Geopolitical framing of the gallium / germanium leverage story. Summary. Rare Earth Exchanges argues gallium's and germanium's leverage comes from refining concentration, not scarcity: China holds ~99% of primary gallium and ~68% of germanium refining (2021), enabling export-control chokeholds over defence and optics applications. Data. Commentary citing USGS and Nordic geological-survey refining-share figures.
