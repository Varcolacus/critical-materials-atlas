Research Library · Fluorine

Fluorine (fluorspar → HF) chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Fluorspar is a mineral almost nobody names, yet its acid — hydrogen fluoride — is the gateway to three unrelated critical products: the etch gases that make semiconductors, the electrolyte and binder in every lithium-ion cell, and the refrigerants in every air conditioner and heat pump. China mines about two-thirds of the fluorspar and makes most of the HF — the chokepoint is the acid, not the mine.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Fluorspar (CaF₂ ore, China ~62% of the mine) → ② Hydrogen fluoride (the reactive gateway chemical) → ③ the three roads HF opens — chip gases · LiPF₆ battery electrolyte · refrigerants — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Fluorspar — the mined mineral 17

Acid-grade CaF₂, China ~62% of the world mine — and the concentration was built over two decades, not handed down by geology.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Fluorspar · USGS (2026) Annual world fluorspar mine production, reserves, trade and salient statistics by country. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: global fluorspar mine output fell ~1% to 10 million t in 2025, with China ~6.0 Mt (~60%) and Mexico ~1.5 Mt second. Chinese supply was constrained by mine-safety rectification, lifting China's fluorspar imports 48% (86% from Mongolia). Acid-grade CaF₂ → HF is the leading use; the US was 100% net import reliant. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade, price and reserve data; world mine-production and reserves table by country.

② Hydrogen fluoride — the gateway chemical 3

Fluorspar + sulphuric acid → HF: the single reactive gateway behind every downstream fluorochemical.
  • Supply Chain Modeling for Fluorspar and Hydrofluoric Acid and Implications for Further Analyses · Institute for Defense Analyses (2015) Defense-stockpile supply-chain model spanning acid-grade fluorspar and HF. Abstract. IDA briefing modelling the acid-grade fluorspar and HF supply chains for the DLA National Defense Stockpile. Using its Downstream Risk Assessment Methodology (DRAM), it projects a gross acid-grade fluorspar shortfall and treats imported HF as a partial substitute — a rare public model of the HF processing stage itself. Data. IDA DRAM downstream supply-chain model with DLA stockpile-requirement inputs and projected fluorspar/HF shortfalls.
  • Fluorosilicic Acid Supply Chain — Executive Summary · US EPA (2023) Adjacent fluorine-derivative profile — the phosphate by-product route into HF/AlF₃. Abstract. US EPA supply-chain profile of fluorosilicic acid (FSA), a fluorine derivative recovered as a phosphate-fertiliser by-product and an alternative feedstock for HF and AlF₃. Rates supply-disruption risk moderate-low across five US plants, with China the primary import partner — the by-product route that supplements mined fluorspar. Data. EPA 2022–23 supply-chain assessment: domestic FSA plants, water-sector consumption share, and 2019 trade data.
  • Fluorochemical industry · Overview reference Overview of the fluorspar → HF → fluorochemicals industry structure. Summary. Overview of the fluorspar→HF→fluorochemicals structure: acidspar (≥97% CaF₂) reacts with sulphuric acid to make HF; roughly a third of HF goes to inorganic fluorides (cryolite, AlF₃) and over 40% to organofluorides (refrigerants, fluoropolymers). Notes China's rising fluorochemical production. Data. Encyclopaedic synthesis with cited industry figures (e.g. 2003 fluorspar ~4.5 Mt/yr) — no primary dataset.

③ The three roads — chips · batteries · refrigerants 1

One acid, three roads: semiconductor etch gases, LiPF₆ electrolyte and PVDF binder, and HFC/HFO refrigerants. The thinnest evidence base is the downstream branches.
  • Fluorspar: the EV critical mineral no one has heard of · The Oregon Group Investor-facing framing of fluorspar's role in the EV / battery chain. Summary. Investor-facing case that fluorspar is an overlooked EV/battery critical mineral: China holds >60% of output but ~10 years of reserves, shifting to Mongolian imports (912,732 t in 2023, +348%). US DOE sees demand outpacing supply by 40–70% by 2035; roughly 100 kg fluorspar per 100 kWh battery. Data. Secondary analysis citing USGS figures, US DOE demand forecasts and Mongolian export data.
The semiconductor-gas branch (NF₃, WF₆) and the refrigerant-specific (HFC/HFO) branch have almost no public, country-level supply source — customs data lump them into broad chemical headings, and this pass surfaced only market commentary. That thinness is itself a finding — see Gaps & good next searches for the leads worth chasing.

Gaps & good next searches

  • No single public dataset separates HF or fluorochemical output (LiPF₆, NF₃/WF₆, HFC/HFO) by country the way USGS/BGS does for raw fluorspar — BACI's HS codes lump them into broad chemical headings, exactly as the chain's trade note flags. Next: "hydrogen fluoride production capacity by country" or IEA/OECD chemical-industry statistics.
  • The semiconductor-gas branch (NF₃, WF₆) is thin in this pass — worth a dedicated search: "NF₃ tungsten hexafluoride semiconductor etch gas supply chain China."
  • The HFC/HFO refrigerant branch and the Kigali Amendment / AIM Act effect on fluorochemical demand were not directly searched — try "Kigali Amendment HFO refrigerant fluorspar demand forecast."
  • No fluorspar-specific peer-reviewed criticality paper or DOI dataset surfaced beyond the Iran case study and the China material-flow study — a targeted Resources Policy / Journal of Cleaner Production search on "fluorspar criticality China 2024 2025" could find newer work.
  • US revival-project details (ARES Strategic Mining's Lost Sheep, the Delta, Utah plant) came from secondary aggregators — verify against a company/SEC filing or the USGS MCS note on the Delta processing plant before citing as fact.
