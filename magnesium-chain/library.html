Research Library · Magnesium

Magnesium chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Magnesium's feedstock — dolomite, magnesite, even seawater — is abundant everywhere; the concentration is built downstream. China makes roughly 87% of the world's primary metal, mostly by the coal-fired Pidgeon process, and in 2021 an energy crunch cut that output while Europe sat weeks from a shortage. The mine is not the chokepoint; the smelter is.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Reduction (Pidgeon process, coal-fired, ~87% China) → ② magnesium metal (production data & tracking) → ③ end use (aluminium alloys — near-unsubstitutable — and die-casting) → ④ the 2021 energy-curb shock — then the cross-cutting criticality frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Reduction — China's coal-fired Pidgeon monopoly 5

The binding stage: not a geological monopoly (feedstock is everywhere) but an industrial one — cheap, coal-fired capacity clustered in Shaanxi.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Magnesium Metal · USGS (2026) Annual world primary-magnesium production / reserves / uses data; sources the chain's ~87%-China headline. Abstract. USGS commodity summary for magnesium metal: world primary production is dominated by China via the coal-fired Pidgeon process, with chief uses in aluminium alloying, die-casting and steel desulfurization. PDF returned HTTP 403 on two passes — not retrievable; the ~87%-China headline is carried from the atlas record and USGS's published series, not quoted here. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported production and trade data.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Magnesium Metal · USGS (2025) Prior-year magnesium figures; notes the sole US primary smelter's 2025 bankruptcy filing. Abstract. USGS 2025 magnesium-metal summary — the prior-year comparison point, and the source flagging financial distress at the sole US primary producer. PDF returned HTTP 403 on two passes — not retrievable; noted from the library entry, figures not quoted. Data. USGS voluntary producer surveys plus reported import / export data.
  • USGS Minerals Yearbook 2022 — Magnesium Metal · USGS (2022) Detailed annual industry structure and end-use breakdown for magnesium. Abstract. USGS Minerals Yearbook chapter carrying the detailed annual industry structure and end-use split (aluminium alloying, die-casting, desulfurization) behind the summary tables. PDF returned HTTP 403 on two passes — not retrievable; end-use quantification left as a noted gap. Data. USGS annual industry canvass — production, consumption, end-use and trade tables.
  • Life cycle assessment of primary magnesium production using the Pidgeon process in China · Int. J. Life Cycle Assessment (2009) Cradle-to-gate carbon footprint of Chinese Pidgeon-process magnesium. Abstract. Life-cycle assessment of primary magnesium made by the coal-fired Pidgeon process in China, quantifying the cradle-to-gate energy and CO₂ footprint of the dominant production route. Springer full text behind an authentication redirect — abstract not retrievable on this pass. Data. Cradle-to-gate LCA inventory of Chinese Pidgeon-process magnesium production.
  • Research on the Process, Energy Consumption and Carbon Emissions of Different Magnesium Refining Processes · PMC (2023) Compares the Pidgeon process against alternative magnesium reduction routes on energy and carbon. Abstract. Compares magnesium smelting routes under low-carbon policy. The traditional Pidgeon process has a long cycle (~10–14 h), high energy use and high emissions; a relative-vacuum continuous route cuts standard-coal use by >1.5 t and carbon by >10 t per tonne of magnesium, shortening the cycle >9.5 h. Data. Process modelling of Pidgeon vs vacuum-carbothermal / aluminothermic reduction (energy in tce, CO₂ per tonne Mg).

③ End use — aluminium alloys & die-casting 2

Where magnesium disappears into other metals: a small, essential dose in aluminium alloys, plus die-cast light parts.
  • How a Magnesium Shortage is Affecting Aluminum Production · Industrial Metal Service Explainer on magnesium's role as an alloying element in aluminium. Summary. Industry explainer: magnesium alloys aluminium into the 5xxx (Mg) and 6xxx (Mg-Si) series that give strength and corrosion resistance, so a magnesium shortage feeds straight into aluminium production; recycling was the 2021 stopgap. Data. Trade explainer citing China ~87% of magnesium (~54% Shaanxi) and the 2021 price peak (~$11,470/t).
  • Prospective life cycle assessment of an electric vehicle equipped with a model magnesium battery · Energy, Sustainability and Society (2024) Forward-looking life-cycle assessment of a model magnesium-battery EV. Summary. Prospective life-cycle assessment of an electric vehicle using a model magnesium battery — an adjacent, forward-looking use of magnesium beyond alloying. Springer / BMC full text behind an authentication redirect — abstract not retrievable on this pass. Data. Prospective LCA of a magnesium-battery EV (open-access journal).

④ The 2021 energy-curb shock 8

When a domestic Chinese power decision became a global supply event — the chain's vivid stress test.
  • Joint statement on the magnesium supply shortage · Eurofer et al. (2021) Cross-industry joint statement during the 2021 China energy-curb shock. Summary. Joint statement (22 Oct 2021) by twelve EU industry bodies — European Aluminium, Eurofer, ACEA and others — warning Europe, 95% dependent on China, would exhaust magnesium stocks by end-November as prices leapt from ~$2,000 to $10,000–14,000/t. Data. Cross-industry position statement citing EU import dependence and spot-price figures.
  • Call on EU policymakers to address the imminent magnesium shortage · European Aluminium (2021) European Aluminium's 27 Sep 2021 position paper on the magnesium shortage. Abstract. Position paper on the imminent shortage: China holds an ~87% production share, Europe depends on it for 95% of supply (the last EU plant closed in 2001), and ~45% of Chinese exports go to Europe. Aluminium alloys typically use ~4.9% magnesium; 25 Shaanxi/Shanxi plants had shut. Data. Position paper with a 1.2 Mt/yr market frame, worldwide use-split (aluminium 38%, die-casting 36%, steel 12%, metal reduction 8%) and EU import figures (155 kt in 2020, 95% China).
  • Magnesium supply shortage: cross-industry coalition and workers warn against catastrophic impact · ACEA (2021) European automobile manufacturers' warning during the 2021 magnesium shock. Summary. ACEA's statement as the automotive arm of the same cross-industry coalition, warning of catastrophic impact on car production from the 2021 Chinese magnesium cut. Page returned HTTP 403 — not retrievable; covered by the Eurofer joint statement it co-signed. Data. Industry-association statement — no primary dataset.
  • Aluminum makers warn power cuts in China could cause magnesium shortages · Supply Chain Dive (2021) Early coverage of the 2021 magnesium shortage from aluminium makers. Summary. Reports US aluminium makers (Alcoa, Matalco) flagging magnesium supply risk after suppliers declared force majeure; China ~80% of supply (Shaanxi ~60%), Europe 95% China-dependent, with ~75% of aluminium products needing magnesium and no substitute. Data. Trade-press reporting citing company statements and industry shares.
  • The magnesium supply chain crunch reaches carmakers · Fortune (2021) Automaker-level impact reporting on the 2021 magnesium crunch. Summary. Reports the 2021 magnesium crunch reaching carmakers: Volkswagen compared it to the chip shortage, Mercedes-Benz weighed lower-magnesium designs, Stellantis saw no immediate hit; prices ran from ~$2,000 to $10,000–14,000/t as Chinese power cuts bit. Data. Trade-press reporting citing automaker executives and price figures.
  • Chinese magnesium shortage: global car industry to grind to a halt within weeks · NZ Herald (2021) International wire coverage warning the car industry could halt within weeks. Summary. Wire coverage of the 2021 shortage: China ~87% of magnesium, Europe 95%-dependent and ~45% of Chinese exports Europe-bound; 25 Shaanxi/Shanxi plants shut after 20 September, stocks projected to run out by end-November. Data. Wire reporting citing the industry joint statement and price figures.
  • China says the West should respect its 'own pace' as Europe and US face a magnesium supply crisis · ABC News (2021) Geopolitical framing of the 2021 magnesium crisis and China's response. Summary. Frames the crisis geopolitically: China (~87% of supply, Europe 95%-dependent) cut magnesium plants to ~40% utilisation for power-saving / emissions targets; state media urged the West to respect China's "own pace"; Morgan Stanley saw tightness into May 2022. Data. News reporting citing production shares, capacity-utilisation and analyst estimates.
  • Magnesium Price Spike: A Flash in the Pan? · U.S. International Trade Commission USITC economic analysis of a magnesium price spike. Abstract. USITC working paper "Magnesium Price Spike: A Flash in the Pan?" — an economic analysis of a magnesium price spike and whether it was transient, a method reproducible on the 2021 episode. PDF returned HTTP 403 — not retrievable on this pass. Data. USITC economic analysis of magnesium prices and supply — working paper.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 1

The assessment that classifies magnesium as critical for the EU.
  • Study on the Critical Raw Materials for the EU 2023 · European Commission (2023) The EU's 2023 Critical Raw Materials assessment, which lists magnesium. Abstract. The EU's 2023 criticality assessment underpinning the Critical Raw Materials Act: it screens 70 candidate materials on Economic Importance × Supply Risk and lists 34 Critical Raw Materials — magnesium among them, flagged for its extreme single-country (China) supply concentration. Data. EU criticality methodology (Economic Importance × Supply Risk) over 70 candidate materials.
