Research Library · Rare Earths

Rare earths (full basket) chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

The seventeen rare earths are not scarce in the ground; they come out of the ore mixed together, and because the elements are chemically near-identical, pulling them apart into pure single-element oxides takes long, capital-heavy solvent-extraction cascades. That separation industrial base is roughly 90% China — and it gates the whole basket at once: magnets, phosphors, polishing powders and refining catalysts. The mine is not the chokepoint; separation is.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Mixed ore (~70% China) → ② Separation / refining — the ~90% China chokepoint → ③ Oxides, metals & recovery → ④ End uses (magnets, phosphors, polishing, catalysts) → ⑤ the export-licensing lever — then the cross-cutting criticality frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

② Separation & refining — the real chokepoint 3

Splitting the chemically-alike elements into pure oxides is a vast solvent-extraction industrial base — roughly 90% China, gating the whole basket at once.
  • IEA — Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 (executive summary) · IEA (2025) Executive summary covering mine vs processing/refining shares and the demand outlook. Summary. IEA finds China the dominant refiner for 19 of 20 minerals analysed (~70% average share); rare-earth mining's top-three share rose from 73% (2020) to 77% (2024), while refined-supply concentration is projected to ease only marginally to ~82% by 2035. Rare-earth demand rose 6–8% in 2024. Data. IEA supply-concentration and demand modelling across mining and refining stages.
  • Recent advances in the global rare-earth supply chain · MRS Bulletin / Springer (2022) Mineralogy, separation/extraction, mining economics and circular-economy potential for REEs. Abstract. The Springer page redirected to an authentication gate (paywalled) — the abstract was not retrievable this pass. The article reviews rare-earth mineralogy, the separation/extraction step, mining economics and circular-economy potential — precisely the separation-chokepoint mechanism at the centre of this chain. Data. Review article (MRS Bulletin, 2022) — no primary dataset; abstract not accessed.
  • Reshaping heavy rare-earth supply chains amidst China's stringent environmental regulations · PMC (NIH) How Chinese environmental policy — not quotas — reshapes heavy-REE (terbium) supply. Abstract. Finds stricter Chinese environmental standards, not production quotas, are the real heavy-REE constraint: post-2011 pollution rules forced nearly 90% of producers to close, official terbium output fell ~90% (2007–2018) leaving a 3,300 t cumulative shortfall, and only 25% of the 2018 heavy-REE quota was used. Data. China terbium supply/demand and quota-utilisation modelling, 2007–2018, with scenarios to 2060.

④ End uses & demand 3

One separation step feeds every rare-earth product: magnets (Nd/Dy), phosphors (Eu/Tb/Y), cerium polishing, lanthanum catalysts.

EU Strategic-Raw-Materials factsheet: rare earths (Nd/Pr/Tb/Dy/Gd/Sm/Ce) drive permanent magnets for EVs, wind and defence; EU dependency is 100% at extraction and >99% at processing; global extraction is China 68% / Myanmar 10% / US 9%, processing China 85%; EU 2030 demand ~6,800 t, five times 2020. ⑤ The export-licensing lever 5

The step that turns a ~90% separation share into geopolitical leverage — China's 2025 licensing of medium and heavy rare earths.
  • China's Export Controls: Critical Minerals and Strategic Pressure Points · Andersen Institute How China's export-control system uses processing dominance as strategic leverage. Summary. Traces China's unified export-control architecture (2020 Export Control Law → 2024 dual-use rules): April 2025 added seven medium and heavy rare earths, October 2025 a stricter 0.1% de-minimis rule; only about half of EU licence applications were approved by November 2025, and supplier switching keeps alternatives roughly a decade out. Data. Policy/legal analysis of China's Export Control Law and 2023–2025 measures — no primary dataset.
  • China tightens export controls on rare-earth metals: Why this matters · Al Jazeera (Oct 2025) The October 2025 expansion of export controls to more rare earths. Summary. On 10 Oct 2025 China added five more rare earths (holmium, erbium, thulium, europium, ytterbium); with April's seven it now licenses 12 of the 17 elements plus refining equipment, and any foreign magnet or chip containing ≥0.1% Chinese heavy rare earth. Cites China mining ≥60% and processing ~90%. Data. News reporting citing CSIS figures (China ~60% mine, ~90% processing).
  • China's 2025 rare-earth exports hit highest since at least 2014 despite restrictions · Reuters / Yahoo Finance (2026) 2025 export tonnage despite the licensing regime. Summary. China exported 62,585 t of rare earths in 2025, up 12.9% year-on-year — the highest since at least 2014 — despite April's control list; magnet exports slumped in April–May, then rebounded from June under trade agreements with the US and EU. Data. Reuters wire citing Chinese customs export tonnage, 2025.
  • Annual volume of rare earth exports from China · Statista Chart of China's annual rare-earth export tonnage through the 2025 control period. Summary. The Statista host did not resolve this pass (DNS failure) — the chart was not retrievable. Per the chain record it plots China's annual rare-earth export volume through the 2025 licensing period, peaking at 62,585 t in 2025 — the highest since 2014. Data. Statista chart of Chinese customs export volumes (per record) — not accessed.
  • China's rare earths trade flow analysis: Trade patterns and strategic considerations · Bulletin of the Chinese Academy of Sciences Trade-pattern analysis of China's rare-earth exports and imports. Abstract. Analyses China's rare-earth trade 2015–2022 and finds it turned from net exporter to net importer of rare-earth resources after 2018, repositioning as a processing hub that imports both ores and refined goods while exporting lower-value separation products and mid-grade magnets. Data. China rare-earth trade-flow analysis, 2015–2022, on customs data.

Diversification — building separation outside China 3

A handful of non-China anchors — and the recurring finding that mining, not separation, is what is actually being rebuilt.

SEC EDGAR returned 403 to automated fetch this pass — the filing text was not retrieved. The 2021 MP Materials prospectus documents the Mountain Pass, California mine and its on-site separation facility, the main non-Asian light-rare-earth separation operation and the anchor of US supply. Data. SEC registration statement (prospectus), 2021 — not accessed this pass.
  • MP vs. LYSDY: Which Rare-Earth Stock Is the Smarter Pick Now? · Yahoo Finance Comparison of the two leading non-China separation producers. Summary. Compares MP Materials (Mountain Pass; record 2,599 t NdPr oxide in 2025, first US commercial-scale magnets) and Lynas (Mt Weld; REO up 33% to 9,608 t, and "the first production of separated heavy rare earths outside China in decades") — the two anchors of non-China supply. Data. Trade-press comparison citing company 2025 production and financial figures.
  • Mapping rare earths projects outside China · Mining Technology Landscape of non-Chinese mine and separation projects under development. Summary. Maps non-China projects — MP Materials (~15% of world supply), Energy Fuels (White Mesa), Lynas (Mt Weld plus a Texas plant), Iluka (Eneabba) and Australian Strategic Materials (Dubbo) — and stresses the persistent gap: most operations must still export raw material for separation. Data. Trade-press survey of company projects with reserve and capacity figures.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks, policy & analysis 8

List methodologies, EU/US assessments and analysis that classify the whole basket and the market it trades in.
  • Special Report 04/2026 — Critical raw materials for the energy transition · European Court of Auditors (2026) EU auditors' review of progress and gaps on critical raw materials. Summary. European Court of Auditors audit of EU preparedness on critical raw materials for the energy transition: it credits the CRMA and assessment work but flags import concentration, thin domestic extraction and processing, weak recycling and poor member-state coordination, singling out rare earths for permanent magnets. Data. EU performance audit of Commission CRM policy, programmes and data.
  • RMIS — EU Critical Raw Materials · European Commission JRC JRC Raw Materials Information System — background for the EU CRM list and the CRMA. Summary. The JRC's Raw Materials Information System hosts the triennial EU criticality assessments (the 2023 round listed 34 critical and 16 strategic raw materials, rare earths among them), plus material profiles and supply-risk × economic-importance dashboards that underpin the Critical Raw Materials Act. Data. JRC criticality assessments (Economic Importance × Supply Risk) and material profiles.
  • Critical raw materials — DG GROW policy page · European Commission Official Critical Raw Materials Act policy hub. Summary. The Commission's CRM page: the 2023 assessment lists 34 critical raw materials from 70 candidates, and flags severe concentration — China 100% of heavy rare earths, Turkey 99% of boron, South Africa 71% of platinum-group metals — as the risk the Critical Raw Materials Act is meant to address. Data. Commission CRM assessment and policy statements.
  • Rare Earth Elements: The Global Supply Chain (R41347) · Congressional Research Service (2013) Long-standing US legislative primer on the rare-earth supply chain. Abstract. CRS primer (updated 16 Dec 2013): the 17 rare earths are moderately abundant but seldom concentrated enough to mine economically, and the US went from self-reliant to 100% import-reliant — mainly on China — over 15 years. Covers Molycorp's Mountain Pass "mine-to-magnet" restart, end uses and policy options. Data. CRS synthesis of USGS/industry production, demand (~136,000 t in 2010) and trade data.
  • Is Europe losing the race to secure rare earth materials? · Euronews (Jan 2026) EU-focused reporting on rare-earth dependency and the CRMA response. Summary. Reports the EU imports ~95% of its rare earths (recycling below 1%), with China supplying ~85% of the EU's rare earths and ~98% of its magnets and controlling up to ~90% of processing; concludes the EU will likely miss its 2030 CRMA extraction, processing and recycling targets. Data. News reporting citing EU import-dependency and CRMA-target figures.
  • The EU's critical raw materials predicament: ReSourceEU to the Rescue? · Jacques Delors Centre Think-tank analysis of the EU's CRM predicament, including rare earths. Summary. Argues EU dependence is above all on China's midstream processing, not just ores; reviews the ReSourceEU proposal (€3 bn in 12 months for magnets, batteries and defence, a European CRM Centre, joint purchasing) and warns most tools remain indicative, with permitting delays such as Kiruna running 10–15 years. Data. Policy analysis of the ReSourceEU initiative and CRMA instruments — no primary dataset.
  • Rare-earth elements market: A historical and financial perspective · Resources Policy / ScienceDirect Market-structure and pricing history of the rare-earth sector. Abstract. The ScienceDirect abstract page returned 403 (paywall) — not retrievable this pass. By its title it offers a historical and financial perspective on the rare-earth market — the price spikes and market structure (notably the 2010–2011 episode) behind the sector's boom-and-bust reputation. Data. Resources Policy journal article — abstract not accessible.
  • Supply risk propagation in multilayer trade networks of rare earth — from the industry-chain perspective · ScienceDirect (2025) Network analysis of how rare-earth supply risk propagates along the industry chain. Abstract. The ScienceDirect abstract page returned 403 (paywall) — not retrievable this pass. Per its title it models supply-risk propagation across multilayer rare-earth trade networks from an industry-chain perspective — a method for tracing how a separation-stage shock spreads downstream. Data. Multilayer trade-network model (2025) — abstract not accessible.
