# Rare earths (full basket) chain — research library

Rare earths are not scarce in the ground — separating the 17 chemically-alike elements into pure oxides is the real chokepoint, and that solvent-extraction industrial base is roughly 90% China, gating magnets, phosphors, polishing powders and catalysts alike. A curated reference library for deeper study — public sources, gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source.

## Core references (already cited in the chain)
- [USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Rare Earths](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths.pdf) — USGS (2026). Two-page synopsis: world mine production, reserves, China's April/October 2025 export-control timeline.
- [IEA, Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 — rare earths](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2025/executive-summary) — IEA (2025). Executive summary covering mine/processing/separation shares and demand outlook.
- [BGS, World Mineral Statistics — rare-earth production](https://www2.bgs.ac.uk/mineralsuk/statistics/worldStatistics.html) — British Geological Survey (2024). Country-level rare-earth mine production series.
- [CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade](https://www.cepii.fr/CEPII/en/bdd_modele/bdd_modele_item.asp?id=37) — CEPII (2026). Harmonised bilateral trade database used for the chain's HS-code trade extract.

## Government & agency reports
- [Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Rare Earths (Heavy)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths-heavy.pdf) — USGS (2026). Companion two-pager isolating the heavy rare-earth (Dy/Tb/Y) supply picture.
- [Rare Earths — Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2025/mcs2025-rare-earths.pdf) — USGS, D.J. Cordier (2025). Prior-year edition, useful for tracking the shift into 2026's export-control language.
- [Special Report 04/2026 — Critical raw materials for the energy transition](https://www.eca.europa.eu/ECAPublications/SR-2026-04/SR-2026-04_EN.pdf) — European Court of Auditors (2026). Audit of EU progress/gaps on critical raw materials incl. rare earths.
- [RMIS — More on Critical Raw Materials](https://rmis.jrc.ec.europa.eu/eu-critical-raw-materials) — EU Joint Research Centre. JRC's Raw Materials Information System, background for the EU CRM list and CRMA.
- [Critical raw materials — DG GROW policy page](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/raw-materials/areas-specific-interest/critical-raw-materials_en) — European Commission. Official CRMA policy hub, targets (10% extraction/40% processing/25% recycling by 2030).
- [Strategic Raw Materials — Rare Earth Elements factsheet](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/document/download/1e925ebe-d23f-4bc2-8801-1e49824c2aaf_en?filename=RMSP_REEs_Factsheet_V2.pdf) — European Commission (RMSP). Two-page EU factsheet on REE demand, uses and supply risk.
- [Rare Earth Elements: The Global Supply Chain](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R41347/R41347.20.pdf) — Congressional Research Service (updated 2013, still cited/archived on congress.gov). Long-standing US legislative-branch primer on the REE supply chain; useful for historical baseline, not current figures.

## Academic papers & reviews
- [Recent advances in the global rare-earth supply chain](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1557/s43577-022-00305-6) — MRS Bulletin / Springer Nature (2022). Mineralogy, separation/extraction, mining economics and circular-economy potential for REEs.
- [Rare Earth Elements in the Energy Transition: A Review of the Demand-Sustainability-Risk Nexus and Future Perspectives](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4117/7/5/211) — MDPI (2026). PRISMA-based systematic review of 2015–2026 literature; flags that only 1.4% of the literature addresses supply-chain/geopolitical risk vs. 87% on availability/production/recycling.
- [Reshaping heavy rare earth supply chains amidst China's stringent environmental regulations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11997559/) — PMC (NIH). Analyses how Chinese environmental policy is reshaping heavy-REE supply chains.
- [Rare-earth elements market: A historical and financial perspective](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S030142071730079X) — Resources Policy / ScienceDirect. Market-structure and pricing history of the REE sector.
- [Rare earth elements: Sector allocations and supply chain considerations](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1002072124000358) — ScienceDirect (2024). Supply-chain-considerations framing for REE sector investment/allocation.
- [A Review of the Occurrence and Recovery of Rare Earth Elements from Electronic Waste](https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/29/19/4624) — MDPI (2024, also on PMC). Conventional and emerging green technologies for REE recovery from e-waste.
- [Global Potential of Rare Earth Resources and Rare Earth Demand from Clean Technologies](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-163X/7/11/203) — MDPI, Resources journal. Resource-base vs. clean-tech demand comparison.
- [Supply risk propagation in multilayer trade networks of rare earth — from the perspective of the industry chain](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652625018967) — ScienceDirect (2025). Network analysis of how REE supply risk propagates along the industry chain via trade.
- ["China's rare earths trade flow analysis: Trade patterns and strategic..."](https://bulletinofcas.researchcommons.org/journal/vol39/iss12/7/) — Bulletin of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Trade-pattern analysis of China's REE exports.

## Analysis, think-tanks & reporting
