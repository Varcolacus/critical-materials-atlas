Research Library · Chromium

Chromium chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Chromite ore becomes ferrochrome, then stainless steel and superalloys — and the electricity-hungry smelting step, not the mine, is where the map bends. The ore stays in South Africa; the furnace chases cheap power to Kazakhstan and China. The chokepoint is the furnace, not the pit.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Chromite ore (South Africa) → ② Ferrochrome (energy-sited: Kazakhstan, China) → ③ Stainless steel & superalloys — then the cross-cutting toxicity/regulation, criticality frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Chromite — the ore, South-Africa-led 3

The ore, and it stays put: South Africa holds most chromite reserves and leads mine output.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Chromium · USGS (2026) Annual chromite mine and ferrochromium production, reserves and US import reliance by country. Abstract. USGS commodity summary for chromium. The persistent findings are US net import reliance near 100% (no domestic chromite mine production), South Africa leading world chromite reserves and mine output, and stainless steel as the dominant end use via ferrochromium. The 2026 PDF returned HTTP 403 on two passes — specific current-year tonnages were not retrieved here. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade, reserve and consumption data.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Chromium · USGS (2025) Prior-year annual chromium summary, for year-over-year comparison. Summary. The 2025 edition of the USGS chromium commodity chapter — the same production, reserves and import-reliance series one year earlier, used to check the year-over-year read against the 2026 edition. PDF not retrievable this pass (HTTP 403); figures deferred to the 2026 edition. Data. USGS annual mineral-commodity statistics (production, imports, reserves, use).
  • BGS World Mineral Statistics — chromium (chromite) · British Geological Survey Long-run world chromite mine-production series by country. Summary. British Geological Survey's World Mineral Statistics: a searchable database of long-run world production and trade by commodity and country, including chromium ores and concentrates — the historical mine-output series behind "South Africa dominates mining." The statistics portal returned HTTP 404 this pass — the series was not extracted here. Data. BGS compilation of national government production and trade returns, spanning many decades.

② Ferrochrome — the smelter chases power 2

Where the map bends: converting chromite to ferrochrome is ~40% power by cost, so the furnace follows cheap electricity to Kazakhstan and China.
  • South Africa's ferrochrome industry: facing structural decline or adaptation? · Project Blue Market-intelligence analysis of South Africa's ferrochrome industry and the smelting shift to Kazakhstan/China. Summary. Market analysis arguing South Africa's ferrochrome smelting faces structural decline as high power costs and unreliable electricity push capacity toward Kazakhstan and China, which smelt imported South African chromite — direct support for the "furnace follows the powerline" thesis. Full analysis is subscription-gated; only the framing was retrievable. Data. Project Blue proprietary market intelligence (ferrochrome capacity, cost and trade data).
  • International Chromium Development Association — uses and supply · ICDA (cited) The chain's cited chromium-industry association source for uses and supply. Summary. Cited in the chain record as the International Chromium Development Association (uses and supply). Checked live this pass, icdachromium.com now redirects to an unrelated medical ICD-10 coding site — the association's domain appears lapsed or reused; the citation needs a current ICDA URL (see Gaps). Data. No data retrievable — the domain no longer resolves to the association.

③ Stainless steel & superalloys — the end use 1

What the metal becomes: stainless steel (most chromium consumed) and nickel-based superalloys.
  • Material flow analysis case studies of nickel and stainless steel · Reck, Yale (2012) Material flow analysis of nickel and stainless steel — chromium's dominant end use. Summary. A material-flow-analysis study (Reck, Yale) tracing nickel and stainless steel through production, use and end-of-life — a method transferable to chromium, since stainless steel embeds most chromium consumed. Full text not retrieved this pass (host 404); scope taken from the citation. Data. Material-flow analysis of stainless-steel stocks and flows — no chromium-specific dataset extracted here.

Across the chain — toxicity & regulation (Cr-VI) 3

Chromium's other face: hexavalent chromium is a regulated hazard in plating and smelting.
  • Substance Information — chromium(VI) compounds · European Chemicals Agency EU regulatory/hazard database entry on hexavalent chromium compounds. Summary. European Chemicals Agency substance-information entry for chromium(VI) compounds — the EU regulatory and hazard record relevant to chrome plating and smelting exposure. Page returned HTTP 403 this pass; scope taken from the ECHA entry title. Data. ECHA regulatory database (classification, hazard and regulatory-obligation records).
  • Scientific Opinion on Dietary Reference Values for chromium · EFSA Journal (2014) EU agency scientific opinion on chromium as a dietary trace element. Summary. European Food Safety Authority scientific opinion on Dietary Reference Values for chromium — context for chromium's biological trace-element role against its industrial toxicity. Full opinion not retrievable this pass (publisher 403); summarised from the title and journal. Data. EFSA panel review of nutritional and intake evidence; no supply data.
  • Chromium and genomic stability · Mutation Research (2012) Peer-reviewed review of hexavalent-chromium toxicology and genotoxicity. Summary. Peer-reviewed review in Mutation Research on chromium and genomic stability — the genotoxicity and carcinogenicity of hexavalent chromium Cr(VI), relevant to occupational exposure in plating and smelting. Full text paywalled/redirected (Elsevier) — not retrieved; scope taken from the citation. Data. Literature review of Cr(VI) toxicology — no supply or production data.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 2

The datasets the chain's own ore-versus-alloy figures are built from.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's ore/alloy trade panel. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products (1995–2024), derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies — the engine for the chromite (261000) and ferro-chromium (720241) shares. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • Chromium Statistics and Information · USGS National Minerals Information Center USGS chromium data portal — the production/reserves/trade series behind the chain figures. Summary. USGS National Minerals Information Center chromium portal: calls chromium "an essential feedstock in the production of stainless steel and superalloys" and links Mineral Commodity Summaries (1996–2026), Minerals Yearbook (1994–2023), monthly Mineral Industry Surveys and a Chromium Life Cycle Study. Data. USGS monthly and annual mineral statistics (production, consumption, recycling, material flow).
