The turbine design decides which bottleneck matters

Wind grew from a 17 GW niche in 2000 to almost 1.3 TW in 2025. But installed wind farms do not reveal where turbines were made—and “direct drive” alone does not reveal rare-earth exposure. Manufacturing geography and drivetrain architecture must be read together.

Installed stock ≠ annual commissioning ≠ factory capacity ≠ supplier origin ≠ customs trade

The correction. Not every wind turbine uses a large permanent magnet. Most geared turbines use a gearbox; direct-drive turbines can use permanent magnets or electrically excited generators. The design changes copper, rare-earth and gearbox requirements.

Factories and suppliers are another geography

A 2023 IEA project-announcement assessment put about 60% of prospective 2025 onshore-component capacity in China and 70–80% of offshore-component capacity there. These were capacity estimates, not observed output. GWEC’s realised 2025 supplier data show all five largest OEMs headquartered in China—but 93.4% of Chinese-OEM installations remained in their home market.

The drivetrain changes the material problem

JRC technology estimates expose the trade-off. A direct-drive electrically excited generator can avoid permanent magnets but uses more copper. A direct-drive permanent-magnet generator uses less copper than that design, but much more Nd, Pr, Dy and Tb.

Indicative kg/MW technology estimates—not universal recipes. Foundations, turbine scale and site conditions vary.

The bulk materials still dominate mass

Indicative material ranges per MW: 300–500 tonnes of concrete, 90–130 tonnes of steel and 6–9 tonnes of glass/carbon composites. Rare-earth quantities are small by mass but can be strategically important.
About 85–90% of turbine mass is currently recyclable. Composite blades and some other fractions remain difficult. Technical recyclability is not the same as actual collection and recovery.

Direct drive is a technology measure, not a magnet series

DOE sources put direct drive at 17.6% of global turbine supply in 2010, 21.2% in 2011 and 19.5% in 2012. Those observations include different generator types, so they cannot be converted directly into rare-earth demand.

The customs ledger is broad downstream of the turbine

BACI provides a 2002–2024 trade record. HS 850231 is a useful finished generating-set basket. The three component baskets include towers, blades/drivetrain parts and electrical parts for many non-wind applications.
