Research Library · Wind

Wind-turbine chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

A wind turbine turns steel, concrete, copper and composite blades into a nacelle, then a finished generating system installed on site — and at almost no step is a scarce mineral the binding constraint. The chokepoint is rarely the mine; here it is barely a mineral at all — it is scale, logistics and the OEM.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Materials (steel · concrete · copper · composites — magnet rare earths only in direct-drive) → ② Turbine OEM & manufacturing → ③ Installation, logistics & end-of-life — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Materials — steel, copper, composites & magnet rare earths 5

By mass a turbine is ~85–90% steel, concrete and copper; the only critical-mineral thread is magnet rare earths, and only in direct-drive designs.
  • Material requirements for wind turbines (JRC139701) · European Commission JRC (2024) Bill-of-materials study of material intensities (kg/MW) across wind-turbine types. Abstract. JRC technical report (Nov 2024) providing updated estimates of the material intensity — kilograms per MW installed — of 17 materials across existing wind-turbine designs. Underpins the finding that wind is dominated by bulk commodities (steel, concrete, copper), with rare-earth magnets confined to permanent-magnet direct-drive drivetrains. Data. JRC bottom-up material-intensity modelling (kg/MW) across wind-turbine archetypes.
  • Winds of Change: Supply Chain Analysis of a Wind Turbine · Degrowth Journal (2025) Component-level supply-chain analysis of a single wind turbine. Summary. Degrowth Journal paper (Joshua Ayer, University of Alberta, 2025) applying supply-chain and commodity-frontier analysis to a wind turbine, de-fetishising it by tracing its material inputs; argues renewables are necessary but insufficient to offset growth without wider economic restructuring. Data. Qualitative supply-chain / commodity-frontier analysis of one turbine — no primary quantitative dataset.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Rare Earths · USGS (2026) Annual production/reserve/import data for the rare earths used in some turbine magnets. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2025 US mine output ~51,000 t REO (valued $240 million) from Mountain Pass bastnaesite and southeastern monazite; net import reliance 67%, with compound/metal imports 71% from China. The leading global end use is magnets — the Nd–Dy demand linking rare earths to direct-drive wind generators. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade, price and import-source data.
  • Rare Earth Elements: Pathways to Secure and Diversified Supply Chains · IEA (2026) Dedicated rare-earth supply-chain report covering wind and EV magnet demand. Summary. IEA report (April 2026), Pathways to Secure and Diversified Supply Chains, mapping the mine-to-magnet rare-earth value chain and its concentrated processing; frames neodymium–dysprosium magnet demand from wind and EVs and the options to diversify supply. Data. IEA supply-demand scenario analysis of the rare-earth value chain (landing page / executive summary consulted).
  • Global rare earth supply, life cycle assessment, and wind energy · Weng et al., Wind Energy Engineering (2017) Life-cycle look at rare-earth supply constraints on wind energy. Summary. Book chapter in Wind Energy Engineering (Weng et al., 2017) examining global rare-earth supply and life-cycle assessment for wind. Full text paywalled (Elsevier) — not retrievable this pass; entry noted from title and catalogue metadata only. Data. Life-cycle assessment plus rare-earth supply review — no accessible dataset (paywalled).

② Turbine OEM & manufacturing concentration 6

The genuine concentration: China-HQ OEMs supplied ~67% of 2025 units — a corporate, not geological, chokepoint that localises.
  • GWEC records sharp rise in wind-turbine installations as five OEMs exceed 100 GW · Global Wind Energy Council (2026) Source of the ~67% China-HQ OEM supplier-share figure and 178 GW installed. Summary. GWEC news release (2026): a record 178 GW of wind was mechanically installed in 2025 (165 GW grid-connected) across 28,395 turbines. Chinese-HQ OEMs supplied 67% of global units, though 93% of their installations stayed in their vast home market. Data. GWEC market-intelligence tally of 2025 turbine installations and supplier shares.
  • The State of Clean Technology Manufacturing · IEA (2023) Manufacturing-capacity analysis across clean-tech supply chains including wind. Summary. IEA analysis (2023): wind-manufacturing throughput reached ~100 GW in 2022, with China holding over 60% of onshore-nacelle capacity (EU ~15%, US ~10%); announced projects would push China to 55–65% by 2030 and 70–80% of offshore equipment. Data. IEA bottom-up manufacturing-capacity dataset (existing plus announced projects) by technology and region.
  • Prospects for global market expansion of China's wind-turbine manufacturing industry · Gosens, Energy Policy (2014) Foundational analysis of how Chinese OEMs built global wind-turbine market share. Summary. Energy Policy paper (Gosens, 2014) analysing the prospects for global market expansion of China's wind-turbine manufacturing industry — the mechanism behind today's ~67% figure. Full text paywalled (Elsevier) — not retrievable this pass; noted from title and catalogue metadata. Data. Industry analysis of Chinese-OEM export competitiveness — no accessible dataset (paywalled).
  • GWEC Global Wind Report 2026 · Global Wind Energy Council (2026) Annual flagship report on installations, growth pathways and the supplier landscape. Summary. GWEC's annual flagship (2026): 165 GW of new wind connected in 2025 (up 40%), lifting cumulative capacity to 1,299 GW across 138 countries, with China, the USA and India leading additions; positions wind as a scalable grid-anchor amid energy-security pressures. Data. GWEC installation database and member-reported supplier data.
  • GWEC Global Offshore Wind Report 2026 · Global Wind Energy Council (2026) Offshore-specific market and supply-chain report. Summary. GWEC offshore report (2026): offshore capacity reached 92.5 GW by end-2025 (+9.3 GW); five markets — China, UK, Germany, Netherlands, Taiwan — hold 90%, average turbine size passed 10 MW, and it urges governments to diversify supply chains. Data. GWEC offshore installation database and member-reported market data.
  • Wood Mackenzie — Lens Wind · Wood Mackenzie Commercial data product tracking 500+ turbine factories and OEM market share (landing page; full reports paywalled). Summary. Landing page for Wood Mackenzie's Lens Wind, a commercial platform tracking 500+ blade/tower/nacelle/drivetrain factories, the market share of 20+ turbine suppliers, quarterly order intake and 1,000+ turbine models, plus an annual supply-chain report. Full data paywalled. Data. Proprietary commercial database (factory locations, supplier shares, order tracking) — paywalled; landing page only.

③ Installation, logistics & end-of-life 5

The scale problem: 100-metre blades, specialised vessels, coastal factories and an O&M workforce — plus the blade-recycling tail.
  • Wind Turbine Recycling · US Department of Energy (2023) Basis for the blade end-of-life / composite-recycling panel. Summary. US DOE page: 85–90% of a wind turbine's mass is commercially recyclable today; the remaining 10–15% is fibre-reinforced composite (blades) and magnet rare earths. DOE's 2023 Materials Recycling Prize ($5.1 million, six 2024 winners) funds cost-effective blade-composite recovery. Data. DOE program descriptions and recyclability estimates; no primary decommissioning-volume dataset.
  • Offshore Wind Geospatial Analysis · IEA Global offshore-wind resource/siting data tool, relevant to the logistics-chokepoint framing. Summary. IEA interactive tool (with Imperial College London, from the Offshore Wind Outlook 2019) mapping global technical potential for offshore wind by water depth and distance to shore, alongside projects and population density. Archived October 2024. Data. Geospatial technical-potential model (water depth, distance-to-shore) plus project and population layers.
  • GWEC / GWO Global Wind Workforce Outlook 2025–2030 · Global Wind Energy Council (2025) Technician/labour requirements across the manufacturing and installation chain. Summary. GWO/GWEC outlook: the wind industry will need 628,000 technicians by 2030 to build, install and — increasingly — maintain a 2.1 TW global fleet, with operations-and-maintenance the fastest-growing, most skill-intensive segment across six focus markets. Data. GWO/GWEC workforce modelling across six markets (Australia, Brazil, France, Germany, India, USA).
  • WindEurope — Offshore Wind Energy 2026 Mid-Year Statistics · WindEurope (2026) H1 2026 European offshore connections and capacity data. Summary. WindEurope's H1 2026 offshore statistics on European connections and capacity. Page sits behind a member sign-in (B2C login redirect) — not retrievable this pass; entry noted from the product listing only. Data. WindEurope member statistics on European offshore grid connections — access-gated.
  • US Wind Energy Monitor Q2 2026 · American Clean Power / Wood Mackenzie (2026) Quarterly US wind market analysis. Summary. ACP / Wood Mackenzie quarterly (Q2 2026): the US is projected to connect over 50 GW through 2030 (forecast raised 4%); post-FID projects are 58% of greenfield additions to 2028; Q1 turbine orders hit 1.1 GW, fivefold year-on-year. Data. ACP / Wood Mackenzie US project pipeline and turbine-order tracking.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 3

Criticality outlooks and renewable-deployment forecasts that place wind's minor mineral footprint in context.
  • Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 · IEA (2026) Latest annual outlook on minerals demand for clean-energy technologies, including wind. Summary. IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 (July 2026): annual assessment of supply and demand for energy-transition minerals — market, investment and technology trends, supply concentration in mining and refining, and policy implications for resilient, diversified supply chains. Data. IEA critical-minerals supply-demand scenario modelling (Data Explorer, 37 minerals) — landing page consulted.
  • Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 · IEA (2025) Prior-year edition, useful for year-over-year comparison. Summary. IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 (May 2025): demand and supply for copper, lithium, nickel, cobalt, graphite and rare earths across STEPS/APS/NZE scenarios, with a focus on mining/refining concentration and diversification policy. The prior-year baseline for comparison. Data. IEA critical-minerals scenario projections for 37 minerals — landing page consulted.
  • Renewables 2025 · IEA (2025) Market forecasts and capacity additions for wind through 2030. Summary. IEA Renewables 2025 (Oct 2025), the agency's main annual sector report, forecasting deployment of renewable technologies in electricity, transport and heat through 2030 — the source for wind capacity-addition trajectories used across the atlas's clean-energy chains. Data. IEA renewable-deployment forecast model (Renewable Energy Progress Tracker) — landing page consulted.

Across the chain — trade & data 3

The datasets the chain's component-trade figures are built from.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's component-trade extract. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024 (release 202601), derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF freight and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Supplies the wind chain's generating-set and parts trade extract. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • Wind — energy-system overview · IEA Wind generation and capacity data, 2015–2024. Summary. IEA wind energy-system overview: interactive charts (Renewable Energy Progress Tracker) for wind electricity generation, installed capacity and annual net additions, 2015–2024, plus the IEA Wind Energy Systems Technology Collaboration Programme. A data-explorer landing page. Data. IEA Renewable Energy Progress Tracker series (generation, capacity, additions), 2015–2024.
  • UN Comtrade Plus · United Nations Underlying international trade database that feeds BACI. Summary. United Nations trade-statistics portal: the official source for detailed international merchandise-trade data by reporter, partner, commodity and classification (including HS), which is reconciled downstream into CEPII's BACI. The upstream provenance for the chain's trade figures. Data. UN member-state customs declarations of merchandise trade (reporter × partner × HS commodity).

Gaps & good next searches

  • The direct-drive-vs-geared split governs the whole rare-earth exposure of wind, yet no public series gives the permanent-magnet (PMSG) share of the installed fleet — the single most useful missing number. Next: "direct-drive share of global wind installations 2020–2025" and OEM drivetrain-mix disclosures.
  • No CSIS, RMI, Rystad Energy or Reuters/Mining.com analysis of China's wind-OEM dominance could be confirmed this session (site fetches returned 403/blocked). Retry: "China wind turbine OEM dominance CSIS analysis" and "Reuters China wind turbine exports 2026".
  • Gosens (2014) and Weng et al. (2017) are both paywalled (Elsevier); an open-access equivalent or a press summary on Chinese-OEM market-building and on wind rare-earth LCA would let us reproduce the mechanism, not just cite it.
  • IRENA's wind-energy and critical-materials pages returned 403 on fetch — worth a direct search pass ("IRENA wind energy statistics 2026", "IRENA critical materials renewable energy") to add an independent agency source.
  • No dedicated market-research report (Wood Mackenzie, Rystad) was accessible beyond a paywalled landing page; press summaries of their wind-OEM market-share data would corroborate the ~67% figure.
