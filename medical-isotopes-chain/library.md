# Medical-isotopes chain — research library

Technetium-99m images tens of millions of patients a year, but its parent Mo-99 is made in only about six ageing research reactors and decays with a 66-hour half-life — so it cannot be stockpiled, and a single reactor outage cascades into global shortages. A curated reference library for deeper study — public sources, gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source.

## Core references (already cited in the chain)
- [OECD NEA — The Supply of Medical Radioisotopes (Mo-99/Tc-99m)](https://www.oecd-nea.org/med-radio/) — OECD Nuclear Energy Agency (2023). Programme hub for the NEA's medical-radioisotope supply-security work.
- [IAEA — production and supply of medical radioisotopes](https://www.iaea.org/topics/radioisotopes) — IAEA (2024). Agency topic page on radioisotope production and applications.
- [NNSA — molybdenum-99 without highly enriched uranium](https://www.nnsa.energy.gov/aboutus/ourprograms/nonproliferation/programoffices/mo-99) — US National Nuclear Security Administration (2024). The HEU-to-LEU conversion program for Mo-99 production.
- [CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade](https://www.cepii.fr/CEPII/en/bdd_modele/bdd_modele_item.asp?id=37) — CEPII. Harmonised bilateral trade database used for the atlas's own flows.

- The historical 2009-2010 Tc-99m shortage (NRU Chalk River, HFR Petten both down simultaneously) is a well-documented case study but wasn't captured here with a citable primary source — search "2009 molybdenum-99 shortage NRU HFR" for contemporaneous reporting and post-mortems.
