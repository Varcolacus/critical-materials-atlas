Lithium-ion battery chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

A lithium-ion battery turns spread-out mines — lithium in Australia and Chile, cobalt in the DR Congo, nickel in Indonesia — into battery-grade chemical, then cathode and anode powder, then an assembled cell and pack. The mine is the least concentrated stage of all; the midstream runs ~80–90% through China.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Raw materials & battery-grade refining → ② cathode & anode active materials (~85% / ~90% China) → ③ cell manufacturing (~80% China) → ④ pack, EV & grid storage — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Raw materials & battery-grade refining 4

The mine map is genuinely multi-country — but ships to China to become battery-grade chemical.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Lithium · USGS (2026) Annual world lithium production/reserves/uses; battery demand driving growth. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: batteries are 88% of lithium end use. Excluding US output, 2025 world mine production rose 31% to ~290,000 t — Australia ~92,000 t, Chile ~56,000 t, China ~62,000 t, Argentina among the leaders. US net import reliance exceeds 50% (Chile 54%, Argentina 43%); battery-grade carbonate averaged ~$9,000/t. Data. USGS annual producer canvass (US company data withheld) plus reported trade and price data.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Cobalt · USGS (2026) China as leading refined-cobalt producer/consumer; cobalt-free LFP substitution. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2025 world cobalt mine production ~310,000 t, with the DR Congo ~230,000 t (~74%) and Indonesia ~44,000 t the next largest. Cobalt content of lithium-ion batteries — the leading use — is being cut, and cobalt-free LFP substitutes hold significant market share in China. US net import reliance 79%. Data. USGS producer estimates plus S&P Global Platts price data and reported trade.
  • British Geological Survey — World Mineral Statistics · BGS UK/world mine-production statistics dataset since 1913; covers lithium and cobalt. Summary. BGS statistical series giving mineral-production statistics for 70+ commodities by country worldwide, from 1913 to the present (latest edition 2020–2024), compiled to support security-of-supply, economic-planning and policy analysis. Data. BGS compilation from government departments, statistical offices, commodity authorities, company reports and an international contact network.
  • BGS — UK and world mineral statistics datasets · British Geological Survey Downloadable production datasets, recent five-year window. Summary. The downloadable-dataset portal for the BGS World Mineral Statistics series — production tables over a recent five-year window (2020–2024) for lithium, cobalt and other commodities, released for reuse. Data. BGS-compiled national mine and production statistics, published as downloadable tables.

② Cathode & anode active materials — the chemistry midstream 3

Where ore becomes battery powder: cathode ~85% China, anode ~90% — and the recipe keeps moving.
  • IEA — Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025: Beyond NMC batteries · IEA (2025) China's cathode/anode/cell shares and the LFP/sodium-ion transition. Abstract. IEA analysis of battery chemistries beyond nickel-manganese-cobalt: over 98% of LFP cathode material and LFP cells are produced in China, versus under two-thirds for nickel-based cells. Sodium-ion offers upstream diversification (US, Europe), but its downstream — cell, cathode and hard-carbon anode — remains China-dominated. Data. IEA analysis drawing on USGS mineral summaries, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and FAO data for 2024 capacity distribution.
  • IEA — Global EV Outlook 2023: Trends in batteries · IEA (2023) Earlier baseline for chemistry and cost trends. Abstract. IEA baseline: in 2022 LFP reached ~30% of EV-battery capacity against NMC's ~60% as Chinese makers pursued lower cost; average pack price was ~$150/kWh. About 65% of cells and nearly 80% of cathodes were made in China — the earlier anchor for the midstream-concentration read. Data. IEA analysis from EV Volumes, BloombergNEF price surveys, S&P Global and USGS commodity data (2015–2023).
  • JRC — Deep dive on critical raw materials for batteries · European Commission JRC (2025) EU cathode/anode production vs demand gap underpinning the CRMA. Abstract. JRC deep dive on critical raw materials for EU batteries: identifies supply-concentration risks along the lithium-ion chain, projects battery-material demand to 2050 under baseline and aggressive-innovation (alternative-chemistry) scenarios, and assesses how repurposing and recycling — with EU recycling capacity as of December 2024 — can mitigate supply risk. Data. JRC foresight modelling of EU battery deployment/demand scenarios to 2050 plus an EU recycling-capacity inventory (Dec 2024).

③ Cell manufacturing — the ~80% China chokepoint 4

The binding stage: electrode coating and cell assembly at scale run about 80% through China.
  • IEA — Global EV Outlook 2025: Electric vehicle batteries · IEA (2025) Battery demand, chemistry mix and manufacturing capacity by country. Abstract. IEA: 2024 EV-battery demand topped 950 GWh (heading past 3 TWh by 2030), with LFP near half the market. China held ~80% of battery-cell production and near-monopolies on cathode (~85%) and anode (~90%) active material; announced US/EU projects would only cut China's share to about two-thirds by 2030. Data. IEA analysis from EV Volumes, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and BloombergNEF — installed capacity and sales-weighted demand.
  • IEA — Global EV Outlook 2026: Manufacturing and trade · IEA (2026) Latest cell/cathode/anode manufacturing and trade shares. Abstract. IEA update: in 2025 China supplied over 80% of battery-cell production, ~85% of cathode and over 90% of anode active material. Under the Stated Policies Scenario to 2035 the concentration eases only modestly — roughly three-quarters of cells, ~75% of cathode and ~90% of anode — as advanced economies expand slowly. Data. IEA Manufacturing and Trade model (Energy Technology Perspectives 2026) with EV Volumes and Benchmark Mineral Intelligence.
  • CSIS — Friendshoring the Lithium-Ion Battery Supply Chain: Cell Manufacturing · CSIS Policy analysis of onshoring/friendshoring cell manufacturing away from China. Abstract. CSIS analysis: as of 2022 China produced roughly 90% of anodes and lithium electrolyte, while the US held only ~7% of global battery production; a US cell plant cost ~$865M against ~$650M in China. It argues for nearshoring with Japan and South Korea over domestic-only content mandates. Data. CSIS policy analysis citing production-share and plant-cost figures; secondary industry data.
  • NREL / NAATBatt — Online Lithium-Ion Battery Supply Chain Database · NREL Directory of North American facilities across raw materials, electrode, cell and pack manufacturing. Summary. Public NREL/NAATBatt directory of North American lithium-ion supply-chain facilities — raw materials, electrode, cell and pack manufacturing. Database page not retrievable on this pass (host unreachable) — described from its published scope. Data. NREL-maintained facility directory (company-reported North American plants and capabilities).

④ Pack, EV & grid storage — demand & end use 3

The demand that reorganised the whole chain in a decade — cells into vehicles and grid storage.
  • IEA — Batteries and Secure Energy Transitions: Status of demand and supply · IEA (2024) Full-chain demand/supply status report. Abstract. IEA status report: energy-sector battery demand topped 2,400 GWh in 2023, a fourfold rise on 2020, while average battery costs have fallen about 90% since 2010. China holds nearly 85% of global battery-cell production capacity — the supply-side counterpart to the demand surge. Data. IEA synthesis of demand, cost and manufacturing-capacity metrics through 2023–2024.
  • ICCT — Investigating the U.S. Battery Supply Chain and its Impact on EVs · ICCT (2024) US-focused battery supply-chain and EV cost report. Abstract. ICCT (Feb 2024): models the US battery supply chain and its effect on BEV prices, 2023–2032. Finds new lithium supply from US and FTA / critical-mineral-agreement partners may far exceed US light-duty BEV demand; builds bottom-up pack-cost scenarios across lithium, cobalt and nickel prices. Data. ICCT project-level assessment of new lithium projects plus a bottom-up battery-cost model under EPA 2027–2032 standards.
  • McKinsey — Battery 2030: Resilient, sustainable, and circular · McKinsey & Company Demand forecast and value-chain outlook to 2030. Summary. McKinsey outlook on the battery value chain to 2030 under resilient, sustainable and circular framings. Page not retrievable on this pass (host unreachable / connection reset) — noted from title and publisher only; no figures taken. Data. McKinsey proprietary market forecast — no public primary dataset.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & bottleneck analysis 6

Methods that classify supply risk along the whole chain, not just at the mine.
  • Lithium-Ion Battery Supply Chain Considerations: Analysis of Potential Bottlenecks in Critical Metals · Joule / ScienceDirect (2017) Foundational bottleneck analysis for Li, Co, Ni and graphite. Summary. Foundational Joule paper analysing potential bottlenecks in the critical metals of the lithium-ion supply chain — lithium, cobalt, nickel and graphite. Full abstract not openly retrievable (paywalled; no abstract in open indexes) — described from title and venue. Data. Supply-chain bottleneck analysis of Li/Co/Ni/graphite production and reserves — secondary/industry data.
  • Supply risks of lithium-ion battery materials: An entire supply chain estimation · ScienceDirect (2019) Extends criticality analysis beyond the mine to downstream processing. Summary. Estimates supply risk across the entire lithium-ion battery material supply chain, extending criticality analysis beyond the mine to downstream processing stages. Full abstract not openly retrievable (paywalled; no abstract in open indexes) — described from title and venue (2019). Data. Whole-chain supply-risk estimation across material stages — method paper; no open dataset retrieved.
  • Global Competition in the Lithium-Ion Battery Supply Chain: A Novel Perspective for Criticality Analysis · Environmental Science & Technology (2021) Trade-based criticality lens on lithium hydroxide/carbonate competition. Abstract. Quantifies global competition intensity for 15 categories of lithium-ion-battery commodities via a new "competition index". Finds lithium hydroxides, LIBs and lithium carbonates were the focal points of global competition in 2019 — with hydroxide competition set to intensify — and competition among Korea, Japan and the USA the most notable. Data. Author-compiled trade data on 15 LIB commodity categories, processed into a competition index (2019).
  • Insights into the Critical Materials Supply Chain of the Battery Market for Enhanced Energy Security · ACS Energy Letters (2024) Material-flow view plus end-of-life/recycling as a supply lever. Abstract. Examines the battery market's critical-materials supply chain for long-term energy security, treating EV packs as decade-long "locked reserves". Introduces a "Materials Per Gallon-Electric (MPGe)" metric for per-mile consumption of critical reserves, and stresses advancing recycling to recover critical materials and secure a durable domestic supply chain. Data. Material-flow analysis plus a new per-mile critical-reserve consumption metric (MPGe).
  • Market Power in Critical Mineral Supply Chains for Electric Vehicle Batteries · Resources for the Future Market-power / concentration framing for EV battery minerals. Abstract. RFF analysis of concentration in EV battery-mineral chains: China controlled ~65% of lithium supply (2020) and ~74% of cobalt (2021), yet has not aggressively restricted supply or inflated prices. It frames price discrimination — not physical scarcity — as the main risk, with non-Chinese processing capacity the remedy. Data. RFF analysis of trade statistics and mineral-processing shares.
  • "Mineral Security" Policy for Electric Vehicle Battery Minerals · Resources for the Future Policy-report companion on mineral security. Abstract. RFF report on mineral-security policy for EV battery minerals, focusing on China's dominant processing of lithium, cobalt, graphite, nickel and manganese. It argues China's processing overcapacity reflects industrial policy prioritising domestic supply chains rather than market manipulation, tempering common supply-disruption fears. Data. Roskill Information Services data (2019–2020), USGS mineral statistics (1999–2024) and expert consultation.

Across the chain — policy, security & reporting 6

National action plans, comparative policy and the analysis that reads the chain politically.
  • Li-Bridge — Building a Robust and Resilient U.S. Lithium Battery Supply Chain · DOE / Argonne (2023) US federal action plan and 2030 domestic-supply-chain targets. Abstract. Li-Bridge (DOE/Argonne-convened, authored with BCG and NAATBatt): a US action plan to build a robust domestic lithium-battery supply chain. It sets 2030 and 2050 goals, diagnoses the top challenges holding back US industry, and issues objectives and specific recommendations for government and industry stakeholders. Data. Industry-consortium action plan (Li-Bridge / FCAB) synthesising stakeholder input and a US supply-chain gap analysis.
  • Battling for Batteries: Li-ion Policy and Supply Chain Dynamics in the U.S. and China · Kleinman Center, U. Penn Comparative US-China policy and supply-chain analysis. Abstract. Kleinman Center comparison of US and Chinese lithium-ion policy, 1994–2025: China's steady, strategic industrial policy versus intermittent, politically dependent US efforts. Coding 135 policies (85 US, 50 Chinese), it links China's coordination to its dominance, with recent US action shifting toward minerals security over climate goals. Data. Analysis of 135 policies (1994–2025) via the IEA Policy Tracker and government sources, plus 2023–2024 trade/sales data.
  • The Devil is in the Details: Minerals, Batteries, and US Dependence on Chinese Imports · Council on Strategic Risks (2025) US import-dependence detail on battery minerals. Summary. Council on Strategic Risks brief detailing US dependence on Chinese imports across battery minerals and components. Page returned 403 on this pass — not retrievable; noted from title and publisher only, no figures taken. Data. Policy commentary on US–China battery-mineral import dependence — no primary dataset retrieved.
  • From Russian gas to Chinese batteries: Tracking the EU's new energy dependence · CEIAS Frames EU battery-China dependence against the earlier Russian-gas dependence. Summary. CEIAS argues the EU is trading Russian-gas dependence for Chinese-battery dependence: China supplies over 80% of EU battery imports and ~90% of global anode capacity, and dominates processing of three of five key battery raw materials (lithium, manganese, graphite). Data. Analysis citing EU import shares and global battery-material processing/production concentration.
  • NAATBatt International · NAATBatt North American advanced-battery trade association; convened Li-Bridge. Summary. NAATBatt International is a not-for-profit trade association of 385+ member companies working to develop, commercialise and manufacture advanced battery technology in North America; it convened the Li-Bridge initiative. Data. Trade-association landing page — organisational description, no dataset.
  • Benchmark Mineral Intelligence — Battery & Gigafactory Supply Chain Data · Benchmark Mineral Intelligence Project-level gigafactory and price-reporting coverage across the chain. Summary. Benchmark's supply-chain data service tracks global gigafactory build-outs, cell-production capacity and costs from raw materials to cell across lithium-ion and sodium-ion, with price forecasting and demand modelling. Data. Commercial price-assessment and project-tracking datasets (subscription) — not openly reproducible.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 3

The datasets the chain's own figures are built from.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's trade panel. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • China dominates global trade of battery minerals · U.S. EIA (2025) China's share of interregional battery-mineral trade (imports/exports). Summary. US EIA: in 2023 China took ~44% of interregional battery-mineral imports and ~58% of exports, and held 53% of global battery-material exports and 74% of battery-pack and component exports. Data. EIA analysis of UN Comtrade (UN Statistics Division) interregional trade flows, 2023.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 (full volume) · USGS (2026) All ~90 nonfuel commodities, published February 2026. Summary. USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — the full annual volume covering ~90 nonfuel mineral commodities (production, imports, reserves, uses), published February 2026; the umbrella source behind the per-commodity lithium and cobalt chapters. Data. USGS annual mineral-commodity statistics (producer canvass, trade and reserves).

Gaps & good next searches

  • The binding stage — cell and pack manufacturing — has no clean country-level customs code: HS 850760 records a traded cell's assembly origin, not the refining and electrode midstream behind it. A facility-level capacity series (Benchmark or the NREL directory, both partly paywalled or unreachable on this pass) would close the gap.
  • Recycling and second-life material flows are only lightly covered here (ACS Energy Letters 2024 touches it) — a dedicated "battery recycling material-flow analysis EU/US 2026" search would strengthen the circularity angle the chain's "engineered down" thread gestures at.
  • Sodium-ion and solid-state supply-chain implications appear in the IEA 2025 material but lack an independent source — try "sodium-ion battery supply chain China CATL 2026" for a dedicated reference.
  • Graphite/anode (~90%+ China) is asserted across several sources here but belongs in the dedicated graphite-chain library rather than duplicated in this one.
  • Company-level (CATL, BYD, LG Energy Solution, Panasonic) capacity and investment data was not pulled — Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and company IR filings are the next stop for that granularity.
  • No dedicated OECD or NEA battery report surfaced — worth "OECD critical minerals policy tracker" or "OECD battery raw materials trade".
