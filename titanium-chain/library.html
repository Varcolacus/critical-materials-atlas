Titanium chain — from ore to aerospace-qualified metal

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Ninety percent of titanium is white pigment — a broad, diversified commodity mined and made in many countries. The other tenth is metal, produced as "sponge" by the Kroll process for jet engines, airframes, armour and implants — and that sliver is concentrated in China, Japan, Russia and Kazakhstan. The tonnage average hides the risk.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① ilmenite / rutile ore → ② TiO₂ pigment (~90%, diversified) vs sponge (Kroll — the strategic ~10%) → ③ ingot & mill: aerospace-qualified vs commodity metal — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

② TiO₂ pigment — the diversified ~90% 2

Most titanium is white paint. A large, competitive commodity market — the part that makes aggregate "titanium" statistics look safe.
  • Titanium Statistics and Information · USGS National Minerals Information Center Hub page for titanium mineral concentrates, sponge and the annual Minerals Yearbook chapters. Summary. USGS titanium resource hub linking Mineral Commodity Summaries (1996–2026), Minerals Yearbook chapters (1994–2022) and quarterly surveys. States that roughly 95% of titanium is consumed as titanium dioxide — the white pigment in paints, paper and plastics — the rest as high-strength-to-weight metal. Data. Index/portal to the USGS titanium publication series (concentrates and sponge); no standalone dataset on the page.
  • TZMI — Mineral Sands and TiO₂ Pigment Specialists · TZ Minerals International Consultancy covering the titanium-dioxide pigment and mineral-sands industry; publishes market and processing studies. Summary. TZMI (est. 1994, Australia, with a Shanghai office) is a mineral-sands and TiO₂-pigment consultancy offering markets/strategic and technical/engineering advisory, monthly/quarterly/annual reports, mining and processing studies, and the annual TZMI Congress. Data. Proprietary consultancy market/technical analysis — no public dataset on the site.

③ Sponge (Kroll) — aerospace-grade metal 3

The strategic sliver: sponge production concentrated in China, Japan, Russia (VSMPO-Avisma) and Kazakhstan.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Titanium Sponge · USGS (2026) Annual titanium-sponge production, reserves and trade data by country. Scope. USGS commodity summary for titanium sponge — US and world Kroll-process sponge output, import reliance and end uses (chiefly aerospace metal), with producers led by China, Japan, Russia and Kazakhstan. (PDF not retrievable this pass — HTTP 403 after one retry; scope from the chapter title and series.) Data. USGS voluntary producer surveys (much withheld) plus reported sponge import/export trade data.
  • Aerospace-approved Ti sponge supply up in 2024 · Argus Media (2025) Market reporting distinguishing bulk titanium-sponge tonnage from aerospace-qualified supply. Summary. News article (18 Feb 2025): aerospace-approved titanium-sponge output from Japan, Kazakhstan and Saudi Arabia rose to 89,000 t in 2024 from 84,000, at 83% capacity use; uncertified Chinese sponge held steady at 220,000 t. Data. No primary dataset — producer and trade figures reported in market commentary.
  • United Steelworkers — Titanium Sponge Section 232 petition (2018) · United Steelworkers Trade-policy petition documenting the collapse of U.S. domestic titanium-sponge production. Abstract. Section 232 petition by Titanium Metals Corporation (TIMET), Sep 2018, seeking relief from titanium-sponge imports threatening national security. TIMET's Henderson, Nevada plant is the last US sponge producer; without relief the US becomes fully import-dependent on Japan, Kazakhstan, Russia, China and Ukraine. Data. Petition citing DOC/ITC findings and dumping margins, plus industry production, consumption and world sponge-capacity data.

④ Ingot & mill — qualified vs commodity 3

Where sponge becomes ingot, then aerospace-qualified mill product — the stage that takes years to certify.
  • U.S. Strategic Material Supply Chain Assessment: Titanium · U.S. Dept. of Commerce, BIS Government assessment of titanium supply-chain vulnerability for defense/aerospace end uses. Abstract. U.S. Dept. of Commerce BIS (Office of Technology Evaluation) industrial-base assessment of the domestic titanium supply chain (2016). Surveys manufacturers and distributors on products, sourcing, single/sole-source inputs, capacity, defense dependency, financial health and R&D, mapping vulnerabilities and reliance on foreign inputs. Data. Original BIS OTE industrial-base survey of U.S. titanium manufacturers and distributors (respondent-reported sourcing, capacity, financials).
  • Vulnerability Analysis on the Titanium Supply Chain: A Network Approach · Northeastern University Network-based vulnerability analysis of the global titanium supply chain. Abstract. Master's thesis modelling the U.S. titanium supply chain as a network to assess vulnerability. Node-disruption analysis finds TIMET ingot/mill capacity critical, though diverse U.S. ingot capacity (e.g. ATI) permits rerouting; activating domestic sponge reduces redundant ingot capacity, amplifying a TIMET disruption's impact. Data. Network model of the U.S. titanium supply chain (sponge/ingot/mill nodes and capacities — TIMET, ATI, ZTMC) with node-disruption simulation.
  • Life Cycle Assessment and Risk Management of Titanium for Aerospace/Defense Applications · Georgia Tech LCA and supply-risk study of titanium in aerospace/defense use. Abstract. Life-cycle assessment and supply-risk analysis of titanium for aerospace. Examines ore extraction, distribution, mill-product capacity, prices and geopolitical vulnerabilities, finding China and Russia dominant; a national emergency could halt U.S. production via sponge/scrap shortages. Proposes HSCR-steel substitution, domestic capacity and scrap use, with TRL/MRL evaluation. Data. LCA plus supply-chain risk analysis on production-capacity, trade and price data (USGS/industry) and TRL/MRL evaluation of alternatives.

Market & geopolitics — China, Russia, Ukraine 5

Trade-press and analyst reads on who controls sponge and how fast the West can diversify.
  • Global titanium supply squeeze: who controls the market? · AeroTime Market analysis of China/Russia grip on titanium and Western aerospace exposure. Summary. Reports China and Russia together control about 74% of global titanium-sponge production; surging aerospace demand risks tightening, with aerospace-grade certification taking up to a decade — limiting near-term Western diversification. Data. Trade-press reporting citing Project Blue market-intelligence data and interviews; no government/academic sources.
  • China's titanium dominance · The Oregon Group Analysis of China's vertically integrated titanium supply chain and its limits for aerospace qualification. Summary. Analyses China's vertically integrated titanium dominance (34% minerals, 67% sponge, 55% pigment capacity) while noting aerospace-qualification and feedstock constraints; frames Western opportunities via new projects and anti-dumping trade defences. Data. Market analysis citing JRC, USGS, Argus Media, a Stanford critical-minerals report and company figures.
  • China aerospace-grade titanium sponge exports set to rise · The Metalnomist (2026) Reports a Chinese producer's plan to scale qualified aerospace-grade sponge exports toward 2030. Summary. Reports Chinese aerospace-grade titanium-sponge exports projected to rise from ~1,000 t/yr to 10,000 t by 2030 as Western OEMs diversify from Russian supply and aircraft output grows. Data. Trade-press analysis citing producer projections (Chaoyang Jinda Titanium), capacity/pricing and trade-flow figures.
  • Is there a titanium shortage? Aerospace and defense demand outlook 2026 · AlloyIndex (2026) Market outlook on titanium demand/supply balance for aerospace and defense. Summary. Examines whether a titanium shortage is emerging, arguing concentrated aerospace-grade supply, long certification lead times and accelerating aircraft/defence demand could trigger structural tightness in high-grade titanium alloys. Data. Market commentary / analyst opinion; no primary dataset.
  • Ukraine's titanium opportunity · MINING.com Geopolitical angle on titanium supply diversification away from Russia. Summary. Op-ed arguing Ukraine's deep Soviet-era titanium legacy (Kroll-process sponge, zirconium/hafnium metallurgy) positions it to help diversify a now highly concentrated global titanium industry disrupted by Russia's war, where China dominates sponge output. Data. Op-ed commentary (Yegor Perelygin) citing USGS figures (2024 global sponge capacity 410,000 t, output 320,000 t, China ~69%).

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 4

EU/US assessments and legislative background that classify titanium and its metal fraction.
  • Titanium metal in the EU: Strategic relevance and circularity potential (JRC137082) · European Commission JRC (2025) Dedicated EU assessment of titanium metal's strategic role and recycling potential. Abstract. JRC report (20 Jan 2025) examining titanium's strategic importance and circularity for the EU. Classified as both critical and strategic under the CRM Act, titanium metal serves aviation, defence, space and manufacturing; the EU faces a supply–demand gap and heavy import reliance, with enhanced circularity a route to reduce external dependence. Data. Supply-chain and demand-side assessment across EU strategic industries, geopolitical evaluation and macroeconomic modelling of circularity scenarios; R&I intensity via publications/patents.
  • Closing the loop: EU's titanium supply chain · European Commission JRC (2025) Titanium scrap/recycling policy note; flags Kazakhstan as an EU titanium supplier. Summary. JRC update on EU titanium vulnerability: despite heavy consumption, import-to-export ratios reach 6:1 (products) and 10:1 (sponge), with Russia the top aviation-grade producer. Recommends reshoring midstream processing, recycling decommissioned-aircraft titanium, and partnerships with Kazakhstan and Ukraine. Data. JRC analysis citing titanium import-to-export ratios and producer concentration — no primary dataset.
  • Raw materials in the European defence industry · European Commission JRC/SETIS (2021) Assesses EU dependency on titanium and other materials for defence/aerospace. Abstract. Identifies the raw materials important to the European defence industry and their supply risk from import dependency. Finds 39 raw materials needed for advanced defence materials — about half fully import-reliant — and 47 critical alloys/composites whose performance defies substitution, giving higher supply risk than their constituents. Data. JRC synthesis of a prior contract study; inventory of 39 raw materials and 47 alloys/composites plus EU import-dependency data.
  • Critical Mineral Resources: National Policy and Critical Minerals List (R47982) · Congressional Research Service (2024) Legislative/policy background on the U.S. critical minerals list process. Summary. CRS report (Linda Rowan): reviews US minerals policy, the Energy Act of 2020 definition of a critical mineral, and USGS list development (2022: 50 minerals; 2025: 60) for congressional supply-chain considerations. Data. Secondary synthesis of USGS lists/methodology, statute, and production/import-reliance data; no new primary dataset.
