Research Library · Bismuth

Bismuth chain — the non-toxic heavy metal that replaces lead

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Bismuth is the safe heavy metal — dense like lead but non-toxic — so it is the standard replacement as lead is regulated out of drinking-water brass, free-machining alloys, shot and sinkers, and it is the "bismol" in stomach remedies and the shimmer in cosmetics. It is recovered as a by-product of lead and tungsten smelting, and China makes and refines roughly 80% of it. The metal we reach for to escape toxic lead is itself a one-country dependency.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① By-product source — recovered from lead and tungsten smelting, China ~80% of production and refining → ② end uses — the non-toxic lead replacement (plumbing, alloys, shot) plus pharma and pigment — then the cross-cutting criticality frameworks, market reporting and trade data that sit across every stage.

① By-product source — lead & tungsten, China ~80% 7

The raw input no one mines for itself: recovered as a by-product of lead, tungsten (and, in Japan/Korea, zinc) smelting — and China makes and refines roughly 80%.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Bismuth · USGS (2026) Annual world production/reserves/uses data for bismuth; China's dominant share and US import reliance. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: the US ceased primary refined-bismuth production in 1997 and is ~92% import-reliant. Bismuth is recovered mostly as a by-product of lead — and, in China and Vietnam, tungsten — smelting. Estimated 2025 world refinery output was 16,000 t, China 88% (14,000 t); chief uses are pharmaceuticals, non-toxic lead replacement, and fusible alloys. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade, price (Fastmarkets, Rotterdam) and world refinery-production data.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries — Bismuth (2020–2024 series) · USGS Year-by-year annual summaries showing China's leading share and the US import trend. Abstract. Earlier annual USGS bismuth chapters track the same structure: no US primary refinery since 1997, net import reliance 90–97%, China the leading producer and exporter, and China's share of US imports falling from 68% (2019–22) toward 56% (2021–24) as the Republic of Korea and Germany grew. Documents the multi-year China-concentration trend. Data. USGS annual mineral-commodity statistics, year by year (production, imports, net import reliance, price).
  • 2010 Minerals Yearbook — Bismuth · USGS Detailed annual chapter: by-product recovery, US end-use split, world review. Abstract. Detailed 2010 USGS annual chapter: US bismuth consumption ~884 t, split 65% chemical/pharmaceutical, 27% metallurgical additives, 8% alloys/fusible-alloy/solder/ammunition. US primary production ended in 1997 (last recovered as a by-product of lead refining at a Nebraska refinery); leading refined-bismuth producers were China, Mexico, Belgium and Peru. Notes emerging non-toxic lead-replacement uses. Data. USGS annual domestic-consumption survey plus the world refinery-production table.
  • BGS World Mineral Statistics — bismuth · British Geological Survey Long-run public series of world mine/metal production and trade by commodity and country. Abstract. BGS's long-run public dataset of world mine and metal production and trade by commodity and country, from national statistical returns — the standard non-USGS ground-truth series used to cross-check bismuth production shares. (Cited mineralsUK index page returned 404 this pass — the statistics browser has moved; URL needs refreshing.) Data. BGS compilation of national government production and trade returns, spanning several decades.
  • Production of mineral commodities and geospatial map of the mineral industries of China · USGS Open-File Report (2026) Review of China's 2023 mineral production, capacity and processing infrastructure. Abstract. USGS Open-File Report reviewing China's 2023 mineral production and capacity: of 77 commodities in the USGS dataset China produced 74 and ranked first for 39, with global shares up to 98% (gallium); reserves are comparatively smaller (20% zinc ore to 52% tungsten ore). Maps mines and processing infrastructure — context for bismuth's China concentration. Data. USGS global mineral dataset (77 commodities) plus a geospatial GIS map of Chinese mineral mines and processing infrastructure.
  • Bismuth: economic geology and value chains · Ore Geology Reviews (2022) Deposit geology and full value-chain academic review of bismuth. Abstract. Bismuth occurs across many deposit types and is usually a deleterious by-product; its EU (2017) and US (2018) criticality listings reawakened supply-security interest as demand rises to substitute lead and for chemicals. Reviews its geochemistry and sources — W-, Pb- and Au-rich skarns, five-element veins, and Sn/W greisens — and the value chain. Data. Ore-deposit and geochemistry synthesis plus value-chain review — no new primary dataset.
  • Bismuth Mining & Processing · Critical & Strategic Metals Hub Overview of by-product recovery and processing geography. Summary. Overview page: bismuth (~20,000 t/yr) is obtained predominantly as a by-product of lead and copper smelting and tungsten mining rather than by primary extraction; China leads output (cited ~65% share); recovery uses electrolytic refining and the Kroll–Betterton process. Data. Secondary explainer — cites production shares and process descriptions, no primary dataset.

② End uses — non-toxic lead replacement, pharma & pigment 4

Bismuth's whole value proposition is being the harmless heavy metal: lead-free brass, free-machining alloys, shot and sinkers, fusible alloys — plus bismuth subsalicylate and pearlescent pigment.
  • Thermodynamic and Microstructural Analysis of Lead-Free Machining Aluminium Alloys with Indium and Bismuth Additions · Materials (MDPI, 2023) Peer-reviewed study of bismuth (with indium) as a lead-free machinability additive. Abstract. Study testing indium and indium–bismuth additions as substitutes for toxic lead in free-machining aluminium alloys (EN AW 6026, 1370), using Thermo-Calc modelling, DSC and SEM–EDS. Finds indium a promising lead alternative — homogeneous, low-melting particles that avoid interactions — but bismuth does not improve machinability in magnesium-containing aluminium: a nuanced substitution result. Data. Thermodynamic (Thermo-Calc TCAL-6) plus DSC and SEM–EDS characterisation of two aluminium alloys.
  • Using recycled bismuth–tin solder in novel machinable lead-free brass · Journal of Cleaner Production (2017) Academic comparison of bismuth vs other additives for lead-free brass machinability. Abstract. Study using recycled bismuth–tin solder as a machinability additive in lead-free Cu-38Zn-0.5Si brass. Adding 1–3 mass% Bi–Sn cut tensile strength and machining force and broke long continuous chips into short ones; 3% gave the best machinability, matching commercial EnviroBrass III — evidence bismuth can replace lead in free-cutting brass. Data. Experimental mechanical-property, microstructure and machinability tests on Bi–Sn-added lead-free brass.
  • Bismuth Alloys — an overview · ScienceDirect Topics Reference summary of bismuth alloy chemistry and fusible-alloy uses. Summary. Reference aggregation of textbook and handbook excerpts on bismuth alloys — chiefly low-melting "fusible" alloys and their compositions and uses. (Page served behind a 403 block on this pass — not retrievable; entry noted from its title and index role.) Data. Aggregated encyclopaedia/handbook excerpts — reference material, no dataset.
  • Free-Cutting Aluminium Alloys with Tin as Substitution for Lead · BHM (Springer) Comparative lead-substitution metallurgy (tin vs bismuth). Abstract. Study (Univ. Leoben Institute of Nonferrous Metallurgy) responding to the EU End-of-Life Vehicles ban (from 2008) on machining aluminium above 0.4 wt% lead: develops a lead-free tin-alloyed 2030 aluminium with grade-"A" machinability — short, broken chips — matching leaded alloys on microstructure, strength, corrosion resistance and machinability; tin is the lead substitute, bismuth the comparator. Data. Alloy-development study: microstructure, mechanical, corrosion and machinability testing of tin-alloyed aluminium against a leaded reference.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 4

EU/US assessments and analysis that classify bismuth and quantify its supply concentration.
  • European Commission — Critical Raw Materials (2023 assessment) · European Commission (2023) The EU's formal criticality assessment underlying its 2023 CRM list, which includes bismuth. Abstract. The EU's 2023 criticality assessment scores 70 candidate materials on Economic Importance and Supply Risk, yielding 34 Critical Raw Materials — bismuth among them (with copper and nickel added as strategic). It feeds the Critical Raw Materials Act; the assessment reflects bismuth's high supply concentration in China. Data. EU criticality methodology (Economic Importance × Supply Risk) over 70 materials using production, trade, substitution and recycling data.
  • BISMUTH — CRM 2020 Factsheet · SCRREEN2 / EU Horizon (2020) EU criticality factsheet: supply risk, economic importance, end uses, substitution. Abstract. EU Horizon (SCRREEN2/JRC) factsheet: bismuth, very rarely mined as a main metal, is mainly a by-product of lead and tungsten, with China the top mined and refined producer. EU consumption ~1,985 t and ~50% import-reliant, China 93% of EU imports; end uses pharma/animal-feed 62%, fusible alloys 28%. World reserves ~370,000 t. Data. JRC value-chain elaboration (DERA, USGS, Eurostat, Minerals4EU) with EU sourcing, end-use split and reserve figures.
  • Bismuth | CRM Alliance · CRM Alliance (EU) Policy-facing profile of bismuth as an EU critical raw material. Summary. EU industry-alliance profile: bismuth as an eco-friendly lead substitute with growing pharma, electronics and metallurgical uses; supply highly concentrated — China ~80% of refined production (2021) and 47% of EU imports — underpinning its critical-raw-material status. Data. Policy-facing profile — cites production and EU-import shares, no primary dataset.
  • Assessment of critical minerals supply chain for the United States in perspective of trade restriction by foreign countries · Resources Policy (2025) US-focused analysis of trade-restriction exposure, including bismuth's 2024 Chinese export controls. Abstract. Scores the 50 US critical minerals for supply-chain vulnerability by screening each stage (extraction, processing, trade) for single-foreign-country dominance, weighted by a "freedom in the world" indicator (risk score Spr). Bismuth scores Tier 1 (high risk); the approach is validated by China's 2024 export controls on bismuth, antimony and magnesium. Data. Producer-concentration screening of 50 critical minerals across extraction/processing/trade with a governance-weighted Spr score.
