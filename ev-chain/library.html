Electric-vehicle chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

An electric car is mostly two supply chains it contains — the battery pack and the magnet motor — wrapped in final assembly. The battery is the real chokepoint; the magnet is a design choice you can engineer out; and assembly, though ~70% in China, localises near demand. The finished-car map is the least informative layer of all.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Battery pack (the inherited mine-to-cell chokepoint) → ② Traction motor & magnets (substitutable) → ③ EV assembly (~70% China, but diffuse) → ④ market & demand — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Battery — the chokepoint the EV inherits 10

The pack is ~40% of the car's value and the one part with a deep mine-to-cell supply chain — refined and assembled ~80–90% in China.
  • IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026 — Electric vehicle batteries · IEA (2026) Battery chemistry mix and cell-manufacturing geography for 2025. Summary. LFP passed 55% of EV batteries deployed globally in 2025 and cost more than 40% less per kWh than NMC; global cell capacity topped 4 TWh with China over 80%, and Chinese producers reached almost 75% of electric-car battery deployment. Data. IEA analysis on BloombergNEF, EV Volumes, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and Chinese industry data.
  • IEA, Global Supply Chains of EV Batteries · IEA Dedicated mine-to-cell mapping of the battery supply chain, stage by stage. Abstract. Batteries are 30–40% of an EV's value, so the race to net zero centres on battery-mineral security. China makes three-quarters of lithium-ion cells, holds 70% of cathode and 85% of anode capacity, and over half of lithium, cobalt and graphite refining; 70% of capacity announced to 2030 is Chinese. Data. IEA supply-chain mapping using production-capacity, minerals-processing and trade data.
  • IEA, Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 — Beyond NMC batteries · IEA (2025) LFP's cathode/cell concentration in China versus nickel-based chemistries. Summary. Over 98% of LFP cathode material and LFP cells are produced in China, versus under two-thirds for nickel-based cathode material; China also makes ~75% of battery-grade purified phosphoric acid and 95% of battery-grade manganese sulphate — new bottlenecks as LFP grows. Data. IEA analysis citing USGS (2025) Mineral Commodity Summaries, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and FAO data.
  • The Supply Chain for Electric Vehicle Batteries · US International Trade Commission US-focused mapping of the EV battery supply chain and trade flows. Summary. USITC Journal of International Commerce and Economics article mapping the electric-vehicle battery supply chain and US trade flows. PDF behind a bot-challenge (403) — not retrievable this pass; entry from the verified title and publisher. Data. USITC supply-chain and trade-flow analysis — not retrieved.
  • Investigating the U.S. Battery Supply Chain and Its Impact on Electric Vehicles · ICCT (2024) US battery supply-chain report tied to IRA sourcing rules. Abstract. ICCT analyses the US battery supply chain and its impact on BEV costs, 2023–2032. New lithium supply from the US and its FTA/Critical-Mineral-Agreement partners may far exceed light-duty BEV demand; builds bottom-up battery-cost scenarios for lithium, cobalt and nickel to assess BEV price impacts. Data. Bottom-up battery-cost model with lithium/cobalt/nickel price scenarios and a project-by-project lithium-supply assessment.
  • The EV Battery Supply Chain Explained · RMI Accessible mine-to-pack explainer with supply-risk framing. Summary. RMI explainer of the four battery stages — mining, refining, cell/pack, recycling. Notes China makes three-quarters of lithium-ion batteries and 70% of cathode capacity, while US capacity is set to scale from ~72 GWh past 1,000 GWh. Data. RMI synthesis citing S&P Global Mobility forecasts and US legislation (IIJA, IRA, CHIPS).
  • Electric vehicle battery chemistry affects supply chain disruption vulnerabilities · PMC/NCBI (2024) Network model comparing NMC vs LFP exposure to supply disruption. Abstract. Maps supply chains for lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese and computes a vulnerability index for NMC vs LFP cathodes. 80% of NMC and 92% of LFP cathodes contain minerals passing through China; combined multi-stage vulnerability substantially exceeds any single step. Data. Supply-chain mapping plus a vulnerability index and network-flow optimisation over four battery minerals.
  • "Mineral Security" Policy for Electric Vehicle Battery Minerals · Resources for the Future (2025) Policy report on US mineral-security options for EV batteries. Summary. China refined 65% of lithium (2020) and 74% of cobalt (2021) but kept expanding capacity as prices fell — behaviour inconsistent with strategic supply cuts. RFF argues the real exposure is price risk, not physical scarcity, and urges pricing transparency. Data. RFF analysis on Roskill and USGS (1999–2023) production/price data and historical supply episodes.
  • Market Power in Critical Mineral Supply Chains for Electric Vehicle Batteries · RFF Common Resources Market-power / concentration framing of battery-mineral supply. Summary. Examines whether China's processing dominance lets it cut supplies or raise prices. Finds no selective cuts to Japan (2010–19) and continued capacity expansion during price surges — suggesting limited pricing power, though possible international price discrimination. Data. RFF analysis of refining-capacity, price and trade episodes.
  • The Devil is in the Details: Minerals, Batteries, and US Dependence on Chinese Imports · Council on Strategic Risks (2025) Detailed breakdown of US import dependence across battery minerals. Summary. Council on Strategic Risks argues the US's growing dependence on China for critical minerals and battery supply chains is a significant national-security concern. Full text not retrievable (403); noted from the article's own description. Data. CSR policy analysis of US battery-mineral import dependence — full text not retrieved.

② Traction motor & magnets — the chokepoint you design around 6

Permanent-magnet motors tie the car to neodymium and dysprosium — but induction and wound-rotor designs use no magnet rare earths at all.
  • Electric Motors Research and Development · US DOE Federal R&D on traction motors, including rare-earth-free designs. Summary. DOE programme on cutting rare-earth use in traction motors; contrasts internal-permanent-magnet, induction and switched-reluctance designs and funds Beyond-Rare-Earth-Magnet work at Ames and reduced/non-magnet motor designs at Oak Ridge. Data. US DOE programme description — no dataset.
  • Electric vehicle traction motors without rare earth magnets · Sustainable Materials and Technologies (2015) Engineering review of induction and other rare-earth-free EV motor designs. Summary. Review of EV traction-motor designs that avoid rare-earth magnets — induction and reluctance topologies among them. Abstract not retrievable (publisher bot-wall); entry from the title and journal verified via Crossref (10.1016/j.susmat.2015.02.001). Data. Engineering review of motor topologies — abstract not retrieved.
  • Geopolitical risk and the global supply of rare earth permanent magnets: insights from China's export trends · Energy Economics (2025) China's magnet export trends as a geopolitical-risk signal. Summary. Analyses China's rare-earth permanent-magnet export trends as a geopolitical-risk signal for the global magnet supply. Abstract not retrievable (publisher bot-wall); entry from the title and journal verified via Crossref (10.1016/j.eneco.2025.108496). Data. Trade-data analysis of China's magnet exports — abstract not retrieved.
  • A Hybrid Rotor Design with Reduced Rare Earth Magnets for Traction Motors · JOM / Springer (2022) DOE Critical Materials Institute-linked motor redesign to cut rare-earth content. Abstract. Proposes a hybrid-rotor multiple-magnet (HRMM) design placing Nd-Fe-B magnets only at key locations and ferrite elsewhere, so both magnetic and reluctance torque are harnessed. Benchmarked against a 2010 Prius 60 kW motor, it matches performance across the speed range with ~50% less critical rare-earth content at low cost. Data. Electromagnetic motor design and simulation benchmarked to a commercial (Prius 2010) traction motor.
  • Emerging magnetic materials for electric vehicle drive motors · MRS Bulletin / Springer (2024) Review of Sm2Fe17N3, Fe16N2 and other rare-earth-free magnet candidates. Abstract. About 80% of EV drivetrains rely on high-performance permanent magnets, mostly Nd2Fe14B, with samarium-cobalt the only commercialised alternative. Emerging Sm2Fe17N3, Fe16N2 and L10-FeNi use cheaper elements but are temperature-sensitive; advances in low-temperature synthesis could unlock magnets beyond rare earths. Data. Materials-science review of commercialised and emerging permanent-magnet chemistries.
  • EV Motors Without Rare Earth Permanent Magnets · IEEE Spectrum Engineering-press survey of rare-earth-free motor efforts (Tesla, Niron, ORNL). Summary. With ~90% of processed rare earths coming from China, engineers survey REE-free routes — ferrite/alnico/FeN/MnBi magnets (motors 30–65% heavier) and wirelessly-excited synchronous motors (ZF's 220 kW unit, an Oak Ridge 100 kW motor) that can match NdFeB performance. Data. Engineering-press reporting citing Oak Ridge and ZF prototypes.

③ EV assembly & the finished car 5

The EV's own concentration — ~70% built in China — but assembly is switchable and localises near demand, so the finished-car map hides the upstream.
  • IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026 — Trends in electric cars · IEA (2026) Headline EV production/sales trends and China's assembly share. Summary. Over 20 million electric cars were sold in 2025 — one in four new cars — up 20% year on year; China took over half of sales (13M+), around 55% of its own new-car sales were electric, and BEVs were 65% of EV sales. Data. IEA analysis on EV Volumes, ACEA, EAFO and national automotive associations.
  • IEA, Global EV Outlook 2026 — Manufacturing and trade · IEA (2026) Where EVs are built versus traded; the localisation of assembly. Summary. ~22 million electric cars were produced in 2025 with only about a quarter traded between major regions. China made ~16 million (over 70%, ~75% counting exports) and doubled exports past 2.5 million, while the EU (3.2 million) was the second-largest producer. Data. IEA analysis on Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, EV Volumes and manufacturer announcements.
  • IEA, Global EV Outlook 2025 — Executive summary · IEA (2025) Prior-year baseline for EV sales/production trends. Summary. Baseline: 2024 EV sales topped 17 million (over 20% of cars), with China ~11 million — about half its market. Sales were expected above 20 million in 2025 (over 25% share) and above 40% of car sales by 2030. Data. IEA analysis on S&P Global and Bloomberg data.
  • China's Electric Vehicle Industry's Internationalization · CSIS Analysis of China's EV export expansion and global market penetration. Summary. CSIS testimony: China holds ~60% of new-vehicle registrations and 40% of the global electric-car stock, makes 65% of lithium batteries and 80% of cathodes, and is building plants in Europe, Thailand and Brazil to skirt tariffs. Data. CSIS congressional testimony (Mazzocco) synthesising trade and market data.
  • Deep Dive: China's Journey to EV Dominance · Contrary Research Industry deep-dive on how China built EV manufacturing scale. Summary. Contrary Research: China reached over 60% of global EV production by 2025; BYD overtook Tesla; China holds ~85% of cell-manufacturing capacity, built on roughly $29 billion of 2009–22 subsidies and much cheaper, faster factories. Data. Industry synthesis of company production and market figures.

④ Market & demand — sales and production statistics 5

The demand the chain sits on — independent sales and production series to cross-check the production-share figures.
  • Global EV sales reach 20.7 million units in 2025, growing by 20% · Benchmark Mineral Intelligence (2026) 2025 global EV sales figure and regional shares. Summary. 2025 global EV sales hit 20.7 million (+20%) — China 12.9 million (+17%), Europe 4.3 million (+33%), North America 1.8 million (-4%) and rest-of-world 1.7 million (+48%); the US contracted after tax-credit repeal. Data. Benchmark Mineral Intelligence EV-sales tracking.
  • Global lithium ion battery demand rose 29% in 2025 · Benchmark Mineral Intelligence (2026) 2025 battery-demand growth and the EV vs storage split. Summary. 2025 lithium-ion demand rose 29% to 1.59 TWh; EVs were 75% of demand (down from 77%), stationary storage the fastest-growing segment at +51%, and LFP demand jumped 48%. Data. Benchmark Mineral Intelligence battery-demand database.
  • Benchmark Mineral Intelligence — EV & Battery · Benchmark Mineral Intelligence Commercial data/price-reference service for EV battery supply chains. Summary. Benchmark's EV & Battery service: quarterly forecasts to 2050, weekly EV-sales tracking by country, brand and model, production forecasts across 30+ countries and chemistry-share analysis across the value chain. Data. Proprietary subscription datasets — not open.
  • OICA — International Organization of Motor Vehicle Manufacturers, News · OICA World auto-industry association; production/trade statistics source. Summary. OICA (Paris) speaks for vehicle manufacturers in world forums; its news and statistics pages carry members' registration data and global production and sales series. Data. OICA production and sales statistics compiled from member associations.
  • Global Battery Alliance · GBA Sustainable / responsible battery value-chain initiative. Summary. WEF-convened alliance (founded 2017, 120+ members across automakers, miners and NGOs) working toward sustainable, responsible, circular battery value chains by 2030; runs the Battery Passport and critical-minerals/circularity workstreams. Data. No primary dataset — multistakeholder initiative description.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 10

Outlooks, assessments and policy that classify the whole chain's inputs — battery metals and magnet rare earths alike.
  • IEA, Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 — Market overview · IEA (2026) Updated battery-metal demand and pricing outlook. Summary. Battery demand topped 1.5 TWh in 2025 (over 35% growth); the top supplier averaged 70% of refined output (up from 68% in 2020), and China and Indonesia supplied three-quarters of 2023–25 supply growth. Data. IEA analysis on S&P Capital IQ, Bloomberg, KOMIS and its own project pipeline.
  • IEA, Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 — Executive summary · IEA (2026) Top-line concentration and supply-risk findings. Summary. The average top-refiner share reached 72% in 2025 (excluding rare earths). April-2025 Chinese controls on seven heavy rare earths threaten $6.5 trillion of downstream production outside China, and battery-related controls over $300 billion. Data. IEA risk framework over 11 high-risk materials (supply concentration, substitutability, end-use criticality).
  • IEA, Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 — Executive summary · IEA (2025) Processing concentration for cobalt, nickel, lithium and rare earths. Summary. In 2024 the top-three refiners averaged 86% share (up from 82% in 2020); cobalt and nickel growth came ~90% from a single supplier each. For battery metals and rare earths, non-leading supply meets only about half of residual 2035 demand. Data. IEA analysis on S&P Global and Bloomberg data.
  • Deep dive on critical raw materials for batteries in the EU · European Commission JRC (2025) Foresight study underpinning the EU Critical Raw Materials Act, incl. Li-ion cells. Abstract. JRC analyses the EU battery supply chain and the critical raw materials behind lithium-ion cells, identifying supply-concentration and manufacturing bottlenecks. Models battery deployment to 2050 under base and aggressive-innovation scenarios, estimates CRM demand, and assesses circularity and recycling as mitigation, including EU recycling capacity at December 2024. Data. JRC foresight: qualitative supply-chain analysis plus quantitative demand and secondary-material scenarios to 2050.
  • European Critical Raw Materials Act · European Commission The CRMA's benchmark targets for EU mining/processing/recycling capacity. Summary. By 2030 the EU should mine 10%, process 40% and recycle 25% of its annual strategic-raw-material needs, with no single third country supplying over 65% of any strategic material at any processing stage. Data. EU regulation — policy benchmarks, not a dataset.
  • Climate impacts of critical mineral supply chain bottlenecks for electric vehicle deployment · Nature Communications (2024) Models how mineral-supply bottlenecks slow EV deployment and its climate benefit. Abstract. Analyses how US tailpipe standards requiring 10.21 million EVs (2027–32) meet mineral limits: US-and-ally production supports only 5.09 million vehicles, and the shortfall forfeits ~59.5 Mt CO2e of lifecycle benefit. Battery-grade graphite and cobalt are the tightest constraints. Data. Mineral-reserve and production-capacity modelling with lifecycle-emissions accounting.
  • Self-sufficiency of the European Union in critical raw materials for E-mobility · Resources, Conservation and Recycling (2025) Quantifies the EU's import dependence for EV-relevant raw materials. Summary. Quantifies EU self-sufficiency and import dependence for the critical raw materials behind e-mobility. Abstract not retrievable (publisher bot-wall); entry from the title and journal verified via Crossref (10.1016/j.resconrec.2024.108009). Data. Material-flow / self-sufficiency analysis — abstract not retrieved.
  • Unconventional Critical Mineral Resources for Growing Electric Vehicle Industry: Sustainability SWOT Analysis · Mining, Metallurgy & Exploration / Springer (2026) SWOT on alternative (non-traditional) mineral sources for EVs. Abstract. Reviews unconventional EV-mineral sources — waste materials, industrial by-products and end-of-life batteries and magnets — through a SWOT across techno-economic, socio-political and environmental dimensions. Finds strong circular-economy opportunities but scalability, energy and regulatory hurdles; proposes a decision roadmap for sustainable supply. Data. SWOT literature synthesis plus a decision-making roadmap framework.
  • Resource realism: The geopolitics of critical mineral supply chains · Goldman Sachs Market / geopolitical framing of critical-mineral concentration risk. Summary. Goldman Sachs framing of the geopolitics of critical-mineral supply chains ("resource realism"). Page not retrievable (403, no public abstract); entry from the verified title and publisher. Data. Investment-research commentary — not retrieved.
  • Digging Deep: Critical Mineral Supply Chains, Electric Vehicles, and the Role of Technological Innovation · Resources for the Future Issue brief on innovation as a supply-risk mitigant. Summary. RFF issue brief: technological innovation — material, process and system substitution plus recycling — can ease EV-mineral supply risk, but China's ~60–70%+ processing dominance means innovation needs policy support to scale a US midstream. Data. RFF policy synthesis — no primary dataset.
