Strontium (celestite) chain — ferrite magnets & the crimson in fireworks

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Strontium, mined as the mineral celestite, is converted to strontium carbonate, then sintered into rare-earth-free ceramic ferrite magnets — and its salts burn crimson, the red in every firework and signal flare. The ore is spread across Iran, Spain, China and Mexico. No single country holds a chokepoint: this is a diffuse chain, not a lever.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Celestite (the strontium ore — Iran and Spain co-lead, ~39% each; China ~16%) → ② strontium carbonate (the refining step) → ③ ferrite magnets and pyrotechnics / red flame — then the cross-cutting criticality frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Celestite — the strontium ore 4

The raw input: a small celestite market spread across Iran (~39%, tied with Spain), China (~16%) and Mexico — moderately concentrated, no severe chokepoint.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Strontium · USGS (2026) Annual world celestite mine production, reserves, US import sources, end uses and events for strontium. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: the US has not mined strontium minerals since 1959 and ended large-scale strontium-carbonate production in 2006, leaving net import reliance at 100%. Estimated US end uses are drilling fluids 65%, ceramic ferrite magnets 14%, pyrotechnics and signals 14%. World celestite output about 450,000 t in 2025; barium can substitute in magnets. Data. USGS annual producer/trade canvass; celestite converted at 43.88% Sr content; import-source shares 2021–24; world mine-production and reserves table.
  • BGS World Mineral Statistics — celestine (strontium) · British Geological Survey (2024) Long-run world celestite (strontium) mine-production series by country, feeding the atlas's history chart. (The record's legacy www2 link now 404s; this is BGS's current World Mineral Statistics page — same dataset.) Abstract. BGS World Mineral Statistics compiles production and trade for more than 70 mineral commodities by country, continuous from 1913 (digital from 1970) through the current 2020–2024 edition. Celestine (strontium) is among them, supplying the long-run mined-country series behind the atlas's Iran / Spain / China history chart. Data. BGS annual national-statistics compilation, 1913–present; Excel/JSON downloads from 1970; production plus imports/exports for numerous commodities.
  • USGS National Minerals Information Center — Strontium Statistics and Information · USGS Hub page indexing every Mineral Commodity Summary and Minerals Yearbook chapter on strontium back to the 1990s. Summary. USGS NMIC hub for strontium: indexes Mineral Commodity Summaries (from 1996) and Minerals Yearbook chapters (1994–2024), and notes celestite (sulfate) and strontianite (carbonate) are the only minerals with strontium concentrations high enough for practical recovery. Data. USGS commodity-specialist compilation; index of the MCS and Minerals Yearbook series plus Data Series 140.
  • USGS Historical Statistics for Mineral and Material Commodities (Data Series 140) · USGS Century-long US strontium production, consumption, price and trade series. Summary. USGS Data Series 140 compiles US statistics for about 90 nonfuel commodities — production, imports, exports and stocks; reported and apparent consumption; and unit value — much of it reaching back to 1900. A strontium file (XLSX) runs through 2022. Data. USGS surveys of 18,000+ minerals establishments; strontium series to 2022; real and nominal prices in dollars per metric ton.

② Strontium carbonate — the refining step 0

Where celestite ore becomes the traded compound — and the thinnest evidence base in the chain.
No public, country-level source maps the ore→carbonate→ferrite midstream on its own: it is documented only inside the USGS commodity lines, which note US large-scale strontium-carbonate output ceased in 2006 and that carbonate now comes mainly from China, Mexico and Germany. That absence is itself worth flagging — see Gaps & good next searches for the specialty-producer leads (Solvay, Imerys, Iranian celestite miners) worth chasing.

③ Ferrite magnets & pyrotechnics — the end uses 1

The two jobs strontium does: the cheap rare-earth-free ceramic magnet, and the crimson in fireworks and flares.
  • Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 — Executive Summary · IEA (2025) Broader critical-minerals context; permanent-magnet manufacturing diversification — the backdrop to ferrite's rare-earth-hedge role. Summary. IEA outlook: the top-three refiners' share of key energy minerals rose 82%→86% (2020→2024), China leading 19 of 20. It notes new permanent-magnet capacity in Europe, Japan, Korea and the US, but does not itself split ferrite from rare-earth (NdFeB) magnets. Data. IEA analysis drawing on S&P Global commodity databases, Bloomberg pricing, USGS MCS 2025 and the EU Raw Materials Information System.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 3

List methodologies and criticality assessments that classify strontium — and, notably, drop it from the US critical list.
  • U.S. Geological Survey Releases 2022 List of Critical Minerals · USGS (2022) USGS methodology release that notably removed strontium from the critical-minerals list. Summary. USGS news release: the 50-commodity 2022 critical-minerals list added nickel and zinc while removing helium, potash, rhenium and strontium — signalling strontium is no longer judged a severe supply risk, consistent with the atlas's "diffuse, not a lever" read. Data. USGS Energy Act of 2020 methodology using supply, demand and production-concentration data across mineral commodities.
  • Criticality of Seven Specialty Metals · Panousi et al., Journal of Industrial Ecology (2016) Yale-methodology criticality assessment of seven specialty metals — strontium among them. Summary. Applies the Yale three-dimensional criticality method — supply risk, environmental implications and vulnerability to supply restriction — to seven specialty metals: scandium, strontium, antimony, barium, mercury, thallium and bismuth. (Article paywalled — Wiley Online Library; scope, authors and metal list confirmed from indexing, full abstract not served.) Data. Yale criticality framework applied to production, trade, geopolitical and environmental indicators at global and, where relevant, national/company scale.
  • Methodology and Technical Input for the 2021 Review and Revision of the U.S. Critical Minerals List (OFR 2021-1045) · Nassar & Fortier, USGS (2021) The quantitative supply-risk methodology behind the US critical-minerals list; strontium is in the assessed set. Abstract. USGS Open-File Report documenting the quantitative method for the 2021 review of the US critical-minerals list: it scores commodities on disruption potential, economic vulnerability and trade exposure to derive an overall supply risk. Strontium is among the roughly 50 commodities evaluated (2018 base year). Data. USGS supply-risk model on 2018 production, trade and economic data across ~50 mineral commodities; three weighted components combined into a supply-risk score.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 2

The datasets the chain's own figures are built from — with the caveat that strontium cannot be cleanly isolated in trade.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 2002–2024) used for the chain's coarse, flagged-proxy trade layer. Summary. CEPII: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Strontium sits in broad lines (281640 oxides, 253090 minerals) and cannot be isolated. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries series (archive) · USGS Annual by-commodity production/reserves/trade PDFs, strontium included every year. Summary. USGS periodicals archive for the Mineral Commodity Summaries: downloadable PDF chapters for every commodity, 1996–2026, plus full reports and appendixes. The USGS annual reference on domestic and world mineral production, supply and use — the series behind the chain's production figures. Data. USGS annual mineral-commodity statistics series (production, imports, reserves, use).

Gaps & good next searches

  • No academic paper specifically on strontium/celestite criticality or material flow was surfaced beyond the two general assessments here (Panousi 2016, Nassar 2021). Next: "SrFe12O19 ferrite magnet material flow analysis", "celestite Iran production Resources Policy", "strontium ferrite supply chain review."
  • The ore→carbonate→ferrite midstream (stage ②) has no public country-level series — no specialty-producer or industry page (Solvay strontium carbonate, Imerys celestite, Iranian celestite miners) was reachable (blocked/403). Retry those directly for refining-stage capacity.
  • Verify EU standing: confirm whether strontium appears in the EU 2023 Critical Raw Materials Act candidate list or the JRC raw-materials scoreboard — not found in what was checked.
  • BACI cannot isolate strontium (lumped in HS 281640 oxides + 253090 minerals), so the trade shares are coarse; a dedicated HS-code reconciliation would sharpen the trade layer.
  • The Iran ~39% / Spain ~39% co-lead rests on USGS/BGS mine estimates that USGS itself revised (Spain 2024, Iran reserves) — worth a jackknife check as fresh MCS editions land.
