Research Library · Cobalt

Cobalt battery-metal chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Cobalt travels from a Congolese copper by-product to a Chinese refinery to a cathode powder to the battery cell — and it is concentrated twice: at the mine (~75% DR Congo) and again at the refinery (~75% China). A mine-share map shows only half the problem.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① the mine (DR Congo ~75%, with an artisanal / child-labour dimension) → ② refining (China ~75%, the second chokepoint) → ③ the demand-side answer (chemistry engineering cobalt out) — then the cross-cutting trade data that sits across every stage.

① The mine — DR Congo concentration plus an ethics problem 6

The most-discussed chokepoint — but a by-product no one mines alone: ~75% DR Congo, riding on Congolese copper, with a real artisanal / child-labour dimension.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Cobalt · USGS (2026) Annual world/US cobalt mine production, reserves, uses and import reliance by country. Scope. USGS annual mineral-commodity summary for cobalt: a short chapter of salient statistics — US and world mine and refinery production, reserves, principal uses (rechargeable batteries and superalloys) and import sources / net import reliance. (The MCS PDF returned HTTP 403 on this pass; the year's specific figures were not machine-retrievable — scope taken from the standard chapter format.) Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade data — the atlas reads the ~75% DR Congo mine share from this series.
  • BGS World Mineral Statistics — cobalt mine production · British Geological Survey Long-run mine-production series by country, used for the chain's history chart. Scope. BGS World Mineral Statistics: a long-run database of world mineral production (and some trade) by country and commodity, extending back decades — the standard reference for historical mine-production time series. (BGS blocked automated retrieval on this pass — HTTP 404; scope from the dataset's public description.) Data. BGS compilation of national production returns — feeds the atlas's 2000→2024 DRC / Indonesia / Russia mine-share history chart.
  • Cobalt Statistics and Information · USGS National Minerals Information Center USGS data portal: monthly + annual cobalt series plus deposit, criticality and recycling studies. Summary. USGS hub for cobalt: monthly Mineral Industry Surveys and annual Mineral Commodity Summaries (1996–2026), plus specialised reports on US cobalt deposits, Ni–Co laterites, criticality and recycling material-flow — production, consumption and trade over three decades. Data. USGS government mineral surveys and scientific investigations, 1996–2026.
  • Cobalt Institute — Cobalt Data Room · Cobalt Institute Global cobalt industry association; production, demand, recycling and responsible-sourcing figures. Summary. The global industry association for the whole cobalt value chain. Its Data Room reports the DR Congo at ~76% of mined cobalt and Indonesia ~12%, notes ~99% of cobalt is a by-product of copper and nickel, and that ~82% of refined supply is certified to Responsible Minerals Initiative standards; covers both industrial and artisanal mining. Data. Industry-association statistics and member data (production, demand, recycling, responsible-sourcing certification).
  • The cobalt pipeline — tracing the path from deadly hand-dug mines in Congo · The Washington Post (2016) Investigative reporting on DRC artisanal cobalt mining and the lithium-ion battery supply chain. Summary. Washington Post investigation tracing cobalt from hazardous DR Congo artisanal mines through traders into the lithium-ion battery supply chains that power consumer electronics and electric vehicles. (Page returned HTTP 403 on this pass — not retrievable; scope from the article's title and known reporting.) Data. Field investigation — mine-site reporting and supply-chain tracing; no dataset.
  • "This is what we die for": child labour behind smartphone and electric-car batteries · Amnesty International (2016) Human-rights investigation into child labour in DRC artisanal cobalt and battery-maker due diligence. Summary. Amnesty International and Afrewatch documented children as young as seven working in hazardous DR Congo artisanal cobalt mines for $1–2 a day, tracing ore through traders to a Chinese processor and on to battery makers supplying major electronics and carmakers — none of whom could then verify their cobalt's origin. Data. Interviews with 87 miners (17 children) and 18 traders at five DRC sites, April–May 2015, plus ore-flow tracing and investor documents.

② The second chokepoint — refining in China 2

The map a mine-share chart misses: wherever cobalt is dug, roughly three-quarters is refined to battery-grade chemical in China.
  • Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 — Executive summary · IEA (2025) Refining-concentration and demand-outlook analysis; the chain's ~75%-China refining basis. Summary. IEA outlook: cobalt refining grew more concentrated in 2020–2024, the top-three refiners' share rising 82%→86% with China the driver; about 90% of recent supply growth came from a single leading supplier per material (China for cobalt). The DR Congo suspended cobalt exports in February 2025. Only modest diversification is projected. Data. IEA analysis citing S&P Global production/investment data; DRC export-suspension per Reuters (Feb 2025).
  • China's domestic and foreign influence in the global cobalt supply chain · Resources Policy (2019) Peer-reviewed analysis of China's dominance of cobalt refining — this chain's second chokepoint. Abstract. Peer-reviewed study of China's domestic and foreign influence over the global cobalt supply chain — its control of refining capacity together with its investments in DR Congo mining. (Publisher link redirected to a paywall / login shell; the full abstract was not retrievable on this pass. Marked Planned — full text would enable a reproducible refining-share method.) Data. Supply-chain / material-flow analysis of cobalt mining and refining with ownership data — full text paywalled.

③ The demand-side answer — chemistry cuts cobalt out 1

Not new mines but new recipes: LFP and high-nickel low-cobalt cathodes shrink the cobalt per cell.
  • A non-academic perspective on the future of lithium-based batteries · Nature Communications (2023) Perspective on battery-chemistry trends behind the low-cobalt / LFP shift the chain describes. Scope. Nature Communications perspective on the future of lithium-based batteries, relevant to the LFP and high-nickel low-cobalt cathode trends that are engineering cobalt out of cells. (Article served behind a Nature login redirect — the full abstract was not retrievable this pass; scope from the title and DOI.) Data. Expert perspective / review — no primary dataset; full text behind login.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 1

The dataset the chain's trade panel is built from.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the cobalt trade panel. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by converting CIF imports to FOB and reliability-weighting divergent reporters. The atlas reads cobalt ores (260500) and unwrought/intermediate cobalt (810520) from it. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
