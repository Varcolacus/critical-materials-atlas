Research Library · Tin

Tin / solder chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

About half of all tin becomes solder — the joint in every circuit board — yet the supply narrows at the mine, not the factory: concentrated in China and Indonesia and fed by a conflict-linked, politically fragile swing feed from Myanmar's Wa State and the DR Congo. The fragile stage is the ore, not the metal.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Cassiterite ore (mining, concentration, 3TG / Myanmar-Wa) → ② Smelting & refining (China / Indonesia) → ③ Solder & tinplate (~half of demand) → ④ Electronics & recycling — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

② Smelting & refining — China and Indonesia 2

Concentrate becomes refined metal in a narrow set of smelters — China leaning on imported (often Wa) feed.
  • International Supply Chain of Tin Resources in China: Evaluation and Countermeasures · Strategic Study of CAE (2025) Tin-specific evaluation of China's supply-chain security across supply, trade and recycling. Abstract. Tin-specific study of China's supply-chain security across resource supply, trade circulation and recycling. Finds a "V-shaped" risk profile — rising import dependence on thin domestic reserves, limited supplier diversity, inefficient processing and low recycling — and recommends stabilising international supply, boosting exploration/beneficiation, optimising strategic reserves and diversifying procurement. Data. Strategic policy analysis across three supply-chain dimensions; production/trade indicators, no disclosed primary dataset.
  • Critical Minerals in an Age of Geopolitical Rivalry: Stockpiling, Refining Constraints, and the Limits of Friend-Shoring · SSRN working paper Refining-concentration and stockpiling risk relevant to tin's smelting bottleneck. Summary. Working paper on critical-minerals security — stockpiling, refining-concentration risk (China) and the limits of friend-shoring — bearing directly on tin's smelting bottleneck. The SSRN/DOI page returned HTTP 403 — abstract not retrievable; scope from the paper title. Data. Economic/policy analysis of refining concentration — no retrieved dataset.

③ Solder, tinplate & end-use 1

Where the metal earns its keystone status: about half of tin is solder, the joint in every assembly.
  • International Tin Association — tin use survey · ITA / ITRI Annual end-use survey; the source for the "~half is solder" figure. Abstract. Annual end-use survey: solder is the largest use at roughly half of refined tin (about 52% in the surveyed year, down from ~54% in 2007), ahead of tinplate, tin chemicals (over 15%) and alloys. China consumed about 43% of world tin; recycling ran near 32%. Data. ITRI direct survey of ~150 downstream users plus published statistics and expert estimates (2010–2011 baseline, updated annually).

④ Electronics & recycling 1

Solder joins every device — and the dispersed, tiny quantities make recovery only a partial relief.

Summary. JRC analysis of critical-raw-material recovery from battery, vehicle and e-waste streams, supporting the CRM Act; finds small-e-waste collection loses about 46% of the strategic and critical materials in those products. Tin is not named, but the e-waste framing bears on tin recovery. Data. JRC waste-stream and circularity modelling under CRM Act Article 26 — no tin-specific figure.
