Beryllium chain — the reversal

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Beryllium reverses the atlas's usual map: the United States mines about half the world's supply from a single Utah deposit and processes it largely through one firm, guarded by a toxicity so severe that few producers will handle it. Here the chokepoint is the one the West actually holds.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Bertrandite ore (one Utah deposit) → ② extraction & the dominant firm → ③ the toxicity moat → ④ aerospace, defence & the copper alloy — then the cross-cutting criticality/defence frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Bertrandite ore — one Utah deposit 6

The raw input, and it is essentially one place: the Spor Mountain bertrandite deposit in Utah, plus imported beryl.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Beryllium · USGS (2026) Annual mine production, reserves, trade and use data for beryllium. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: one Utah company mined bertrandite ore and, with imported beryl, converted it to beryllium hydroxide, then metal, oxide and copper-beryllium master alloy in Ohio. US mine output was 230 of 433 t worldwide in 2024, China ~18%; apparent consumption ~230 t (~$360M), led by consumer electronics and aerospace/defence. Data. USGS annual producer canvass, plus reported trade, stock and price data; world shares flagged high-uncertainty because beryl is stockpiled before sale.
  • Beryllium — Economic Geology, Material Flow, and Global Importance · USGS USGS research on beryllium deposit geology and material flow. Abstract. USGS research compiling a global inventory of bertrandite and beryl deposits, analysing the geologic processes that concentrate beryllium to ore grade, and tracing material flows to reduce US supply vulnerability. It uses SHRIMP and laser-ablation ICP-MS on ore samples, and notes domestic reserves and production capability are diminishing — nearly all from Spor Mountain, Utah. Data. USGS deposit inventory and material-flow analysis; ore-sample geochemistry (SHRIMP, LA-ICP-MS).
  • Beryllium — A Critical Mineral Commodity: Resources, Production, and Supply Chain (Fact Sheet 2016-3081) · USGS (2016) Dedicated USGS supply-chain fact sheet on why beryllium is single-source-critical. Abstract. USGS fact sheet on beryllium resources, production and supply chain. Its high stiffness-to-weight ratio and temperature resistance make beryllium hard to substitute in defence, so it is stockpiled as critical and strategic. Produced from bertrandite (Utah) and beryl (elsewhere); the US made ~90% of world beryllium in 2015, from Spor Mountain (opened 1969). Data. USGS NMIC production, consumption and supply-chain data; 1946–2015 mine-production series; National Defense Stockpile inventory (78 t, 2015).
  • Beryllium — USGS Minerals Yearbook 2022 · USGS (2022, released 2025) Detailed annual beryllium chapter: production, trade, prices and world review. Abstract. USGS Minerals Yearbook chapter (Brian W. Jaskula): 2022 US mine shipments held at 175 t; the US led world mined beryllium at 56%, China second at 23%. Because beryl is commonly stockpiled before processing, world-production figures and the US share carry high uncertainty. Reviews prices, trade, and the toxicity-driven regulatory regime. Data. USGS domestic survey and company data (incl. Materion), plus reported world production, trade and price series for 2022.
  • Utah Geological Survey Circular C-130 — Spor Mountain beryllium district · Utah Geological Survey State-level geology of the Spor Mountain beryllium district in Utah. Abstract. Utah Geological Survey circular on the geology of the Spor Mountain beryllium district — the volcanic-hosted bertrandite deposit that supplies essentially all US beryllium. Full circular not retrievable on this pass (the state server timed out repeatedly); scope taken from the publication title and catalogue. Data. Utah Geological Survey field mapping and deposit description (Mills, Rupke, Vanden Berg, Boden).
  • Metallogeny and exploration strategy for volcanic rocks hosting world-class Be-U-F mineralization at Spor Mountain, Utah · Ore Geology Reviews Deposit-scale academic geology of the Spor Mountain source. Abstract. Ore Geology Reviews paper on the metallogeny and exploration strategy for the world-class beryllium-uranium-fluorine mineralization in volcanic rocks at Spor Mountain, Utah — the deposit-scale geology of the single source behind US beryllium. Abstract not retrievable (ScienceDirect returned HTTP 403); scope taken from the article title. Data. Deposit-scale economic-geology study (field and geochemical); no public dataset retrieved.

② Extraction & the dominant firm — the built capability 2

The hazardous hydrometallurgy and alloying that turn ore into metal — held largely by a single company.
  • Materion Corp — Form 10-K (FY2024, with FY2022–2023) · SEC EDGAR The dominant firm's own reserve, production and processing disclosures. Abstract. Materion's annual report discloses that it operates the world's largest bertrandite ore mine and refinery, at the Spor Mountain Mining Properties (7,443 acres, Juab County, Utah, open-pit). Beryllium hydroxide is milled there from bertrandite plus purchased beryl, then converted to metal, oxide and copper-beryllium alloys — the firm behind most US beryllium capability. Data. Materion Corp Form 10-K filings (FY2022–FY2024), SEC EDGAR: mine, reserve, capacity and business-segment disclosures.
  • Beryllium Copper — an overview · ScienceDirect Topics Reference overview of copper-beryllium alloy properties and hazards. Summary. Reference overview of copper-beryllium alloy — strong, springy, conductive and non-sparking, used in connectors, springs and safety tools — and its health hazards. Full topic page not retrievable (HTTP 403); described from its title and indexed scope. Data. ScienceDirect Topics reference compilation drawn from book/journal excerpts — no primary dataset.

③ The toxicity moat — the barrier that gates the industry 5

Beryllium is not rare, but working it causes an incurable lung disease — the real reason so few will build the capability.
  • Beryllium — OSHA overview · US OSHA Regulatory framework for the toxicity that gates the industry. Summary. OSHA's beryllium hub: inhaling or contacting beryllium can cause sensitisation, chronic beryllium disease, acute disease and lung cancer. Its standards for general industry, construction and shipyards require exposure controls; OSHA estimates ~62,000 workers at ~7,300 establishments are potentially exposed. Data. US OSHA regulatory standards and enforcement guidance; agency exposure estimates.
  • Epidemiologic and Clinical Studies of Beryllium Sensitization and Chronic Beryllium Disease · NCBI Bookshelf Review of CBD epidemiology — the toxicity moat, in depth. Abstract. Review of the epidemiology and clinical picture of beryllium sensitisation and chronic beryllium disease (CBD) — a lymphocyte-mediated delayed hypersensitivity causing granulomatous lung lesions. CBD prevalence ranges from 0.1% to nearly 8% across facilities; disease occurs below the 2 µg/m³ standard, with no safe threshold, and ~31% of sensitised workers progress to CBD. Data. Synthesis of workplace epidemiologic and clinical studies (blood BeLPT screening, exposure–response); review, no new dataset.
  • Beryllium Toxicity · StatPearls, NCBI Bookshelf Clinical reference on beryllium toxicology and CBD management. Summary. Clinical reference on beryllium toxicity: acute disease presents as chemical pneumonitis, while chronic beryllium disease (berylliosis) is a granulomatous lung disease in sensitised individuals via type-IV hypersensitivity. Diagnosis uses the beryllium lymphocyte proliferation test; management is exposure removal and corticosteroids. Data. StatPearls clinical review — synthesised clinical evidence, no primary dataset.
  • Beryllium copper alloy (2%) causes chronic beryllium disease · PubMed Clinical evidence that even low-beryllium Cu-Be alloy causes CBD. Summary. Clinical case evidence that even a low-beryllium (~2%) copper-beryllium alloy can cause chronic beryllium disease — showing the toxicity barrier extends to the everyday Cu-Be alloy, not only pure beryllium. Full abstract not retrievable (PubMed cookie wall); stated from the record title. Data. Clinical case evidence (PubMed record) — abstract not retrieved.
  • From strategic critical metal to hazardous solid waste: integrated management of beryllium-containing wastes and environmental risk assessment · ScienceDirect Frames beryllium at the intersection of criticality and hazardous-waste management. Summary. Journal article framing beryllium at the intersection of strategic criticality and hazardous-waste management — the integrated handling of beryllium-containing wastes and their environmental-risk assessment. Abstract not retrievable (ScienceDirect HTTP 403); scope taken from the article title. Data. Environmental-management study of beryllium wastes; abstract not retrieved.

④ Aerospace, defence & the copper alloy 1

Where beryllium goes: satellites, missiles, gyroscopes and X-ray windows — and the copper-beryllium connectors everywhere else.
  • From Mine to Mission — Critical Raw Materials for Defence Capability · BeST Industry framing of beryllium's role in defence supply chains. Summary. BeST industry article framing beryllium — alongside nickel and niobium — as indispensable to advanced defence systems, aerospace, communications, sensors and guidance, and arguing that secure raw-material access has become critical amid rising European defence spending and geopolitical tension. Data. Industry-association commentary (BeST); qualitative framing, no dataset.

Across the chain — criticality, defence & stockpile policy 5

Why beryllium is watched: list methodologies, EU/US defence assessments and the stockpile programs that hold it.
  • Critical Mineral Resources: National Policy and Critical Minerals List (R47982) · Congressional Research Service (2025) How beryllium made the US critical-minerals list — a single point of failure. Summary. CRS report on US critical-minerals policy: beryllium, with nickel and zirconium, is on the 2022 list not for a high supply-risk score but because USGS found a single point of failure in its supply chain. Data. CRS synthesis of USGS methodology and list, statute, and production/import-reliance data; no new dataset.
  • Raw materials in the European defence industry · European Commission JRC/SETIS (2021) Beryllium and other critical raw materials in EU defence equipment. Abstract. EU JRC/SETIS report on raw materials for European defence. Beryllium is mainly mined in the USA and used in lightweight alloys for fighter jets, helicopters and satellites — six times lighter and stronger than steel — and in missile gyroscopes and gimbals. The EU is fully import-reliant for 19 of 39 defence raw materials. Data. JRC inventory of 39 defence raw materials and 47 critical alloys/composites, with EU import-dependency data.
  • Critical Raw Materials and European Defence · IISS (2025) Think-tank analysis of CRM dependencies, including beryllium, in European defence. Abstract. IISS paper mapping critical-raw-material dependencies in European defence. It lists beryllium's largest producer as the US at 50%, EU import reliance 100%, and notes the US supplies 60% of the EU's beryllium. It traces beryllium and Cu-Be alloys through F-35 systems — a beryllium-aluminium gimbal housing, and Cu-Be connectors and structural parts. Data. IISS analysis mapping materials to defence platforms; production/import-reliance figures (2016–20 averages) and component-level teardowns.
  • Revitalizing the National Defense Stockpile for an Era of Great-Power Competition · Heritage Foundation Policy analysis of the US strategic stockpile; beryllium is one of the stockpiled materials. Summary. Heritage Foundation report urging the US to rebuild the National Defense Stockpile for great-power competition. It lists beryllium among the strategic materials critical to national security, and explains the Transaction Fund mechanism after decades of drawdown. Data. Policy analysis of the National Defense Stockpile (DLA holdings, appropriations, fund mechanics).
  • Strategic Mineral Stockpiles & National Defense Reserves: US, China & EU Programs · Critical & Strategic Metals Hub Comparative overview of stockpile programs. Summary. Comparative overview of US, Chinese and EU strategic-mineral stockpile programs: the rebuilding US National Defense Stockpile, China's large and opaque state stockpiling, and the EU's 2024 Critical Raw Materials Act framework — context for holding a defence-critical metal. Data. Secondary compilation of public stockpile-program information; no primary dataset.

Market commentary calling beryllium America's rare mineral monopoly: the US controls ~56% of world production, down from ~90% a decade ago, almost entirely through Materion's single Utah mine and refinery, with China's overseas expansion the emerging risk.
