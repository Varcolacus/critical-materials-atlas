Electricity-grid chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

A grid turns abundant copper and aluminium into conductors, then transformers wound around grain-oriented electrical steel, then a working network — and the binding constraint is none of the metals. A large power transformer can now take up to four years to procure. The ore is fine; the transformer is the bottleneck.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① copper & aluminium (abundant conductor metals) → ② transformers (grain-oriented electrical steel cores, years-long queues) → ③ the network that must roughly double by 2040 → ④ the interconnection queues that gate generation — then the trade data that sits across every stage.

① Copper, aluminium & conductors — the abundant metals 1

Grids run on abundant, recyclable copper and aluminium; the live question is conductor capacity and reconductoring, not ore.
  • U.S. DOE — Advanced Conductor Scan Report · US DOE / Idaho National Laboratory (2023) DOE Office of Electricity scan of advanced-conductor technologies and reconductoring on existing lines. Abstract. Idaho National Laboratory report for the U.S. DOE Office of Electricity (INL/RPT-23-75873) surveying advanced overhead conductors — ACSS, ACCC and composite-core aluminium designs — that carry more current on existing rights-of-way. Reviews reconductoring case studies, adoption barriers and opportunities, framing higher-capacity aluminium conductor, not new copper, as a near-term grid-expansion lever. Data. DOE/INL technology scan of conductor types (ACSS/ACCC/ACCR), utility case studies and reconductoring-candidate line segments — engineering review, no single dataset.

② Transformers — the grain-oriented-steel core bottleneck 8

The heart of the chain: large power transformers need grain-oriented electrical steel and skilled winding, and now take years to procure.
  • Large Power Transformer Resilience — Report to Congress · US DOE (2024) DOE assessment of large-power-transformer (LPT) vulnerabilities, lead times and strategic-reserve options. Abstract. U.S. DOE report to Congress (July 2024) on large-power-transformer resilience. Finds LPT acquisition lead times commonly quoted at 36 months and reaching 60, that 82% of new LPTs were imported and roughly 80% of grain-oriented electrical steel was imported in 2019, and argues for surplus/strategic transformer reserves to speed grid recovery. Data. DOE synthesis of prior LPT studies, producer conversations and trade figures on imports, GOES supply and lead times.
  • Transformer troubles: manufacturing and policy constraints hit US transformer supply · Wood Mackenzie Analysis of US transformer supply deficits, ageing stock and the single domestic GOES producer. Summary. Wood Mackenzie opinion: US power-transformer supply runs a modelled ~30% deficit (distribution ~6%), constrained by a single domestic grain-oriented-electrical-steel producer and ageing stock — 55% of distribution transformers are over 33 years old. Data. Wood Mackenzie Supply Chain Analytics estimates plus a 2024 DOE study and manufacturer interviews — no primary dataset.
  • 24+ Month Lead Times: New Normal for Transformer Suppliers · CWIEME Berlin Coil-winding industry publication on why transformer lead times have doubled. Summary. Coil-winding industry publication: transformer procurement now exceeds 24 months versus a 7–14-month pre-pandemic norm, blamed on limited grain-oriented electrical steel, bushing/tap-changer shortages and ~70% average plant utilisation against 5–12.7% annual demand growth to 2030. Data. CWIEME industry commentary citing supplier utilisation, lead-time and demand-growth figures — no primary dataset.
  • Power Transformer Lead Times Hit Record Highs as U.S. Grid Equipment Shortage Deepens · IndustrialSage Trade report on record transformer lead times and prices amid a US grid-equipment shortage. Summary. Trade report: power-transformer lead times average 128 weeks (144 for generator step-up units) and prices are up ~77% since 2019, as data-centre, electrification and grid demand outpaces capacity gated by grain-oriented electrical steel — with ~80% of large US transformers imported. Data. Trade-press analysis citing Wood Mackenzie survey lead-time/price figures and demand-growth estimates.
  • US transformer shortage deepens · Transformer Magazine Industry news on deepening US transformer shortages and their demand drivers. Summary. Industry news: US transformer delivery times stretch toward four years as AI-data-centre, electrification and grid demand surges — generator step-up demand up 274% and substation demand up 116% (2019–2025) — against grain-oriented electrical steel and copper constraints. Data. Trade-press reporting citing demand-growth and lead-time figures; no primary dataset.
  • Power Transformer Lead Times of 128 Weeks Are Driving the Solid-State Transformer Opportunity · mgrid.org Argues record transformer lead times open a commercial window for solid-state transformers. Summary. Argues record transformer lead times — a 128-week average and 144 weeks for generator step-up units (Wood Mackenzie Q2-2025) — open a commercial window for solid-state transformers. Page not retrievable this pass (403); noted from the cited headline figures only. Data. Trade-analysis citing Wood Mackenzie Q2-2025 survey lead-time data — no primary dataset.
  • The AI-Grid Collision & Transformer Crisis · Energy Solutions Intelligence Links the transformer / electrical-steel bottleneck to AI data-centre build-out. Summary. Links the transformer and grain-oriented-electrical-steel bottleneck to AI data-centre demand, arguing the shortage could delay the AI build-out by four-plus years. Page not retrievable this pass (403); noted from the title and thesis only. Data. Trade-analysis report — no primary dataset accessible this pass.
  • Bottlenecks Threaten Power Grid Upgrades · NS Energy Business Trade analysis of equipment and workforce bottlenecks slowing grid upgrades. Summary. Trade analysis of grid-equipment bottlenecks: cable procurement now 2–3 years and transformers up to 4, component costs sharply higher, and a grid workforce that needs at least ~1.5 million more workers by 2030 to keep pace. Data. Trade-press analysis citing IEA lead-time, cost and workforce figures — no primary dataset.

③ The network must double — grids as the weak link 11

The IEA framing: the world must roughly rebuild its grid by 2040, yet grid investment has barely moved.
  • Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transitions — Executive Summary · IEA (2023) The IEA's first global stocktake of electricity grids; source of the "80 million km by 2040" framing. Abstract. The IEA's first global stocktake of electricity grids (2023). Finds the world must add or refurbish ~80 million km of grid by 2040 — the length of today's entire network — while grid investment has stayed flat near USD 300 billion/yr; a "Grid Delay Case" adds ~58 Gt of CO₂ by 2050 and puts 1.5 °C out of reach. Data. IEA scenario modelling built on World Energy Outlook 2022 data, comparing grid-investment and deployment trajectories.
  • Building the Future Transmission Grid · IEA (2025) IEA supply-chain and lead-time survey of transmission-grid equipment. Abstract. IEA report (2025) on transmission-grid equipment supply chains, based on a 2024 survey of industry stakeholders. Documents procurement of 2–3 years for cables and up to 4 for large power transformers — roughly double 2021 — with cable prices nearly doubled and transformer prices up ~75% since 2019, plus capacity and skilled-labour constraints. Data. IEA 2024 survey of leading transmission-equipment manufacturers plus global investment and workforce analysis.
  • Building the Future Transmission Grid — Executive Summary · IEA (2025) Executive-summary companion restating the equipment lead-time and price findings. Summary. Executive-summary companion: reiterates 2–3-year cable and up-to-4-year transformer lead times (twice 2021 levels), price surges since 2019, and manufacturing-capacity and skilled-labour constraints across synchronised global grid expansion. Data. IEA 2024 industry survey — no primary dataset beyond the survey.
  • Rising component prices and supply-chain pressures are hindering transmission-grid infrastructure · IEA news IEA news release quantifying grid-component price rises and procurement delays. Summary. IEA news release: cable prices have nearly doubled and large-power-transformer prices risen ~75% since 2019, with procurement now 2–3 years for cables and up to 4 for large transformers — double the 2021 timeframe — hindering transmission build-out. Data. IEA industry-survey findings — press summary, no primary dataset.
  • Grids — Electricity 2026 · IEA (2026) The Grids chapter of the IEA's annual electricity report; latest queue and investment read. Summary. IEA Electricity 2026 grids chapter: over 2,500 GW of generation, load and storage projects sit stalled in grid queues worldwide, and annual grid investment must rise roughly 50% from ~USD 400 billion by 2030; flags grid-enhancing technologies to unlock existing capacity. Data. 2025 grid-queue data plus literature review of grid-capacity assessments and component pricing.
  • Lack of ambition risks making electricity grids the weak link in clean-energy transitions · IEA news Companion news release to the 2023 grids stocktake. Summary. Companion release to the 2023 grids report: warns grids could become the clean-energy weak link, needing 80 million km of lines by 2040 and grid investment doubling above USD 600 billion by 2030 — with ~60 Gt more CO₂ and 1,500 GW of renewables waiting if investment lags. Data. IEA scenario modelling — press summary, no primary dataset.
  • Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transitions (full report PDF) · OECD/IEA (2023) The full published text behind the executive summary. Summary. Full published text of the IEA's 2023 grids stocktake behind the executive summary — the 80-million-km, flat-investment and Grid-Delay-Case findings in full. PDF body not machine-parsed this pass; summarised from the report's own executive summary. Data. IEA scenario modelling on World Energy Outlook 2022 data.
  • IEA: 80 million kilometres of power lines needed for grid expansions · Power Technology Trade-press summary of the IEA's 80-million-km figure. Summary. Trade-press summary of the IEA's 2023 grids stocktake: 80 million km of power lines must be added or replaced by 2040 (the whole existing grid), with nearly 60 Gt of extra CO₂ between 2030 and 2050 if grid investment lags. Data. Trade-press summary of the IEA report — no primary dataset.
  • IEA report: Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transition · Green Grids Initiative Initiative write-up of the IEA grids report. Summary. Initiative write-up of the IEA grids report: 80 million km of grid by 2040, grid investment needing to near-double above USD 600 billion by 2030, under the banner "no transition without transmission." Data. Secondary summary of the IEA report — no primary dataset.
  • Electricity grids and secure energy transitions (IEA presentation) · IEA via UNESCAP IEA analyst slide deck of the grids findings, hosted by UNESCAP. Summary. IEA analyst slide deck (via UNESCAP) presenting the Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transitions findings — the 80-million-km, investment-gap and Grid-Delay-Case figures. PDF not retrievable this pass (403); noted from the source title only. Data. IEA presentation of the 2023 report — no primary dataset.
  • Building Future Grids: IEA report highlights need to overcome challenges · Global Transmission Report Trade-report coverage of the IEA "Building the Future Transmission Grid." Summary. Trade-report coverage of the IEA "Building the Future Transmission Grid," highlighting supply-chain and lead-time challenges for the clean-energy transition. Page not retrievable this pass (403); noted from the title only. Data. Secondary summary of the IEA report — no primary dataset.

④ Interconnection queues — where the grid gates generation 5

The queue is the grid's downstream gate: generation sits built-but-unconnected, waiting on network capacity.
  • Queued Up: Characteristics of Power Plants Seeking Transmission Interconnection · Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (2026) LBNL's annual US interconnection-queue dataset and report. Summary. LBNL dataset (2026 edition, data through 2025): ~2,060 GW of generation and storage actively seek US grid connection across ~8,200 projects; the median wait now exceeds five years, and only ~13% of 2000–2020 requests were ever built (75% withdrawn). Data. Interconnection-queue data from all seven ISOs/RTOs plus 50 utilities (~98% of US capacity), compiled with GridTracker, CC-BY-4.0.
  • Grid Connection Barriers To New-Build Power Plants In the United States · LBNL Energy Markets & Planning LBNL analysis of the drivers of US interconnection delay. Summary. LBNL analysis (peer-reviewed in Joule, 2024) of what drives US interconnection delay — study costs, queue congestion and network-upgrade cost assignment. Landing page not retrievable this pass (403); nature noted from the linked Joule citation (Gorman et al.). Data. LBNL interconnection-queue dataset behind the Queued Up series (Joule 2024, Gorman et al.).
  • The US interconnection queue is twice its installed capacity · Latitude Media Reporting on the scale of the US interconnection queue. Summary. Reports that the US interconnection queue holds roughly twice the country's installed generating capacity, citing LBNL queue data. Page not retrievable this pass (403); noted from the headline figure only. Data. Trade-press reporting on LBNL queue figures — no primary dataset.
  • Limited Grid Capacity Blocks 1,700 GW of Renewables in EU, 2,600 GW in U.S. · The Energy Mix Cross-region reporting on renewables stalled by grid capacity. Summary. Reports limited transmission capacity blocking ~1,700 GW of renewables in the EU and ~2,600 GW in the US; the EU figure comes from a report by 32 transmission operators across 28 countries, the US figure from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. Data. Secondary reporting of European transmission-operator data and LBNL queue figures.
  • Transmission grid connection processes improving, but still too slow and unpredictable · Advanced Energy United (2026) 2026 progress report on US interconnection reform under FERC Order 2023. Summary. 2026 progress report: US grid-connection processes are improving under FERC Order 2023 but remain slow — median queue time ~60 months (up from 20 in 2005), over 1 million MW of projects stalled, with billions in avoidable consumer costs in a single 2024 PJM auction. Data. Advanced Energy United analysis of interconnection queue times and FERC Order 2023 implementation.

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 1

The dataset the chain's own trade shares are built from.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's trade table. Summary. CEPII database reconciling bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Powers the chain's wire, conductor, transformer and switchgear trade shares. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
