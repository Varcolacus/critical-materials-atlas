Method · consumption over time

Watching the consumption matrix move

The same trade-independent estimate — demand = activity × intensity — run across time. Hold the calibrated intensities fixed and feed each year’s real activity, and two shifts appear that no production map shows: the consumption centre of gravity moving to China, and the energy-transition metals emerging from almost nothing.

Method — how much is real data. Every year 2000–2024. Six drivers are real annual data — electricity, population, nuclear, solar, wind (Ember/OWID) and fertilizer (FAOSTAT); the rest (steel, vehicles, EVs, cement, aluminium…) are interpolated between real benchmark years, because no open annual per-country series exists for them (steel now has 4 real anchors — 2000/2010/2022/2023 from worldsteel — and its level follows the real annual world-steel curve). So each material carries a “real” % (share of its demand driven by real-annual data): phosphate & phosphorus are 100% real; steel-driven metals are 0% (benchmark-interpolated). Levels are re-scaled to the real world-consumption trend, pinned so 2023 = the calibrated value. Read the movement as the finding; it does not model thrifting or substitution.

What moves, and why it matters

Two readings carry the page. China across the board: in 2000 the consumption leaders were spread across the industrialised world; by 2023 China leads almost every column, because its steel, power and construction activity outgrew everyone’s — the country that refines the world’s materials also came to use most of them. A new demand category: lithium, cobalt, graphite and rare-earth magnets barely register in 2000 (no EVs, little wind) and become major columns by 2023 — you are watching an entire material economy be born. Both are demand stories, and demand is exactly what trade and production maps leave out.
