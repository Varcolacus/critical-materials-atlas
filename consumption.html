Method · trade-independent consumption

Who consumes what — estimated without trade data

To check trade, you need a number that doesn’t come from trade. This estimates each country’s material consumption bottom-up: demand = national activity × material intensity — steel output, vehicles, EVs, electricity, solar & wind builds, cement, population — so it can be used to constrain the mirror trade instead of depending on it.

Estimate layer. Intensities are order-of-magnitude and end-use capture is partial for multi-use metals — so each column carries a capture ratio (bottom row) stating how much of world demand it accounts for. Nothing is inflated to fit; a low ratio is honest under-coverage.
How it’s built — and why it’s calibrated, not guessed. Each driver’s intensity is back-solved from a known world total: intensity = (world consumption × end-use share) ÷ world driver total. So every cell is a country’s real 2023 activity × that calibrated intensity, and the world sums can’t drift. Activity data: crude steel (worldsteel), vehicles (OICA), EV sales (IEA), electricity (Ember), solar & wind additions (IRENA), cement (USGS), population (UN). End-use splits from USGS MCS + EU CRM. Reproducible: build_consumption.py → consumption.json. This is the input to the production-constrained anchor.

 

Cells are tonnes of contained material consumed per year, shaded by intensity within each column. — = no activity in that material’s drivers (a genuine ~0 consumer). Click a header to sort. Bottom row = capture ratio (share of world demand the column accounts for).
