Research Library · Germanium

Germanium — the infrared metal, a by-product China controls

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Germanium lets you see in the dark and carries light down a fibre — yet no one mines it. It is recovered in trace amounts from zinc-refining residues and coal fly ash, China makes most of it, and in July 2023 China placed it under export licensing alongside gallium. The chokepoint is the recovery step, not a mine.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① By-product source (zinc residues / coal fly ash, ~60% China) → ② recovery & separation → ③ Ge / GeO₂ → infrared optics, optical-fibre cores, GaAs substrates → ④ the July-2023 export-control lever with gallium — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

② Recovery & separation — from coal ash and zinc residues 6

The chokepoint stage: getting germanium out of the host stream. Ample resources, under 3% recovered worldwide.
  • Germanium: A review of its US demand, uses, resources, chemistry, and separation technologies · Separation and Purification Technology (also at Penn State PURE) Comprehensive US-focused review of germanium demand, resources and separation. Abstract. Review of germanium's US demand, uses, resources, chemistry and separation technologies. World production is ~130 t/yr, mostly China, recovered from zinc refineries and coal fly ash. Resources are ample (7–13 kt in zinc ores; 25–112 kt in coal), yet under 3% is recovered worldwide — the constraint is inefficient extraction, not scarcity. Data. Literature review synthesising production, resource and separation-technology data; no new experimental dataset.

Abstract. Review of hydrometallurgical recovery of germanium from coal by-products — coal, coal gangue and combustion/gasification residues. Fly ashes are promising, with recovery exceeding 90% under optimal conditions, but low concentrations and high impurities demand selective separation. ④ The July-2023 export-control lever 7

Named with gallium, the same week: how the by-product concentration became a policy lever.
  • China's gallium and germanium export controls · CSIS (2023) Explainer on the July 2023 licensing measures and their strategic context. Abstract. CSIS analysis of China's July-2023 export licensing on gallium and germanium, effective 1 August 2023. China produces ~90% of gallium and ~60% of germanium; the measure is read as retaliation for Western chip controls. Exports collapsed — October 2023 germanium exports fell to 590 kg from 7,965 kg in July. (The cited CSIS page now returns 404; figures confirmed from CSIS's current gallium/germanium analyses.) Data. CSIS synthesis of MOFCOM announcements, USGS production data and Chinese customs figures.
  • Germanium and Gallium: U.S. Trade and Chinese Export Restrictions · US International Trade Commission (2024) Trade-flow and economic-impact briefing on the two minerals. Summary. USITC executive briefing (Mar 2024): US net import reliance for germanium exceeds 50% (China ~60% of imports); germanium reached $2,839/kg in December 2023, up 21% on the year; after the controls, materials route to the US via third countries such as Belgium. Data. USITC analysis of US trade statistics and USGS import-reliance data.
  • China's Germanium and Gallium Export Restrictions: Consequences for the United States · Stimson Center (2025) Downstream-impact assessment for US industry and defence. Summary. Stimson analysis: Chinese germanium/gallium exports to the US and Taiwan fell 97–100% between 2022 and 2024, yet material still arrives via third countries. A full germanium cut-off could cost ~$0.4 bn of US GDP; gallium ~$3.1 bn; both together ~$3.4 bn. Data. Trade-data analysis (US and Chinese customs) plus economic-impact modelling.

Gaps & good next searches

  • No dedicated germanium producer / industry association surfaced (analogous to a metals trade body) — try the Minor Metals Trade Association (MMTA) germanium page and infrared-optics industry bodies.
  • Optical-fibre-specific sourcing data (GeO₂ / GeCl₄ demand from fibre makers) is thin — search "germanium tetrachloride optical fibre demand forecast" for the fibre-optics cross-link.
  • Post-2023 price and export-volume trend data would sharpen the "recoverable but slow" claim — search "germanium price 2024 2025 export licence volume China customs".
  • Germanium has no clean HS6 code (unwrought germanium sits in 811292 with gallium and indium), so BACI cannot isolate it — the same limit the atlas flags for gallium and indium.
  • The downstream product stages (high-purity metal, GeO₂, IR lenses, fibre preforms, GaAs substrates) have no country-level production series; only recovery-chemistry papers and market notes turn up.
