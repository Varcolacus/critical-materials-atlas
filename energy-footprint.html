Supply structure · the energy footprint

The furnace concentrates at cheap power — now in numbers

The atlas calls the most stubborn refining chokepoints thermodynamic: a continuous, high-temperature process that can’t be switched off, so it must be one big unit sited where power is cheapest and never fails. That was a thesis. Here it is measured: the more energy a refining stage burns per tonne, the more concentrated it is — and the concentration comes with an enormous carbon bill.

Energy intensity vs concentration. Bars are electricity/energy per tonne of product (MWh/t); the right column is how concentrated the stage is (top producer’s share). Read top to bottom: as the energy bill falls, so does the concentration — until copper, low-energy and diffuse, the control case. Energy figures are approximate literature values (the extremes — magnesium ~78 MWh/t and ~25 t CO2/t, polysilicon ~40–70 kWh/kg — are web-verified); reproducible: build_energy_footprint.py → energy_footprint.json. Sources: IMA/LCA magnesium studies, Fraunhofer ISE polysilicon, IAI aluminium, worldsteel, USGS.

Bar = MWh per tonne · number in bar = t CO₂ per tonne · right = top producer’s share

Why this is the whole mechanism

A high-temperature furnace cannot be turned down at night or stopped for the weekend without wrecking it, so it runs flat out and its economics are dominated by one input: the price of power. That single fact explains the map. The world’s aluminium, magnesium, polysilicon and ferro-alloys did not concentrate in China because the ore is there — it mostly isn’t — but because the power was cheap and, for two decades, unpriced for carbon. So the chokepoint is really an energy chokepoint wearing a metal’s name. Two consequences follow, and they are the decision. (1) Diversifying it means building at cheap, reliable power elsewhere — not opening a mine, which is why an ore embargo can’t touch these stages and a power-price shock can. (2) Decarbonising and diversifying are the same project: the only way to move magnesium or polysilicon out of China’s coal grid and cut its ~25–36 t of CO2 per tonne is to build the furnace at cheap clean power — hydro, nuclear, firmed renewables.
