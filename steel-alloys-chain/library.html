Research Library · Steel & structural alloys

Steel / structural-alloys chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Iron ore and coking coal are everywhere, so steel itself is not a chokepoint. But the alloying metals that give steel its strength, hardness and corrosion resistance — niobium, vanadium, chromium, manganese, molybdenum — come from a handful of concentrated sources. The base metal is the least concentrated stage of all; a few hundred grams of the additive decide the grade.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① iron ore + coking coal (abundant base) → the alloying metals, most concentrated first — ② niobium (~93% Brazil) → ③ vanadium (~75% China) → ④ chromium (South Africa ore → China ferrochrome) → manganese & molybdenum → the microalloyed / stainless / tool-steel grades they gate — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

② Niobium — one mine for the world 2

The most geographically concentrated of all the alloying metals: ~93% Brazil, largely one company (CBMM).
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Niobium (Columbium) · USGS (2026) Annual niobium production/reserves/use data by country; Brazil's ~93% share of mine production. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: no significant US niobium mine output since 1959, so the US is 100% import-reliant (imports ~$525 million). Brazil supplies 104,000 t of ~112,000 t world mine production (~93%), Canada a distant second. Major end uses: steels ~77% and superalloys ~23%, consumed mostly as ferroniobium. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported import/export trade and price data.
  • Niobium Tech — CBMM products, applications and sustainability · CBMM The dominant Brazilian niobium producer's technical and sustainability publications. Summary. Producer site of CBMM, the Brazilian company that supplies most of the world's niobium from Araxá, serving 500+ customers in 50 countries. Publishes technical and sustainability content — steel alloy-design optimisation, battery niobium oxides — via its Niobium Hub. Data. No primary dataset — producer technical and sustainability publications.

③ Vanadium — coupled to Chinese steel 3

China dominates output, much of it recovered from steelmaking slag — so vanadium supply is tied to China's own steel industry.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Vanadium · USGS (2025) Annual vanadium production/reserves/trade data by country. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: over 90% of US vanadium use is metallurgical, mainly as an iron-and-steel alloying agent, the rest catalysts. China dominates world output (~70,000 t of ~100,000 t), with Russia and South Africa next; much vanadium is recovered as a by-product/co-product of steel slag, ore and spent catalysts. Data. USGS producer surveys plus reported trade, price and stock data.
  • The Use of Vanadium in High Strength Low Alloy Steels · Vanitec (Crowther & Li) Technical paper on vanadium's role in microalloyed (HSLA) steel. Abstract. Vanitec technical paper: reviews vanadium in high-strength low-alloy steels. Among the V/Nb/Ti microalloying elements, vanadium carbo-nitrides have the highest solubility, suiting medium/high-carbon steels — enabling precipitation strengthening in seismic reinforcing bar, forgings, rail and wire rod, often without costly quench-and-temper heat treatment. Data. Technical review of vanadium microalloying metallurgy — no supply/trade dataset.
  • Vanadium set for "disruptive" demand growth as battery storage booms · Vanitec Industry commentary on vanadium demand beyond steel (vanadium-flow grid batteries). Summary. Vanitec industry article: vanadium-redox-flow-battery deployments could reach ~32.8 GWh/yr by 2031 (41% CAGR), adding ~127,500–173,800 t of new vanadium demand a year — more than doubling current output — as grids need long-duration storage. Data. Industry commentary citing Guidehouse Insights and Eurometaux projections — no primary dataset.

④ Chromium — the ore is not the ferroalloy 2

South Africa mines the chromite; China makes the ferrochrome and the stainless steel — the chokepoint sits at processing, not the mine.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Chromium · USGS (2025) Annual chromium production/reserves/trade data; South Africa mine / China ferrochrome split. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: the US mines no chromite and is 77% net import-reliant; stainless- and heat-resisting-steel producers are the leading consumers of ferrochromium. South Africa dominates chromite-ore mining (~21,000 kt of world output, 96% of US ore imports), while China leads ferrochromium and stainless-steel production. Data. USGS producer/consumer surveys plus reported chromite, ferrochromium and metal trade and price data.
  • ICDA — market insights · International Chromium Development Association Industry body covering chromite / ferrochrome / stainless-steel market data. Summary. ICDA member hub: tracks global chromite resources (South Africa holds most, >12,000 Mt), chrome-ore production (South Africa ~25,000 kt/yr) and ferrochromium output by carbon grade (China leads, ~9,900 kt in 2025) — the data behind the SA-mine / China-ferrochrome split. Data. Member-submitted production and reserves statistics under an ICDA code of practice.

Manganese & molybdenum — the other ferroalloy additions 5

Manganese has no substitute in steelmaking; molybdenum makes the tool and heat-resisting grades. Diversified at the mine, concentrated downstream.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Manganese · USGS (2025) Annual manganese production/reserves/trade data by country. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: no US manganese-ore output since 1970, so the US is 100% net import-reliant; ore is consumed by five companies for ferromanganese, silicomanganese and non-metallurgical uses. World mine production is diversified — South Africa (~7,400 kt), Gabon (~4,600) and Australia (~2,800) lead. Manganese has no substitute in steelmaking. Data. USGS producer surveys plus reported ore/ferroalloy trade, price and stock data.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Molybdenum · USGS (2025) Annual molybdenum production/reserves/trade data by country. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: US mine output ~33,000 t (2024), from two Colorado primary mines and seven by-product operations. Molybdenum is a refractory alloying agent for cast iron, steel and superalloys, plus catalysts. China is the largest producer (~110,000 t), ahead of Chile (~38,000) and the US; much is recovered as a copper by-product. Data. USGS producer surveys plus reported concentrate/product trade and price data.
  • Manganese materials flow patterns · USGS Information Circular 9399 (Jones, 1994) Classic material-flow study of manganese from mine to steel. Abstract. USGS Information Circular 9399: a materials-flow study tracing manganese from ore through ferroalloys into steel and other end uses. Full text is a scanned 1994 document with no machine-readable layer — not retrievable this pass; summary from the title and USGS catalogue record. Data. Historical USGS materials-flow accounting (mine → ferroalloy → steel) — figures not extractable from the scan.
  • Manganese market research (public, monthly and annual reports) · International Manganese Institute (IMnI) Industry body's market-data and report hub. Summary. International Manganese Institute (Paris) market-research hub: member monthly/annual reports give regional and country-level analysis of manganese-ore and alloy markets — production, consumption, trade and inventories — across steel, ferroalloy, battery and electrolytic-manganese-metal uses. Data. Member-submitted market data; selected public reports free, detailed reports by subscription.
  • IMOA Annual Review and MolyReview · International Molybdenum Association Industry body's annual review, newsletter and Mo4Steel conference proceedings. Summary. International Molybdenum Association: a non-profit trade body publishing an Annual Review, the MolyReview newsletter and Mo4Steel symposium proceedings, documenting molybdenum's role in stainless steels, high-strength pipeline and automotive steels, and its markets. Data. No primary dataset — association review, newsletter and conference material.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 8

List methodologies, EU/US assessments and domestic-resource work that classify the whole chain's alloying inputs.
  • Draft critical mineral list — Summary of methodology and background information · USGS Open-File Report 2018-1021 Methodology behind the US critical-minerals screening that includes these alloying metals. Abstract. USGS OFR 2018-1021 (Fortier, Nassar, Lederer & Brainard): under Executive Order 13817, USGS and partner agencies applied multiple criteria to identify 35 critical minerals — including chromium, manganese, niobium and vanadium — establishing the screening methodology and background for the first US critical-minerals list. Data. USGS multi-criteria screening (production concentration, import reliance, uses) across candidate mineral groups.
  • Investigation of U.S. foreign reliance on critical minerals · USGS Open-File Report 2020-1127 Quantifies US import reliance by metal. Abstract. USGS OFR 2020-1127 (Nassar, Alonso & Brainard): documents growing US reliance on foreign mineral supply (1990–2019) and increasingly concentrated sourcing. Introduces indirect, embedded and foreign-ownership trade-reliance measures and a framework to evaluate strategies for reducing net import reliance and supply risk. Data. USGS analysis of net-import-reliance and trade data, 1990–2019, per mineral commodity.
  • Methodology and technical input for the 2021 review and revision of the U.S. Critical Minerals List · USGS Open-File Report 2021-1045 Updated criticality screening methodology. Abstract. USGS OFR 2021-1045 (Nassar & Fortier): pursuant to the Energy Act of 2020, documents the updated methodology for evaluating mineral criticality and the resulting revised draft US Critical Minerals List, refining the supply-risk and economic-importance screening applied to these alloying metals. Data. USGS criticality methodology (supply risk × economic vulnerability) over candidate commodities.
  • United States and Puerto Rico — mineral resource assessment (chromium and others) · USGS Open-File Report 2019-1023-D Domestic resource assessment covering chromium among other metals. Abstract. USGS OFR 2019-1023-D (Earth MRI phase 3): describes data sources and results for 13 critical minerals assessed across the conterminous US and Puerto Rico — including chromium, manganese and vanadium — mapping geologic framework data to identify potential domestic resources and reduce import reliance. Data. Earth MRI geologic, geochemical, topographic and geophysical mapping delineating mineral-system focus areas.
  • Domestic sources of critical minerals in Alaska · USGS Open-File Report 2019-1023-E Domestic-supply assessment relevant to diversification away from concentrated sources. Abstract. USGS OFR 2019-1023-E (Kreiner, Jones & Case): Earth MRI phase 3 for Alaska, defining geologic focus areas favourable for hosting chromium, manganese, vanadium and other critical minerals. Alaska focus areas together address 22 of the 35 minerals then deemed critical. Data. Mineral-systems mapping (geology, geochemistry, geophysics) delineating Alaskan focus areas.
  • Study on the Critical Raw Materials for the EU 2023 — Final Report · European Commission (2023) The EU's fifth CRM criticality assessment underlying its 2023 list. Abstract. European Commission's fifth Critical Raw Materials assessment (2023): screens candidate materials on Economic Importance and Supply Risk, corrected for substitution and recycling. Niobium, vanadium and the manganese/chromium ferroalloy inputs feature among CRMs, underpinning the Critical Raw Materials Act. Data. EU criticality methodology (Economic Importance × Supply Risk) using production, trade, value-added, substitution and recycling data.
  • Critical Raw Materials Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1252) · European Union (2024) Legal text of the EU's policy response to raw-material supply concentration. Summary. Regulation (EU) 2024/1252 (11 April 2024): sets 2030 capacity benchmarks — 10% domestic extraction, 40% processing and 25% recycling of strategic-material consumption — and a rule that no single third country supply more than 65% of any strategic raw material. Data. EU legislative text — policy benchmarks, not a dataset.
  • The security evaluation of nickel industrial and supply chains · Resources Policy (2025) Supply-chain security methodology directly transferable to ferroalloy metals. Summary. Resources Policy (2025) study by Sun, Yuan, Wang, Chen & Zha proposing a security-evaluation method for the nickel industrial and supply chain — a framework directly transferable to the ferroalloy metals. Abstract behind a publisher paywall (403) — not retrievable; noted from the citation. Data. Supply-chain security-evaluation methodology (metric not retrievable — paywalled).

Across the chain — trade & commodity data 2

The datasets the chain's own trade figures are built from.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's ferroalloy trade layer. Summary. CEPII database: BACI reconstructs bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products (1995–2024) from UN Comtrade, converting import CIF values to FOB and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies into a single reconciled flow. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • UN Comtrade Plus · United Nations Statistics Division Official international merchandise trade statistics platform underlying BACI. Summary. The UN Statistics Division's official platform for international merchandise trade statistics — by reporter, partner, commodity and year — the raw declarations from which BACI is reconstructed. Live page returned a script-only shell; described from the platform's published scope. Data. National customs import/export declarations compiled by the UN Statistics Division.
