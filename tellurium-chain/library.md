# Tellurium chain — research library

One of the rarest metals recovered industrially, tellurium comes almost entirely from copper-refinery anode slimes — a by-product of a by-product that nonetheless underwrites CdTe thin-film solar and bismuth-telluride thermoelectrics. A curated reference library for deeper study — public sources, gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source.

## Core references (already cited in the chain)
- [USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Tellurium](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-tellurium.pdf) — USGS (2026). Annual by-product recovery volumes, US import sources and events for tellurium.
- [First Solar / NREL — Cadmium-Telluride (CdTe) Solar Cells](https://www.nrel.gov/pv/cadmium-telluride-solar-cells.html) — NREL (2024). Technical overview of CdTe thin-film PV, the chain's largest end use.
- [BGS, World Mineral Statistics — tellurium](https://www2.bgs.ac.uk/mineralsuk/statistics/worldStatistics.html) — British Geological Survey (2024). World tellurium production data.
- [CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade](https://www.cepii.fr/CEPII/en/bdd_modele/bdd_modele_item.asp?id=37) — CEPII (2026). Bilateral trade database used for the chain's trade-flow layer.

## Government & agency reports
- [USGS National Minerals Information Center — Selenium and Tellurium Statistics and Information](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/selenium-and-tellurium-statistics-and-information) — USGS. Hub page indexing every selenium/tellurium Mineral Commodity Summary, Minerals Yearbook chapter and special publication (tellurium is tracked jointly with selenium as the other major copper-slime by-product).
- [USGS Circular 1365, "Byproduct Mineral Commodities Used for the Production of Photovoltaic Cells"](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/1365/) — USGS. A dedicated USGS study of tellurium (and other by-product metals) as PV inputs — directly on-topic for the chain's central thesis.
- [USGS Professional Paper 1802, "Critical Mineral Resources of the United States"](https://doi.org/10.3133/pp1802) — USGS. Chapter-length geological/economic profile of tellurium as a US critical mineral.
- [USGS Open-File Report 2003-018, "Mineral Commodity Profiles: Selenium"](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2003/of03-018/) — USGS (2003). Companion by-product-metal profile (selenium is recovered from the same copper anode slimes as tellurium).
- [USGS Circular 1196-T, "Selenium Recycling in the United States in 2004"](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/circ1196t/) — USGS. Recycling-flow study of the sister by-product metal, relevant methodology for tellurium recovery/recycling.
- [USGS Fact Sheet 2014-3077, "Tellurium — The Bright Future of Solar Energy"](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2014/3077/) — USGS (2014). USGS's own short framing of tellurium's role in solar energy.
- [USGS Scientific Investigations Report 2012-5188, "Metal Prices in the United States through 2010"](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2012/5188/) — USGS (2012). Long-run tellurium (and other minor-metal) price series.
- [USGS, U.S. Geological Survey Releases 2022 List of Critical Minerals (news release)](https://www.usgs.gov/news/national-news-release/us-geological-survey-releases-2022-list-critical-minerals) — USGS (2022). Confirms tellurium's inclusion on the US critical-minerals list, used in solar cells, thermoelectric devices and as an alloying additive.

## Academic papers & reviews
- ["Global Tellurium Supply Potential from Electrolytic Copper Refining"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921344922002774) — ScienceDirect (Resources, Conservation and Recycling-family journal, 2022). Quantifies how much tellurium could realistically be recovered from world copper-refining anode slimes — the direct empirical backbone of the chain's "ceiling" argument.
