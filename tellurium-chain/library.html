Tellurium chain — copper's rarest by-product

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Tellurium is recovered from the anode slimes left at the bottom of copper-refining tanks — a by-product of a by-product — then turned into cadmium telluride and bismuth telluride, then thin-film solar modules and solid-state coolers. At every step the supply is bounded by copper, not by demand. There is no tellurium mine to open.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Recovery from copper anode slimes → ② the sister by-product selenium (recovery & recycling method) → ③ CdTe thin-film solar & thermoelectrics — then the cross-cutting criticality frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Recovery — the copper-anode-slime by-product 3

An extreme by-product: >90% of tellurium is recovered from the slimes of electrolytic copper refining — a few hundred tonnes a year, bounded by copper.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Tellurium · USGS (2026) Annual by-product recovery volumes, refinery production by country, uses, prices and trade for tellurium. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: tellurium is recovered chiefly as a by-product of electrolytic copper refining, accumulating in anode slimes, with more than 90% coming from that route. China held ~80% of 2025 refined output; global end use was ~70% CdTe solar and 15% thermoelectrics. China's February 2025 export licensing pushed European prices up ~84%. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade and price data (Argus), and a world refinery-production/capacity table by country.
  • USGS National Minerals Information Center — Selenium and Tellurium Statistics and Information · USGS Hub page indexing every selenium/tellurium Mineral Commodity Summary and Minerals Yearbook chapter. Summary. USGS hub page indexing every selenium and tellurium Mineral Commodity Summary (1996–2026) and Minerals Yearbook chapter (1994–2023); the two are tracked jointly as the major by-products of electrolytic copper refining. Data. Index of USGS mineral-commodity statistics (MCS, Minerals Yearbook, special reports) in PDF/XLS — no new dataset.
  • Global tellurium supply potential from electrolytic copper refining · ScienceDirect (2022) Quantifies how much tellurium world copper-refining anode slimes could realistically supply. Summary. Peer-reviewed study estimating how much tellurium world electrolytic copper refining could realistically recover — the empirical ceiling on independent supply, and the direct backbone of the chain's "can't scale" finding. Full text 403/paywalled this pass — headline figures not extracted. Data. Material-flow / supply-potential estimate on world copper-refining volumes and tellurium recovery rates (Resources, Conservation & Recycling family, 2022).

② The sister by-product — selenium 2

Selenium is recovered from the same copper anode slimes as tellurium, so its commodity profile and recycling flows are transferable method for the tellurium stage.
  • USGS Open-File Report 2003-018 — Mineral Commodity Profiles: Selenium · USGS (2003) Companion by-product-metal profile: selenium recovered from copper (and minor lead) refining. Abstract. USGS mineral commodity profile of selenium — a chalcogen recovered, like tellurium, as a by-product of copper (and minor lead) refining. In 2000, 14 countries produced more than 1,800 t of refined selenium, with Japan, Canada, the US and Belgium making ~85%. Reviews uses, resources and secondary recovery. Data. USGS commodity profile citing world production, reserves and use statistics for selenium (2003).
  • USGS Circular 1196-T — Selenium Recycling in the United States in 2004 · USGS Material-flow recycling study of the sister copper-slime by-product. Abstract. USGS recycling-flow study of selenium, tellurium's sister copper-slime by-product. Most US selenium goes to dissipative uses (alloys, glass, pigments); of ~350 t reaching the 2004 market, ~300 t was dissipated and only ~4 t recovered from old scrap. No US selenium recycling facilities remained; scrap was exported. Data. USGS material-flow study of US selenium supply, use and recovery for 2004 (George & Wagner, Circular 1196-T).

③ CdTe thin-film solar & thermoelectrics 3

Where the trickle goes: cadmium telluride is the main non-silicon solar technology (~70% of use), bismuth telluride the standard thermoelectric.
  • First Solar / NREL — Cadmium-Telluride (CdTe) Solar Cells · NREL (2024) Technical overview of CdTe thin-film photovoltaics — the chain's largest end use. Summary. NREL technical overview of cadmium-telluride thin-film photovoltaics — tellurium's largest end use (~70% of consumption) and the main non-silicon solar technology, commercialised at scale by First Solar. NREL page did not resolve from this environment this pass — described from its title and role. Data. NREL research-program overview of CdTe cell technology (efficiency, thin absorber layers) — descriptive; no dataset extracted this pass.
  • USGS Circular 1365 — Byproduct Mineral Commodities Used for the Production of Photovoltaic Cells · USGS (2010) USGS study of the by-product metals (tellurium, indium, gallium, selenium) that feed thin-film PV. Abstract. USGS study (Bleiwas, 2010) of the by-product metals — tellurium, indium, gallium, selenium — that feed thin-film photovoltaic cells. Sets out their sources and supplies against fast-growing PV output (world ~7 GW in 2008, ~30% CAGR from 1990), examining whether by-product supply can support solar's expansion. Data. USGS Circular 1365 (10 p.): sources-and-supply analysis of by-product commodities used in PV cells, on production/capacity data.
  • USGS Fact Sheet 2014-3077 — Tellurium: The Bright Future of Solar Energy · USGS (2014) USGS short framing of tellurium's abundance, occurrence and role in solar energy. Summary. USGS fact sheet on tellurium's role in solar energy: one of Earth's rarest elements (~3 ppb, rarer than the rare earths and eight times scarcer than gold), recovered mainly from copper and used in CdTe photovoltaics. Data. USGS two-page fact sheet (Goldfarb, 2015) — descriptive summary of tellurium abundance, occurrence and solar use; no primary dataset.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 2

List methodologies and resource assessments that classify tellurium as a US critical mineral.
  • USGS Professional Paper 1802 — Critical Mineral Resources of the United States · USGS Multi-chapter reference on the geology, economics and future supply of US critical minerals, with a tellurium chapter. Summary. USGS Professional Paper 1802, "Critical Mineral Resources of the United States" — a multi-chapter reference on the geology, economics and future-supply prospects of US critical minerals, including a dedicated tellurium chapter on its occurrence and by-product supply. Data. USGS peer-reviewed professional paper; per-commodity chapters synthesising deposit geology, production and resource data.
  • U.S. Geological Survey Releases 2022 List of Critical Minerals · USGS (2022) News release confirming tellurium's place on the US critical-minerals list. Summary. USGS news release (Feb 2022) on the 2022 List of Critical Minerals — 50 commodities, up from 35 in 2018, assessed under Energy Act of 2020 criteria. Tellurium is listed, cited for use in solar cells, thermoelectric devices and as an alloying additive. Data. USGS methodology under the Energy Act of 2020: supply-chain vulnerability and manufacturing-function screening; multi-agency review and public comment.

Summary. USGS report presenting long-run US price histories (1970–2010) for a wide range of metals, including tellurium, with current- and constant-dollar series and notes on the events that moved each price.   • BGS — World Mineral Statistics (tellurium) · British Geological Survey (2024) Free database of world production (and some trade) by commodity and country, including tellurium. Summary. British Geological Survey's World Mineral Statistics — a free database of world production (and some trade) by commodity and country, including tellurium, used here as an independent cross-check on the USGS refinery figures. This BGS page returned 404 this pass — the minerals-statistics site has been restructured. Data. BGS compilation of national statistical-office and government production returns; multi-decade world mineral series.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's trade layer. Summary. CEPII's BACI reconstructs reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, from UN Comtrade — removing CIF costs and reliability-weighting reporter discrepancies. Tellurium falls inside the shared HS 280450 line and cannot be isolated. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF→FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).

Data. USGS annual mineral-commodity statistics series (production, imports, reserves, use), by-commodity chapters.

Gaps & good next searches

  • The academic base is genuinely thin — only the ScienceDirect supply-potential paper cleared as a peer-reviewed source, and its full text is paywalled. Next: a life-cycle / recycling study of CdTe module end-of-life tellurium recovery, and a dedicated criticality paper once access allows.
  • No producer or market-reporting source (5N Plus, Rio Tinto/Kennecott, Fastmarkets, Reuters, Mining.com) was retrievable this pass; tellurium has no trade association — production is captive to copper refiners. Next: "5N Plus tellurium producer", "Kennecott tellurium by-product recovery".
  • China's February 2025 tellurium export licensing is visible in the USGS MCS and the IEA Critical Minerals Outlook (already cited in the solar and strontium chains) but has no dedicated primary citation here — check MOFCOM / Reuters directly.
  • Tellurium shares HS 280450 with boron, so BACI cannot isolate it — no independent trade series exists. The BGS production series is the only country-level cross-check, and its page needs a current, working URL.
  • CdTe module end-of-life recycling recovery rates are under-covered; try "CdTe module recycling tellurium recovery rate First Solar" and "bismuth telluride thermoelectric cooler market".
