# Research notes

## Narrative correction

Hydrogen is an industrial molecule, not a production technology. Nearly all current hydrogen is still produced and consumed in established fossil-based industrial systems. Water electrolysis is one route to low-emissions hydrogen, while fossil production with sufficiently effective carbon capture is another.

Electrolysis itself is not one material recipe: alkaline systems centre on nickel; PEM systems expose scarce iridium plus platinum and titanium; solid-oxide systems use nickel and specialised zirconia/rare-earth ceramics; AEM is an emerging alternative.

## Interpretation rules

- Never equate total hydrogen demand with renewable or electrolytic hydrogen.
- Keep GW electrical-input capacity separate from tonnes of hydrogen.
- Preserve project status: announced, FID, under construction and operating are not interchangeable.
- Keep nameplate factory capacity separate from factory output and installed projects.
- Use the mineral histories only as economy-wide scale context.
- Do not call HS 854330 “electrolyser trade”; it includes electroplating and other electrolysis equipment.
