# Electrolyser / hydrogen chain — research library

Green-hydrogen electrolysis is still tiny (a few GW worldwide) but growing fast; its future chokepoint is not the metals people expect — it is iridium, needed by PEM stacks — and that chokepoint is optional, because alkaline electrolysers use nickel instead. A curated reference library for deeper study — public sources, gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source.

## Core references (already cited in the chain)
- [IEA, Global Hydrogen Review 2025 — Executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-hydrogen-review-2025/executive-summary) — IEA (2025). Annual state-of-play: installed capacity, low-emissions hydrogen share, project pipeline.
- [IEA, Global Hydrogen Review 2026 — Production](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-hydrogen-review-2026/production) — IEA (2026). Latest production figures and outlook.
- [IEA, The Role of Critical Minerals in Clean Energy Transitions](https://www.iea.org/reports/the-role-of-critical-minerals-in-clean-energy-transitions/mineral-requirements-for-clean-energy-transitions) — IEA (2021). Foundational mineral-demand-scenario report covering electrolyser materials.
- [IRENA, Green Hydrogen Cost Reduction](https://www.irena.org/publications/2020/Dec/Green-hydrogen-cost-reduction) — IRENA (2020). Cost-curve analysis underlying the "cost, not minerals, is the near-term limit" framing.
- [CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade](https://www.cepii.fr/CEPII/en/bdd_modele/bdd_modele_item.asp?id=37) — CEPII. The trade database behind the chain's flagged electrical-machinery context.

## Academic papers & reviews
- [Addressing the material gap in GW-scale PEM electrolyser deployment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921344925005646) — ScienceDirect (Resources, Conservation and Recycling). Quantifies the iridium "material gap" against GW-scale PEM deployment goals.
- [Is iridium demand a potential bottleneck in the realization of large-scale PEM water electrolysis?](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360319921016219) — International Journal of Hydrogen Energy / ScienceDirect. Early, widely-cited quantitative bottleneck study.
- [Critical Iridium Demands arising from future Expansion of Proton Exchange Membrane Electrolysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05357) — arXiv preprint (Sept 2025). Finds supply shortages could arise as early as 2030 and net-zero paths need ~30% of global iridium output.
- [Severe supply risk of iridium on hydrogen energy and its sustainable development strategy](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1383586625040262) — ScienceDirect. Supply-risk assessment plus mitigation strategy (recycling, loading reduction, alloying).
- [Methodological insights of defining material criticality by assessing different electrolysis and fuel cell stacks](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S266737892500015X) — ScienceDirect. Cross-technology criticality methodology for electrolyser/fuel-cell stacks.
- [Sustainable supply of critical materials for water electrolysers and fuel cells](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02621-6) — Communications Earth & Environment / Nature. Broader materials-sustainability assessment across electrolyser technologies.
- [Present and future cost of alkaline and PEM electrolyser stacks](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360319923022590) — International Journal of Hydrogen Energy / ScienceDirect. Cost-trajectory comparison underpinning the alkaline-vs-PEM economics.
