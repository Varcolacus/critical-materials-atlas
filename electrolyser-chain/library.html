Research Library · Electrolyser

Electrolyser / hydrogen chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Electrolysis splits water into hydrogen using either a PEM stack — which needs iridium and platinum — or an alkaline stack, which needs only nickel. Today the sector is a few gigawatts and the near-term limit is cost and demand, not metals; the feared iridium chokepoint is real but future-tense. And it is optional — chemistry, not the mine, decides it.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Catalyst materials — iridium & platinum (PEM) vs nickel (alkaline) → ② the escape hatch: alkaline chemistry, thrifting & recycling → ③ stacks, systems & manufacturing → ④ green hydrogen at scale — then the cross-cutting criticality frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Catalyst materials — the iridium bottleneck (PEM) 7

PEM's oxygen-evolution catalyst is iridium — among Earth's scarcest elements, a by-product of platinum mining. The literature that quantifies the future gap.
  • The Role of Critical Minerals in Clean Energy Transitions — Mineral requirements · IEA (2021) Foundational mineral-demand-scenario report covering electrolyser and other clean-energy materials. Summary. IEA scenario report: clean-energy mineral demand roughly doubles by 2040 under stated policies and quadruples under sustainable development, with technology choice — including electrolyser design — a major uncertainty. The demand-side reference for electrolyser materials. Data. IEA STEPS/SDS deployment scenarios × sub-technology market shares × mineral-intensity data, with 11 alternative technology cases plus a base case.
  • Addressing the material gap in GW-scale PEM electrolyser deployment · Resources, Conservation & Recycling Quantifies the iridium "material gap" against GW-scale PEM deployment goals. Abstract. Finds plausible iridium availability insufficient for projected PEM deployment even under optimistic catalyst-loading cuts: staying within supply demands an 89–97% reduction in iridium use by 2050, with loadings falling from 0.64 kg/MW to 0.07–0.02 kg/MW by 2030. Platinum is judged less pressing, aided by future secondary supply. Data. Scenario / material-flow analysis coupling PEM deployment scenarios with catalyst material-intensity and iridium/platinum secondary-supply assumptions.
  • Is iridium demand a potential bottleneck in the realization of large-scale PEM water electrolysis? · Int. J. Hydrogen Energy (2021) Early, widely-cited quantitative bottleneck study. Abstract. Assesses whether iridium — PEM's scarce oxygen-evolution catalyst — bottlenecks large-scale water electrolysis. Concludes it does: meeting future demand requires two preconditions together, a dramatic cut in iridium catalyst loading and a recycling infrastructure reaching end-of-life recycling rates of at least 90%. Data. Model combining iridium reserves and mine production, PEM technical loading-optimisation prospects, and a ~50-year market ramp-up model.
  • Critical Iridium Demands arising from future Expansion of Proton Exchange Membrane Electrolysis · arXiv preprint (2025) Finds supply shortages could arise as early as 2030 and net-zero paths need ~30% of global iridium output. Abstract. Estimates future iridium demand from expanding PEM electrolysis against a by-product supply of only ~7.5 t/yr from platinum mining. Meeting net-zero targets needs both major catalyst-efficiency gains and roughly 30% of annual global iridium output; shortages could arise as early as 2030, earlier than previously anticipated, with post-2040 needs underestimated. Data. Demand model across PEM deployment and catalyst-efficiency scenarios benchmarked against ~7.5 t/yr iridium by-product supply.
  • Severe supply risk of iridium on hydrogen energy and its sustainable development strategy · Separation and Purification Technology Supply-risk assessment plus mitigation strategy (recycling, loading reduction, alloying). Abstract. Warns PEM electrolysers could consume 32–40 tonnes of iridium by 2030 against global annual production near 7 tonnes — a severe supply–demand imbalance constraining the hydrogen industry. Proposes three complementary mitigations: substitution, loading reduction, and recycling, alongside a full supply–demand analysis. Data. Supply–demand analysis (iridium production versus projected PEM demand) plus a qualitative mitigation-strategy framework; no primary dataset.
  • Methodological insights of defining material criticality by assessing different electrolysis and fuel cell stacks · ScienceDirect Cross-technology criticality methodology for electrolyser/fuel-cell stacks. Abstract. Compares product-level material-criticality indicators using different electrolysis and fuel-cell stacks as the test case. Alkaline electrolysis scores lowest criticality in seven of nine indicators; iridium drives PEM criticality, nickel drives alkaline, and rare earths drive solid-oxide. Five of nine indicators converge on the same hotspots. Data. Nine criticality indicators applied to bill-of-materials / composition data for PEM, alkaline and solid-oxide stacks — a methodological comparison.
  • Sustainable supply of critical materials for water electrolysers and fuel cells · Communications Earth & Environment Broader materials-sustainability assessment across electrolyser technologies. Abstract. Assesses sustainable supply of eight critical materials (nickel, zirconium, platinum, iridium, titanium, lanthanum, yttrium, strontium) across three electrolyser and two fuel-cell types over six dimensions — supply-disruption risk, environmental and social impact, depletion pressure, circularity, substitutability. Alkaline's weak points are depletion and substitutability; PEM's is supply-disruption risk. Data. Six-dimension criticality review synthesising published data across five device types; no new primary dataset.

② The escape hatch — alkaline, thrifting & recycling 8

The chokepoint is optional. Alkaline chemistry avoids PGMs entirely; where PEM is used, thrifting and recycling can stretch the iridium — the counter-case, in the industry's own words.
  • Present and future cost of alkaline and PEM electrolyser stacks · Int. J. Hydrogen Energy (2023) Cost-trajectory comparison underpinning the alkaline-vs-PEM economics. Abstract. Techno-economic assessment of alkaline and PEM stack costs to 2030. Total stack cost falls from 242–388 €/kW (alkaline) and 384–1071 €/kW (PEM) in 2020 to 52–79 €/kW (alkaline) and 63–234 €/kW (PEM) by 2030 — the economics underlying the alkaline-versus-PEM choice. Data. Techno-economic analysis combining bottom-up (component/material) and top-down cost approaches.
  • Green hydrogen catalyst materials 2026: PEM & alkaline · Patsnap Technology-landscape overview of catalyst materials by electrolyser type. Summary. Technology-landscape blog framing green-hydrogen scaling as a catalyst-materials problem: PEM needs scarce PGMs (iridium ~7–8 t/yr, ~$4,700/oz) while alkaline/AEM use abundant nickel–iron / nickel–molybdenum (~$14/kg). Surveys five catalyst-innovation themes. Data. Commercial patent-analytics vendor commentary; patent-landscape framing plus generic price/production figures, no primary data.
  • Iridium availability — 60 Seconds in Platinum · World Platinum Investment Council PGM-industry view of iridium's by-product supply constraints. Summary. PGM-industry view arguing iridium supply suffices for PEM demand to the IEA's 2050 level (1,550 GW) despite ~250,000 oz/yr output, resting on three levers: substitution to other PGMs, thrifting (400→100→possibly 30 kg/GW), and recycling toward 99%. Data. World Platinum Investment Council research citing IEA capacity projections and the US clean-hydrogen roadmap; industry advocacy, no primary dataset.
  • Recycling and thrifting: the answer to the iridium question in electrolyser growth · Johnson Matthey (2022) PGM refiner/catalyst maker's perspective on iridium mitigation strategies. Summary. PGM refiner concedes the arithmetic — iridium output ~7–8 t/yr, and at ~400 kg/GW 2030 targets would need 30–40 t — but argues a ~75% loading cut brings needs to ~8–10 t, within supply, aided by existing closed-loop recycling. Data. Johnson Matthey expert commentary; loading-versus-supply estimation, no primary dataset.
  • Critical Materials in Electrolyzers and Fuel Cells (white paper) · JCDREAM / CHARGE consortium Industry-research white paper on PGM use in electrolysers and fuel cells. Abstract. US-focused white paper warning of a likely primary platinum/iridium shortage in the 2030s as clean hydrogen scales, naming iridium PEM's main bottleneck (US 100% import-reliant on iridium, 79% on platinum). Cites loadings of 0.45 kg Ir/MW and 0.26 kg Pt/MW and DOE thrifting targets; profiles graphite, strontium and yttrium too. Data. Synthesis of US DOE production/loading data, Hydrogen Council 2022 demand projections and Johnson Matthey prices; no new primary data.
  • Why iridium supply will not constrain PEM electrolyser growth · Mining Review Counter-take arguing the iridium bottleneck is manageable. Summary. Trade-press write-up of WPIC research arguing substitution, thrifting and recycling make iridium supply sufficient for PEM growth to 2050. Page returned HTTP 403 — not retrievable; noted from the visible search snippet, which adds a ruthenium catalyst enabling ~85% iridium saving. Data. Trade-press reporting relaying WPIC forecasts; no primary dataset.
  • Will rising platinum and iridium prices restrict the growth of PEM hydrogen electrolysers and fuel cells? · Hydrogen Insight Trade-press analysis of PGM price effects on PEM economics. Summary. Trade-press analysis of PGM price effects on PEM economics — platinum a tiny share of electrolyser cost, iridium about 8%. Page behind an NHST/Zephr paywall — not retrievable; noted from the visible snippet only. Data. Specialist hydrogen journalism drawing on WPIC/industry figures; no primary dataset.
  • Platinum group metals: an enabler for hydrogen, not a barrier · Hydrogen Tech World Industry-friendly counter-narrative to the scarcity framing. Summary. Industry counter-narrative (Johnson Matthey author): PGM scarcity incentivises efficiency and recycling rather than blocking hydrogen; ~80% of ~190 t/yr platinum comes from southern Africa, and aggressive thrifting-plus-recycling could let ~1.5 t/yr iridium support 1,000+ GW by 2050. Data. Johnson Matthey commentary with an illustrative modelling scenario; no independent primary dataset.

③ Stacks, systems & manufacturing 6

Where materials become electrolysers — and where the near-term constraint really sits: factory build-out and utilisation, not the metal.
  • Hydrogen Shot: Water Electrolysis Technology Assessment · US DOE (2024) Technology assessment of PEM, alkaline, AEM and solid-oxide electrolysers and their materials. Abstract. US DOE assessment (Dec 2024) concluding electrolysis can meet the Hydrogen Shot goal of $1/kg clean hydrogen, contingent on advances in three areas — manufacturing, technology efficiency, and integrated energy systems. Reviews the status of and cost-reduction pathways for PEM, alkaline, AEM and solid-oxide electrolysers. Data. DOE status-and-pathways review of electrolysis technologies against the $1/kg target; quantitative detail in the full 58-page report.
  • Electrolysers (Energy System tracking page) · IEA Technology tracking page for electrolyser deployment and manufacturing. Summary. IEA technology-tracking page (refreshed June 2026) charting low-emissions hydrogen production and electrolyser installed capacity, 2021–2026, with 2026 values estimated. The headline installed-capacity and manufacturing figures sit in the interactive charts and the full report. Data. IEA Global Hydrogen Review datasets; deployment and manufacturing-capacity tracking.
  • European Hydrogen Observatory — Electrolyser manufacturing capacity · Clean Hydrogen Partnership / EU Tracks European electrolyser manufacturing build-out. Summary. European Hydrogen Observatory dataset (May 2026 snapshot) of cumulative electrolyser manufacturing capacity for EU27, EFTA and UK facilities — current and planned to 2027, split by technology and by operational / under-construction / planned status, with a downloadable spreadsheet. Data. Public-source data verified with companies; EU27 + EFTA + UK scope, not global.
  • Water electrolysis and hydrogen — European Union 2025 status report · European Commission SETIS/JRC (2026) Technology development and trends status report for EU water electrolysis. Abstract. JRC Clean Energy Technology Observatory report (Jan 2026) assessing EU water-electrolysis technology, markets and competitiveness. Cumulative operational capacity by end-2025 is projected at 514–800 MW; EU manufacturing capacity ~8.9 GW/yr, with announced plans reaching 41.7 GW/yr by 2030. The EU leads in patents but faces state-backed competition and imported-materials dependence. Data. JRC CETO assessment; capacity projections from announced company plans plus JRC analysis.
  • Hydrogen Insights 2024 · Hydrogen Council / McKinsey (2024) Annual industry report on deployment, investment and manufacturing capacity. Summary. Hydrogen Council/McKinsey report: projects reaching FID rose from 102 (2020) to 434 (2024) and committed investment from ~$10bn to ~$75bn, but only ~12–18 Mt of the 48 Mtpa announced 2030 supply looks deployable amid regulatory and offtake headwinds. Data. Hydrogen Council & McKinsey Project & Investment Tracker (Dec 2020, May 2022, May 2024 snapshots).
  • Hydrogen Insights 2023 · Hydrogen Council / McKinsey (2023) Prior-year baseline for deployment and manufacturing-capacity trends. Summary. Prior-year Hydrogen Council/McKinsey baseline: over 1,000 project proposals and $320bn announced 2030 investment, but only $29bn past FID; announced 2030 clean-hydrogen supply 38 Mtpa versus under 1 Mtpa deployed — momentum outrunning investment decisions. Data. Hydrogen Council & McKinsey Project & Investment Tracker, as of 31 Jan 2023.

④ Green hydrogen at scale — production & demand 9

The sector state: a tiny built base, a shrinking 2030 pipeline, and demand — not minerals — as the binding near-term constraint.
  • Global Hydrogen Review 2025 — Executive summary · IEA (2025) Annual state-of-play: installed capacity, low-emissions hydrogen share, project pipeline. Summary. IEA flagship: 2024 hydrogen demand neared 100 Mt but low-emissions supply stayed under 1%; the 2030 announced pipeline fell to 37 from 49 Mtpa as projects slipped. China holds ~65% of installed electrolyser capacity and ~60% of manufacturing. Data. IEA Hydrogen Production Projects Database (Sept 2025) plus IEA demand tracking.
  • Global Hydrogen Review 2026 — Production · IEA (2026) Latest production figures and outlook. Summary. IEA update: low-emissions hydrogen reached ~1 Mt in 2025; installed electrolysis doubled to over 4 GW (Europe leading new build), while the 2030 pipeline shrank 10 Mt to 27 Mt and new FIDs fell below 0.8 Mtpa. Data. IEA hydrogen project/production tracking underlying the Global Hydrogen Review 2026.
  • Green Hydrogen Cost Reduction · IRENA (2020) Cost-curve analysis underlying the "cost, not minerals, is the near-term limit" framing. Abstract. IRENA report: green hydrogen still costs 2–3× blue, with electricity the largest cost and the electrolyser the second, so cheap power is a precondition. Larger standardised designs, GW-scale manufacturing (learning-by-doing) and optimised operation could cut electrolysis cost ~40% short-term and up to ~80% long-term, pushing green hydrogen below $2/kg within a decade. Data. IRENA electrolyser technology characterisation, 1-MW component cost breakdowns, 16–21% learning rates, and 1.5°C scenario pathways to 2050.
  • Global Hydrogen Review 2025 — Progress summary dashboard · IEA (2025) Dashboard tracking capacity additions and FIDs. Summary. IEA interactive tracker organising hydrogen progress into four areas — production and electrolysers, manufacturing capacity, policy strategies, and investment — with the headline values held in the downloadable figures rather than the page text. Data. IEA Global Hydrogen Review 2025 project, manufacturing, policy and investment tracking databases.
  • Global Hydrogen Review 2025 — Production prospects to 2030 · IEA (2025) Pipeline-to-2030 figures and their likelihood tiers. Summary. IEA finds only a fraction of the announced pipeline realistic by 2030: over 4 Mtpa "almost certain," +6 "strong" and +7 "moderate" potential, with over half low or uncertain; over half of announced projects have slipped their start dates. Data. IEA Hydrogen Production Projects Database (Sept 2025); likelihood tiering by status, size, location and end-use.
  • Global Hydrogen Review 2025 — Five key questions about hydrogen · IEA (2025) Framing piece on the sector's near-term uncertainties. Summary. IEA framing piece: the largest electrolyser grew 30 MW (2021) to 500 MW (2025); firm offtake under 2 Mtpa (~5% of potential 2030 supply) is the binding constraint; China holds nearly 60% of electrolyser manufacturing at 40–45% lower renewable-hydrogen cost. Data. IEA analysis of project status, offtake-agreement data 2021–2025 and comparative policy across seven markets.
  • Global Hydrogen Review 2025 (full report) · IEA (2025) Full report: China ~65% of installed capacity and ~60% of electrolyser manufacturing capacity. Summary. The full report underpins the headline that China holds ~65% of installed electrolyser capacity and ~60% of manufacturing. The 9.6 MB PDF returned as compressed binary — text not extractable this pass; the China shares are corroborated by the report's own web sections. Data. IEA Global Hydrogen Review 2025 full report (project, manufacturing and demand tracking).
  • The IEA's Global Hydrogen Review 2025: green hydrogen ramp-up is slower than we wanted · Green Hydrogen Organisation (2025) Sector-advocacy commentary on the IEA report. Summary. Green Hydrogen Organisation commentary accepting the IEA's slower-than-hoped ramp but stressing real electrolyser-led progress; it challenges the loose "low-emissions" label for blue hydrogen (asking ≤0.2% methane leakage, ≥95% capture) and calls for carbon pricing and stronger support. Data. Advocacy commentary interpreting the IEA Global Hydrogen Review 2025; no independent dataset.
  • Stalled European hydrogen demand leaves electrolyser factories underused · gasworld / EIC Reporting on demand (not materials) as the binding near-term constraint. Summary. Reports an EIC finding that weak demand leaves Europe's ~8.5 GW/yr electrolyser factories underused — only 3 of 31 GW targeted for 2030 is past FID — and cites the World Bank's 86% China share of alkaline capacity. Data. EIC industry report plus cited IEA (2024) and World Bank figures.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 3

Ranking methodologies and analyses that place the chain's inputs — iridium, platinum, nickel — against the wider material system.
  • Ranking critical materials for the energy transition · IRENA (2024) Ranks nickel, cobalt, iridium, platinum, copper and others by energy-transition criticality. Abstract. IRENA–NUPI brief building a renewable-energy-specific criticality ranking, arguing generic lists mix in medical, military and fossil uses. Ranks lithium, cobalt, gallium, rare earths and neodymium most critical, with PGMs and nickel high and platinum only moderately critical. Stresses criticality shifts over time and warns against micromanaging single materials. Data. Two-step method: a renewable-filtered meta-list plus a composite index over six indicators (listing trends, market HHI, abundance, 1.5°C demand, recycling, substitution).
  • Are There Enough Critical Minerals for Hydrogen Electrolyzers? · The Breakthrough Institute Think-tank assessment concluding materials are broadly sufficient if policy accelerates. Summary. Think-tank assessment: geological reserves are broadly adequate — the real constraints are market and geopolitical, not scarcity. Alkaline and solid-oxide materials suffice to 2050, but PEM's PGMs are the vulnerability, with iridium possibly needing 2× to over 10× cumulative mine production. Data. Analysis synthesising published reserve/production statistics and demand scenarios; no new primary dataset.
  • The material risk of green hydrogen · Ramboll Engineering-consultancy risk framing of electrolyser materials. Summary. Engineering-consultancy risk framing: electrolysers are highly exposed to critical-mineral price volatility yet developers rarely mitigate it. Exposure differs by technology (alkaline common nickel/zinc, PEM scarce platinum/iridium); it claims ~10% of alkaline CAPEX ties to critical minerals — the most exposed energy technology it surveyed. Data. Consultancy risk-advisory framing; qualitative analysis with one CAPEX-share figure, no primary dataset.
