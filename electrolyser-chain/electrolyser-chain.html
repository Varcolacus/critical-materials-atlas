Product-chain pilot · electrolysers and hydrogen

Hydrogen is not one technology—or one material recipe

The existing hydrogen economy is large, mature and overwhelmingly fossil-based. Water electrolysis is growing quickly from a small base, and alkaline, PEM and solid-oxide stacks shift exposure among nickel, iridium, platinum, titanium, zirconium and rare-earth ceramics.

hydrogen demand ≠ low-emissions supply ≠ electrolyser GW ≠ factory capacity ≠ announced projects

The correction. More than 100 Mt of hydrogen demand does not mean a 100 Mt market for renewable electrolysis. Low-emissions hydrogen production was still around 1 Mt in 2025, and that category includes routes other than electrolysis.

The stack design decides which material matters

PEM’s bottleneck is quality and scarcity, not bulk mass. The IEA reported an indicative platinum intensity near 0.3 kg/MW, but that number cannot be reused for iridium. Catalyst loadings change as technology improves.

The stack is not the whole plant. In the IEA comparison it represented only about 15–20% of installed investment; engineering, construction, water treatment, rectification, gas handling and local standards narrow equipment-price differences.
