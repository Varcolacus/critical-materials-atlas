Research Library · Aerospace

Aerospace / jet-engine superalloy chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

A jet engine turns nickel, cobalt, rhenium, hafnium and titanium into superalloys, then single-crystal turbine blades, then a finished engine — and at every step downstream the supply gets narrower. The mine is the least concentrated stage of all.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Rhenium (raw element) → ② Titanium (sponge to aerospace-qualified metal) → superalloys → ③ single-crystal blade casting → ④ jet engines — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Rhenium — the scarce superalloy element 3

The raw input, and a by-product no one mines for: ~55% Chile, recovered from copper–molybdenum.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Rhenium · USGS (2026) Annual world production/reserves/uses data for rhenium; ~81 t world mine output, ~55% Chile. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2025 US primary rhenium output rose 5% to 9,800 kg, recovered as a by-product of porphyry copper–molybdenum roasting in Arizona and Montana. Chief uses are superalloy turbine-engine components (80%) and petroleum-reforming catalysts (15%). Net import reliance 75%; imports from Chile, Canada, Germany, Kazakhstan and Poland. Data. USGS annual company/producer canvass plus reported trade and price data.
  • Assessment of supply interruption of rhenium, recycling, processing sources and technologies · ScienceDirect Supply-risk and recycling-pathway analysis specific to rhenium. Abstract. Rhenium — critical to aerospace superalloys and petrochemical catalysts — is a "by-product of a by-product" (from molybdenum, itself from copper), making resource estimates highly uncertain. The paper assesses secondary sources, examines historical cost and supply-interruption indicators, and discusses recovering and reusing existing stocks through industrial ecology to mitigate supply risk. Data. Assessment of secondary rhenium sources using historical price and supply-interruption indicators; industrial-ecology framing — no new experimental dataset.
  • Review of rhenium extraction and recycling technologies from primary and secondary resources · Minerals Engineering Reviews primary/secondary rhenium recovery routes and yields. Abstract. Review of rhenium extraction and recycling technologies from primary and secondary resources. Rhenium is scarce yet vital in high-temperature superalloys and platinum–rhenium catalysts; the paper surveys extraction and recycling routes from both source types, assessing their processes and challenges (Minerals Engineering, 2021). Data. Literature review of primary and secondary rhenium extraction and recycling technologies — no primary dataset.

② Titanium — from sponge to aerospace-qualified metal 12

Production (China ~71% of sponge) is not the same as aerospace-qualified supply (Japan / Kazakhstan / Russia).
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025 — Titanium and Titanium Dioxide · USGS (2025) Annual titanium-sponge production/reserves/trade data by country. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: US titanium sponge made by one Utah operation (data withheld); most titanium metal goes to aerospace. 2024 imported-sponge customs value about $450 million, up 7%. Sponge net import reliance exceeds 95% (Japan 82%, Kazakhstan, Saudi Arabia). TiO₂ pigment production separately valued near $3 billion. Data. USGS voluntary producer surveys (much withheld) plus reported import/export trade data.
  • Aerospace-approved Ti sponge supply up in 2024 · Argus Media (2025) Market reporting distinguishing bulk titanium-sponge tonnage from aerospace-qualified supply. Summary. News article (18 Feb 2025): aerospace-approved titanium-sponge output from Japan, Kazakhstan and Saudi Arabia rose to 89,000 t in 2024 from 84,000, at 83% capacity utilisation; uncertified Chinese sponge held steady at 220,000 t. Data. No primary dataset — producer and trade figures reported in market commentary.
  • U.S. Strategic Material Supply Chain Assessment: Titanium · U.S. Dept. of Commerce, BIS Government assessment of titanium supply-chain vulnerability for defense/aerospace end uses. Abstract. U.S. Dept. of Commerce BIS (Office of Technology Evaluation) industrial-base assessment of the domestic titanium supply chain (2016). Surveys manufacturers and distributors on products, sourcing, single/sole-source inputs, capacity, government and defense dependency, financial health, employment and R&D, mapping vulnerabilities and reliance on foreign inputs. Data. Original BIS OTE industrial-base survey of U.S. titanium manufacturers and distributors (respondent-reported products, sourcing, capacity, financials).
  • Vulnerability Analysis on the Titanium Supply Chain: A Network Approach · Northeastern University Network-based vulnerability analysis of the global titanium supply chain. Abstract. Master's thesis modelling the U.S. titanium supply chain as a network to assess vulnerability. Node-disruption analysis finds TIMET ingot/mill capacity critical, though diverse U.S. ingot capacity (e.g. ATI) permits rerouting; activating domestic sponge production reduces redundant ingot capacity, amplifying a TIMET disruption's impact on meeting demand. Data. Network model of the U.S. titanium supply chain (sponge/ingot/mill nodes and capacities — TIMET, ATI, ZTMC) with node-disruption simulation.
  • Life Cycle Assessment and Risk Management of Titanium for Aerospace/Defense Applications · Georgia Tech LCA and supply-risk study of titanium in aerospace/defense use. Abstract. Life-cycle assessment and supply-risk analysis of titanium for aerospace. Examines ore extraction, global distribution, mill-product capacity, feedstock prices and geopolitical vulnerabilities, finding China and Russia dominant; a national emergency could halt U.S. titanium production through sponge/scrap shortages. Proposes HSCR-steel substitution, domestic capacity and scrap use, and assesses technology and manufacturing readiness. Data. LCA plus supply-chain risk analysis on production-capacity, trade and price data (USGS/industry) and TRL/MRL evaluation of alternatives.
  • United Steelworkers — Titanium Sponge Section 232 petition (2018) · United Steelworkers Trade-policy petition documenting the collapse of U.S. domestic titanium-sponge production. Abstract. Section 232 petition by Titanium Metals Corporation (TIMET), Sep 2018, seeking relief from titanium-sponge imports threatening national security. TIMET's Henderson, Nevada plant is the last US sponge producer; without relief the US becomes 100% import-dependent on Japan, Kazakhstan, Russia, China and Ukraine, jeopardising the defence supply chain. Data. Petition citing DOC/ITC findings and dumping margins, plus industry production, consumption and world sponge-capacity data.
  • Closing the loop: EU's titanium supply chain · European Commission JRC (2025) Titanium scrap/recycling policy note; notes Kazakhstan as an EU titanium supplier. Summary. JRC update on EU titanium vulnerability: despite heavy consumption, import-to-export ratios reach 6:1 (products) and 10:1 (sponge), with Russia the top aviation-grade producer. Recommends reshoring midstream processing, recycling decommissioned-aircraft titanium, and partnerships with Kazakhstan and Ukraine. Data. JRC analysis citing titanium import-to-export ratios and producer concentration — no primary dataset.
  • Ukraine's titanium opportunity · MINING.com Geopolitical angle on titanium supply diversification away from Russia. Summary. Op-ed arguing Ukraine's deep Soviet-era titanium legacy (Kroll-process sponge, zirconium/hafnium metallurgy) positions it to help diversify a now highly concentrated global titanium industry disrupted by Russia's war, where China dominates sponge output. Data. Op-ed commentary (Yegor Perelygin) citing USGS figures (2024 global sponge capacity 410,000 t, output 320,000 t, China ~69%).
  • Global titanium supply squeeze: who controls the market? · AeroTime Market analysis of China/Russia grip on titanium and Western aerospace exposure. Summary. Reports China and Russia together control about 74% of global titanium-sponge production; surging aerospace demand risks supply tightening, with aerospace-grade certification taking up to a decade — limiting near-term Western diversification. Data. Trade-press reporting citing Project Blue market-intelligence data and interviews; no government/academic sources.
  • China's titanium dominance · The Oregon Group Analysis of China's vertically integrated titanium supply chain and its limits for aerospace qualification. Summary. Analyses China's vertically integrated titanium dominance (34% minerals, 67% sponge, 55% pigment capacity) while noting aerospace-qualification and feedstock constraints; frames Western opportunities via new projects and anti-dumping trade defences. Data. Market analysis citing JRC, USGS, Argus Media, a Stanford critical-minerals report and company figures.
  • China aerospace-grade titanium sponge exports set to rise · The Metalnomist (2026) Reports a Chinese producer's plan to scale qualified aerospace-grade sponge exports toward 2030. Summary. Reports Chinese aerospace-grade titanium-sponge exports projected to rise from ~1,000 t/yr to 10,000 t by 2030 as Western OEMs diversify from Russian supply and aircraft output grows. Data. Trade-press analysis citing producer projections (Chaoyang Jinda Titanium), capacity/pricing and trade-flow figures.
  • Is there a titanium shortage? Aerospace and defense demand outlook 2026 · AlloyIndex (2026) Market outlook on titanium demand/supply balance for aerospace and defense. Summary. Examines whether a titanium shortage is emerging, arguing concentrated aerospace-grade supply, long certification lead times and accelerating aircraft/defence demand could trigger structural tightness in high-grade titanium alloys. Data. Market commentary / analyst opinion; no primary dataset.

Superalloys — alloying, standards & recycling 3

Where the metal becomes the alloy: the qualification standards and recovery routes between metal and blade.
  • Resource recycling of superalloys and hydrometallurgical challenges · ResearchGate Pyro-/hydrometallurgical recovery of Ni, Co and Re from superalloy scrap. Abstract. Overview of processes to recycle superalloys — the high-melting, creep- and corrosion-resistant alloys used in aircraft-engine and power-plant turbines. Their toughness makes recycling among the most demanding, and rising alloyed-element counts add difficulty. Reviews each process's advantages and disadvantages, finding them not yet technologically or economically feasible, and urges value-added recycling over simple remelting. Data. Literature review of superalloy recycling processes (pyro-/hydrometallurgical) — commentary/synthesis, no primary dataset.
  • SAE International — Aerospace Material Standards (AMS) overview · SAE / Visure summary Explains the AMS specification system that qualifies nickel/cobalt superalloys for aerospace use. Summary. Explainer of SAE-issued Aerospace Material Standards (AMS): specifications defining composition, properties, manufacturing and quality of aerospace metals, composites, coatings and adhesives. Cites examples (AMS4911, AMS5599, AMS2460), compares to MIL-STD, and notes FAA/EASA/NASA compliance and testing methods. Data. No primary data — descriptive vendor commentary on a standards system, no statistics.
  • SAE AMS2774F — Heat Treatment, Wrought Nickel Alloy and Cobalt Alloy Parts · SAE International Governing specification for heat-treating nickel/cobalt superalloy aerospace parts. Scope. SAE aerospace material specification governing the heat treatment of wrought nickel- and cobalt-alloy parts. (Full standard is paywalled / served as a JS app shell — not retrievable; scope taken from the standard's title.) Data. SAE consensus engineering standard — a specification, not a dataset.

④ Jet engines & OEM concentration 3

The most concentrated stage: three Western groups build nearly every large engine.
  • Commercial aircraft engine market analyses · Mordor Intelligence (2024) Secondary market-size and OEM-share analysis for commercial jet engines. Summary. Market-research page: global commercial aircraft-engine market projected to grow from USD 95.56 bn (2025) to USD 117.13 bn (2031) at 3.45% CAGR; dominated by CFM, GE Aerospace, Safran, Rolls-Royce and Pratt & Whitney; turbofans and narrowbody lead. Data. Proprietary Mordor Intelligence market modelling and estimates — no public primary dataset.
  • GE, Rolls-Royce, Pratt & Whitney: who rules the engine market? · Simple Flying Secondary market analysis of commercial jet-engine OEM market shares. Summary. Describes the commercial aircraft-engine market as an oligopoly among GE, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-Royce, each specialising in different aircraft segments; reviews their key engine programmes and operational challenges. Data. Trade-press reporting citing Statista 2020 market-share figures (GE 55% incl. CFM, P&W 26%, Rolls-Royce 18%) and company examples.
  • Global Aircraft Engine Market report summary · GlobeNewswire (2022) Cites Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce, GE Aviation and Safran holding ~80% of the aircraft-engine market. Summary. Market-research press release cited for the ~80% combined aircraft-engine market share of Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce, GE Aviation and Safran. (Press release not retrievable on this pass — repeated timeouts; noted from the cited headline figure only.) Data. Market-research press release — no primary dataset.

Across the chain — criticality frameworks & policy 8

List methodologies, EU/US assessments and analysis that classify the whole chain's inputs.
  • About the 2025 List of Critical Minerals · USGS (2025) Explains the revised methodology (trade-disruption/GDP-loss modeling across 84 commodities) that newly qualified rhenium. Summary. USGS page: the final 2025 critical-minerals list holds 60 minerals (2022's 50 plus 10 additions), posted in the Federal Register 7 Nov 2025; built from an economic model grouping 84 commodities into supply chains across 1,200+ disruption scenarios. Data. USGS economic supply-disruption model on 84 commodities; production, trade and import-reliance data, 1,200+ scenarios.
  • Federal Register pre-publication notice, USGS 2025 critical-minerals rulemaking · U.S. Dept. of the Interior / USGS (2025) Official notice tied to rhenium's addition to the critical minerals list. Abstract. Federal Register notice presenting the final 2025 List of Critical Minerals — 60 minerals, revising the 2022 list of 50 — and an enhanced methodology quantifying the economic effects of foreign trade-disruption scenarios and single-domestic-producer failure points. Draft additions included potash, silicon, copper, silver, rhenium and lead. Data. USGS supply-risk methodology: trade-disruption economic modelling plus supply-chain single-point-of-failure analysis; public comment docket.
  • Critical Mineral Resources: National Policy and Critical Minerals List (R47982) · Congressional Research Service (2024) Legislative/policy background on the U.S. critical minerals list process. Summary. CRS report (Linda Rowan): reviews US minerals policy, the Energy Act of 2020 definition of a critical mineral, and USGS list development (2022: 50 minerals; 2025: 60) for congressional supply-chain considerations. Data. Secondary synthesis of USGS lists/methodology, statute, and production/import-reliance data; no new primary dataset.
  • Raw materials in the European defence industry · European Commission JRC/SETIS (2021) Assesses EU dependency on rhenium, hafnium, titanium and other materials for defence/aerospace. Abstract. Identifies the raw materials important to the European defence industry and their supply risk from import dependency. Finds 39 raw materials needed for advanced defence materials — about half fully import-reliant — and 47 critical alloys, compounds and composites whose special performance defies substitution, giving higher supply risk than their constituents; urges securing supply and keeping alloy-manufacturing leadership. Data. JRC synthesis of a prior contract study; inventory of 39 raw materials and 47 alloys/composites plus EU import-dependency data.
  • Study on the Critical Raw Materials for the EU 2023 · European Commission (2023) The EU's formal CRM criticality assessment underlying its 2023 list. Abstract. The EU's fifth technical criticality assessment (2023), feeding the Critical Raw Materials Act. Screens 70 candidate raw materials against Economic Importance and Supply Risk thresholds, adding neon, krypton, xenon, roundwood and titanium metal. Defines the CRM and Strategic Raw Materials lists underpinning EU supply-diversification and strategic-autonomy policy. Data. EU criticality methodology (Economic Importance × Supply Risk) over 70 candidate materials using production, trade, value-added, substitution and recycling data.
  • Critical Minerals and the Future of the U.S. Economy · CSIS (2025) Think-tank framing of critical-minerals supply-chain risk for strategic industries including aerospace/defense. Abstract. CSIS edited volume (Feb 2025, eds. Baskaran & Wood, 15 experts) arguing that securing mineral supply chains for advanced technologies and defence is an economic and national-security imperative. Examines lithium, graphite, tungsten and rare earths, proposing domestic development, processing/recycling, international partnerships and sustainable practices. Data. Policy synthesis of expert contributions; no primary dataset presented on the page.
  • CSIS Critical Minerals Security Program · CSIS Ongoing think-tank research hub on minerals security and diplomacy. Summary. Landing page for the CSIS Critical Minerals Security Program — an initiative to strengthen resilient US minerals supply chains via market-led solutions and allied partnerships, countering foreign-adversary (notably China) dominance. Produces analysis, reports, commentary, events and newsletters. Data. No primary data — program description and commentary.
  • Rhenium returns to critical mineral status · Metal Tech News (2025) Reporting on rhenium's re-addition to the U.S. critical minerals list under the 2025 methodology. Summary. Trade-press report on rhenium's addition to the 2025 US critical-minerals list. (Article behind a Cloudflare challenge — not fully retrievable; note from the headline only.) Data. Trade-press reporting — no primary dataset.

Gaps & good next searches

  • No public country-level production series exists for single-crystal blade casting (stage ③) — only patents and metallurgy papers turned up, no supply-chain-mapping study. Next: "directional solidification foundry capacity Precision Castparts Doncasters", or trade-press profiles of the major independent casters.

  • Engine-OEM market-share figures are all secondary/trade-press; a harder primary number would come from FAA/EASA type-certificate holder counts or company 10-K segment disclosures.
  • No IEA-specific source surfaced; try "IEA Advanced Materials for Clean Energy aerospace superalloys."
