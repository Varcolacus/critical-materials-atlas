# IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026 — full narrative

**Source:** International Energy Agency, *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026*, published 16 July 2026 (revised version July 2026). CC BY 4.0. `https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2026`

This file preserves the report's **narrative text and chart descriptions** section by section. All **numeric tables and shares** are in the companion data files:
- `annex-supply-demand.json` — the 12 annex supply/demand tables (exact tonnages by country/year/scenario)
- `concentration-and-controls.json` — export-control table, refined/mining shares, prose shares, nuclear fuel cycle, minor-minerals scatter
- `byproducts-and-costs.json` — by-product roots map, cost shares, value-at-risk, diversification economics

## What the IEA is
The International Energy Agency is an autonomous intergovernmental organisation (Paris, within the OECD framework), founded 1974 after the oil crisis. 32 member countries + accession/association countries (incl. China, India, Brazil, Indonesia, Ukraine). Originally oil-security focused; now the leading public authority on energy and, increasingly, critical minerals. Report directed by Tae-Yoon Kim with Tim Gould (Chief Energy Economist); data from S&P, Wood Mackenzie, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, USGS, worldsteel, IAI, and others.

# Executive summary — key findings

- **Concentration in refining edged higher again in 2025, rare earths the exception.** Over 2 years the top refining countries (Indonesia for nickel, China for others) accounted for over three-quarters of total growth in refined supply. For manganese, nickel and graphite, virtually all supply growth came from the dominant supplier. Rare earth refining declined modestly (US MP Materials + Malaysia Lynas). **Excluding rare earths, the average share of the top refining country rose to 72% in 2025, up from 70% in 2020/2023.**
- **Prices rebounded in 2025.** Base metals +~one-third Jan 2025–Apr 2026; copper hit record highs (>USD 14,000/t in May 2026). Lithium >doubled; cobalt +~130% (DRC export restrictions). Strategic minor minerals >doubled; **tungsten surged sixfold.** Export controls caused a sharp China-vs-ex-China price divergence — European gallium and heavy-rare-earth prices ~5× Chinese domestic; germanium ~3×.
- **Export controls turned concentration risk into reality.** China-controlled tariff codes tripled since 2023. April 2025: controls on 7 heavy rare earths → automakers halted lines. October 2025: expanded to "internationally made" products containing Chinese-sourced REs (suspended 1 year to Nov 2026) — full implementation could put ~USD 6.5 trillion/yr of downstream production outside China at risk. October 2025 also: battery-supply-chain controls (cathode, precursors, graphite anodes) — full graphite disruption ≈ >USD 300 bn/yr at risk.
- **Middle East conflict (started 28 Feb 2026)** — Strait of Hormuz closure hit aluminium, sulphur, helium. Middle East = ~8% of primary aluminium, ~one-quarter of sulphur supply; half of seaborne sulphur trade transits Hormuz. Disrupted sulphur → China curbed sulphuric-acid exports (May 2026); acid costs overtook energy costs for some minerals.
- **Investment fell 9% in 2025** (first substantial decline since 2020). Battery metals −>20% (lithium −~40%); copper +8%. Exploration −>10%. Public finance in advanced economies ~USD 65 bn in 2025 (>4× 2023).
- **Structural imbalance:** mining diversifying, but refining/downstream capacity lags. RE magnet pipeline outside China only ~one-third of expected mined supply; battery cathode capacity only ~one-third of lithium mining capacity.
- **Base-metal smelters under stress** — copper benchmark TC/RCs settled at USD 0/t in 2026 (record low); zinc/lead negative. Smelters increasingly reliant on by-product sales. Since 2005 China = >90% of copper-smelting growth, 15%→50% of capacity by 2025; ex-China utilisation <70% vs ~85% in China.
- **Strategic minor minerals** — small markets, extreme concentration, outsized impact. Highest-risk (IEA framework): gallium, magnet REs, yttrium, graphite, tungsten, tellurium, cobalt, germanium. Diversifying magnet REs would need ~USD 60 bn over a decade.
- **Recycling** could ~double its contribution: ~10% today → ~20% by 2040. Battery recycling: China >three-quarters of pre-treatment, ~90% of material recovery.
- **Nuclear:** top three countries hold ~three-quarters of uranium mining and ~70% of conversion & enrichment capacity.
- **Latin America** — >20% of tin/zinc, ~40% of copper mine output, ~one-quarter of lithium; but refines only ~one-fifth of mined output. Local refining could raise the region's value ~50% to ~USD 220 bn by 2035.
- **Mineral security premium** — the extra cost of diversification framed as economic insurance. Critical minerals are a small share of final product prices (a tripling of RE prices raises a car price by 0.1%), so much diversification cost can be absorbed downstream.

# Chapter 1 — Market review

## Prices & market trends (p.20-30)
- Categories (price-index chart, Jan 2023=100): base metals (Al, Cu, Pb, Sn, Zn); alloy metals (Cr, Mo, V); battery metals (Li, Ni, Co, graphite, high-purity manganese, purified phosphoric acid); rare earths (Nd, Pr, Dy, Tb); strategic minor minerals (Sb, Bi, Ga, Ge, In, Ta, Te, Ti, W). Strategic minor minerals index rose to ~280 by Apr 2026.
- Largest 2025-2026 price increases: tungsten (+620%), tantalum, cobalt, lithium, rare earths.
- Copper >USD 12,000/t Dec 2025, record >14,000 May 2026. Aluminium +>10% in 2025, +20% from Feb 2026 (Middle East). Tin +~40% (Myanmar disruptions). Lithium carbonate nearly doubled to ~USD 20,000/t LCE early 2026 (Zimbabwe export restrictions, Jiangxi permitting). Cobalt +~120%/130% (DRC quota). PPA +~50% (sulphur). Graphite subdued (persistent oversupply). Yttrium sharp gains.
- **Refining/mining concentration** — see `concentration-and-controls.json` (p.31 refined shares, p.32 mining shares). Text: avg top refined supplier 72% in 2025 (from 70% in 2020); lithium the largest increase; nickel via Indonesian industrial parks (Morowali, Weda Bay, Pomalaa); graphite virtually all growth from synthetic anode. China dominates by ownership too.
- **Reserves 2010-2025** (chart p.34): energy-mineral reserves +120% avg vs +7% base metals. Natural graphite +~300% (largest); lithium +~200% (Chile/Australia/Argentina); nickel Indonesia 5%→>40% of reserves by 2025; Australia 40%→~20%. Rare earths the only decline in reported reserves.
- **Smelter TC/RCs** (p.25, p.130-133): copper annual benchmark USD 0/t in 2026 (Antofagasta–Chinese smelters). Since 2005 China >90% of copper-smelting growth, 15%→50% capacity. Ex-China utilisation <70% vs ~85% China. China top smelters agreed >10% capacity cut in 2026; govt halted ~2 Mt planned capacity.

## Export controls (p.38-44)
- China tariff codes under control tripled Dec 2023→Apr 2025. Antimony oxide exports to Japan/Taiwan/US halted since Sept 2024. See export-control table in `concentration-and-controls.json`.
- April 2025: 7 heavy REs (incl. Dy, Tb, samarium). October 2025: +5 REs (incl. holmium) + "internationally made"/"parts, components, assemblies" clause (would apply from 1 Dec 2025); suspended 1 yr to Nov 2026. Jan 2026: tightened dual-use controls on Japan.
- October 2025: battery-supply-chain controls (LFP cathode, LFP batteries, graphite anode, precursors, equipment); suspended to Nov 2026. LFP = >half of EV battery market, >90% of storage market.
- Value of controlled exports >USD 11 bn in 2025.

## Middle East conflict (p.45-57)
- Started 28 Feb 2026; Strait of Hormuz disruption = largest oil supply disruption in history.
- **Aluminium:** Middle East 8% of primary (one-fifth ex-China). Alba (Bahrain, world's largest smelter outside China, 1.6 Mt) at 30% April 2026; Qatalum (0.65 Mt) at 60%; EGA Al Taweelah (high-purity Al for aerospace) hit/halted. Restart takes 6-12 months. Al prices ~USD 3,700/t (4-yr high). Green petroleum coke +~35%. China hit 45 Mt production cap in 2025.
- **Sulphur/sulphuric acid:** Middle East ~25% of sulphur, half of seaborne trade via Hormuz. China produces 40% of world sulphuric acid, ~one-third of demand; 55% of China's sulphur imports from Middle East. China capped acid exports Jan-Apr 2026, then halted May 2026 (affecting ~a quarter of ex-China acid needs). Acid is feedstock for Cu/Ni/Co leaching, RE processing, phosphate fertiliser. >15% of copper via SxEw (DRC ~45% of its Cu, Chile ~20%). Nickel HPAL needs ~9 t sulphur/t Ni; Indonesia 75% of its sulphur from Middle East. Lithium: >99% of hard-rock processing needs acid; acid rose from 3% to >11% of hard-rock lithium cash cost.
- **Helium:** Qatar ~35% of production (Ras Laffan halted), US ~45%. Prices spiked USD 15→~70/m³. Qatar >half of imports for China/India/Korea/Taiwan.
- **Strontium:** Iran produces >half of global supply (ferrite magnets). **Steel:** Iran ~11% of semi-finished steel trade; Khuzestan + Mobarakeh Steel hit. **Needle coke** (synthetic graphite feedstock) +>20%.

## Downstream trends (p.58-74)
- Renewables +16% in 2025 to 800 GW (23rd record year). Solar >three-quarters of additions, wind 20%. China ~370 GW solar + 117 GW wind. Solar PV +~12% to >600 GW; cumulative ~2,800 GW. c-Si ~98% of production; silicon intensity 3.2→~2 g/W (2020-2024); module ~400→~700 W; silver ~16→~10 mg/W (~5 mg/W in TOPCon/SHJ pilots); tin ~70→~53 mg/W; copper rising.
- EVs: >20M in 2025 (+20%), 25% of car sales. China >13M sold (>60% of global sales, ~55% of China sales electric). Europe +30% (>4M). US ~1.5M (slightly down, tax-credit end). China ~70% of EV *production* (~16M of ~22M). Battery demand >1.5 TWh (+35%); China ~60% of deployment; storage 330 GWh (+70%). LFP 55% of EV batteries; anode ~75% graphite/~25% Si-Gr. Pack price −8% to USD 108/kWh; minerals ~20% of pack cost. Battery supply chain: see `concentration-and-controls.json` p.73.

## Investment & policy (p.75-107)
- Capex −9% in 2025; battery metals −>20% (lithium specialists −~40%); copper +8%. Exploration −>10% (Cu steady; Li/Ni −~40%). China domestic geological exploration >USD 15 bn in 2024. China >35% of critical-mineral patents (IPFs) in 2024; two-thirds of battery-recycling patents. M&A rebounded (Rio Tinto–Arcadium USD 6.3 bn). VC ~USD 2 bn (KoBold ~one-third).
- Public finance ~USD 65 bn in 2025 (>4×2023). US OBBBA >USD 7 bn + USD 250 bn loan-guarantee authority; Canada USD 1.4 bn sovereign fund; India USD 3 bn mission; EU USD 3.4 bn RESourceEU; Australia AUD 1 bn reserve. Between 2018-2023 China poured >USD 98 bn upstream across ~20 countries; ~USD 120 bn outbound 2023-2025.
- Policy: US critical-minerals list 50→60 (added copper, uranium, lead); UK "growth minerals" tier; single-supplier thresholds (EU CRMA 65%, Korea 50% by 2030, UK <60% by 2035, G7 <60% RE by 2030). Quad Critical Minerals Initiative (up to USD 20 bn). US Project Vault reserve (up to USD 12 bn, RE emphasis). DRC cobalt: export ban Feb 2025 → quota (96,600 t) Oct 2025 → strategic reserve Apr 2026. Indonesia: RKAB quotas shortened Oct 2025; HPM ore price revised Apr 2026 (values cobalt content).
- Sustainability: reporting slowed in 2024 cycle. Women in workforce 19%→22% (2020-2024); rehabilitation ratio 18%→20%. GHG intensity stagnating; water intensity deteriorating. Copper Mark ~50→~100 sites (2023-2024).

# Chapter 2 — Outlook for key minerals

STEPS demand to 2040: lithium +>3.5× (14× vs 2020), graphite ~2×, nickel +65%, rare earths +~50%, copper +>25% (+~7 Mt). >USD 750 bn capex needed for mining+refining to 2040 (copper ~USD 310 bn, nickel ~USD 280 bn). N-1 gaps by 2035: nickel and graphite most exposed. Recycling ~10%→~20% by 2040. Refining concentration: top refiner avg 72% today → ~67% by 2035 (still high).

## Copper (p.124-137)
Prices record; ore grades −40% since 1991; only 5% of last-35-yr deposits found in last decade; 17-yr lead times. 2025 disruptions 1.5 Mt (Grasberg mudflow Sept 2025 → half of 2024 output; Kamoa-Kakula −one-third). Projected 2035 deficit ~25% (narrowed from 30%). China ~50% of refining (~47% in annex). China 15%→50% of smelting since 2005. Aluminium the main substitute (~60% conductivity). Secondary supply 18% in 2025.

## Lithium (p.138-146)
2025 demand >275 kt (+23%); China ~90% of demand growth. Chemical output +32%, mined +18%. Africa +44% of mining (now 14% of supply, 26× since 2020; >65% China-owned). **China refining share 70% (2024) → 75% (2025)**; falls to 66% by 2035. Argentina ~10% of chemical capacity by 2035. DLE ~10% of supply (mostly with ponds); recovery >90% vs 40-60% conventional.

## Nickel (p.147-154)
200 kt surplus 2025; prices subdued then +20% late-2025 on Indonesia policy. **Indonesia ~two-thirds mined (rising to ~75% by 2035), just under half refined (~45%→~50%).** China dominant nickel-sulphate producer (75% of production; Indonesia only 10%). Indonesia exports MHP/matte to China. RKAB quotas shortened; HPM revised. Two routes: RKEF (NPI/ferronickel, stainless) vs HPAL (MHP, battery-grade, acid-intensive). Batteries → 30% of nickel demand by 2035.

## Cobalt (p.155-162)
2025 mined ~320 kt; DRC export ban (Feb 2025) → quota (96,600 t, Oct 2025). CMOC produced 117 kt but sales −92%. Cobalt metal ~USD 58,000/t (3× prior). **DRC ~two-thirds of mined output** (production ~75% per USGS; IEA counts marketed/sold volumes cut by quota, ~66%); **Indonesia ~1.5%(2020)→~15%(2025)**. **China >75% of refining** (unchanged through projection). ~50% of cobalt to be nickel by-product by 2035. LFP + high-nickel chemistries cut cobalt per cell; NCM 622/532 persist in premium EVs. Superalloys ~7% of demand (aerospace, non-substitutable).

## Graphite (p.163-172)
2025 demand +6% (battery +14%); oversupplied, prices subdued. Synthetic +22%, natural spherical −3%. **China ~80% mined natural (from 84% in 2023, →53% by 2035); ~94% battery-grade refined (→91% by 2035).** Anode active material in China now almost entirely synthetic; natural spherical higher outside China. HF purification, spheronisation (yield loss), graphitisation (~3000°C, energy) and coating are the moats. Needle coke +>20% (oil). China demand two-thirds of graphite. Specialised graphite (isostatic, nuclear-grade 99.999%, C-C composites) critical for defence/aerospace/nuclear/semis.

## Rare earths (magnet) (p.174-185)
2010 controls (WTO-ruled non-compliant) then 2025 controls. NdPr oxide ~doubled to ~USD 125/kg (Feb 2026). MP Materials (US DoW partnership, USD 110/kg NdPr floor; USD 500M Apple deal), Lynas (US + Japan floor deals), USA Rare Earth (Serra Verde USD 2.8bn). **Mining outside dominant supplier ~50% of ex-China demand; refining ~25%; magnets <20% (2035).** **Refining China 90% (2024) → 85% (2025)** (US + Malaysia gaining) → 73% (2035 base) / 70% (high-prod). China ~60% of magnet-RE mining → 55%/45% by 2035. Magnet-RE mining top3 (China+Myanmar+US) ~85%. HREE content: EV magnets ~8.5-11% (latest designs ~2%). Alternatives: ferrite, AlNiCo, Niron iron-nitride (no RE). Japan RE demand ~30% below 2010 levels.

## Strategic minor minerals (p.186-212)
- **Manganese:** battery-grade sulphate China >95% (2025); mine South Africa ~40%, Gabon ~25%, Ghana 10%, Australia 8%. US ~20% of sulphate capacity by 2035. Deficit narrowed 45%→~20% (2035) on LFP.
- **PPA (purified phosphoric acid):** China 70% (2025) → 75% capacity (2035). Morocco 5%, US ~5%. LFP driver.
- **Phosphate rock:** Morocco 70% reserves, <15% mine; China 45% mine (5% reserves). Egypt export halt; Jordan disruption.
- **Tin:** mine China 24%, Indonesia 21%, Peru 11%; refining China ~50%; >half of consumption is solder; ~one-third recycled.
- **PGM:** platinum mine South Africa ~70%, Russia 12%; palladium Russia ~45%. Recycling +17% in 2025. Platinum ~doubled (record Jan 2026); rhodium USD 11,000/oz.
- **Silver:** by-product of Cu/Au/Pb/Zn; ~1.1 bn oz supply 2025; prices +150% (~USD 72/oz), >USD 100 Jan 2026; recycled ~200 Moz. Mexico largest producer. LAC >half of production.
- **Risk assessment (p.209-212):** dimensions = supply risk (refining/mining concentration, price volatility, export restriction, supply-demand balance), alternative supply routes (# suppliers, substitution, by-product dependence, price transparency), strategic importance. Highest-risk: gallium, magnet REs, yttrium, graphite, tungsten, germanium, tellurium, cobalt. "For gallium, graphite, manganese and rare earths, the top refiner China accounts for over 90%."
- Minor-minerals top-refiner scatter (p.199): see `concentration-and-controls.json`.

## Nuclear supply chains (p.214-231)
420 GW installed (→~800 GW by 2050); 78 GW under construction (30-yr high); China +35 GW since 2015. Uranium spot USD 60→>200/kg (2020-2024). Uranium demand ~70 ktU; enrichment ~49 M SWU; fabrication ~11.5 kt HM. Reactor types: LWR (PWR 62%, BWR 13%, VVER 14%, PHWR 7%). SMRs → ~40 GW by 2050; need LEU+/HALEU. **Concentration:** uranium mining top3 ~three-quarters (Kazakhstan/Canada/Namibia); ownership 6 companies 88%. Kazakhstan+Uzbekistan ~45% of mine (landlocked); ~half of uranium transits Russia/China (2015-2025). Conversion top3 ~70% (5 facilities: Rosatom/Cameco/Orano/ConverDyn/CNNC); ex-Russia/China covers only ~80% of needs. **Enrichment top3 ~70%; Russia ~45%, China ~14%, France ~11%; 4 companies (Urenco/Rosatom/Orano/CNNC) = 92%.** China 10.8 M SWU (domestic). US phasing out Russian supply by 2028. HALEU only Russia/China commercial; Centrus pilot <1 t. Fabrication top3 47%; 5 companies ~65%; VVER (14% of fleet) locked to Russia's TVEL. **Specialised nuclear minerals (Box 2.2):** zircon mining Australia+South Africa ~60%; **zirconium refining China ~38%**; niobium Brazil ~90% (one mine 80%); **indium China ~close to 70%** (refined); hafnium co-product of Zr, China leading; boron ~73% Türkiye.

# Chapter 3 — Pathways to resilient and diversified supply chains

## Emergency preparedness (p.233-258)
- Economic value at risk: rare earths USD 6.5 tn/yr (7% of ex-China GDP); graphite >USD 300 bn/yr (20× smaller). By region/sector: see `byproducts-and-costs.json`. Indirect impacts (data centres, AI, robotics) could be several times larger.
- Cost shares (p.234, 241-248): batteries — minerals 26% of cell, 3% of EV; rare earths 40% of magnet, <1% of car; copper 10-15% of grid gear. Tripling RE prices raises a car by 0.1%. → "mineral security premium" framing.
- Emergency response: supply-side (spare capacity, stockpile release), demand-side (allocation, efficiency, rationing). Market monitoring, protocols, tabletop exercises.
- **Stockpiling:** 11 high-risk materials net annual cost <USD 900 M (asset value USD 9.2 bn). Governance: government-held (direct/agency) vs industry-held (govt-owned/industry-owned) + PPP. Warehousing: lithium hydroxide degrades ~6 months; gallium melts ~30°C. Stockpiling operating-cost chart (p.254): graphite/CAM/rare earths highest.

## Policy & market frameworks (p.259-282)
- Diversified projects cost more: capex 20-150% higher (graphite plant costs >100% higher); opex ~50% higher (feedstock, energy, reagents). Cost of capital: EU/US metals-mining pay ~50% premium vs power.
- Supply-side tools: CAPEX (equity, grants, concessional loans, guarantees), OPEX (feedstock/energy support), risk mitigation (contract-for-difference, price cap-and-floor, offtake backstop). Demand-side: diversified sourcing obligations, demand aggregation, trade measures.
- For a rare-earth refining project: CAPEX/OPEX support most efficient per fiscal dollar; cap-and-floor +2.4 pp IRR, ~USD 18 M/yr fiscal; RE cap-and-floor to 2035 ~USD 3.7 bn (~USD 370 M/yr at USD 85,000/t floor).
- Policy instruments table (p.275): UK GBP 26.8M equity (Cornish Lithium); Australia AUD 1.65bn loan (Iluka Eneabba); US up to USD 250bn loan guarantee; Canada CAD 1.5bn grant; Japan NdPr offtake floor to 2038.

## Technology, equipment, workforce (p.283-303)
- The "built capability" moat is equipment + reagents + know-how, concentrated in China. Innovation patents concentrated in China (>35% of IPFs; two-thirds of battery-recycling).
- **Rare earths (p.287-289):** separation solvent-extraction equilibrium-data gap; China uses chloride (HCl/PVC) route, others nitric (HNO3/stainless, costlier). Metallisation electrolysis/vacuum-chamber energy efficiency held in China. Alloy strip casters, jet mills few outside China. Magnet: alignment pressers ~10× costlier; **grain-boundary-diffusion equipment single supplier outside China, ~12× costlier, highly patented.**
- **Lithium (p.291-293):** mining mature; bottleneck is process optimisation (heat integration, impurity control, crystallisation) + reagent cost (acid, soda ash) + battery-grade qualification (months-years).
- **Graphite (p.294-297):** strongest concentration in spherical/anode. HF purification (China largest fluorochemical industry); spheronisation yield know-how; graphitisation (~3000°C, electricity); carbon coating; qualification.
- **Gallium (p.298-301):** recovered from Bayer liquor (China largest alumina producer, "nearly all refined gallium in China"); ultra-high-purity (6N-8N) refining; GaAs (LEC/VGF crystal growth) and GaN (MOCVD epitaxy) — very few equipment suppliers (PVA TePla, Aixtron, Veeco); trimethylgallium concentrated; arsenic toxicity.

# Chapter 4 — Special focus: Latin America and the Caribbean (p.305-335)

- LAC holds significant reserves: niobium, rhenium, lithium, silver, copper, graphite, molybdenum, tin. Chile/Peru/Mexico ~40% of mined copper; molybdenum ~40% (Cu by-product); **Brazil >90% niobium; Chile >one-third rhenium; LAC >half of silver production**; lithium triangle (Argentina/Bolivia/Chile) ~one-third reserves, ~one-quarter production.
- **LAC refines little of what it mines: copper 26%, cobalt 10%, rare earths 7%, graphite 0%.** Economic complexity declined 2000-2023 (raw-commodity concentration). Local refining could raise value ~50% to ~USD 220 bn (2035).
- Chile/Peru export-dependence: critical minerals ~55%/40% of export revenue.
- **2035 base-case China refining (LAC chapter):** graphite 91%, lithium 66%, nickel 50%. Ex-dominant-supplier covers ~28% graphite / ~36% nickel / ~82% lithium of demand.
- Structural advantages: >60% renewable electricity (Brazil/Colombia hydro; Chile solar). Water stress a rising constraint (Chile copper water demand >650 M m³ by 2035; desalination shift; brine impacts).
- Technology: direct lithium extraction (Argentina — Fénix/Rio Tinto, Eramet Centenario, Rio Tinto Rincón); ionic-adsorption-clay REs (Brazil Serra Verde); hydrogen-based copper; AI exploration; bioleaching; anode composites (Brazil niobium/graphite/silicon). E-waste collection only 3% (vs 22% global).
- Country policies: Argentina RIGI (9 projects, USD 10 bn approved); Brazil National Mining Plan 2050, MagBras (RE magnets), Critical Minerals Bill; Chile National Lithium Strategy (preferential-price lithium), National Critical Minerals Strategy Jan 2026; Colombia priority Cu/Ni/phosphates/Mn; Mexico 2023 mining reform; Peru VAT refund; Venezuela new mining law Apr 2026. Escazú Agreement (participation/Indigenous FPIC).

# Annex — methodology & data
- **Scope:** models mineral demand for low-emissions power (solar, wind, CSP, hydro, geothermal, bioenergy, nuclear), grids, EVs, battery storage, hydrogen. Minerals covered: copper, Li, Ni, Co, Mn, graphite, REs, arsenic, boron, cadmium, chromium, gallium, germanium, hafnium, indium, iridium, lead, magnesium, molybdenum, niobium, PGM, selenium, silicon, silver, tantalum, tellurium, tin, titanium, tungsten, vanadium, zinc. **Steel and aluminium excluded from aggregate demand** (steel: no substantial security implications, energy not a major growth driver; aluminium assessed for grids only). ← *This is why the atlas uses worldsteel, not IEA, for its steel figure.*
- **Scenarios:** CPS (Current Policies), STEPS (Stated Policies), HDS (High Demand). From WEO 2025 + Global EV Outlook 2026.
- **Supply:** asset-by-asset project pipeline; base case (existing + high-chance) vs high-production case (advanced-stage seeking finance/permits). Primary supply requirement = demand net of secondary supply, adjusted for refining losses.
- **Key projection results** (supply/demand tables): see `annex-supply-demand.json`.
- **By-product roots map:** see `byproducts-and-costs.json`. Validates the atlas by-product taxonomy.
- Data providers: S&P Global, Wood Mackenzie, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, USGS, worldsteel, IAI, GWEC, OECD-NEA, WNA, IAEA.
