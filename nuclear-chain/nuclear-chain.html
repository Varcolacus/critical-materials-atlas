A uranium mine is not a nuclear fuel supply chain

Nuclear electricity depends on a sequence of specialised capabilities: mining and milling, conversion, enrichment where the design requires it, licensed fuel fabrication, reactor operation, and spent-fuel management. Control of one step does not imply control of the next.

uranium tonnes ≠ conversion capacity ≠ enrichment work ≠ fabricated fuel ≠ reactor GW ≠ electricity

The correction. “Uranium-rich” does not mean fuel-secure. In the 2023 Red Book evidence, Canada was the only uranium-producing country with nuclear generation whose mine output was sufficient to cover its own annual reactor requirements.

Reactor design changes the material map

SMR is a size and deployment category, not a fuel recipe. Some designs use familiar low-enriched uranium; some advanced designs require HALEU—uranium enriched above 5% and below 20% U-235. The two labels are not interchangeable.

Long material histories are context, not a nuclear bill of materials

World production histories, logarithmic scale

USGS economy-wide gross-weight series. Zircon concentrates are not nuclear-grade cladding; natural graphite is not nuclear-grade moderator; boron minerals cover all uses. Hafnium is omitted because the local workbook has no world-production series.

Customs records show transactions—not fuel security

BACI offers a consistent 2002–2024 monetary lens, but nuclear trade can be confidential and the headings are coarse. Enrichment services, stocks, domestic transfers, qualification and long-term contracts remain outside the picture.

Do not call this “nuclear supply-chain market share.” Natural and enriched-material headings have different contents; reactor shipments are lumpy projects; parts are broad; and zero or low recorded trade can reflect confidentiality rather than no flow.

The chain does not end at the reactor

once-through route

Store, then dispose

Spent fuel leaves the reactor, cools in storage and is managed toward eventual disposal without recovering fissile material.

recycling route

Store, reprocess, fabricate again

Selected material is recovered and recycled, for example in mixed-oxide fuel; high-level waste still requires management and disposal.

These are system choices, not two stages to add together. Countries can operate different combinations and timelines.
