Nuclear fuel chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

A reactor turns uranium ore into yellowcake, then UF₆, then enriched fuel licensed to one machine — and the binding steps are industrial services, not the rock. Conversion sits at five plants, enrichment is led by Russia, and fabrication is locked to a reactor design. The mine is the least fragile link of all.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Uranium mine & resources (Kazakhstan, Canada — diversified) → ② Conversion (five plants worldwide) → ③ Enrichment (a service; Russia the largest) → ④ Fuel fabrication & the reactor (licensed to one design) — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Uranium mine & resources 8

Kazakhstan ~40%, then Canada, Namibia, Australia, Niger — widely spread and cushioned by multi-year stockpiles.
  • Uranium 2024: Resources, Production and Demand · OECD NEA & IAEA (2025) The biennial "Red Book" — the definitive global survey of uranium resources, production and demand. Abstract. 30th edition of the NEA/IAEA "Red Book," the biennial global reference on uranium. Covering 2021–2022, it reports identified recoverable resources of 7,934,500 tonnes (as of 1 Jan 2023, at $40–260/kgU), a 4% production rise over 2020–2022, and installed-capacity and uranium-requirement projections to 2050. Data. Official government submissions from 62 uranium-producing and consuming countries.
  • Sufficient Uranium Resources Exist, However Investments Needed… · IAEA (2025) Press release framing the 2024 Red Book's headline findings on resource adequacy. Summary. IAEA press release on the 2024 Red Book: identified recoverable resources reach 7.9 million tonnes — sufficient for projected growth — but new exploration, improved processing and new production centres need investment, given long lead times and today's risk-averse markets. Data. NEA/IAEA Red Book official government data (2021–2022).
  • Analysis of Uranium Supply to 2050 · IAEA IAEA long-range supply-scenario modelling for uranium. Abstract. IAEA study projecting alternative uranium-supply scenarios to 2050 from then-current knowledge of resources and production facilities, assuming state-of-the-art, lowest-cost production. Assesses cumulative supply against demand, projected production cost, secondary supply and sensitivity to key assumptions. Data. IAEA supply-scenario modelling on a resource and production-facility inventory — no new field data.
  • Invest now to secure long-term uranium future, Red Book says · World Nuclear News (2025) Trade-press summary of the 2024 Red Book's investment warning. Summary. Reports the 2024 Red Book's warning that substantial investment in new mining projects is essential to meet demand to 2050; exploration and development spending rebounded to USD 840 million in 2023, but high-demand scenarios risk supply shortfalls without continued expansion. Data. World Nuclear News reporting on NEA/IAEA Red Book figures.
  • Kazatomprom 4Q25 Operations and Trading Update · Kazatomprom (2026) Primary production and trading data from the world's largest uranium miner. Summary. Operations and trading update from the world's largest uranium miner: 2025 production 25,839 tU (100% basis), 2026 guidance 27,500–29,000 tU and group sales 18,495 tU — with output still "subject to sulphuric-acid availability," the very input constraint behind the 2024 shortfall. Data. Kazatomprom 4Q25 Operations and Trading Update (Feb 2026) — primary company data.
  • Uranium prices could rally past 16-year highs as the world's largest producer runs short · CNBC (2024) Market reporting on Kazatomprom's 2024 supply shortfall. Summary. Market report that the world's largest yellowcake producer faced production issues over the following two years, as Kazatomprom's sulphuric-acid shortage constrained output and uranium spot prices pushed toward multi-year highs. Data. CNBC market reporting citing Kazatomprom guidance and analyst commentary.
  • Niger's Contested Uranium Sales Intensifies Procurement Risk… · Crux Investor Market analysis of the Niger coup's effect on Orano and European procurement. Summary. Analysis arguing Niger's nationalisation and contested uranium sales disrupt roughly 6–7% of world supply, raising procurement risk for European utilities dependent on 12,000–13,000 tonnes a year and strengthening producers in stable jurisdictions such as Canada, the US and Australia. Data. Crux Investor analysis of uranium supply concentration and market mechanics (Dec 2025).
  • Orano Halts Uranium Output At Niger's Arlit Mine Amid Financial Strain · allAfrica (2024) Reporting on Orano's October 2024 shutdown of the Somaïr operation. Summary. Reports that Orano suspended production at its Arlit (Somaïr) uranium mine in northern Niger amid the subsidiary's financial strain, after Niger–Benin border closures triggered by the July 2023 coup blocked all uranium exports. Data. allAfrica news reporting (Oct 2024).

② Conversion — turning yellowcake into UF₆ 2

Only five conversion plants operate worldwide — the first deliberately narrow neck between a diversified mine and a Russia-led enrichment stage.
  • Uranium — Deposits, Production and Resources, Market Dynamics, and Supply Chain Risks · USGS (2026) US government fact sheet on mining, conversion and enrichment supply-chain risk. Abstract. USGS fact sheet on uranium deposits, production, resources, market dynamics and supply-chain risk. Documents that only five conversion plants operate worldwide (Canada, China, France, Russia, US), Kazakhstan supplies ~39% of mine output, three commercial enrichers (Rosatom, Urenco, Orano) hold most non-domestic capacity, and fabrication runs in 12 countries. Data. USGS Fact Sheet 2025-3057 (April 2026), compiled from NEA/IAEA, WNA and US-agency sources.
  • Nuclear Fuel Cycle Overview · World Nuclear Association Front-end/back-end fuel-cycle reference. Summary. Industry reference mapping the fuel cycle — front-end mining and milling, conversion, enrichment and fabrication, then reactor operation and the back-end of storage, optional reprocessing and disposal — with typical material balances for a 1000 MWe reactor. Data. WNA reference (updated Sep 2025); illustrative material balances, no primary dataset.

③ Enrichment — the chokepoint 14

A licensed service measured in separative work, more concentrated than conversion — with Russia the single largest supplier, and HALEU a sub-chokepoint almost entirely Russian.
  • Uranium Enrichment Explained · US DOE (2024) Plain-language explainer of the enrichment step and why it is the chokepoint. Abstract. DOE explainer on enrichment — raising the uranium-235 fraction that sustains fission. The US uses gas-centrifuge technology at two facilities to make LEU (3–5%) for today's reactors and HALEU (5–19.75%) for advanced designs; Russia supplies roughly 44% of global enrichment, prompting a multi-billion-dollar domestic build-out. Data. US DOE Office of Nuclear Energy explainer.
  • High-Assay Low-Enriched Uranium: Drivers, Implications and Security of Supply · OECD NEA (2024) Why advanced/SMR reactors need HALEU and why supply is nearly all Russian today. Abstract. OECD NEA assessment of high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU, 5–20% U-235) required by many small modular and advanced reactors. Analyses demand drivers, supply-chain implications and security-of-supply risk, noting that commercial HALEU production in OECD countries is limited and today concentrated in Russia. Data. NEA evidence-based policy analysis; no primary dataset.
  • Uranium Enrichment · World Nuclear Association Technical and market reference on centrifuge enrichment and the Rosatom/Urenco/Orano/CNNC oligopoly. Abstract. WNA technical and market reference on centrifuge enrichment, measured in separative-work units (SWU). Reports 2022 capacity shares — Rosatom 27.1M SWU, Urenco 17.9M, CNNC 8.9M and Orano 7.5M of roughly 61,500k total — an oligopoly that has recently run in structural oversupply. Data. WNA Nuclear Fuel Report 2023 and company websites.
  • High-Assay Low-Enriched Uranium (HALEU) · World Nuclear Association Industry reference on the HALEU sub-chokepoint. Summary. Industry reference: HALEU (greater than 5% to less than 20% U-235) is needed by most advanced and small modular reactor designs; commercial supply is currently dominated by Russia's Tenex, with the US and UK investing heavily to build domestic capacity. Data. WNA reference (updated Aug 2026).
  • US DOE HALEU Consortium · US DOE The federal program coordinating domestic HALEU supply-chain buildout. Summary. The DOE HALEU Consortium coordinates the domestic HALEU supply chain — estimating demand, purchasing supplies, running demonstration projects and identifying gaps. Membership is free and open to US entities in the nuclear fuel cycle. Data. US DOE program page; no primary dataset.
  • Russian Uranium Ban Will Speed up Development of U.S. Nuclear Fuel Supply Chain · US DOE Official framing of the 2024 import ban and the domestic build-out response. Summary. DOE framing of the 2024 Russian-uranium import ban and the response: $2.72 billion in congressional funding for domestic enrichment, the first HALEU produced at Piketon, Ohio, and roughly $4.2 billion mobilised through allied partnerships. Data. US DOE article (May 2024).
  • Backgrounder on Uranium Import Ban · US Nuclear Regulatory Commission Regulator's summary of the Russian enriched-uranium import ban and waivers. Scope. NRC backgrounder summarising the Russian enriched-uranium import ban and its waiver process. (Page returned an edge-server block on this pass — not retrievable; scope taken from the title.) Data. US NRC fact sheet — a regulatory summary, not a dataset.
  • Prohibiting Imports of Uranium Products from the Russian Federation · US Department of State (2024) State Department statement on the ban's rationale and scope. Summary. State Department statement (14 May 2024) on prohibiting Russian uranium imports — framing the ban as securing a nuclear-fuel supply independent of adversarial influence, alongside expanded US enrichment and conversion capacity and the pledge to triple nuclear capacity by 2050. Data. US Department of State press statement.
  • U.S. Senate Passes Bill Banning Russian Uranium Imports · Covington & Burling (2024) Law-firm client note explaining the ban's mechanics and waiver process. Summary. Law-firm note on the Prohibiting Russian Uranium Imports Act (H.R. 1042): Senate-passed 30 April 2024, effective 90 days after enactment, waivable by the DOE through 2027, and permanent from 1 January 2028. Data. Covington & Burling client alert citing the statute and enrichment-capacity figures.
  • Enriched Uranium Fuels Russia's War Machine. But the U.S. Still Imports It · The Moscow Times (2025) Reporting on continued US reliance on Russian enrichment despite the ban. Summary. Reports that, despite the ban (permanent from 2028), the US kept importing Russian enriched uranium through waivers, with Rosatom's Tenex earning roughly $1 billion a year from continued sales. Data. The Moscow Times, citing trade-tracking data and company reports (Mar 2025).
  • DOE selects HALEU enrichment providers · World Nuclear News Coverage of the Louisiana Energy Services / Orano Federal Services / General Matter / American Centrifuge awards. Summary. DOE selected four firms — Louisiana Energy Services (Urenco), Orano Federal Services, General Matter and American Centrifuge (Centrus) — for HALEU enrichment services, each a minimum $2 million with up to $2.7 billion available over ten years. Data. World Nuclear News reporting (Oct 2024).
  • Urenco to add 2.1 million SWU of enrichment capacity at its New Mexico plant · American Nuclear Society (2026) Latest Western enrichment-capacity expansion. Summary. Urenco USA will add 2.1 million SWU of enrichment capacity at its Eunice, New Mexico plant via 24 new centrifuge cascades, with production beginning in 2032 and completion by 2036. Data. ANS / Nuclear Newswire reporting (June 2026).
  • HALEU · Orano Industry supplier's own HALEU program page. Summary. Orano's US HALEU program page: plans domestic enrichment for advanced reactors targeting HALEU by 2030, backed by DOE enrichment and deconversion contracts and a new centrifuge facility selected for Oak Ridge, Tennessee. Data. Orano USA program page; company statements.
  • Centrus Energy — February 2026 Investor Presentation · Centrus Energy Company-level HALEU production and SWU capacity figures for the sole US commercial enricher. Scope. Investor presentation from the sole US-owned commercial enricher, covering HALEU production at Piketon and separative-work capacity and backlog. (Static-file deck timed out repeatedly on this pass — not retrievable; scope taken from the title.) Data. Centrus Energy investor presentation (Feb 2026) — company figures.

④ Fabrication & the reactor 2

Fabricated fuel is engineered and licensed to one reactor design — the dependence embedded in the machine, not the fuel market.
  • PRIS — World trend in electrical production · IAEA (2026) Reactor fleet and nuclear-generation data. Summary. The IAEA Power Reactor Information System — the authoritative database of the world's nuclear reactor fleet and generation. This view tracks the long-run world trend in nuclear electrical production, the demand end of the fuel chain. Data. IAEA PRIS, compiled from member-state reporting.
  • Merits and Viability of Different Nuclear Fuel Cycles and Technology Options… · National Academies Press Peer-reviewed committee assessment of fuel-cycle technology options. Abstract. National Academies committee assessment of nuclear fuel-cycle technology options and the waste aspects of advanced reactors. Finds the US lacks domestic HALEU capacity for most advanced designs, and recommends continuing the once-through cycle while sustaining research into advanced reprocessing and transmutation as contingencies. Data. National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine consensus report.

Market reviews, EU criticality context and think-tank analysis that classify the whole chain — and explain why uranium sits outside the official CRM list.
  • Nuclear Fuel Revival: Uranium Markets, SMRs, and Global Energy Security · MDPI Commodities (2026) Review of uranium as a strategic resource amid SMR-driven demand growth. Scope. Peer-reviewed review of nuclear fuel's revival — uranium markets, SMR-driven demand and global energy security. (Abstract page returned a server block on this pass — not retrievable; scope taken from the title and journal listing.) Data. MDPI Commodities review article (2026).
  • Uranium at the nexus of energy security and sustainable development in a renuclearised world · Springer, BMC Environmental Science (2025) Demand trajectories vs. mining/conversion/enrichment/HALEU expansion capacity. Abstract. Integrated assessment of uranium demand and front-end fuel-cycle readiness in a "renuclearised" world. Projects annual reactor-related requirements rising from ~70,000 tU to 130,000–150,000 tU by 2040 — driven by new large reactors, life extensions and SMRs — and flags advanced-fuel and supply-chain bottlenecks. Data. Integrated demand-and-technology modelling on reactor and fuel-cycle data.
  • Critical raw materials, circular economy, sustainable development: EU policy reflections… · Journal of Cleaner Production (2024) EU CRM policy context relevant to why uranium sits outside the official CRM list. Scope. EU policy paper on critical raw materials, the circular economy and sustainable development — context for why uranium is not on the official EU CRM list. (Publisher page blocked retrieval on this pass — ScienceDirect 403; scope taken from the title.) Data. Journal of Cleaner Production article (2024).
  • RMIS — More on Critical Raw Materials · EU Joint Research Centre The EU's raw-materials data and foresight platform (uranium not listed as a CRM). Summary. The EU's Raw Materials Information System — the JRC platform of criticality assessments, foresight and dashboards covering the 34 critical raw materials identified in 2023. Notably, uranium is not on the EU CRM list, so this chain sits outside the official European frame. Data. EU triennial criticality assessments (Economic Importance × Supply Risk).
  • Fueling the Future: Recommendations for Strengthening U.S. Uranium Security · CSIS Policy recommendations on reducing Russia/China enrichment dependence. Summary. CSIS recommendations to strengthen US uranium security — accelerate domestic enrichment, build preferential trade ties with allies such as Canada and Australia, and secure offtake abroad — to cut dependence on Russia and China, which together hold over 62% of enrichment capacity. Data. CSIS analysis using government and industry supply-chain data.
  • The Geopolitics of Russia's Civil Nuclear Exports Four Years into the War · CSIS Analysis of Rosatom's export strategy and sanctions resilience. Summary. CSIS analysis of Rosatom's civil-nuclear exports four years into the war: about 20 reactor units under construction across seven countries; some states (Finland) exited while others (Hungary, Turkey, Bangladesh) continued, as Russia pivots toward Global South and CIS markets. Data. CSIS analysis (Jane Nakano, Mar 2026), citing industry and government reports.
  • Rosatom — the growing influence of Russia's nuclear industry · Oxford Institute for Energy Studies (2026) In-depth paper on Rosatom's vertically integrated reactor-financing-fuel model. Abstract. Think-tank paper on Rosatom, Russia's state nuclear holding. Details its vertically integrated model — subsidiary TENEX for enrichment, TVEL for VVER fuel — and overseas expansion via cheap finance and full fuel-cycle offerings, while questioning whether it can finance an ageing domestic fleet and its growing commitments. Data. Oxford Institute for Energy Studies, Energy Insight 182 (June 2026); IAEA PRIS and company disclosures.
