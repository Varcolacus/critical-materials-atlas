The chokepoint moves as silicon becomes a chip

China dominates basic silicon. The scarce electronic-grade purification capability is concentrated in the United States and Germany. Blank wafers are led by Japanese suppliers. Fabrication then splits: Taiwan in advanced logic, Korea in memory, and China in several mature categories.

Thirty years of fabrication geography

Commercial capacity for fabs using 200 mm and larger wafers moved from a US–Europe-centred system in 1990 to a much more East Asian system. The forecast does not show one universal winner: geography differs sharply by chip type.

The purification bottleneck

Very small, very demanding. Electronic-grade polysilicon requires at least 11N purity and represents only about 2.4% of polysilicon demand. The public record gives 38.8 kt produced in 2023 and 33.5 kt forecast demand in 2025; these are different measures and are not drawn as one continuous series.
Concentrated capability. Germany and the United States account for about 65%; China is estimated below 10%. Wacker and Hemlock alone account for roughly three-quarters of the supplier market.

What the customs ledger says—and cannot say

BACI still adds value as a global trade layer from 2002 onward. It tells us who ships each broad category. It cannot identify the end use inside the two downstream codes.

Do not bridge the layers arithmetically. A 65% production estimate for electronic-grade polysilicon and a 13% export-value share for HS 280461 do not measure the same product universe. Their difference is a warning about customs aggregation, not a calculated “origin gap.”
