Semiconductor-inputs chain — the chemicals beneath the fab

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

A chip fab is famous for its machines, but it runs on a stream of ultra-pure consumable chemicals: the photoresist that patterns every layer, the process gases that etch and deposit, the slurries that polish wafers flat, the pellicles that protect masks. Advanced photoresist is roughly 90% Japanese — a consumable can halt a fab full of the world's most advanced machines.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Photoresist (~90% Japan — the sharpest input) → ② process gases, CMP slurries & EUV pellicles → ③ the fab & supply-chain assessments — then the cross-cutting frameworks, policy and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Photoresist — the sharpest chokepoint 4

The light-sensitive polymer that records every circuit layer — advanced grades (KrF, ArF, EUV) are ~90% made by a few Japanese firms.
  • SEMI — Semiconductor Materials Market · SEMI (2024) Industry market-data service tracking the wafer-fab and packaging materials markets. Summary. SEMI market-data product covering the semiconductor materials markets — photoresist and ancillaries, process/specialty gases, CMP slurries, wet chemicals, sputtering targets and packaging materials — by annual revenue and region. The advanced-photoresist concentration in these data underpins the chain's "~90% Japan" input picture. Data. SEMI member surveys and materials-market statistics program (annual revenue by material segment and region); the detailed figures sit behind SEMI's paid service — the public page is a product listing.
  • CSIS — Japan's photoresist and the 2019 Japan–Korea dispute · CSIS (2019) Analysis of Japan's July 2019 export curbs on photoresist and two fab chemicals to Korea. Summary. In July 2019 Japan imposed export-licensing controls on three chemicals central to Korean chip and display making — photoresist, hydrogen fluoride and fluorinated polyimide — where Japanese producers held the dominant share, briefly threatening Samsung and SK Hynix and prompting a Korean drive to localise. (The cited CSIS page returned 404 on this pass; the episode is corroborated by the Yoshioka / Kyushu paper below.) Data. CSIS analysis (page not retrievable this pass) — no primary dataset; event corroborated by Yoshioka (Kyushu, 2022).
  • Partners in Chips: Pathways for Korea–Japan Cooperation in Semiconductors · SSRN (2026) Working paper on Korea–Japan semiconductor cooperation and the fab-chemical dependency. Summary. SSRN paper (Choi, Kyung & Kim) on pathways for Korea–Japan semiconductor cooperation, directly addressing the photoresist and fab-chemical dependency exposed by the 2019 export controls. (SSRN abstract page returned 403 — not retrievable this pass; listed from its title and authors only, not summarised from the text.) Data. Not retrievable — SSRN page blocked (403); no content read.
  • Changes in Relations between Japan and Korea in the IT Industry · Kyushu University (2022) Study of the Japan–Korea division of labour in semiconductor components, materials and equipment. Abstract. Yoshioka (Kumamoto/Kyushu repository) traces the shifting Japan–Korea relationship in the IT industry — semiconductor and flat-panel components, materials and manufacturing equipment. Korea long depended on Japanese materials and equipment before partially localising (equipment exports HS8486 ~$8.6 bn in 2018); this dependence is the context in which Japan's 2019 export-control measures landed. Data. Kyushu University repository article using bilateral HS-level trade statistics (e.g. HS8486 equipment) and industry analysis.

② Process gases, CMP slurries & EUV pellicles 2

The rest of the chemical stack — each a niche of a few qualified suppliers, and the thinnest evidence base in the chain.
  • The State of Clean Technology Manufacturing · IEA (2023) IEA briefing on manufacturing-capacity concentration across clean-energy technologies. Summary. IEA briefing for the 2023 G7 on how manufacturing capacity for clean-energy technologies (solar PV, wind, batteries, electrolysers, heat pumps) is expanding but geographically concentrated. Not semiconductor-specific — used only as a method analogue for how a few countries can dominate a manufacturing stage. Data. IEA manufacturing-capacity dataset by technology and country/region — no fab-input figures.
  • European Commission — Critical Raw Materials · European Commission (2023) The EU criticality framework and 2023 CRM list, covering several fab-relevant inputs. Summary. The European Commission's criticality assessment and 2023 list — 34 critical raw materials screened from 70 candidates on Economic Importance × Supply Risk. It covers several semiconductor-relevant inputs: silicon metal, gallium, germanium, hafnium and the lithography gases neon, krypton and xenon. Data. Study on Critical Raw Materials for the EU 2023 (Economic Importance × Supply Risk over 70 candidate materials; production, trade, substitution data).
No dedicated public, supplier-level source maps CMP slurries or EUV pellicles: a targeted search returned only industry pages and product listings, no supply-chain study and no production series to cite. That absence is itself the finding — the concentration in these inputs is a qualified-supplier fact, not a customs one. See Gaps & good next searches for the market/technology reviews worth chasing (Cabot / Fujimi for slurry, Mitsui / ASML for pellicles).

③ The fab & supply-chain assessments 3

Government and think-tank assessments of the fab and where its material inputs concentrate.
  • Building Resilient Supply Chains — 100-day review (semiconductors) · Executive Office of the President (2021) The US 2021 100-day supply-chain review's semiconductor volume. Abstract. The semiconductor section of the US 2021 100-day supply-chain review. Finds US fab capacity fell from ~37% to ~12% of global output over two decades; examines the chain in five segments (design, fabrication, ATP/packaging, materials, equipment) and notes that while many gases and wet chemicals are US-made, foreign suppliers dominate silicon wafers, photomasks and photoresists. Recommends CHIPS-style funding. Data. Interagency review (Dept. of Commerce) drawing on BCG/SIA analysis and industry consultation. (Original whitehouse.gov URL 404s post-administration; linked via the Biden White House archive.)
  • Bureau of Industry and Security — semiconductor supply-chain assessments · U.S. Dept. of Commerce, BIS The agency administering US export controls and supply-chain risk assessment for semiconductors. Summary. The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security administers US export controls on advanced-computing semiconductors and is running a Section 232 national-security investigation of semiconductor imports — the government body behind supply-chain and materials risk assessments for the sector. Data. BIS regulatory / enforcement portal and the Section 232 semiconductor investigation — no single dataset.
  • CSET — Center for Security and Emerging Technology (Georgetown) · CSET / Georgetown Policy research group publishing detailed semiconductor supply-chain analyses. Summary. Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology is a data-driven policy research group; its Supply Chains programme publishes detailed analyses of the semiconductor supply chain, including the materials and chemical chokepoints upstream of the fab. Data. CSET publications library — analysis and synthesis, no single primary dataset.

Across the chain — frameworks, policy & risk 3

Analyses and risk frameworks that classify the whole chain's inputs and the upstream leverage they carry.
  • Effective Availability in Global Semiconductor Trade · Yu & Yu, Modern Economy (2025) Trade-based assessment of US semiconductor supply-chain risk and policy response. Abstract. Develops an "effective availability" framework for US dependence on imported semiconductor inputs, combining supplier diversification, tariff-equivalent restrictions and geopolitical risk. Finds US reliance on East Asia significant but diversifying (Japan, Korea, Singapore, Malaysia) after the CHIPS Act; concludes resilience comes from diversified, trade-embedded supply chains, not self-sufficiency. Data. Trade data, tariff measures and industry reports (Modern Economy, 16(11), 2025) — analytical framework, no new experimental dataset.
  • Competing at the upstream of innovation: the US–China balance in critical minerals · Naval Postgraduate School (2025) Upstream materials/minerals leverage in the US–China technology competition. Summary. Picarsic & de La Bruyère argue China holds an advantage in critical and strategic minerals — "the upstream of legacy and emerging technological applications" — and is investing disproportionately in early-stage materials research, framing upstream materials as strategic leverage over downstream technologies. (Only the repository abstract was read; full text not retrieved this pass.) Data. Repository abstract / metadata — analytical, no primary dataset read.
  • CSIS — Critical Minerals Security Program · CSIS Ongoing think-tank research hub on minerals-supply-chain security. Summary. CSIS programme on US dependence on foreign adversaries for security-critical minerals, backing market-led expansion of production and processing and resilient supply chains through diplomacy and allied partnerships. Context for the materials layer beneath the fab. Data. Program landing page — analysis, reports and commentary, no primary dataset.

Across the chain — trade & data 4

The datasets the chain's own trade figures are built from — read as a coarse proxy, since no clean HS6 code isolates fab-grade resist or the specialty gases.
  • CEPII BACI V202601, based on UN Comtrade · CEPII Harmonized bilateral trade database (HS6, 1995–2024) used for the chain's trade proxy. Summary. CEPII database: BACI provides reconciled bilateral trade flows for ~200 countries across ~5,000 HS6 products, 1995–2024, derived from UN Comtrade by removing CIF freight/insurance and reliability-weighting mirror discrepancies. Supplies the chain's coarse HS-370790 photo-chemical resist proxy. Data. UN Comtrade import/export declarations, HS6 bilateral, mirror-reconciled (CIF/FOB, reporter-reliability weighting).
  • UN Comtrade · United Nations Statistics Division The raw customs declarations that BACI reconciles. Summary. The UN Statistics Division's repository of official international merchandise-trade statistics, reported by ~200 countries at HS-code level — the underlying record (photographic / photoresist chemical preparations, specialty gases) that BACI cleans and reconciles. Data. National customs declarations compiled by UNSD (Comtrade Plus platform), HS-level. (Live page is a JavaScript app shell — summarised from the platform's documented scope, not scraped this pass.)
  • SEMI — market data (equipment, materials & fab capacity) · SEMI SEMI's broader industry-statistics service beyond the materials segment. Summary. SEMI's industry-statistics service — equipment, materials and fab-capacity market data for the semiconductor supply chain, from the global materials-and-equipment industry association. The source of the materials figures behind the photoresist framing; detailed data is subscription-only. Data. SEMI member surveys and market-statistics program (subscription).
  • Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA) · SIA US industry body publishing supply-chain reports and policy analysis. Summary. The Semiconductor Industry Association, the US industry body, publishes supply-chain and policy analysis (annual State of the Industry, CHIPS-Act work, the BCG/SIA supply-chain studies) covering chip manufacturing and its material inputs. (Homepage returned 403 on this pass; described from its known, documented role, not scraped.) Data. SIA reports and industry statistics — no single dataset.
