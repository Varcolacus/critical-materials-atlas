Research Library · Rhenium

Rhenium chain

A curated reference library of public sources for deeper study — gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source and carries an abstract (or factual summary) plus the data it rests on. A research-support layer of the Critical Materials Atlas.

Rhenium is a by-product of a by-product — recovered from the flue dust of roasting molybdenum, itself mostly a copper by-product — yet a few percent of it in a single-crystal nickel superalloy is what lets a jet-engine turbine blade run hotter. No one mines rhenium; its supply is set by copper and molybdenum output, not by its own demand.

Ordered to follow the chain: ① Copper → molybdenum (host) → ② rhenium by-product recovery (roasting flue dust → ammonium perrhenate → metal) → ③ superalloys → single-crystal turbine blades (plus petroleum-reforming catalysts) → recycling — then the cross-cutting frameworks and trade data that sit across every stage.

① Copper → molybdenum → rhenium 4

The raw input, and a by-product no one mines for: world output ~81 t, Chile ~37% (USGS 2026) — market-intelligence sources claim nearer half; we use the USGS read.
  • USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Rhenium · USGS (2026) Annual world production/reserves/uses data for rhenium; ~81 t world output, Chile ~37%. Abstract. USGS commodity summary: 2025 US primary rhenium output rose 5% to 9,800 kg, recovered as ammonium perrhenate and metal powder from roasting molybdenum concentrates of porphyry copper–molybdenum deposits in Arizona and Montana. Superalloy turbine components (80%) and petroleum-reforming catalysts (15%) dominate use; net import reliance 75%. World output ~81,000 kg, Chile ~37%. Data. USGS annual producer canvass plus reported trade and price (Argus Media) data; world mine-production and reserves table.
  • The availability of primary rhenium as a by-product of copper and molybdenum mining · Mineral Economics / Springer (2023) Models how much rhenium in copper–moly ore is actually captured versus geologically present. Abstract. Bottom-up model tracking rhenium from deposit grades through mining, concentration and processing, 2000–2019. Finds less than 12% of the rhenium present in ore is actually captured (30–44% at technical recovery limits), with ~32% lost during ore concentration alone. Mean recoverable rhenium 2015–2019 was 161–213 t/yr out of 599–732 t geologically present. Data. Deposit Re grades from Re–Os geochronology literature; production data covering 96.7% of copper and 83.8% of molybdenum mines; concentrate trade flows and per-stage recovery rates.
  • Assessing the by-production of critical materials: the rhenium case study · AGU Fall Meeting / ADS (2018) Early academic framing of rhenium as a textbook by-production criticality case. Abstract. Conference abstract framing rhenium as a by-production case: because it is recovered only as a by-product of molybdenum — itself a copper by-product — its availability is decoupled from its own demand. Full abstract text not retrievable this pass (405); framing taken from the ADS record title and library note. Data. Conference abstract (AGU / ADS bibliographic record) — no dataset presented on the page.
  • BGS, World Mineral Statistics — rhenium · British Geological Survey (2024) World production statistics database by commodity and country, including minor metals. Summary. BGS World Mineral Statistics database page not retrievable this pass (404). BGS's long-running world production dataset by commodity and country, including minor metals such as rhenium — used as a non-US cross-check on the USGS output read. Data. British Geological Survey compilation of national mine-production returns, by commodity and country (annual series).

② Rhenium metal & ammonium perrhenate — the market 3

Perrhenate to metal: a tiny, concentrated, near-monopolistic trade priced off copper and molybdenum.
  • The Rhenium Market and rhenium price drivers · SFA (Oxford) Minor-metals consultancy overview of rhenium market structure and price drivers. Summary. Minor-metals consultancy overview: rhenium is recovered only as a molybdenum/copper by-product from porphyry deposits, so actual output is far below geological availability; aerospace superalloys dominate demand, with prices driven by copper/moly volumes, roasting and recovery capacity, and recycling. Data. Qualitative market analysis (SFA Oxford) — no quantitative production or price series on the page.
  • Global Market of Rhenium · Quest Metals Overview of global rhenium production, demand and trade structure. Summary. Market overview: rhenium supply is tied to molybdenum/copper output; Chile's Molymet is described as the largest producer at nearly half of global supply, Poland's KGHM ~7%, with a concentrated, near-monopolistic trade and aerospace-plus-catalyst demand. Data. Trade commentary citing academic sources (Int. J. Refractory Metals 2019; Resources Policy 2023) — limited primary figures.
  • Rhenium Surge · Quest Metals Market commentary on rhenium price spikes. Summary. Commentary on a rhenium price spike: European catalytic-grade ammonium perrhenate rose toward $2,300–2,400/kg and Chinese APR ~17%; aerospace is ~75% of demand; notes China imported 26 t of rhenium from Chile in 2023 versus 2 t in 2018. Data. Market/broker commentary and producer statements; cites a commercial market-outlook report — figures unverified.

③ Superalloys & single-crystal turbine blades 4

The defining use — 2–6% rhenium raises the temperature a blade survives — and the scarcity that pushes engineers to design it back out.
  • Industry / NRC — rhenium in single-crystal superalloys · Industry / NRC context (2024) Background context, cited in the chain record, on rhenium's role in single-crystal nickel superalloys. Summary. General industry / NRC context (cited in the chain record) on rhenium's role in single-crystal nickel superalloys, where a few percent of rhenium raises the temperature a turbine blade can survive and improves creep resistance. Record carries only a generic institutional link — no specific document retrievable. Data. Cited as background context in the chain record; no specific dataset or page behind the generic link.
  • Low rhenium single crystal superalloy for turbine blades and vane applications · Cannon-Muskegon, patent (2018) Patent literature on reducing rhenium content while preserving high-temperature creep resistance. Summary. Patent application (Cannon-Muskegon): a single-crystal nickel superalloy holding rhenium to just 1.4–1.6% — roughly half of CMSX-4's ~3% — while keeping high-temperature creep and rupture life, evidencing scarcity-driven pressure to cut rhenium content. Data. Patent specification with claimed alloy-composition ranges and rupture-life conditions — no external dataset.
  • Rhenium-free single crystal superalloy for turbine blades and vane applications · Google Patents · EP2612936B9 Patent literature on eliminating rhenium from single-crystal superalloys — substitution pressure from scarcity. Summary. Patent (Cannon-Muskegon): a rhenium-free single-crystal nickel superalloy for turbine blades and vanes that rebalances tantalum, tungsten and molybdenum (17–20% refractory total) to keep creep resistance without rhenium's usual 3–7% — the strongest form of the scarcity-driven substitution signal. Data. Patent specification (composition ranges, density, creep claims) — no dataset.
  • Single Crystal Superalloy Turbine Blades Market — Global Forecast 2025-2032 · ResearchAndMarkets (2025) Commercial market-sizing report for the downstream product rhenium enables. Summary. Commercial market-sizing report for the product rhenium enables: single-crystal superalloy turbine blades valued ~$724M in 2025, forecast to ~$1.15B by 2032 (6.79% CAGR); segments by casting process and alloy grade (CMSX-4/10, René N5). Data. Proprietary market model from primary interviews, technical literature and filings — paywalled; figures from the public overview.

Scarcity managed by recycling 1

Because rhenium cannot be scaled, supply is stretched by reclaiming it from scrapped blades and spent catalysts.
  • The Ongoing Deficit Of Rhenium May Lead To An Increase In Recycling Efforts · Quest Metals Market commentary on structural rhenium deficits and the push toward recycling. Summary. Commentary: a structural rhenium deficit against ~50 t/yr demand (jet blades ~40 t) is pushing recycling; current recovery is 25–50%, with up to ~80% claimed recoverable from post-use scrap, plus ~4–15 t/yr recovered from spent petroleum-reforming catalysts. Data. Market commentary citing Werner et al. 2023 (Resources Policy) and Argus Media — figures not independently sourced.
