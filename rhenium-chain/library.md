# Rhenium chain — research library

Rhenium is a by-product of a by-product — recovered from the flue dust of roasting molybdenum, itself mostly a by-product of copper — yet a few percent of it in single-crystal nickel superalloy is what lets a jet-engine turbine blade run hotter. A curated reference library for deeper study — public sources, gathered August 2026. Each entry links to a real source.

## Academic papers & reviews
- [The availability of primary rhenium as a by-product of copper and molybdenum mining](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13563-023-00392-0) — Mineral Economics / Springer Nature (2023). Models how much rhenium is actually captured vs. geologically present in copper-moly ore (found only ~12% captured, up to 30-44% at technical recovery limits).
- [Assessing the by-production of critical materials: the rhenium case study](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018AGUFM.V31G0200B/abstract) — AGU Fall Meeting abstract, via ADS (2018). Early academic framing of rhenium as a textbook by-production criticality case.
- [Low rhenium single crystal superalloy for turbine blades and vane applications](https://www.freepatentsonline.com/y2018/0100401.html) — Cannon-Muskegon Corporation, patent (2018). Technical patent literature on reducing rhenium content in single-crystal superalloys while preserving high-temperature creep resistance.
- [Rhenium-free single crystal superalloy for turbine blades and vane applications](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP2612936B9/en) — Google Patents, EP2612936B9. Patent literature on eliminating rhenium from single-crystal superalloys, evidence of substitution pressure from scarcity.
